Assessment

Essential hypertension of approximately 9 years’ duration with a current office blood pressure of 164/97 mmHg and no established treatment documented. The associated dizziness and fatigue may occur in the setting of poorly controlled blood pressure but are nonspecific. She denies chest pain, dyspnea, visual disturbance, severe headache, palpitations, and epistaxis, and there is no current history suggesting hypertensive emergency. Given the prolonged duration and lack of active management, confirmation of persistent blood pressure elevation and timely initiation of evidence-based therapy are indicated.

Management is particularly important because of the reported comorbid chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and hyperlipidemia, all of which increase long-term cardiovascular and renal risk. Current sodium and potassium levels are 139 and 3.7 mmol/L, respectively; these values do not identify an electrolyte contraindication to commonly used antihypertensive strategies, although renal function and urinary protein status should guide medication selection and monitoring. Her normal BMI does not suggest obesity as a contributing factor. Tobacco use is an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement after appropriate seated rest and arrange home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, including measurement technique education. Confirm persistent hypertension across multiple readings rather than relying on a single office value, while recognizing that the current reading is sufficiently elevated to warrant prompt follow-up.

Discuss initiation of antihypertensive pharmacotherapy at an early follow-up visit, with selection informed by chronic kidney disease, diabetes, renal function, and the presence or absence of albuminuria. Renal function and potassium should be monitored after initiation or dose adjustment of therapies affecting the renin-angiotensin system. No specific medication regimen is documented today.

Recommend evaluation with urinalysis, assessment of renal function, lipid profile, and electrocardiogram as clinically appropriate for baseline cardiovascular and end-organ assessment. Continue coordinated management of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and chronic kidney disease.

Counsel regarding reduction of dietary sodium, avoidance of excess alcohol if applicable, regular physical activity as tolerated, smoking cessation, and adherence to future blood pressure monitoring and treatment. Because she reports dizziness, she should rise slowly and avoid driving or hazardous activity if symptoms are active or severe.

She was advised to seek emergency care for severe or persistent headache, new visual loss, chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, confusion, syncope, or markedly worsening blood pressure accompanied by symptoms. Prompt outpatient follow-up is recommended to review home readings, complete cardiovascular and renal risk assessment, and establish a durable hypertension treatment plan.
