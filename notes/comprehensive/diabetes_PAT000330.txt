Chief Complaint

Annual review of type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic medical conditions.

History of Present Illness

Victor Pierce is a 33-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 4-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus that has not been actively managed. Current laboratory data show HbA1c 9.7% and fasting glucose 222 mg/dL, consistent with persistent hyperglycemia. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, or unintentional weight loss. He also denies slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet. There is no reported history of diabetes-related complications.

His weight is 93.8 kg at a height of 161 cm, with BMI 36.2 kg/m². Obesity has been present for approximately 10 years. He has dyslipidemia of approximately 2 years’ duration and depression of approximately 10 years’ duration. He does not smoke. No current diabetes medication regimen is provided.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue and unintentional weight loss.

Endocrine: Denies increased thirst, frequent urination, and increased hunger.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Neurologic: Denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet.

Skin and infectious: Denies slow-healing wounds and frequent infections.

The available review is otherwise not contributory to today’s concerns.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 115/77 mmHg, pulse 79 beats per minute, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 161 cm. Weight 93.8 kg. BMI 36.2 kg/m².

Laboratory data: HbA1c 9.7%. Fasting glucose 222 mg/dL.

Physical examination: The patient’s available objective data demonstrate obesity with normal blood pressure, pulse, temperature, and oxygen saturation. No additional examination findings are provided today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications. Glycemic control is substantially above goal, with HbA1c 9.7% and fasting glucose 222 mg/dL after approximately 4 years of disease and no established management plan. Although he currently denies polyuria, polydipsia, visual changes, constitutional symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, wounds, or recurrent infections, the degree of hyperglycemia places him at increased risk for future microvascular and cardiovascular complications. There is no current evidence in the available history of neuropathy, infection, or other symptomatic complication.

Obesity, BMI 36.2 kg/m², longstanding for approximately 10 years and an important contributor to insulin resistance and diabetes risk.

Dyslipidemia, present for approximately 2 years. The available lipid values are not provided.

Depression, longstanding for approximately 10 years. Current symptom burden and treatment status require review as part of comprehensive chronic disease care.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of establishing active diabetes management. Recommend prompt initiation of a structured diabetes treatment plan, including nutrition counseling, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, and gradual progression toward at least 150 minutes of moderate physical activity weekly as tolerated. Weight reduction should be addressed as a central component of diabetes care.

Pharmacologic therapy should be discussed and initiated based on shared decision-making, comorbidities, contraindications, and renal function. Metformin would be a reasonable first-line consideration if renal function permits; given the HbA1c of 9.7%, additional therapy may be needed if adequate improvement is not achieved. Medication reconciliation and assessment of prior diabetes treatment are recommended before finalizing the regimen.

Obtain renal assessment with serum creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate, along with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, to evaluate for occult diabetic kidney disease and to guide medication selection. A fasting lipid profile is recommended to reassess dyslipidemia and cardiovascular risk. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval to evaluate response to treatment.

Recommend routine comprehensive dilated retinal examination because of the duration and current degree of hyperglycemia. Perform a complete diabetic foot examination, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensory testing, at follow-up and at least annually. Counsel regarding daily foot inspection, appropriate footwear, and prompt evaluation of wounds or infections.

Blood pressure is currently normal at 115/77 mmHg. Continue lifestyle-based cardiovascular risk reduction. Smoking cessation counseling is not indicated at present because he reports no tobacco use.

Review depression symptoms, functional status, and current supports using a standardized screening tool, with treatment planning guided by symptom severity and patient preference. Encourage coordinated attention to mood, sleep, activity, and weight because these factors may affect diabetes self-management.

Preventive care should be updated according to age-appropriate recommendations, including immunization review and routine screening based on personal and family risk factors. Follow up soon after initiation of diabetes management to review home glucose data, treatment tolerance, adherence, and laboratory evaluation rather than waiting for the next annual visit.
