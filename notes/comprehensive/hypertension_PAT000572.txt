Chief Complaint

Annual review of hypertension and chronic medical conditions.

Subjective

Victor Pierce is a 79-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 37-year history of essential hypertension that is currently not actively managed. Today’s blood pressure is elevated at 169/89 mmHg. He reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency.

Medical history is significant for coronary artery disease for approximately 27 years, hyperlipidemia for 48 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 6 years. He continues to smoke. Height is 184 cm and weight is 93.8 kg, with BMI 27.7 kg/m².

Review of systems is otherwise negative based on the information provided. No focal neurologic symptoms, acute visual change, respiratory distress, or ischemic chest symptoms are reported.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 169/89 mmHg. Pulse 77 beats/minute. Respiratory rate 18/minute. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Height 184 cm. Weight 93.8 kg. BMI 27.7 kg/m².

General: Older adult male seen for outpatient follow-up.

Cardiovascular: Pulse 77 beats/minute. No chest pain or dyspnea reported.

Respiratory: Respiratory rate 18/minute with oxygen saturation 96% on room air. No shortness of breath reported.

Neurologic: Dizziness reported intermittently. No headache, blurred vision, or other focal neurologic symptoms reported.

Laboratory data: Sodium 141 mmol/L. Potassium 5.3 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension of longstanding duration, with current blood pressure 169/89 mmHg and inadequate control in the setting of no active management. The patient has associated dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue but no current symptoms of hypertensive crisis. His advanced age and established coronary artery disease, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, tobacco use, and overweight status place him at substantially increased cardiovascular risk.

Mild potassium elevation at 5.3 mmol/L. This warrants confirmation and assessment of renal function before selecting or initiating antihypertensive therapy, particularly therapies that may further increase potassium.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding. He denies current chest pain or shortness of breath, but uncontrolled blood pressure and ongoing smoking increase cardiovascular risk.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 6 years’ duration. Blood pressure control is particularly important for vascular and renal risk reduction.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Overweight by BMI, with BMI 27.7 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current elevated blood pressure and the importance of establishing a consistent treatment plan. Repeat blood pressure measurement after seated rest and home blood pressure monitoring are recommended, with documentation of readings and symptoms. Given the persistent elevation and cardiovascular comorbidities, prompt outpatient reassessment is appropriate rather than continued observation without treatment.

Repeat serum electrolytes, including potassium, and obtain renal function testing before initiating or titrating antihypertensive therapy. Medication selection should account for the potassium of 5.3 mmol/L, coronary artery disease, diabetes, age, and risk of orthostatic symptoms. Treatment should begin cautiously with monitoring for dizziness, falls, renal function changes, and electrolyte abnormalities.

Recommend ECG for assessment of palpitations and hypertensive cardiovascular effects. Recommend urinalysis and assessment of urine protein, along with diabetes-related monitoring, as clinically appropriate. A lipid profile should be reviewed or obtained to guide cardiovascular risk reduction.

Counseled regarding a lower-sodium eating pattern, regular activity appropriate to functional capacity, weight maintenance or modest weight reduction, and avoidance of excess alcohol if applicable. Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered cessation support.

Continue preventive care appropriate for age and comorbid disease, including review of immunizations, fall-risk assessment, vision care, diabetes complications, and age-appropriate cancer screening based on prior screening history and overall health goals.

He should seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, severe headache, new neurologic deficit, acute visual loss, severe shortness of breath, syncope, or markedly worsening palpitations. Arrange close follow-up after repeat blood pressure and laboratory assessment to establish and monitor antihypertensive treatment.
