Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for diabetes management and elevated blood glucose.

Subjective

Richard Lawson is a 71-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. Current diabetes control has not been actively managed. Recent HbA1c is 9.1% and fasting glucose is 189 mg/dL.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No acute hyperglycemic symptoms are reported.

Relevant history includes longstanding obesity, approximately 30 years in duration. Current weight is 85.8 kg at 164 cm, BMI 31.9 kg/m². He currently smokes. No diabetes medications are provided in the current history.

Constitutional: Denies fatigue and unintentional weight loss.
Endocrine: Denies increased thirst, frequent urination, and increased hunger.
Eyes: Denies blurred vision.
Skin: Denies slow-healing wounds.
Infectious: Denies frequent infections.
Neurologic: Denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently significantly above individualized glycemic target based on HbA1c 9.1% and fasting glucose 189 mg/dL. Although he denies polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual changes, neuropathic symptoms, or recurrent infections, treatment should be intensified promptly to reduce risk of future microvascular and cardiovascular complications.

Obesity, BMI 31.9 kg/m², longstanding.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed laboratory results and the importance of active diabetes treatment despite the absence of symptoms. Recommend prompt initiation of an evidence-based glucose-lowering regimen after reviewing contraindications, renal function, medication preferences, cost, and ability to monitor glucose. Medication reconciliation and shared decision-making are needed at the next treatment discussion; no current diabetes medication regimen is documented.

Begin structured nutrition counseling focused on reducing refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened foods, portion control, and weight reduction. Encourage gradual, regular physical activity as tolerated.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with documentation of fasting and occasional postprandial readings to guide treatment adjustment and review for symptomatic hyperglycemia.

Recommend assessment of serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before or during treatment planning, along with a fasting lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment. Recommend routine comprehensive foot examination and diabetic retinal evaluation, as clinically appropriate.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and offered support with a cessation plan.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including persistent vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, marked weakness or confusion, severe drowsiness, or rapidly worsening hyperglycemic symptoms.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up within 1–2 weeks to review glucose readings and establish or adjust therapy, with repeat HbA1c monitoring after treatment is established.
