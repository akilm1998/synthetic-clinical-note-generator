Assessment and Plan

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function

Longstanding chronic kidney disease of approximately 29 years’ duration with creatinine 3.02 mg/dL, BUN 89 mg/dL, and eGFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m². The elevated BUN and reduced filtration are consistent with substantial chronic renal impairment. She has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, change in urination, edema, or shortness of breath to suggest clinically overt fluid overload or a symptomatic uremic syndrome at this visit. Blood pressure is above ideal for a patient with chronic kidney disease.

Recommend prompt nephrology involvement for comprehensive assessment, clarification of disease trajectory, renal-protective management, and discussion of future planning based on renal function, symptoms, overall health, and patient goals. Obtain or update serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Renal ultrasound may be considered if structural assessment has not been performed or if the underlying cause is unclear.

Perform complete medication reconciliation before prescribing. Review all over-the-counter products and avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Ensure all medications are appropriately renally dosed. Counsel regarding prompt evaluation for worsening fatigue, nausea or vomiting, confusion, new itching, progressive weakness, reduced urine output, edema, or shortness of breath.

2. Blood pressure elevation in the setting of chronic kidney disease

Blood pressure today is 143/87 mmHg. Confirm readings with repeat office measurements and, if feasible, home monitoring. Review sodium intake, volume status, adherence once the medication list is available, and the presence of albuminuria before selecting or adjusting therapy. Treatment should be individualized for age, orthostatic symptoms, kidney function, and overall goals of care.

3. Secondary hyperparathyroidism

History of secondary hyperparathyroidism for approximately 32 years in the setting of chronic kidney disease. Recommend reassessment of calcium, phosphorus, alkaline phosphatase, and intact parathyroid hormone, with management coordinated with nephrology. Avoid empiric calcium or phosphate-binding therapy until the current medication list and mineral parameters have been reviewed.

4. Gout

Gout diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. No current flare symptoms are reported. Review prior flare frequency, urate-lowering therapy, analgesic use, and renal dosing. Avoid NSAIDs given the degree of renal impairment unless specifically directed by a specialist. Any urate-lowering treatment should be selected and dosed with kidney function in mind.

5. Fatigue

Fatigue may be multifactorial and may relate in part to advanced chronic renal impairment, although anemia and other reversible contributors should be assessed. Recommend CBC, thyroid testing, nutritional review, sleep assessment, mood screening, and medication review as clinically appropriate. Monitor for progression or development of exertional dyspnea, chest discomfort, bleeding, or functional decline.

6. Overweight status

BMI is 28.7 kg/m². Encourage a kidney-conscious, heart-healthy eating pattern with attention to sodium intake and individualized protein, potassium, and phosphorus recommendations once laboratory values are available. Encourage activity appropriate to functional status, balance, and fall risk rather than aggressive weight loss.

7. Preventive and geriatric health maintenance

Review immunization status and update age- and risk-appropriate influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, shingles, and tetanus vaccination as indicated. Assess fall risk, gait and balance, vision, hearing, cognition, mood, nutrition, dental care, and ability to perform instrumental activities of daily living. Review osteoporosis risk and ensure bone-health assessment is appropriate for age and chronic kidney disease.

Continue age-appropriate cancer screening only when consistent with life expectancy, functional status, and patient preferences. Discuss advance care planning and health-care decision-maker designation. Reinforce avoidance of tobacco; she reports that she does not smoke.

Follow-up

Arrange expedited follow-up for review of medication reconciliation, renal assessment, blood pressure management, and recommended laboratory testing. Continue routine primary care follow-up with earlier evaluation for any new uremic or volume-overload symptoms.
