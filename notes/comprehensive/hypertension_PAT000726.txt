Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Nicole Harper is a 47-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension, first identified approximately 1 year ago. Blood pressure remains elevated today, and her hypertension has not been actively managed with a documented treatment regimen. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and nosebleeds.

Relevant medical history includes coronary artery disease for approximately 1 year, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 17 years, and hyperlipidemia for 8 years. She does not smoke. Current weight is 54.5 kg at a height of 153 cm, with BMI 23.3 kg/m². No medication or allergy information is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 154/96 mmHg. Pulse 77 bpm. Respiratory rate 14/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

General: No acute hypertensive symptoms reported.

Cardiopulmonary: Heart rate regular by vital sign assessment. Respiratory rate and oxygenation are normal.

Laboratory data: Serum sodium 142 mmol/L. Serum potassium 3.7 mmol/L.

Review of systems is otherwise notable for absence of headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure of 154/96 mmHg. She is asymptomatic without reported features concerning for hypertensive emergency. Given the duration of hypertension, diabetes, and known coronary artery disease, improved blood pressure control is important for cardiovascular and renal risk reduction. Serum sodium and potassium are currently within expected ranges, although potassium is toward the lower end of the reference range.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, approximately 17 years.

Coronary artery disease, approximately 1 year.

Hyperlipidemia, approximately 8 years.

Normal BMI of 23.3 kg/m². Patient is a nonsmoker.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated blood pressure and the need for consistent outpatient management. Recommend confirming the pattern of elevation with properly obtained home blood pressure readings, including measurements at different times of day, and bringing the log to follow-up.

Recommend initiation of antihypertensive therapy after medication reconciliation and review of renal function, with selection influenced by her diabetes and coronary artery disease. An ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker would be reasonable to consider if clinically appropriate; a calcium-channel blocker or thiazide-type diuretic may also be considered based on the complete medication and renal profile. Do not start or adjust therapy without reviewing current medications and contraindications.

Recommend a low-sodium, heart-healthy eating pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and continued tobacco avoidance. Her BMI is currently in the normal range; maintain current healthy weight.

Recommend obtaining an ECG, urinalysis, lipid profile, and renal function assessment as part of cardiovascular risk and hypertension evaluation. Continue coordinated management of diabetes, coronary artery disease, and hyperlipidemia.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including severe or sudden headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, vision loss, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up in the outpatient setting after home blood pressure monitoring and medication review, sooner for worsening symptoms or persistently severe readings.
