Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Emily Cooper is a 72-year-old woman, 169 cm tall and 72.8 kg (BMI 25.5 kg/m²), presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 19 years. Current management has not been established. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath.

She has secondary hyperparathyroidism of approximately 3 years’ duration. Medication use was not provided. She is a current smoker. No interval hospitalization, procedure, or other acute change is reported.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Positive for fatigue.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.

Gastrointestinal: Denies nausea.

Genitourinary: Denies change in urination frequency.

Musculoskeletal: Denies muscle cramps.

Skin: Denies pruritus.

Neurologic/Sleep: Denies sleep disturbance.

Cardiovascular/Peripheral vascular: Denies peripheral edema.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 139/91 mmHg, pulse 81/min, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Weight 72.8 kg; height 169 cm; BMI 25.5 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Serum creatinine 4.06 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m². BUN 81 mg/dL.

Physical examination: No peripheral edema is reported. Respiratory rate and oxygen saturation are within acceptable range at rest. The remainder of the examination is directed toward assessment of chronic kidney disease and its complications.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, present for approximately 19 years, with creatinine 4.06 mg/dL, eGFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 81 mg/dL. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal impairment. She currently denies symptoms suggestive of fluid overload or prominent uremic symptoms, including nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, dyspnea, edema, or urinary frequency change. Given the degree of renal dysfunction and lack of established management, she requires prompt comprehensive renal evaluation and longitudinal nephrology care.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, present for approximately 3 years, requiring reassessment as a complication of chronic kidney disease.

Blood pressure is mildly elevated, particularly diastolic, at 139/91 mmHg. No antihypertensive regimen was provided for review.

Current tobacco use. Smoking cessation is strongly advised, particularly in the setting of severe chronic kidney disease and cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive management of significantly reduced kidney function, evaluation for complications, and discussion of future renal replacement therapy planning as clinically appropriate.

Obtain updated renal evaluation, including serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, intact parathyroid hormone, complete blood count, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and repeat renal function testing. Renal ultrasound is recommended if not previously completed or if clinically indicated.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications at the next visit and avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a treating clinician. Medication dosing should be adjusted for renal function.

Monitor blood pressure outside the office if possible and bring readings to follow-up. Dietary counseling should include sodium moderation and individualized renal dietary guidance pending nephrology evaluation.

Discuss symptoms requiring urgent assessment, including new or worsening shortness of breath, rapidly progressive swelling, chest pain, persistent vomiting, confusion, marked reduction in urination, or profound weakness.

Provide smoking-cessation counseling and offer assistance with a cessation plan.

Follow up promptly after laboratory evaluation and nephrology consultation, with earlier assessment for any worsening symptoms.
