Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and markedly abnormal renal function.

Subjective

Alexander Fisher is a 62-year-old man, height 163 cm, weight 58.7 kg, BMI 22.1 kg/m², presenting for urgent outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. Current management has not been established. Recent laboratory results show creatinine 5.58 mg/dL, BUN 69 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m².

He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. No acute constitutional or cardiopulmonary complaints reported today. He continues to smoke. History is also significant for anemia of chronic disease for approximately 7 years.

Medication and allergy information are not provided.

Respiratory: Respirations even and unlabored; oxygenation is normal.

Serum creatinine: 5.58 mg/dL
BUN: 69 mg/dL

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with severely reduced renal function and significant azotemia. Creatinine 5.58 mg/dL, BUN 69 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m² are concerning for advanced renal impairment. Although he currently denies uremic, fluid-overload, or cardiopulmonary symptoms, the absence of symptoms does not exclude clinically important electrolyte, acid-base, or volume complications. His kidney disease has been present for approximately 4 years and is currently without an established management plan.

2. Anemia of chronic disease, present for approximately 7 years. Current hemoglobin and iron studies are not available for review.

3. Elevated blood pressure, 152/84 mmHg, in the setting of significant chronic kidney disease.

4. Active tobacco use.

Plan

Due to the markedly reduced estimated GFR and elevated creatinine/BUN, advised same-day evaluation in the emergency department or other setting capable of urgent renal assessment. He needs prompt evaluation for electrolyte abnormalities, acid-base disturbance, volume status, and other complications of advanced kidney dysfunction. He should not drive himself if he develops weakness, confusion, dizziness, chest pain, worsening shortness of breath, vomiting, or other acute symptoms.

Recommend urgent nephrology consultation for comprehensive CKD management and discussion of renal replacement planning if clinically indicated. Recommended testing includes repeat renal function and serum electrolyte panel, CBC for anemia assessment, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and consideration of renal ultrasound and intact parathyroid hormone testing as part of CKD evaluation.

Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications unless specifically approved by a clinician. Avoid self-directed use of supplements or salt substitutes until renal function and electrolytes have been assessed. Review all current medications with the treating clinician before use or dose changes.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, blood pressure control, and the importance of urgent follow-through despite currently minimal symptoms. Close follow-up is required after emergency/renal evaluation.
