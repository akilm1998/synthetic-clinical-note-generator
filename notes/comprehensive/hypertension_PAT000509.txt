Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure with dizziness and palpitations.

Subjective

Ashton Murray is a 79-year-old male presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding essential hypertension, present for approximately 21 years and currently without active management. Blood pressure today is 166/98 mmHg. He reports dizziness and intermittent palpitations. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, and epistaxis. He does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 44 years and hyperlipidemia for approximately 22 years. Current medication use, allergies, and prior antihypertensive treatment are not provided. Height is 172 cm, weight 76.9 kg, and BMI 26.0 kg/m².

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 166/98 mmHg; pulse 76 bpm; respiratory rate 18/min; temperature 36.8°C; oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

General: Hemodynamically stable by current vital signs, without reported chest pain, dyspnea, or acute visual symptoms.

Cardiovascular: Pulse 76 bpm. Patient reports palpitations; no additional auscultatory findings provided.

Respiratory: Respiratory rate 18/min and oxygen saturation 98%; no reported shortness of breath.

Neurologic: Dizziness reported. No headache or blurred vision reported.

Laboratory Data: Sodium 142 mmol/L; potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent elevation at 166/98 mmHg in the setting of approximately 21 years of disease and no current management. The patient has associated dizziness and palpitations but no reported chest pain, dyspnea, severe headache, visual disturbance, or epistaxis to suggest a hypertensive emergency based on the information available today. Given his age and longstanding hypertension, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia, he is at increased cardiovascular and renal risk and warrants prompt reassessment and initiation or adjustment of treatment by the treating clinician.

Dizziness and palpitations accompanying the elevated blood pressure. These symptoms require clinical reassessment, including rhythm evaluation, particularly if persistent, worsening, or associated with syncope, chest pain, or shortness of breath.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, increasing overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding, adding to cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure after a period of seated rest using an appropriately sized cuff and begin home blood pressure monitoring with documented morning and evening readings. Arrange prompt outpatient clinician follow-up to establish an antihypertensive regimen; medication selection should account for his age, diabetes, renal risk, potassium of 4.8 mmol/L, and overall cardiovascular profile. Avoid empiric medication assumptions until the current medication and allergy history are confirmed.

Obtain an ECG as a recommended evaluation for palpitations and longstanding hypertension. Consider urinalysis, renal function assessment, and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular and diabetic risk evaluation; only sodium and potassium results are available today.

Review dietary sodium reduction, avoidance of excess alcohol, regular activity as tolerated, and continued smoking avoidance. Maintain a balanced diet appropriate for both hypertension and diabetes.

Seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic deficit, confusion, severe or sudden headache, acute visual change, syncope, or markedly worsening palpitations or dizziness. Recheck blood pressure and symptoms promptly rather than waiting for routine follow-up if readings remain substantially elevated.
