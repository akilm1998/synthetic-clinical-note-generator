Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Gabriel Marshall is an 85-year-old man, 172 cm tall and 65.1 kg (BMI 22.0 kg/m²), presenting for outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 9 years ago. His blood pressure has not been actively managed. Today he reports intermittent dizziness and palpitations. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, or epistaxis. He is a nonsmoker.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for approximately 15 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 5 years. No current antihypertensive regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital Signs:
Blood pressure 166/100 mmHg
Pulse 74 bpm
Respiratory rate 16/min
Temperature 36.7°C
Oxygen saturation 96% on room air
Height 172 cm
Weight 65.1 kg
BMI 22.0 kg/m²

Laboratory Data:
Sodium 142 mmol/L
Potassium 5.1 mmol/L

Review of Systems:
Neurologic: Positive for dizziness. Denies headache.
Cardiovascular: Positive for palpitations. Denies chest pain.
Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.
Eyes: Denies blurred vision.
Constitutional: Denies fatigue.
ENT: Denies epistaxis.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure at 166/100 mmHg in the setting of approximately 9 years of untreated or insufficiently managed disease. Dizziness and palpitations are present, without reported chest pain, dyspnea, severe headache, visual disturbance, or epistaxis to suggest an acute hypertensive emergency based on today’s history.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, with potassium 5.1 mmol/L. This is clinically relevant to antihypertensive selection and requires caution with therapies that may further increase potassium.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 5 years, increasing the patient’s cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Discussed that the current blood pressure is significantly above goal and warrants active outpatient management rather than observation alone. Recommend confirming blood pressure with repeat measurements and home monitoring using an appropriately sized cuff, with a written log of readings, pulse, symptoms, and timing.

Recommend prompt initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy through the treating clinician. Medication selection should account for advanced age, chronic kidney disease, diabetes, and potassium of 5.1 mmol/L. Avoid empiric escalation of agents that may increase potassium until renal function and repeat potassium are reviewed.

Recommend repeat serum electrolytes and assessment of renal function before and after initiating or changing therapy. Recommend urinalysis, lipid profile, and ECG as part of cardiovascular and renal risk assessment.

Counseled on limiting dietary sodium, avoiding nonprescription medications or supplements that may raise blood pressure or potassium unless cleared by his clinician, maintaining appropriate hydration, and rising slowly from sitting or lying positions because of dizziness risk.

Because of reported palpitations, advise prompt reassessment if symptoms persist or worsen. Seek emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, new neurologic symptoms, severe headache, acute visual changes, or persistently extreme blood pressure readings.

Follow up soon for blood pressure reassessment, review of home readings, medication decision, and repeat potassium/renal testing.
