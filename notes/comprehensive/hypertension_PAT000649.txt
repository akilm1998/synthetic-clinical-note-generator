Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for uncontrolled blood pressure with dizziness and fatigue.

Subjective

Julia Holland is a 46-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 9 years and currently without an established management regimen. She reports dizziness and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, and epistaxis. She is a nonsmoker.

Relevant comorbidities include hyperlipidemia for approximately 4 years and chronic kidney disease for approximately 2 years. Weight is stable by provided measurements, with BMI 21.4 kg/m². No medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 156/103 mmHg; pulse 72 bpm; respiratory rate 18/min; temperature 37.0°C; oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 168 cm; weight 60.4 kg; BMI 21.4 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Sodium 135 mmol/L; potassium 4.2 mmol/L.

Focused review of available examination data shows no reported chest pain, dyspnea, visual disturbance, palpitations, or epistaxis. No additional examination findings are documented in the supplied encounter data.

Assessment

Persistent essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure and associated dizziness and fatigue. She does not report symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency at this time, including chest pain, acute dyspnea, severe headache, visual change, or neurologic symptoms. However, the diastolic pressure is significantly elevated and warrants prompt treatment assessment rather than continued observation alone.

Chronic kidney disease increases the importance of reliable blood pressure control and monitoring of renal function and electrolytes. Current potassium is 4.2 mmol/L and sodium is 135 mmol/L. Hyperlipidemia is an additional cardiovascular risk factor. BMI is normal and she does not smoke.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement after seated rest and confirm the reading with appropriate cuff size and technique. Begin or promptly establish an antihypertensive treatment plan with consideration of renal function, albuminuria, and electrolyte status; medication selection should be individualized in the context of chronic kidney disease. Avoid assuming or restarting any prior regimen without medication reconciliation.

Obtain home blood pressure readings twice daily, seated and after several minutes of rest, and bring the log to follow-up. Counsel regarding sodium restriction, avoidance of excess alcohol, regular physical activity as tolerated, and maintenance of current healthy weight.

Recommend renal function assessment, including serum creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate, and urinalysis or urine albumin assessment to guide hypertension management in the setting of chronic kidney disease. Recommend ECG and updated lipid profile as cardiovascular risk assessment, with treatment decisions based on results and overall risk.

Because of current dizziness, advise caution with driving, climbing, or other activities where sudden lightheadedness could cause injury. Seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe or sudden headache, new neurologic deficit, confusion, fainting, acute visual change, or persistent worsening symptoms.

Arrange prompt outpatient reassessment for blood pressure response, dizziness, renal monitoring, and medication management.
