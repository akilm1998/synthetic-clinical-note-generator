Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced kidney function

Longstanding chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 13 years, with current creatinine 4.42 mg/dL, BUN 80 mg/dL, and eGFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m². The degree of renal impairment is substantial and warrants prompt nephrology-directed evaluation, even though he currently lacks nausea, pruritus, edema, dyspnea, urinary frequency changes, or other overt volume-overload symptoms. Fatigue may be related to advanced chronic kidney dysfunction, although anemia and other metabolic complications also require evaluation.

The condition has not been actively managed, and the absence of a documented treatment regimen increases concern for unaddressed complications, including electrolyte or acid-base abnormalities, anemia, mineral and bone disorder, hypertension, and progressive uremic symptoms.

2. Secondary hyperparathyroidism

Secondary hyperparathyroidism has been present for approximately 1 year in the setting of longstanding chronic kidney disease. Further characterization of mineral metabolism is indicated before treatment decisions are made.

3. Hypertension or elevated blood pressure in the setting of chronic kidney disease

Blood pressure today is 140/82 mmHg. This is above the preferred range for many patients with significant chronic kidney disease and should be confirmed with serial measurements. No antihypertensive medications were provided, so no assumptions are made regarding current therapy.

4. Tobacco use

Active smoking is an important modifiable cardiovascular and renal risk factor. Smoking cessation should be addressed as part of renal and cardiovascular risk reduction.

5. Overweight status

BMI is 29.4 kg/m². Weight management, dietary counseling, and avoidance of excessive sodium intake are appropriate in the context of chronic kidney disease and elevated blood pressure.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for comprehensive evaluation of markedly reduced kidney function and to establish ongoing renal care. The consultation should address evaluation for complications of advanced chronic kidney disease, renal replacement therapy education and planning when clinically appropriate, and determination of the underlying etiology if not previously established.

Obtain a complete metabolic assessment, including serum electrolytes and bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, and repeat renal function testing. Obtain complete blood count to evaluate fatigue and assess for anemia. Check intact parathyroid hormone and other relevant mineral-bone parameters. Obtain urinalysis and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio for proteinuria assessment. Renal ultrasound is reasonable if prior structural evaluation is not available or if clinically indicated.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplemental medications at follow-up with particular attention to renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents, especially nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. Medication decisions should be individualized after review of blood pressure, potassium, volume status, urine albumin excretion, and the complete metabolic profile.

Recommend a kidney-conscious diet emphasizing sodium restriction and avoidance of excessive dietary protein or phosphorus as advised by nephrology or a renal dietitian. Maintain appropriate hydration without excessive fluid intake, particularly if urine output or volume status changes.

Monitor for urgent symptoms, including new or worsening shortness of breath, progressive swelling, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, chest pain, or significant change in urine output; these symptoms require immediate medical assessment.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and reduction of cardiovascular risk. Continue blood pressure monitoring and arrange close primary care follow-up while nephrology care is being established.

Follow-up after completion of the recommended evaluation, or sooner for any worsening symptoms.
