She is not reporting osmotic or constitutional symptoms despite biochemical hyperglycemia. The absence of reported wounds or recurrent infections is reassuring; however, the distal numbness and tingling may represent diabetes-associated peripheral sensory symptoms and should be evaluated clinically rather than assumed to be medication-related.

Hypertension with above-goal office blood pressure of 146/86 mmHg in the setting of chronic kidney disease and diabetes.

Plan

The immediate priority is to establish and reconcile the complete medication list, including all prescription medications, over-the-counter products, and supplements, and to clarify whether she has been taking any glucose-lowering therapy, how consistently it is taken, and whether cost, access, dosing complexity, or adverse effects have limited adherence. Treatment should be intensified promptly rather than relying on lifestyle measures alone given the HbA1c of 9.3%.

Obtain current renal assessment, including serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, before selecting or dosing diabetes medications. A fasting lipid profile is also appropriate for comprehensive cardiometabolic risk assessment. These are recommendations for ongoing management and are not reported as completed today.

After medication reconciliation and review of kidney function, initiate or adjust glucose-lowering therapy using a regimen appropriate for chronic kidney disease. Metformin may be considered if renal function permits; an SGLT2 inhibitor may also be considered when appropriate for kidney function and individualized risk assessment. The final regimen should account for tolerability, adherence, hypoglycemia risk, renal dosing, and patient preference. She should be instructed to report symptomatic hypoglycemia, worsening paresthesias, new foot wounds, visual changes, polyuria, polydipsia, or unexplained weight loss.

Recommend a structured nutrition plan emphasizing reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened foods, portion control, increased fiber, and regular meal timing. Encourage gradual physical activity as tolerated, with attention to appropriate footwear and daily inspection of the feet. Weight reduction would likely improve glycemic and blood pressure control.

Perform a comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including monofilament sensation, skin inspection, and assessment of pulses, given her numbness and tingling. Recommend a dilated retinal examination as part of diabetes complication surveillance. Continue monitoring for neuropathic symptoms, wounds, and recurrent infections.

Blood pressure remains elevated at today’s measurement. Review current antihypertensive therapy during medication reconciliation and optimize treatment in the context of diabetes and chronic kidney disease, with attention to kidney-protective therapy when clinically appropriate.

Continue management of obstructive sleep apnea and reinforce adherence to its established treatment plan if one is in use; no specific device or medication is documented in this encounter.

Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval following treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, with earlier clinical review for medication intolerance, adherence barriers, or worsening hyperglycemic symptoms.
