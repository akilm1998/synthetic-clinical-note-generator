Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for anemia and headache.

Subjective

Spencer Lawson is a 66-year-old man, nonsmoker, presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding anemia, present for approximately 21 years. He reports headache and appears pale. He denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms are reported.

Relevant chronic conditions include rheumatoid arthritis for 8 years and inflammatory bowel disease for 5 years. His anemia has not been actively managed. Current medications, allergies, bleeding history, and recent changes in bowel symptoms are not provided in the available history.

Objective

Height 172 cm, weight 72.8 kg, BMI 24.6 kg/m². Blood pressure 125/80 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Patient is noted to be pale. Cardiovascular examination is normal, with no tachycardia noted. Respiratory examination is normal, without reported respiratory distress.

Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL, hematocrit 25.4%, RBC count 3.1 million/µL, and MCV 82 fL. Serum iron is 48 µg/dL, ferritin 177 ng/mL, TIBC 246 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 19.5%.

Assessment

Chronic anemia with a normocytic pattern and significant reduction in hemoglobin. The combination of low serum iron, relatively preserved ferritin, low TIBC, and chronic rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory bowel disease is most consistent with anemia related to chronic inflammation. Long duration and lack of active management warrant renewed evaluation. The reported headache and pallor may be associated with the anemia, although he currently has stable vital signs and no dyspnea, chest pain, palpitations, dizziness, or exercise intolerance.

Plan

Repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear is recommended promptly to confirm the current degree and pattern of anemia. Recommend evaluation with renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers, along with reassessment of iron studies as clinically indicated. Review for occult blood loss and current inflammatory bowel disease activity, including gastrointestinal symptoms, is important before selecting therapy.

Given hemoglobin of 8.6 g/dL, arrange timely outpatient evaluation and consider hematology consultation, particularly if the anemia is worsening, symptomatic, or not explained by control of underlying inflammatory disease. Treatment should be guided by the workup and may include management of the underlying inflammatory conditions and consideration of iron replacement or an erythropoietin-stimulating agent when clinically appropriate; no current anemia-directed medication is documented.

Seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, marked dizziness, palpitations, weakness, or evidence of gastrointestinal bleeding. Close follow-up is recommended after repeat testing to review results and establish an active management plan.
