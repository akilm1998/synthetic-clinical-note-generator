Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and markedly abnormal renal function.

Subjective

Charles Bennett is an 83-year-old man, 167 cm and 77.8 kg, with a BMI of 27.9 kg/m², longstanding chronic kidney disease of approximately 28 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 31 years, and hyperlipidemia for 48 years. He does not smoke. Renal disease has not been under active management. He presents for urgent review of serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 94 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m².

He reports fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath. No other acute symptoms are reported in the available history. There is no reported chest pain, confusion, or overt uremic symptomatology.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 149/91 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Anthropometrics: Height 167 cm; weight 77.8 kg; BMI 27.9 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL. BUN 94 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m².

Focused examination is notable for the documented vital signs. No peripheral edema, dyspnea, or other fluid-overload symptoms are reported. No additional examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with severely reduced renal function and substantial azotemia, in the setting of a 28-year history of kidney disease and longstanding type 2 diabetes. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction, although anemia and other metabolic complications require evaluation. Despite the absence of nausea, pruritus, confusion, dyspnea, edema, or urine output change, the current renal indices warrant urgent specialist assessment for complications and possible need for renal replacement therapy.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, with chronic kidney disease.

Hyperlipidemia.

Elevated blood pressure today at 149/91 mmHg. No medication regimen is documented.

Plan

Given creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 94 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m², recommend same-day contact with nephrology or immediate emergency department evaluation if urgent nephrology assessment is not readily available. He should proceed directly to emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, marked weakness, persistent vomiting, inability to maintain intake, new swelling, or a substantial reduction in urination.

Recommend urgent repeat renal function and serum electrolyte testing, along with bicarbonate, complete blood count, urinalysis, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and assessment for metabolic complications of chronic kidney disease. Renal ultrasound and intact parathyroid hormone testing should be considered as part of comprehensive renal evaluation.

Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Medication reconciliation is needed before initiating or adjusting therapy. Review blood-pressure and diabetes management with nephrology/internal medicine, with attention to renal dosing and avoidance of hypoglycemia.

Close follow-up is required after urgent renal assessment; routine follow-up alone is not appropriate for these laboratory values.
