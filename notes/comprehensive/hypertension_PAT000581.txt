Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure with dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue.

Subjective

Sadie Hunter is a 69-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 19 years and currently without active treatment documented. She reports dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, and epistaxis. No smoking history. She has a 14-year history of coronary artery disease. Weight is 72.7 kg at a height of 173 cm, with BMI 24.3 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 163/91 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory data: Sodium 147 mmol/L, potassium 4.0 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings are provided for this encounter.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled. Blood pressure is significantly elevated at 163/91 mmHg. She has associated dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue but no reported chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic visual symptoms, or epistaxis to suggest an acute hypertensive emergency. Her coronary artery disease increases cardiovascular risk and supports prompt reassessment and treatment planning.

2. Dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. These symptoms may occur with uncontrolled blood pressure but require assessment for cardiac rhythm or ischemic contributors given her age and history of coronary artery disease.

3. Coronary artery disease, reported duration 14 years. No current chest pain or shortness of breath.

4. Mild elevation in serum sodium at 147 mmol/L. Potassium is normal at 4.0 mmol/L.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure after seated rest using appropriate cuff size and obtain home readings twice daily for the next several days, with documentation of symptoms and readings. Arrange prompt same-day or very early outpatient cardiovascular evaluation, including an ECG, because of palpitations, dizziness, fatigue, and known coronary artery disease.

Discuss initiation of antihypertensive therapy and selection of an appropriate regimen at this visit or promptly with her treating clinician; treatment should account for coronary artery disease, renal status, electrolytes, and orthostatic symptoms. Recommend evaluation of renal function, urinalysis, and cardiovascular risk profile, including lipid testing, as part of hypertension assessment.

Encourage a lower-sodium diet, regular activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Maintain hydration while avoiding excessive sodium intake.

Seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, new weakness or numbness, confusion, severe headache, vision loss, or persistently markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Close follow-up is recommended after blood pressure reassessment and ECG.
