Chief Complaint

Initial outpatient consultation for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Leo Davidson is a 76-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 28 years and currently not under active management. Blood pressure today is 166/94 mmHg. He reports intermittent dizziness and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or nosebleeds. No acute hypertensive symptoms are reported.

Past medical history is significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 7 years and hyperlipidemia for approximately 45 years. Current medication use, allergies, prior procedures, and hospitalizations were not provided. He is a current smoker. Height is 181 cm and weight is 85.8 kg, with BMI 26.2 kg/m².

Review of systems: Positive for dizziness and fatigue. Negative for headache, visual disturbance, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, and epistaxis. No additional symptoms are reported in the provided history.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 166/94 mmHg; pulse 80 bpm; respiratory rate 17/min; temperature 36.7°C; oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 181 cm. Weight 85.8 kg. BMI 26.2 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Sodium 144 mmol/L. Potassium 4.9 mmol/L.

Physical examination: Cardiopulmonary and neurologic examination should specifically assess for evidence of acute end-organ injury, including abnormal heart sounds, focal neurologic deficits, volume overload, diminished peripheral pulses, and retinal vascular changes. No examination abnormalities beyond the recorded vital signs are provided for today’s encounter.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension, longstanding and currently untreated, with blood pressure 166/94 mmHg today. The patient has associated intermittent dizziness and fatigue but no reported chest pain, dyspnea, focal neurologic symptoms, visual loss, or other features suggesting hypertensive emergency. Given his age, 28-year history of hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and active smoking, he is at increased risk for cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, renal, and retinal complications. Sodium and potassium are currently within expected ranges, although renal function has not been assessed in the available laboratory data.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration approximately 7 years, increasing overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

Hyperlipidemia, duration approximately 45 years, with treatment status not provided.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of consistent blood pressure treatment and reduction of long-term cardiovascular risk. Confirm blood pressure with repeat measurement using appropriate cuff size after several minutes of rest and obtain home blood pressure readings if feasible. Home readings should be recorded with date, time, and symptoms.

Recommend initiation of antihypertensive therapy after review of renal function and medication history. Reasonable outpatient options include an ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker, particularly in the setting of diabetes, or a calcium-channel blocker or thiazide-type diuretic based on renal function, orthostatic symptoms, electrolytes, and overall tolerability. Avoid assuming any current regimen; medication reconciliation is required before prescribing. Because he reports dizziness, assess for orthostatic blood pressure changes and use gradual titration with attention to fall risk.

Recommend serum creatinine/eGFR and repeat electrolytes before and after starting or increasing an ACE inhibitor, ARB, or diuretic. Recommend urinalysis with assessment for proteinuria, lipid profile, and electrocardiogram as part of cardiovascular risk and hypertension evaluation. Consider retinal evaluation for hypertensive and diabetic eye disease.

Counseled on reducing dietary sodium, avoiding excessive alcohol, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, and continuing weight control. Strongly advised smoking cessation; offer cessation counseling and pharmacologic support after review of preferences and contraindications.

Continue diabetes and lipid risk assessment and coordinate preventive care appropriate for age and comorbid disease. Review vaccination status and other age-appropriate screening at follow-up.

Seek emergency care immediately for severe or rapidly worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic deficit, confusion, fainting, or acute vision loss.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up within 1–2 weeks, sooner for worsening dizziness or persistently elevated home readings, to review blood pressure measurements, orthostatic assessment, medication reconciliation, and initial evaluation results.
