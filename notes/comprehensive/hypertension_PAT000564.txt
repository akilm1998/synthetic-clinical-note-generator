Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Caleb Freeman is a 57-year-old male presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension, present for approximately 25 years and currently without active treatment documented. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. No interval hypertensive symptoms are reported.

Relevant comorbidities include hyperlipidemia for approximately 25 years and coronary artery disease for approximately 12 years. He does not smoke. Current weight is 97.1 kg at a height of 182 cm, with BMI 29.3 kg/m².

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue.
Eyes: Denies blurred vision.
Cardiovascular: Denies chest pain and palpitations.
Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.
Neurologic: Denies headache and dizziness.
Hematologic/ENT: Denies epistaxis.
All other systems reviewed and are negative based on the history provided.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 155/96 mmHg. Pulse 78 bpm. Respiratory rate 16/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 99% on room air. Weight 97.1 kg. Height 182 cm. BMI 29.3 kg/m².

General: Alert and well appearing without acute distress.

Cardiovascular: Regular rate and rhythm. No acute ischemic symptoms reported.

Respiratory: Respirations even and unlabored. No respiratory distress.

Neurologic: No focal neurologic symptoms reported.

Laboratory Data:
Serum sodium 137 mmol/L.
Serum potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding, with persistently elevated office blood pressure at 155/96 mmHg. He is currently asymptomatic, without features suggesting acute hypertensive emergency. Given the approximately 25-year duration and coexisting coronary artery disease and hyperlipidemia, sustained blood pressure control and assessment for hypertensive target-organ involvement are important. Electrolytes are currently within the provided reference ranges, with sodium 137 mmol/L and potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, approximately 12 years by history, without current chest pain, dyspnea, or palpitations.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding, approximately 25 years.

Overweight by BMI, 29.3 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current blood pressure and the increased cardiovascular risk associated with longstanding hypertension, coronary artery disease, hyperlipidemia, and excess weight.

Confirm blood pressure control with repeat standardized office measurements and home blood pressure monitoring, including a written log of morning and evening readings. Review technique, cuff sizing, and adherence once a treatment regimen is established.

Recommend initiation or re-establishment of guideline-directed antihypertensive therapy after medication reconciliation and review of prior tolerability. Given the history of coronary artery disease, selection of therapy should account for cardiovascular risk and renal/electrolyte status. Do not discontinue or start medication without clinician-directed review.

Recommend a reduced-sodium, heart-healthy dietary pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and moderation of alcohol intake. Continue smoking avoidance.

Recommend evaluation of cardiovascular risk and hypertensive target-organ involvement with an ECG, lipid profile, urinalysis, and renal function assessment as clinically appropriate. Continue monitoring serum electrolytes if antihypertensive therapy is initiated or adjusted.

Reviewed emergency precautions, including immediate evaluation for chest pain, severe headache, acute neurologic symptoms, shortness of breath, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up soon for blood pressure reassessment and medication planning, with earlier evaluation for any new cardiovascular or neurologic symptoms.
