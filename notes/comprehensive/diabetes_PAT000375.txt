Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for poorly controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Ivy Henderson is a 57-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 7 years ago. Diabetes has not been actively managed. Recent laboratory results show HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 228 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has obesity with current weight 95.3 kg and BMI 36.3 kg/m², present for approximately 5 years. She also has obstructive sleep apnea of approximately 16 years’ duration. She currently smokes. No medication regimen, allergies, or prior diabetes complications are provided.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue and unintentional weight loss.
Endocrine: Denies polydipsia, polyuria, and polyphagia.
Eyes: Denies blurred vision.
Skin: Denies slow-healing wounds.
Neurologic: Denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet.
Infectious: Denies frequent infections.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 124/78 mmHg, pulse 84 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 162 cm. Weight 95.3 kg. BMI 36.3 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: HbA1c 9.9%. Fasting glucose 228 mg/dL.

Physical examination today is limited to the documented vital signs and available clinical information. No diabetic foot, retinal, skin, or neurologic examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently markedly above glycemic goal based on HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 228 mg/dL. She is asymptomatic without reported polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual changes, neuropathic symptoms, wounds, or recurrent infections. The duration of diabetes and lack of active management increase her risk for microvascular and cardiovascular complications.

Obesity, BMI 36.3 kg/m², present for approximately 5 years.

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding, approximately 16 years.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the need for prompt initiation of an individualized diabetes treatment plan. Review medication options, contraindications, renal function, cost, and preferences before selecting therapy; pharmacologic treatment is indicated rather than lifestyle measures alone.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with fasting and, if directed, postprandial checks. Review glucose goals and provide education regarding symptoms of hypoglycemia and appropriate treatment once a medication regimen is established.

Counseled on reducing refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, increased dietary fiber, and gradual activity as tolerated. Weight reduction was encouraged given obesity and diabetes risk.

Recommend assessment of renal function with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and a comprehensive diabetic foot examination. Recommend retinal evaluation for diabetes-related eye disease. These evaluations have not been documented as completed.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and offered support with cessation planning.

Reviewed urgent precautions, including persistent vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, confusion, severe weakness, chest pain, shortness of breath, or markedly elevated glucose with symptoms.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after treatment initiation and glucose review, with repeat HbA1c monitoring at an appropriate interval.
