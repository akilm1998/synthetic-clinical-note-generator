Chief Complaint

Medication review and management of hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Cameron West is an 81-year-old man presenting for outpatient review of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 13 years and currently without documented lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 305 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 216 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 43 mg/dL, and triglycerides 230 mg/dL. He is not documented as taking a statin, ezetimibe, fibrate, or other lipid-lowering medication, so medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be assessed for a specific agent today.

He has known coronary artery disease of approximately 18 years’ duration and hypertension of approximately 41 years’ duration. He does not smoke. Current weight is 85.1 kg at a height of 174 cm, with BMI 28.1 kg/m².

He denies chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. No medication-related symptoms are reported. The visit is focused on cardiovascular risk reduction and review of treatment options.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: No symptom concerns reported today.

Cardiovascular: Denies chest discomfort.

Peripheral vascular: Denies intermittent leg pain with walking.

Neurologic: Denies dizziness.

Skin: Denies fatty skin deposits or eyelid deposits.

Medication-related review: No adverse effects reported; no active lipid-lowering medication is identified for effectiveness or tolerance review.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 148/82 mmHg. Pulse 77 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 36.8°C. Oxygen saturation 95%.

Anthropometrics: Height 174 cm. Weight 85.1 kg. BMI 28.1 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: Total cholesterol 305 mg/dL. LDL cholesterol 216 mg/dL. HDL cholesterol 43 mg/dL. Triglycerides 230 mg/dL.

Physical examination today is otherwise directed to medication and cardiovascular risk review. No xanthomas or xanthelasma are reported, and no symptomatic manifestations of peripheral vascular disease are reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides. The lipid pattern is consistent with significant mixed dyslipidemia, with LDL 216 mg/dL and triglycerides 230 mg/dL. This is not at treatment goal for a patient with established coronary artery disease. The absence of current lipid-lowering therapy represents an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor. There are no reported symptoms suggesting active angina, claudication, or medication intolerance.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, approximately 18 years. Lipid management should be approached as secondary cardiovascular prevention.

Hypertension, longstanding, with blood pressure today 148/82 mmHg.

Overweight, BMI 28.1 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the substantial cardiovascular risk associated with LDL cholesterol of 216 mg/dL in the setting of known coronary artery disease. Recommend initiation of guideline-directed statin therapy after shared decision-making, with a goal of substantial LDL reduction. Reasonable options include atorvastatin or rosuvastatin at an appropriate intensity for secondary prevention, adjusted for age, comorbidities, renal or hepatic considerations, and patient preference. Because no current lipid-lowering regimen is documented, begin with a tolerable regimen and assess response and adverse effects rather than assuming prior treatment failure.

Reviewed adherence expectations, including taking the medication consistently and notifying the office of new muscle pain, weakness, dark urine, or other concerning symptoms. At follow-up, specifically assess actual medication use, missed doses, perceived benefit, and tolerance. If LDL remains above goal despite adherence or if statin intolerance develops, consider addition of ezetimibe and further risk-based therapy.

Recommend a heart-healthy dietary pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, fish, and lean proteins while reducing saturated fat, trans fat, processed foods, and excess refined carbohydrates. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated and continued avoidance of tobacco. Weight reduction toward a healthier range may also improve triglycerides and overall cardiovascular risk.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after approximately 4–12 weeks of confirmed therapy and adherence to assess treatment response. Consider liver and muscle safety monitoring as clinically indicated when therapy is initiated or symptoms develop. Further risk stratification with ApoB or lipoprotein(a) measurement may be considered if it would alter management; coronary calcium scanning is unlikely to add value given the established history of coronary artery disease.

Continue routine blood pressure management and cardiovascular follow-up. Seek urgent evaluation for new or persistent chest discomfort, shortness of breath, syncope, or other acute cardiovascular symptoms. Follow up after initiation of lipid-lowering therapy to review tolerance, adherence, blood pressure, and repeat lipid results.
