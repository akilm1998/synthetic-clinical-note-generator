Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal filtration

Creatinine is 5.81 mg/dL, BUN 71 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 9 mL/min/1.73 m², consistent with advanced chronic renal impairment in a patient with approximately 10 years of known kidney disease. She currently reports fatigue but no nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, change in urination, edema, or dyspnea. There are no reported clinical features of acute volume overload or overt uremic syndrome at this visit. The available laboratory profile does not include electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, albumin, blood counts, urine protein assessment, or parathyroid hormone level, all of which are important for comprehensive renal risk assessment.

The combination of longstanding diabetes and hypertension is a clinically plausible contributor to progressive chronic kidney disease. Her current renal function warrants prompt specialty-level evaluation and active planning for renal replacement therapy if clinically indicated, while also assessing whether any reversible contributors are present.

2. Hypertension

Blood pressure is 168/94 mmHg, above goal and a significant modifiable risk factor for further renal and cardiovascular injury. The current antihypertensive regimen is not provided, and no medication regimen should be assumed.

3. Type 2 diabetes mellitus

History of diabetes for 21 years. Glycemic control data are not provided today. Diabetes remains an important risk factor for progression of chronic kidney disease and should be reassessed in coordination with renal-appropriate treatment planning.

4. Fatigue

Likely multifactorial, with advanced renal dysfunction a significant potential contributor. Anemia of chronic disease and metabolic complications of kidney disease should be evaluated rather than presumed.

5. Tobacco use

She reports current smoking. This increases cardiovascular and renal risk, particularly in the setting of severe chronic kidney disease and uncontrolled blood pressure.

Plan

Chronic kidney disease:
Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive assessment of advanced renal impairment, management of complications, and discussion of renal replacement therapy options in the context of her age, comorbidities, functional goals, and preferences.

Obtain renal-focused laboratory assessment promptly, including repeat renal function testing, serum electrolytes and bicarbonate, complete blood count, calcium, phosphorus, albumin, intact parathyroid hormone, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. Renal ultrasound is recommended to assess renal anatomy and exclude potentially reversible obstructive or structural disease.

Review all prescription medications, over-the-counter products, and supplements at the next medication reconciliation. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a treating clinician. Medication dosing should be adjusted for her markedly reduced renal function.

Provide education regarding symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, progressive weakness, marked reduction in urine output, or new swelling.

Hypertension:
Repeat blood pressure measurement and initiate or intensify a renal-appropriate antihypertensive plan after medication reconciliation and review of renal laboratory data. Encourage sodium restriction and home blood pressure monitoring if feasible. Given the current blood pressure and degree of renal impairment, blood pressure management should not be deferred.

Diabetes:
Review home glucose data and obtain diabetes-control testing as clinically appropriate. Coordinate glycemic management with renal function in mind, as medication selection and dosing may require adjustment.

Fatigue:
Evaluate with CBC and metabolic assessment as part of the renal workup. Further management will depend on identification of anemia, acid-base disturbance, nutritional issues, or other complications of chronic kidney disease.

Smoking:
Strongly advise smoking cessation and offer structured cessation support. Continued smoking substantially increases cardiovascular and kidney disease risk.

Follow-up:
Prompt follow-up after nephrology assessment and renal laboratory testing, with earlier evaluation for any warning symptoms described above.
