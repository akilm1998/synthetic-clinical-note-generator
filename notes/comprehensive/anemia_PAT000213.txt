Assessment

Chronic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of chronic kidney disease. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are significantly reduced, with low RBC count and a low-normal MCV. The combination of low serum iron, low TIBC, elevated ferritin, and reduced transferrin saturation supports an iron-restricted inflammatory pattern rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency, although concomitant iron deficiency cannot be excluded solely from the available results. The chronicity of approximately 3 years and coexisting kidney disease further support impaired erythropoiesis related to chronic disease.

She is currently hemodynamically stable and denies fatigue, weakness, dyspnea, dizziness, palpitations, headache, or reduced exercise tolerance. No tachycardia or hypoxemia is present today. Despite minimal symptoms, the degree of anemia warrants further evaluation and active outpatient management.

Chronic kidney disease, approximately 1 year in duration, likely contributing to the anemia. No formal stage assigned based on the information available.

Plan

Review and reconcile all current medications and supplements at the next clinical contact, including any use of oral iron or other agents that may affect anemia management.

Recommend repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count to assess persistence and marrow response. Recommend peripheral blood smear, vitamin B12, folate, and renal function testing as part of the anemia evaluation. Inflammatory markers may help further characterize the contribution of chronic inflammation. These studies should be interpreted together with the existing iron profile.

Recommend coordination with the clinician managing her chronic kidney disease regarding anemia treatment. Depending on renal function, reticulocyte response, and the remainder of the evaluation, consideration may be given to iron replacement, including intravenous iron when clinically appropriate, or an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent under appropriate supervision. Treatment should not be based on ferritin alone given the low transferrin saturation and possible inflammatory iron sequestration.

No immediate treatment response can be assessed because the anemia is not currently documented as being actively managed. Continue close outpatient monitoring rather than relying on symptoms alone.

Discussed urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, persistent tachycardia, significant dizziness, palpitations, marked weakness, or other evidence of poor oxygen delivery or acute bleeding.

Arrange follow-up after repeat laboratory evaluation to review the trend in hemoglobin and determine next management steps.
