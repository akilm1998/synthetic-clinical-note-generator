Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Hailey Johnston is a 38-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. Blood pressure has not been actively managed to date. She reports intermittent dizziness and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or nosebleeds. No symptoms suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency are reported today.

Her medical history is notable for coronary artery disease for approximately 1 year, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 2 years, and chronic kidney disease for approximately 1 year. No current medication regimen is provided for review. She does not smoke. Height is 165 cm and weight is 70.5 kg, with BMI 25.9 kg/m².

Review of systems: Constitutional positive for fatigue. Neurologic positive for dizziness; negative for headache. Eyes negative for blurred vision. Cardiovascular negative for chest pain and palpitations. Respiratory negative for shortness of breath. Hematologic negative for epistaxis. No additional interval symptoms reported.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 149/101 mmHg. Pulse 72 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Weight 70.5 kg. Height 165 cm. BMI 25.9 kg/m².

General: Outpatient vital signs reviewed. No acute cardiopulmonary instability evident from the documented vital signs.

Cardiovascular: Regular pulse at 72 bpm by vital-sign measurement. No additional examination findings provided.

Respiratory: Respiratory rate 17/min with oxygen saturation 97% on room air. No reported dyspnea.

Neurologic: Dizziness reported; no headache or visual disturbance reported.

Laboratory data reviewed: Sodium 136 mmol/L. Potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, suboptimally controlled. Blood pressure remains elevated at 149/101 mmHg after approximately 1 year of known hypertension without active management. She has associated dizziness and fatigue but no chest pain, dyspnea, visual change, severe headache, or other reported features of acute end-organ injury. Her coronary artery disease, diabetes mellitus, and chronic kidney disease substantially increase cardiovascular and renal risk and support prompt initiation or re-establishment of a structured blood pressure treatment plan. Potassium is 4.8 mmol/L and sodium is 136 mmol/L on the available electrolyte panel.

Coronary artery disease, duration approximately 1 year, clinically without reported angina or dyspnea today.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration approximately 2 years. Glycemic data are not available in the information provided. Diabetes increases the importance of blood pressure and renal risk-factor control.

Chronic kidney disease, duration approximately 1 year. Available electrolytes are within expected ranges, although potassium is toward the upper end of normal; medication selection and monitoring should account for renal disease and potassium level.

Overweight by BMI at 25.9 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed that the current blood pressure is persistently above goal and requires active management, particularly in the setting of coronary artery disease, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease. Confirm blood pressure with repeat measurement using appropriate cuff size after seated rest and begin home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, including readings at different times of day and associated symptoms.

Arrange prompt outpatient medication review and initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy. Selection should account for her coronary artery disease, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, current potassium of 4.8 mmol/L, and renal function. If a renin-angiotensin system agent is selected, repeat serum electrolytes and renal function after initiation or dose changes. Do not use potassium supplements or salt substitutes containing potassium unless specifically advised.

Recommend evaluation with ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular and renal risk assessment. Recommend review of kidney function and diabetes control as clinically appropriate; no additional results are available today.

Counseled on reducing dietary sodium, limiting alcohol if applicable, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, pursuing gradual weight optimization, and avoiding tobacco exposure. Dietary and exercise changes should be coordinated with her cardiovascular and renal comorbidities.

Continue monitoring for dizziness and fatigue. Seek emergency care immediately for severe or worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, fainting, significant visual change, or markedly higher blood pressure accompanied by symptoms.

Follow up promptly for blood pressure reassessment and treatment response, with earlier evaluation if symptoms worsen.
