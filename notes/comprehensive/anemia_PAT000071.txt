Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Naomi Myers is an 80-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 19 years. She reports no current fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, exertional dyspnea, palpitations, headache, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor remains present. She denies any smoking history.

Her relevant chronic conditions include chronic kidney disease of 17 years’ duration, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 10 years, and a chronic infection reported for 44 years. The anemia has not been actively managed. No current anemia-directed medication regimen is documented.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue, weakness, or reduced exercise tolerance.

Cardiovascular: Denies palpitations.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.

Neurologic: Denies dizziness or headache.

Hematologic: Pallor reported/present. No other bleeding-related symptoms provided.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 139/87 mmHg, pulse 79 beats/min, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Anthropometrics: Height 152 cm, weight 61.0 kg, BMI 26.4 kg/m².

Physical Examination: Pallor is present. Cardiovascular examination is normal. Respiratory examination is normal, with oxygen saturation maintained at 98%. No tachycardia or respiratory distress is evident from the recorded examination and vital signs.

Laboratory Data:
Hemoglobin 9.5 g/dL
Hematocrit 29.2%
RBC count 3.36 million/µL
MCV 87 fL
Serum iron 44 µg/dL
Ferritin 203 ng/mL
TIBC 246 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 17.9%

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation. Hemoglobin is reduced at 9.5 g/dL with corresponding reductions in hematocrit and RBC count. The MCV is normal at 87 fL. Low serum iron and transferrin saturation with ferritin of 203 ng/mL and relatively low TIBC support impaired iron availability in the setting of chronic inflammation and chronic kidney disease rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Her longstanding chronic infection and renal disease are likely important contributors.

She is currently clinically stable without reported cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms, with normal pulse and oxygen saturation. Pallor persists. Given the prolonged duration and lack of active management, further characterization and disease-directed treatment should be pursued while avoiding empiric assumptions regarding iron deficiency.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, likely contributing to reduced erythropoietic capacity.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Plan

Obtain an updated complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to assess marrow response and confirm the continuing pattern of anemia.

Recommend serum vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as part of evaluation for reversible contributors and to better define the relative effects of chronic kidney disease and inflammation.

Review for occult blood loss, nutritional factors, and other sources of chronic inflammation during follow-up, guided by history and examination.

Given the transferrin saturation of 17.9%, assess for functional or absolute iron deficiency before initiating therapy. Oral or intravenous iron may be considered based on the broader clinical evaluation, renal function, tolerance, and response; avoid assuming that elevated ferritin excludes clinically relevant iron-restricted erythropoiesis.

If anemia remains persistent and is attributable substantially to chronic kidney disease after evaluation of reversible causes, consider nephrology-directed discussion of an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent. Such therapy should be individualized with attention to hemoglobin response and cardiovascular risk.

Continue clinical monitoring for development of fatigue, weakness, dyspnea, palpitations, dizziness, headache, or declining exercise tolerance. Arrange follow-up after repeat evaluation to review trends and establish an active anemia management plan.
