Fatigue may be related to advanced kidney dysfunction, but anemia and other metabolic contributors have not been evaluated in the available data.

Plan

Chronic kidney disease: Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive assessment of advanced renal dysfunction and renal replacement therapy planning. Dialysis indications should be assessed clinically and with current metabolic studies; the present absence of nausea, dyspnea, edema, or other reported uremic symptoms does not eliminate the need for prompt evaluation. Discuss modality education and access planning as appropriate after nephrology assessment.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, complete blood count, albumin, iron indices, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Obtain urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to characterize proteinuria and renal risk. Renal ultrasound may be considered if structural assessment has not been performed or if the etiology remains uncertain.

Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter products. Any medication requiring renal clearance should be reviewed for dose adjustment. She should seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, persistent vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, new edema, or substantially reduced urine output.

Medication review: Complete medication reconciliation at the next contact, including prescription medications, over-the-counter agents, supplements, and adherence patterns. Do not initiate or intensify renal medications without confirming current renal function, electrolyte status, blood pressure, and the complete medication list. Assess tolerance and adherence directly once the active regimen is confirmed.

Diabetes mellitus: Coordinate diabetes management with nephrology and primary care/endocrinology, with selection and dosing of glucose-lowering therapy based on the current eGFR. Avoid hypoglycemia, particularly if oral intake declines or kidney function worsens.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism: Review current treatment, if any, after obtaining calcium, phosphorus, and intact parathyroid hormone levels. Nephrology should guide phosphate management and the choice of calcium-based or non-calcium-based therapy.

Coronary artery disease: Verify the complete cardiovascular medication regimen and adherence. Review blood pressure management and cardiovascular risk-reduction therapy in the context of severe kidney impairment and medication safety.

Follow-up: Prompt nephrology follow-up is recommended because of the eGFR of 7 mL/min/1.73 m² and BUN of 73 mg/dL. Reassess symptoms, volume status, medication use, adherence, and treatment tolerance at that visit.
