Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Sarah Griffin is a 51-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 11 years ago. Current glycemic control is suboptimal, with most recent HbA1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 205 mg/dL. She is not currently engaged in an active diabetes management regimen. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. She also denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for approximately 6 years, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 2 years, and depression for approximately 20 years. No tobacco use. Current weight is 89 kg; BMI 28.4 kg/m².

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, present for approximately 11 years, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c of 9.2% and fasting glucose of 205 mg/dL. She is asymptomatic from hyperglycemia and denies neuropathic or infectious complications, but prolonged elevation increases risk for microvascular and macrovascular disease.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Renal function values were not provided today; this is clinically relevant to medication selection and diabetes monitoring.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, present for approximately 2 years.

Depression, longstanding, approximately 20 years.

Overweight, BMI 28.4 kg/m².

Blood pressure is mildly elevated at 140/79 mmHg and warrants continued monitoring in the setting of diabetes and chronic kidney disease.

Plan

Discussed that current glycemic control is above goal and requires active management. Reviewed lifestyle measures including reducing refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened foods, portion control, gradual weight reduction, and regular physical activity as tolerated.

Discussed initiation of evidence-based diabetes pharmacotherapy. Medication selection should account for her chronic kidney disease and liver history; obtain current renal function before finalizing therapy. Review adherence, access, preferences, and prior diabetes treatment history at the next medication discussion.

Obtain HbA1c at an appropriate interval to assess response to treatment. Check fasting lipid profile, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio as part of comprehensive diabetes and kidney disease monitoring. Consider retinal evaluation and routine comprehensive foot assessment as clinically appropriate.

Continue monitoring home glucose values if available, with attention to fasting and postprandial patterns. Reviewed symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and advised earlier evaluation for marked polyuria, polydipsia, vomiting, confusion, or inability to maintain oral intake.

Monitor blood pressure closely and reinforce dietary sodium reduction and regular physical activity. Reassess cardiovascular and kidney risk-reduction needs once updated laboratory data are available.

Continue routine follow-up for depression and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Return for diabetes management follow-up after updated laboratory evaluation, or sooner for worsening symptoms or persistently elevated glucose readings.
