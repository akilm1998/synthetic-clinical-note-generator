Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Melanie Knight is an 88-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. Recent laboratory results show HbA1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 253 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimal glycemic control. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, unintentional weight loss, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. She denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet.

No acute diabetic concerns are reported today. Medication use, adherence, home glucose monitoring, and dietary pattern were reviewed as part of chronic disease follow-up; a complete current medication list is to be reconciled before treatment changes are made.

Past medical history is significant for hypertension of approximately 44 years’ duration, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of approximately 46 years’ duration, and obstructive sleep apnea of approximately 31 years’ duration. She currently smokes. No new cardiopulmonary, gastrointestinal, neurologic, or constitutional symptoms are reported in association with today’s visit.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 146/86 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 173 cm. Weight 71.5 kg. BMI 23.9 kg/m².

General: No acute diabetic symptoms reported. Patient is clinically stable by vital signs.

Respiratory: Respiratory rate and oxygen saturation are within acceptable range; no respiratory distress observed.

Neurologic: No reported distal numbness or tingling.

Skin/feet: No slow-healing wounds or recurrent infections reported.

Laboratory data:
HbA1c 9.2%.
Fasting glucose 253 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently poorly controlled. HbA1c and fasting glucose are significantly above usual outpatient targets, although she is not reporting polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual changes, neuropathic symptoms, or infectious complications. Given her age and comorbidities, glycemic goals should be individualized with emphasis on improving control while avoiding hypoglycemia and excessive treatment burden. No evidence of an acute hyperglycemic emergency is described today.

Hypertension with blood pressure above goal at today’s visit.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Review and reconcile all current medications, including any diabetes therapies, antihypertensive agents, supplements, and over-the-counter medications, before initiating or intensifying treatment. Discussed that the current HbA1c and fasting glucose warrant active management. Pharmacologic therapy should be selected conservatively based on renal function, hepatic status, nutritional intake, functional status, hypoglycemia risk, and patient preferences. Avoid overly aggressive glucose lowering in this 88-year-old patient.

Recommend home glucose monitoring if feasible, with documentation of fasting and occasional postprandial values. Review recognition and treatment of hypoglycemia, particularly if a medication capable of causing hypoglycemia is started or intensified.

Reinforce a consistent carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, adequate hydration, and regular physical activity appropriate to her mobility and fall risk. Her BMI is 23.9 kg/m²; weight loss is not a treatment goal.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to guide diabetes treatment and evaluate for diabetic kidney involvement. Recommend fasting lipid assessment as part of cardiovascular risk management. Continue periodic HbA1c monitoring after treatment adjustment.

Perform comprehensive diabetic foot examination at follow-up, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensation, and reinforce daily foot checks and prompt reporting of sores or skin changes. Recommend routine dilated retinal examination if not current.

Continue blood pressure monitoring and reassess hypertension management after medication reconciliation. Counsel regarding smoking cessation and offer cessation support, given increased cardiovascular and microvascular risk.

Return for close outpatient follow-up to review medication reconciliation, home glucose readings, tolerance of any treatment changes, and repeat glycemic assessment. Seek urgent care for persistent vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, inability to maintain hydration, severe hyperglycemic symptoms, or symptomatic hypoglycemia.
