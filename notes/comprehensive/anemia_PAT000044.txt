Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Zachary Barnes is a 50-year-old man, 160 cm tall and 58.1 kg (BMI 22.7 kg/m²), presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 20 years. He has not been receiving active anemia-directed management. He reports no current fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor has been noted.

Relevant chronic conditions include rheumatoid arthritis for 7 years, chronic infection for 13 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 7 years. He does not smoke. No medication regimen is provided.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue, weakness, or reduced exercise tolerance.
Cardiovascular: Denies palpitations.
Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.
Neurologic: Denies dizziness or headache.
Hematologic: Pallor present. No additional bleeding-related symptoms reported.
Other systems: No additional concerns reported today.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, currently asymptomatic apart from pallor. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are reduced, with RBC count of 3.2 million/µL and normal MCV of 91 fL. Low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC are most consistent with impaired iron availability in the setting of chronic inflammation, rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. His longstanding rheumatoid arthritis and chronic infection are plausible contributors. The 20-year duration warrants confirmation of the chronic pattern and reassessment for additional or contributing causes, including renal, nutritional, hematologic, or ongoing inflammatory disease. There is no current evidence of hemodynamic instability or symptomatic cardiopulmonary compromise.

Rheumatoid arthritis, longstanding, with potential contribution to chronic inflammatory anemia.

Chronic infection, longstanding, with potential contribution to inflammatory anemia.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding; relevant to evaluation for renal contribution to anemia, although renal function is not provided.

Plan

Discussed laboratory pattern and likely inflammatory mechanism of anemia. No urgent intervention is indicated based on current stable vital signs and absence of fatigue, dyspnea, dizziness, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance.

Recommend repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to confirm persistence and characterize marrow response.

Recommend renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers as part of the evaluation for contributing causes. Continue assessment and treatment of the underlying rheumatoid arthritis and chronic infection with the appropriate treating clinicians, as control of inflammation may improve anemia.

Given the low transferrin saturation, review for nutritional deficiency or chronic blood loss should be considered before initiating iron therapy. Ferritin is not low, so empiric iron supplementation should not be assumed to be beneficial without further clinical assessment. If anemia persists or worsens despite addressing inflammatory drivers, consider hematology consultation for further evaluation and discussion of anemia-directed therapy.

Continue routine diabetes management and assess renal function as part of ongoing care.

Monitor for development of fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, headache, or reduced exercise tolerance. Seek urgent evaluation for new chest pain, significant shortness of breath, syncope, or rapidly worsening symptoms.
