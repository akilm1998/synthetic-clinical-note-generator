Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for poorly controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Cameron West is a 74-year-old man with a 34-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, currently without an active management regimen documented. Recent laboratory testing shows HbA1c 10.0% and fasting glucose 217 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or frequent infections.

He reports numbness in the hands or feet without associated tingling. No acute wound or infectious concern is reported. He continues to smoke. Relevant comorbidities include obstructive sleep apnea of approximately 35 years’ duration and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of approximately 12 years’ duration.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 132/82 mmHg, pulse 79 beats/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Anthropometrics: Height 187 cm, weight 75.5 kg, BMI 21.6 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: HbA1c 10.0%. Fasting glucose 217 mg/dL.

Physical Examination: Patient is an older adult male with normal-range BMI. Cardiopulmonary vital signs are stable. No diabetic wound or acute infectious finding is reported. Neurologic symptom review is notable for distal numbness involving the hands or feet; formal sensory and vascular foot examination should be performed as part of diabetes care.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented acute complication, currently markedly above glycemic goal based on HbA1c of 10.0% and fasting glucose of 217 mg/dL. Despite the absence of classic hyperglycemic symptoms, these values indicate clinically significant chronic hyperglycemia and require prompt treatment planning.

Distal numbness is concerning for possible diabetes-associated peripheral sensory neuropathy, although a focused neurologic and foot examination is needed for characterization.

Tobacco use, obstructive sleep apnea, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease are relevant comorbid risk factors in the overall metabolic and cardiovascular risk assessment.

Plan

Arrange prompt outpatient diabetes management rather than continued observation. Review prior treatment history, adherence barriers, dietary pattern, glucose monitoring practices, and readiness for medication initiation or intensification. Discuss evidence-based pharmacologic therapy, with selection guided by renal function, hepatic status, hypoglycemia risk, cost, and patient preference; no specific current medication regimen is documented today.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with review of fasting and postprandial values. Provide counseling regarding reduction of concentrated carbohydrates, regular physical activity as tolerated, and recognition of symptomatic hypoglycemia and marked hyperglycemia.

Obtain renal function, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and repeat glycemic assessment as clinically appropriate before or during treatment planning. Perform a comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including skin inspection, monofilament sensation, and peripheral pulses, given the reported numbness. Recommend routine dilated retinal evaluation.

Counsel regarding smoking cessation and its contribution to vascular and neuropathic risk. Continue attention to obstructive sleep apnea and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease in longitudinal care.

Seek urgent evaluation for vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, severe weakness, inability to maintain oral intake, rapidly worsening polyuria or polydipsia, or other symptoms suggesting acute metabolic decompensation. Close follow-up is recommended after treatment is initiated or within a short interval to review glucose data and tolerance.
