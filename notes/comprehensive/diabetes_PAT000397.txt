Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for long-standing type 2 diabetes mellitus and review of glycemic control.

Subjective

Michelle Cross is a 74-year-old woman seen for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 30 years ago. Current glycemic control is poor, with recent hemoglobin A1c of 9.2% and fasting glucose of 224 mg/dL. There is no active diabetes management regimen documented for review today.

She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. She reports numbness in the hands or feet without associated tingling. No acute hyperglycemic symptoms are reported.

She has a 9-year history of dyslipidemia. She does not smoke. Current weight is 71.7 kg at a height of 168 cm, with BMI 25.4 kg/m².

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue and unintentional weight loss.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Endocrine: Denies polydipsia, polyuria, and polyphagia.

Neurologic: Reports numbness in the hands or feet. Denies tingling.

Skin: Denies slow-healing wounds.

Infectious: Denies frequent infections.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 127/79 mmHg, pulse 68 beats/min, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 168 cm. Weight 71.7 kg. BMI 25.4 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Hemoglobin A1c 9.2%. Fasting glucose 224 mg/dL.

Physical examination is notable for the documented vital signs and anthropometric measurements. A focused diabetic foot and neurologic examination should specifically assess the reported distal numbness, including protective sensation, vibration, skin integrity, and peripheral perfusion.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complication, with markedly suboptimal glycemic control. Hemoglobin A1c of 9.2% and fasting glucose of 224 mg/dL are consistent with persistent hyperglycemia despite the absence of prominent osmotic or constitutional symptoms. Diabetes duration is approximately 30 years, increasing the importance of systematic evaluation for microvascular and macrovascular disease.

Distal numbness of the hands or feet. This may represent diabetes-associated sensory neuropathic symptoms, although objective examination is required before attributing the symptom definitively to diabetes.

Dyslipidemia, present for approximately 9 years, relevant to overall cardiovascular risk in the setting of long-standing diabetes.

BMI 25.4 kg/m², in the overweight range.

Plan

Discussed that current glycemic control is above the usual outpatient treatment range and warrants prompt treatment review. Confirm the current diabetes medication history and adherence, then establish or intensify a structured glucose-lowering regimen appropriate for her age, renal function, hypoglycemia risk, comorbidities, preferences, and overall treatment goals. Avoid overly aggressive glucose lowering without individualized assessment.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with review of fasting and, when clinically indicated, postprandial values. Reinforce recognition and treatment of hypoglycemia if glucose-lowering therapy is initiated or intensified.

Counseled regarding a consistent carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages and concentrated sweets, portion control, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage continued smoking abstinence.

Recommend assessment of renal status with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and a fasting lipid profile to support medication selection and cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend comprehensive dilated retinal evaluation because of the prolonged diabetes duration and current hyperglycemia.

Perform a complete diabetic foot examination at the next clinical assessment, including inspection for skin breakdown, assessment of protective sensation and vibration, and evaluation of peripheral pulses. Counsel on daily foot inspection, appropriate footwear, and prompt reporting of wounds, redness, or infection.

Evaluate the reported numbness with focused neurologic and foot examination; consider other reversible contributors if symptoms persist or progress. Advise the patient to report new weakness, gait difficulty, rapidly progressive sensory loss, or foot ulceration.

Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management, including ongoing attention to dyslipidemia and blood pressure. Reassess glycemic response and treatment tolerance after therapy is established or adjusted, with repeat hemoglobin A1c at an appropriate interval.
