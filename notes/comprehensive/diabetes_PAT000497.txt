Annual review of type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic medical conditions.

She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications

Glycemic control is substantially above target, based on hemoglobin A1c of 9.6% and fasting glucose of 243 mg/dL. Although she denies classic hyperglycemic symptoms, the laboratory findings indicate clinically significant persistent hyperglycemia. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of peripheral neuropathy, visual complications, or infection. Her BMI is 25.0 kg/m², and she has no documented diabetic complications in the supplied history.

2. Dyslipidemia

Chronic dyslipidemia of 5 years’ duration in the setting of diabetes, increasing overall cardiovascular risk. No lipid results or current treatment regimen are provided.

3. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease

Chronic condition present for 7 years. This commonly coexists with insulin resistance and dyslipidemia. No liver biochemistry or imaging results are provided for review today.

4. Depression

History of depression for 2 years. Ongoing mood assessment is appropriate as part of comprehensive diabetes care, given the effect of depression on self-management and cardiometabolic health.

5. Tobacco use

Current smoking is an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor, particularly in the context of diabetes and dyslipidemia.

6. Preventive health maintenance

Annual review completed with attention to chronic disease control, cardiovascular risk reduction, tobacco use, mental health, diabetic eye and foot health, renal surveillance, and immunization-based prevention.

Plan

Type 2 diabetes mellitus: Discussed the significance of the elevated hemoglobin A1c and fasting glucose and the need to initiate or re-establish active diabetes management. Recommend individualized nutrition counseling emphasizing reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight maintenance, and home glucose monitoring if clinically appropriate. Medication therapy should be reviewed and initiated or adjusted through shared decision-making; reasonable options include metformin and, depending on renal function, cardiovascular risk, weight goals, tolerability, and treatment preferences, other evidence-based glucose-lowering therapy. Obtain renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before and during therapy selection, and repeat hemoglobin A1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response.

Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot assessment, including inspection and sensory evaluation, at an appropriate clinical visit. Recommend a dilated retinal examination because of the duration of diabetes and degree of hyperglycemia. Counsel regarding prompt evaluation of new visual changes, polyuria, polydipsia, unexplained weight loss, foot wounds, or symptoms of infection.

Dyslipidemia and cardiovascular risk: Recommend fasting lipid evaluation and review of overall cardiovascular risk. Discuss lipid-lowering therapy as clinically indicated, together with dietary modification, physical activity, and smoking cessation. Blood pressure is currently 122/77 mmHg.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease: Continue metabolic risk reduction through improved glycemic control, avoidance of excess alcohol if applicable, regular physical activity, and maintenance of a healthy weight. Review liver-related laboratory monitoring when available and coordinate further evaluation if clinically indicated.

Depression: Screen for current depressive symptoms, functional impairment, and suicidal ideation at follow-up. Encourage engagement with appropriate behavioral health or primary care treatment based on screening results.

Tobacco use: Strongly advised smoking cessation. Discuss cessation counseling and pharmacologic support at a subsequent visit according to patient preference and clinical suitability.

Preventive care: Review age-appropriate cancer screening, immunizations, dental care, vision care, and cardiovascular risk factors. Diabetes-specific preventive care should include annual renal surveillance, retinal examination, and foot assessment. Reinforce dietary counseling, physical activity, and safety planning related to glycemic monitoring.

Follow-up: Arrange timely follow-up to establish a specific diabetes treatment plan, review recommended renal, lipid, and urine testing, assess adherence and tolerance, and monitor glycemic response. Given the current degree of hyperglycemia, follow-up should occur sooner than a routine annual visit.
