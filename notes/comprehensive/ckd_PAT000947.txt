Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Ruby Marshall is a 73-year-old woman, height 151 cm, weight 55.2 kg, BMI 24.2 kg/m², presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 11 years ago. Her chronic kidney disease has not been actively managed. Interval symptoms include persistent fatigue. She reports no pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, or shortness of breath. She has no smoking history.

She also has a 6-year history of hypertension. No current medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 154/94 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 151 cm. Weight 55.2 kg. BMI 24.2 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Serum creatinine 3.78 mg/dL, BUN 84 mg/dL, estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m².

Focused review of systems is notable for fatigue. No reported pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, urinary frequency change, or dyspnea.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, longstanding for approximately 11 years and currently without documented active management. Creatinine is 3.78 mg/dL, BUN 84 mg/dL, and eGFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m². The patient has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, asterixis-type symptoms, fluid retention, urinary change, or dyspnea to suggest clinically evident volume overload or symptomatic uremia at this visit. The degree of renal impairment warrants prompt renal specialty evaluation and preparation for potential kidney replacement therapy if clinically indicated.

Hypertension, present for 6 years, currently above goal at 154/94 mmHg. Poor blood pressure control may contribute to ongoing renal decline. The current antihypertensive regimen is not documented.

Fatigue, potentially related to advanced chronic kidney disease; anemia and other metabolic complications should be assessed.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for comprehensive evaluation of advanced chronic kidney disease, assessment of progression and complications, and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if appropriate. Recommend renal function and serum electrolyte assessment, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone measurement. Consider renal ultrasound as part of the structural evaluation if not previously completed.

Obtain a complete medication reconciliation at the next encounter and ensure all medications are appropriately renally dosed. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Review use of over-the-counter medications and supplements. Counsel regarding monitoring of blood pressure at home, moderate sodium intake, and prompt reporting of reduced urine output, new swelling, worsening fatigue, nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest discomfort, or shortness of breath.

Hypertension requires active management in the setting of significant chronic kidney disease. Confirm home readings and adherence once the medication list is available, then adjust therapy appropriately with renal function and potassium status taken into account.

Follow up promptly after recommended laboratory evaluation and nephrology assessment, with earlier evaluation for any new uremic or fluid-overload symptoms.
