Chief Complaint

Annual review and evaluation of longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Brian Simmons is an 83-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease assessment. He has a 26-year history of hyperlipidemia that is currently not being actively managed. Recent lipid values are markedly abnormal, with total cholesterol 294 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 216 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 30 mg/dL, and triglycerides 241 mg/dL.

He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain consistent with intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. He does not smoke. Weight is 77.9 kg at a height of 179 cm, with BMI 24.3 kg/m². He has a 33-year history of hypertension. No medication regimen, medication intolerance, allergies, family history, or additional social history is provided for review today.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding and presently untreated, with substantially elevated LDL cholesterol at 216 mg/dL, elevated triglycerides at 241 mg/dL, low HDL cholesterol at 30 mg/dL, and total cholesterol of 294 mg/dL. The LDL level is associated with a high lifetime atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk and warrants active evaluation and treatment discussion, particularly given the patient’s advanced age and longstanding hypertension. He currently reports no chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness and has no reported xanthomas or xanthelasma.

Hypertension, longstanding for approximately 33 years. Blood pressure today is 135/85 mmHg. The current antihypertensive regimen is not documented, so treatment effectiveness and adherence cannot be assessed from today’s information.

Preventive health review performed in the context of an annual visit. Smoking risk is favorable, as he reports no tobacco use. BMI is in the normal range.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current lipid profile, particularly the LDL cholesterol of 216 mg/dL and HDL cholesterol of 30 mg/dL. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of lipid-lowering therapy, with a moderate- to high-intensity statin generally considered first-line treatment if consistent with his goals of care, overall health, and medication preferences. If LDL reduction is inadequate or a statin is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe may be considered; referral for consideration of other lipid-lowering therapy, including a PCSK9 inhibitor, may be appropriate in selected circumstances.

Before or alongside treatment initiation, review for potentially reversible secondary contributors and medication-related causes of dyslipidemia. Recommended evaluation may include thyroid, hepatic, renal, and glycemic assessment as clinically appropriate; no additional laboratory results are available from today’s review. A repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment is recommended to assess response and tolerance.

Obtain a detailed history of prior lipid-lowering therapy, medication adverse effects, dietary pattern, physical activity, and family history of premature cardiovascular disease at follow-up. Measurement of lipoprotein(a) or apolipoprotein B may be considered for additional risk characterization if it would affect management. Coronary artery calcium imaging can be considered only if treatment decisions remain uncertain after discussion of age, comorbidity, life expectancy, and patient preferences; it should not delay consideration of therapy given the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol.

Continue cardiovascular risk reduction through a diet low in saturated and trans fats, increased intake of fiber-rich plant foods, regular physical activity appropriate to functional capacity, and avoidance of tobacco exposure. His BMI is currently 24.3 kg/m², so weight reduction is not indicated.

Review blood pressure management, home blood pressure readings if available, and the complete medication list at the next visit. Continue routine preventive care appropriate to age and health goals, including vaccination review, fall-risk and functional assessment, and individualized cancer screening decisions. Discuss advance care planning and preventive interventions in the context of his preferences and overall health.

Follow up after medication and laboratory review to finalize lipid-lowering management and reassess cardiovascular risk. Seek urgent evaluation for new chest pain, exertional dyspnea, focal neurologic symptoms, or acute limb ischemic symptoms.
