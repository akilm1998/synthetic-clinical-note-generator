Chief Complaint

Annual review of longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Christopher Perry is a 95-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease assessment. He has a 31-year history of essential hypertension that is currently not under active management. He also has chronic kidney disease of approximately 6 years’ duration. No antihypertensive medications are documented.

He reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or nosebleeds. No acute neurologic or cardiopulmonary symptoms are reported today.

He does not smoke. Height is 185 cm, weight 96.2 kg, and BMI 28.1 kg/m².

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Reports fatigue. No other constitutional symptoms reported.
Eyes: Denies blurred vision.
Cardiovascular: Reports palpitations. Denies chest pain.
Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.
Neurologic: Reports dizziness. Denies headache.
ENT: Denies epistaxis.
Other systems: No additional concerns reported during today’s review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 174/91 mmHg. Pulse 68 bpm. Respiratory rate 15/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Anthropometrics: Height 185 cm. Weight 96.2 kg. BMI 28.1 kg/m².

Laboratory data reviewed:
Serum sodium 144 mmol/L.
Serum potassium 3.6 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Longstanding essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure, currently without documented active treatment. The patient has associated dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. He denies chest pain, dyspnea, severe headache, visual disturbance, or epistaxis. Given his advanced age and chronic kidney disease, prompt reassessment and treatment planning are appropriate.

2. Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 6 years. Current sodium is within the reported reference range and potassium is low-normal at 3.6 mmol/L. Renal function values are not provided today; blood pressure management should account for kidney disease and avoid unnecessary electrolyte or renal complications.

3. Intermittent palpitations. Hemodynamically stable by the provided pulse and oxygen saturation, but further evaluation is warranted, particularly before initiating therapy that may affect heart rate or electrolytes.

4. Dizziness and fatigue, potentially related to uncontrolled blood pressure or another cardiovascular/metabolic process. No acute focal or cardiopulmonary symptoms are reported.

5. Overweight by BMI, with BMI 28.1 kg/m².

Plan

Repeat blood pressure after several minutes of seated rest using an appropriately sized cuff and confirm with repeat measurements. If the pressure remains in this range, arrange prompt outpatient evaluation for initiation of antihypertensive therapy rather than continued observation without treatment. Selection of therapy should consider age, orthostatic symptoms, chronic kidney disease, baseline potassium, renal function, fall risk, and medication tolerance. No current antihypertensive regimen is documented, so medication reconciliation should be completed before prescribing.

Obtain recommended renal and hypertension assessment, including serum creatinine with estimated kidney function, repeat electrolytes, urinalysis, urine albumin assessment when clinically appropriate, and lipid profile. Review prior renal values if available to guide safe treatment. An ECG is recommended to evaluate palpitations and assess for hypertensive cardiac effects or rhythm disturbance.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring if he can perform this safely, with a written log including seated readings and symptoms. Assess for orthostatic blood pressure changes given dizziness and advanced age. Counsel on gradual position changes, adequate hydration consistent with kidney-care recommendations, fall prevention, limiting dietary sodium, regular age-appropriate physical activity as tolerated, and moderation of alcohol if applicable.

Discuss emergency precautions clearly: seek emergency care for chest pain, new shortness of breath, severe or sudden headache, acute confusion, weakness or numbness, difficulty speaking, fainting, major visual change, or persistently very high blood pressure associated with symptoms.

Review preventive care appropriate to a 95-year-old, including immunizations, fall-risk assessment, hearing and vision review, medication and advance-care-planning review, and individualized cancer screening based on overall health, preferences, and prior screening history. Continue smoking avoidance.

Arrange close follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning, sooner for worsening dizziness, palpitations, or any emergency warning symptoms.
