Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Catherine Nichols is a 63-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 19 years ago. Current glycemic control is poor based on recent laboratory testing, with hemoglobin A1c 9.7% and fasting glucose 238 mg/dL. She is not currently described as having an active diabetes management regimen.

She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. She also denies numbness or tingling in the hands or feet. No acute diabetic symptoms are reported today.

She has obstructive sleep apnea, present for approximately 5 years. She does not smoke. No other active concerns are reported.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue or unintentional weight loss.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Endocrine: Denies increased thirst, frequent urination, or increased hunger.

Skin: Denies slow-healing wounds or frequent infections.

Neurologic: Denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet.

Respiratory: No respiratory symptoms reported.

Cardiovascular: No cardiovascular symptoms reported.

Gastrointestinal: No gastrointestinal symptoms reported.

Genitourinary: No additional urinary symptoms reported beyond denial of polyuria.

Psychiatric: No psychiatric symptoms reported.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 122/82 mmHg. Pulse 70 bpm. Respiratory rate 14/min. Temperature 37.0°C. Oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 176 cm. Weight 72.2 kg. BMI 23.3 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: Hemoglobin A1c 9.7%. Fasting glucose 238 mg/dL.

Physical examination today is notable for the documented stable vital signs and normal-weight BMI. No additional examination findings are documented.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications

Long-standing type 2 diabetes mellitus with substantially elevated A1c and fasting glucose, consistent with inadequate glycemic control. She is currently asymptomatic despite significant hyperglycemia. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of neuropathy, recurrent infection, visual disturbance, or catabolic weight loss. Her BMI is 23.3 kg/m², without current overweight or obesity. Given the approximately 19-year duration of diabetes and current degree of hyperglycemia, treatment and complication surveillance should be addressed promptly.

2. Obstructive sleep apnea

History of obstructive sleep apnea for approximately 5 years. No related symptoms or treatment details were provided today.

3. Preventive diabetes care

Long duration of diabetes and current hyperglycemia warrant assessment for renal, retinal, neurologic, and cardiovascular risk complications even in the absence of symptoms.

Plan

1. Diabetes mellitus

Review current diabetes treatment history, medication use, adherence, home glucose monitoring, dietary pattern, and barriers to care at this visit. Discuss initiation or intensification of pharmacologic therapy rather than relying on lifestyle measures alone, with medication selection individualized to renal function, cardiovascular risk, hypoglycemia risk, cost, and patient preferences.

Provide nutrition counseling focused on consistent carbohydrate intake, limiting concentrated sugars and refined carbohydrates, and maintaining regular meals. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated, with a goal of moderate-intensity activity most days of the week if no contraindication.

Recommend home glucose monitoring while treatment is being established or adjusted. Review signs and symptoms of hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia and provide precautions for seeking urgent care if vomiting, marked weakness, confusion, inability to maintain oral intake, or rapidly worsening symptoms develop.

Obtain renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, along with a fasting lipid profile, as clinically appropriate for diabetes risk assessment and medication planning. Arrange a comprehensive diabetic eye examination and perform a complete foot examination, including skin integrity, pulses, and protective sensation.

2. Obstructive sleep apnea

Continue routine outpatient management and assess current sleep quality, daytime somnolence, and adherence to any prescribed therapy at a future medication and problem review.

3. Follow-up

Return for diabetes management follow-up after treatment planning and review of recommended monitoring, preferably within several weeks. Repeat hemoglobin A1c after an appropriate interval to assess response to therapy and continue adjustment toward individualized glycemic goals.
