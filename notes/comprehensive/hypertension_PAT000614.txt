Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension and review of disease control.

Subjective

Patricia Greene is a 39-year-old woman, nonsmoker, with a 6-year history of essential hypertension that has not been actively managed. She presents for routine outpatient follow-up. She reports intermittent dizziness and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or epistaxis. No symptoms suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency are reported today.

She has type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 4 years and hyperlipidemia for approximately 7 years. Current medication use, allergies, and additional social or family history are not provided. She has no reported tobacco use.

Review of systems is notable for positive dizziness and fatigue. Neurologic review is otherwise negative for headache or blurred vision. Cardiopulmonary review is negative for chest pain, dyspnea, or palpitations. No epistaxis reported.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 148/91 mmHg. Pulse 75 bpm. Respiratory rate 14/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 151 cm. Weight 47.2 kg. BMI 20.7 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Serum sodium 135 mmol/L. Serum potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension, present for approximately 6 years and currently above goal at 148/91 mmHg in the setting of no active management. She has intermittent dizziness and fatigue but no chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic visual symptoms, or other features of hypertensive crisis. Her normal pulse and oxygen saturation are reassuring. Diabetes and hyperlipidemia increase her long-term cardiovascular and renal risk despite a normal BMI of 20.7 kg/m². Serum potassium is normal; sodium is at the low end of the reference range.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 4 years’ duration. Glycemic control cannot be assessed from the information available today. This comorbidity is important in selecting and monitoring antihypertensive therapy.

Hyperlipidemia, approximately 7 years’ duration, contributing to overall cardiovascular risk.

Dizziness and fatigue, intermittent and nonspecific. These symptoms should be reassessed after blood pressure evaluation and treatment planning; alternative causes should be considered if persistent or worsening.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given the persistent elevation, 6-year duration of hypertension, and coexisting diabetes and hyperlipidemia. Recommend confirmation of the blood pressure pattern with properly performed home readings, including morning and evening measurements over the next several days, with documentation of associated symptoms. Ensure appropriate cuff size and seated rest before measurement.

Recommend initiating antihypertensive therapy after review of renal function and urine albumin assessment, with consideration of an ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker because of the coexisting diabetes, unless contraindicated. Renal function and electrolytes should be monitored after treatment is started or adjusted. The current potassium is 4.6 mmol/L.

Counseled regarding reduction of dietary sodium, avoidance of excess alcohol, regular physical activity as tolerated, and continued maintenance of a healthy body weight. She is a nonsmoker.

Recommend updating cardiovascular risk assessment with lipid profile and diabetes monitoring, and obtaining urinalysis with assessment for albuminuria as clinically appropriate. Recommend ECG as part of evaluation for chronic hypertension. These are recommendations and have not been completed at this visit.

Continue coordination of diabetes and hyperlipidemia management, as control of these conditions is important for reduction of cardiovascular and renal complications.

Because of dizziness and fatigue, advised her to record the timing of symptoms in relation to blood pressure readings and activity. Seek urgent evaluation for severe or rapidly worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, acute visual changes, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up after home blood pressure data and recommended baseline evaluation are available to finalize and monitor therapy, or sooner if symptoms worsen.
