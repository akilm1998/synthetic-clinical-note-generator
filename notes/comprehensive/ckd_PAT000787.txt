Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal filtration, present for approximately 3 years. Creatinine is 3.52 mg/dL, estimated GFR 15 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 62 mg/dL. Although he is currently without nausea, pruritus, edema, dyspnea, or urinary frequency change, the degree of renal impairment warrants prompt comprehensive evaluation and renal-specialty involvement. The condition has not had an established management plan, and the cause is not confirmed in the available information.

2. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration approximately 9 years. Diabetes is a significant potential contributor to chronic kidney disease, although urine albumin status and other markers of diabetic kidney involvement are not available.

3. Secondary hyperparathyroidism, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, likely related to chronic kidney disease. Current calcium, phosphorus, and intact parathyroid hormone values are not available.

4. Fatigue, likely multifactorial and potentially related to advanced chronic kidney disease or anemia; no CBC is available to assess for anemia.

5. Tobacco use. Ongoing smoking may increase renal and cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current creatinine, BUN, and estimated GFR, including the risk of progression and the need for coordinated renal care. Recommend prompt referral to nephrology for comprehensive CKD evaluation, treatment planning, assessment of secondary hyperparathyroidism, and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if clinically appropriate. This referral is recommended even though he currently lacks significant uremic or volume-overload symptoms.

Recommend obtaining a comprehensive serum electrolyte panel, repeat renal function testing, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Recommend renal ultrasound to assess kidney size and structural abnormalities. These studies should be interpreted together with prior renal records and the clinical course.

Review all prescribed, over-the-counter, and supplemental products at the next visit. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically approved by a treating clinician. Medication dosing should be adjusted for the current level of renal function. No medication regimen is initiated today because a current medication list and additional laboratory data are not available.

Optimize diabetes care with individualized glycemic monitoring and treatment through primary care and nephrology. Obtain available prior diabetes and kidney laboratory records if possible. Continue monitoring blood pressure; today’s reading is 128/81 mmHg.

Discussed smoking cessation and offered counseling/support as part of renal and cardiovascular risk reduction.

Counseled regarding prompt medical evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, markedly reduced urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, chest pain, or other acute change.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after the recommended laboratory and imaging evaluation, with earlier review if symptoms develop.
