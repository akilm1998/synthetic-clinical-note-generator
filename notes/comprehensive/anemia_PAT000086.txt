Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of anemia.

Subjective

Alice Mathew is a 38-year-old woman, 178 cm tall and weighing 89.0 kg, with BMI 28.1 kg/m², presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 1-year history of anemia in the setting of inflammatory bowel disease of approximately 5 years’ duration. The anemia is not currently under active management.

She denies fatigue, generalized weakness, reduced exercise tolerance, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, or visible pallor. No anemia-related limitation is reported at present. She is a current smoker. No medication use, allergies, recent hospitalization, or prior anemia treatment is documented in the information provided.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue, weakness, and reduced exercise tolerance.

Cardiovascular: Denies palpitations.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.

Neurologic: Denies dizziness and headache.

Hematologic: Denies awareness of pallor. No bleeding symptoms are reported in the available history.

Gastrointestinal: Known inflammatory bowel disease. No current gastrointestinal symptoms are reported in the available history.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 115/77 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

General: Hemodynamically stable by vital signs and without reported acute distress. No pallor is reported.

Cardiovascular: Normal cardiovascular examination.

Respiratory: Normal respiratory examination.

Anthropometrics: Height 178 cm, weight 89.0 kg, BMI 28.1 kg/m².

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.7 g/dL.
Hematocrit 26.2%.
Red blood cell count 3.05 million/µL.
MCV 86 fL.
Serum iron 25 µg/dL.
Ferritin 395 ng/mL.
TIBC 190 µg/dL.
Transferrin saturation 13.2%.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation associated with inflammatory bowel disease. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are substantially reduced, with a normocytic pattern. Low serum iron and low transferrin-binding capacity in the setting of elevated ferritin and reduced transferrin saturation support iron-restricted erythropoiesis related to inflammation rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency alone. The absence of symptoms and stable vital signs are reassuring, but the degree of anemia warrants timely evaluation and active management. There is no current evidence from the provided information of hemodynamic instability or cardiopulmonary compromise.

Inflammatory bowel disease, present for approximately 5 years, is a likely contributor to chronic inflammation and may also increase the risk of gastrointestinal blood loss or impaired iron utilization. Current inflammatory bowel disease activity and treatment status are not established in the available history and should be clarified.

Current tobacco use. Smoking may adversely affect inflammatory disease control and overall cardiovascular and pulmonary health.

BMI 28.1 kg/m², in the overweight range.

Plan

Anemia: Arrange prompt repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to confirm persistence and characterize marrow response. Obtain vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate. Review the trend in hemoglobin and assess for menstrual, gastrointestinal, or other blood loss, as well as symptoms of inflammatory bowel disease activity. The current laboratory pattern should not be treated as simple iron deficiency without further clinical correlation.

Inflammatory bowel disease: Review current gastrointestinal symptoms, disease activity, nutritional intake, and prior treatment with the patient. Coordinate timely follow-up with the clinician managing her inflammatory bowel disease, particularly if there is active bowel inflammation, suspected gastrointestinal blood loss, or need for assessment of iron replacement. Depending on clinical findings and inflammatory bowel disease activity, intravenous iron may be more appropriate than oral iron; this should be determined after evaluation. Erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy would not be indicated without an appropriate underlying indication such as selected renal or other chronic disease circumstances.

Safety counseling: Seek urgent medical attention for new shortness of breath at rest, chest pain, syncope, sustained palpitations, marked weakness, overt gastrointestinal bleeding, or rapidly worsening symptoms. Although she is currently asymptomatic with stable vital signs, the hemoglobin level merits timely follow-up rather than observation alone.

Tobacco use: Strongly advised smoking cessation. Discuss cessation strategies and pharmacologic support at a follow-up visit if desired.

Weight and preventive care: Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated, balanced nutrition, and gradual weight management. As part of the annual review, confirm age-appropriate preventive care, including cervical cancer screening, immunization status, and other routine cardiovascular risk assessment. Preventive care should be coordinated with the needs of her inflammatory bowel disease.

Follow-up: Review the anemia evaluation and laboratory trend promptly after testing, with earlier reassessment if symptoms develop. Continue routine annual preventive follow-up and ongoing monitoring of inflammatory bowel disease.
