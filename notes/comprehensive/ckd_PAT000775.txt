Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Aaron Price is a 69-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 23 years. Current renal indices remain markedly abnormal with serum creatinine 4.32 mg/dL, BUN 85 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m². His kidney disease is not currently under active management.

He reports ongoing fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No acute uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported today.

Past medical history is also notable for hyperlipidemia for approximately 29 years and coronary artery disease for approximately 8 years. He continues to smoke. Height is 161 cm, weight 75.2 kg, BMI 29.0 kg/m².

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Positive for fatigue.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.

Cardiovascular: Denies peripheral swelling.

Gastrointestinal: Denies nausea.

Genitourinary: Denies change in urinary frequency.

Musculoskeletal: Denies muscle cramps.

Skin: Denies itching.

Neurologic/Sleep: Denies sleep disturbance.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 147/87 mmHg, pulse 84/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 161 cm. Weight 75.2 kg. BMI 29.0 kg/m².

Laboratory Results: Serum creatinine 4.32 mg/dL. BUN 85 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m².

Physical Examination: Patient is ambulatory and in no apparent acute respiratory distress. No peripheral edema is evident. Respirations are nonlabored. Skin is without reported pruritus. No asterixis is observed.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, present for approximately 23 years and currently not under active management. Creatinine 4.32 mg/dL, BUN 85 mg/dL, and eGFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m² indicate advanced renal impairment. He is presently without nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, edema, urinary frequency changes, sleep disturbance, or dyspnea, but persistent fatigue may be related to chronic kidney disease. No current clinical evidence of fluid overload or overt uremic syndrome based on today’s history and examination.

Blood pressure is elevated at 147/87 mmHg in the setting of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease.

Coronary artery disease, reported duration 8 years.

Hyperlipidemia, reported duration 29 years.

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Overweight by BMI at 29.0 kg/m².

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation and active management of advanced chronic kidney disease. Discussed the importance of not delaying renal specialist assessment given the eGFR of 13 mL/min/1.73 m² and BUN of 85 mg/dL, even in the absence of significant uremic or fluid-overload symptoms.

Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as part of CKD evaluation. Renal ultrasound should be considered to assess renal structure and chronicity if not previously completed.

Review blood pressure management promptly, with a goal of kidney and cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring and review of the current medication list before making treatment changes. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter products. Encourage appropriate dietary sodium restriction, avoidance of excessive dietary protein, and maintenance of hydration without overhydration.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, particularly given the combination of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease. Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management, including review of lipid therapy and preventive care at follow-up.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including new or worsening shortness of breath, rapidly developing swelling, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, marked reduction in urine output, chest pain, or severe weakness.

Follow up after nephrology evaluation or sooner for any change in symptoms.
