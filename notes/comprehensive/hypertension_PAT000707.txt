Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

History of Present Illness

Rebecca Burke is a 46-year-old woman, 161 cm in height and 71.8 kg, with a BMI of 27.7 kg/m², presenting for routine follow-up of essential hypertension diagnosed approximately five years ago. Blood pressure control has not been actively managed. Today’s office blood pressure is 152/96 mmHg. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. There are no current symptoms to suggest hypertensive emergency.

Her medical history is also significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for six years, coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately one year ago, and hyperlipidemia diagnosed approximately one year ago. She does not smoke. No current antihypertensive medication regimen is provided.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Cardiovascular: Denies chest pain or palpitations.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.

Neurologic: Denies headache or dizziness.

Hematologic/ENT: Denies epistaxis.

Objective

Height is 161 cm, weight 71.8 kg, and BMI 27.7 kg/m². Blood pressure is 152/96 mmHg. Pulse is 74 beats per minute. Respiratory rate is 16 per minute. Temperature is 37.0°C. Oxygen saturation is 99%.

Serum electrolytes show sodium 144 mmol/L and potassium 4.0 mmol/L.

Physical examination is notable for the documented elevated office blood pressure. The patient is clinically asymptomatic at this visit, with normal respiratory rate and oxygen saturation. No additional abnormal examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, present for approximately five years, with persistent elevation at today’s visit and inadequate active management. The current reading of 152/96 mmHg is above the desired range, particularly in the setting of type 2 diabetes mellitus and established coronary artery disease. She has no symptoms or vital-sign findings suggesting an acute hypertensive emergency. Sodium and potassium are within expected ranges on the available electrolyte panel.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration six years.

Coronary artery disease, duration approximately one year.

Hyperlipidemia, duration approximately one year.

Overweight by BMI at 27.7 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management and reduction of long-term cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, and renal risk. Blood pressure should be reassessed with repeat office measurements and home monitoring using a validated upper-arm cuff, with a written log brought to follow-up. Attention should be paid to proper seated technique and repeated measurements after an appropriate rest period.

Given persistent elevation and the presence of diabetes and coronary artery disease, initiation of guideline-directed antihypertensive therapy should be addressed promptly with consideration of an agent appropriate for her cardiovascular and metabolic comorbidities. Medication selection should take into account renal function, albuminuria status, cardiovascular history, and tolerance. The available sodium and potassium values are suitable for medication planning, but ongoing electrolyte and renal monitoring will be needed after therapy is initiated or adjusted.

Recommend a heart-healthy, lower-sodium dietary pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction toward a healthier BMI, and limitation of alcohol intake. Continued smoking avoidance was reinforced.

Recommend completion or review of an ECG, lipid profile, urinalysis, and renal function assessment as part of cardiovascular risk and hypertension evaluation; these are recommendations and no results are available for review today. Diabetes and lipid management should remain coordinated with her overall cardiovascular risk-reduction plan.

She was advised to seek urgent medical attention for severe or sudden headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, visual loss, or other acute concerning symptoms. Follow-up should occur soon for blood pressure reassessment and treatment initiation or adjustment.
