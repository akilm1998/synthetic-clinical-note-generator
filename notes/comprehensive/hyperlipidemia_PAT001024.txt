Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Anna Cook is a 40-year-old woman seen for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia present for approximately 10 years and currently without an established treatment regimen. Recent lipid testing is markedly abnormal, with total cholesterol 384 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 277 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 37 mg/dL, and triglycerides 351 mg/dL. She reports fatty deposits involving the skin and eyelids, consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain consistent with claudication, and dizziness.

She currently smokes tobacco. Her medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 1 year and chronic kidney disease for approximately 1 year. Weight is 57.0 kg at a height of 161 cm, with BMI 22.0 kg/m². No current lipid-lowering medications are documented.

Review of Systems

Cardiovascular: No chest discomfort. No intermittent claudication reported.

Neurologic: No dizziness reported.

Skin: Reports xanthomas and xanthelasma.

Endocrine/metabolic: Known type 2 diabetes mellitus and markedly elevated cholesterol and triglycerides.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 136/84 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Anthropometrics: Height 161 cm, weight 57.0 kg, BMI 22.0 kg/m².

Physical Examination: The available examination is notable for reported xanthomas and xanthelasma. No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms are present by history. No additional examination findings are provided today.

Laboratory Data: Total cholesterol 384 mg/dL. LDL cholesterol 277 mg/dL. HDL cholesterol 37 mg/dL. Triglycerides 351 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe elevation of LDL cholesterol and significant hypertriglyceridemia, longstanding for approximately 10 years and not currently managed. The LDL level of 277 mg/dL is substantially above the threshold at which pharmacologic therapy is strongly indicated, independent of calculated short-term risk. The associated low HDL level, tobacco use, type 2 diabetes, and chronic kidney disease further increase her long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk. Xanthomas and xanthelasma support clinically significant lipid accumulation. She has no current chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness to suggest symptomatic vascular disease.

The degree of LDL elevation warrants evaluation for secondary contributors and possible inherited dyslipidemia, although a specific inherited diagnosis cannot be established from the information available. Triglycerides are also elevated and should be addressed through dietary intervention, glycemic control, smoking cessation, and LDL-focused therapy. Diabetes and chronic kidney disease are important comorbidities in determining treatment intensity and overall cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the seriousness of the lipid abnormalities and the importance of initiating active treatment rather than continued observation. Recommend initiation of a high-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, unless contraindicated, with shared decision-making regarding risks, benefits, and pregnancy-related considerations. Given the marked LDL elevation, recommend reassessment of response and addition of ezetimibe if LDL remains substantially above goal despite adherence and maximally tolerated statin therapy. Referral to a lipid specialist may be considered if the LDL remains markedly elevated or if inherited dyslipidemia is suspected.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation and assessment of adherence. Consider evaluation for secondary causes of dyslipidemia, including thyroid, hepatic, renal, and glycemic contributors, using clinically appropriate testing. ApoB and lipoprotein(a) may help further characterize risk, and coronary artery calcium imaging can be considered if treatment decisions remain uncertain; these would be future evaluations, not completed studies.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy diet emphasizing reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess dietary sugars; increased intake of vegetables, fiber, legumes, and unsaturated fats; and regular physical activity as tolerated. Her normal BMI does not eliminate the need for dietary and metabolic risk reduction.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered support with cessation planning. Continue coordinated management of type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic kidney disease, as both materially increase cardiovascular risk and may contribute to lipid abnormalities. She was advised to seek urgent evaluation for new chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain. Follow up after initiation of therapy and repeat lipid assessment to review response and adjust treatment.
