Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Henry Walsh is a 43-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 10 years ago. He is not currently described as following an active diabetes treatment regimen. Recent laboratory testing shows HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 245 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimal glycemic control. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. He also denies numbness or tingling in the hands or feet.

He has longstanding obesity, with a reported duration of approximately 11 years. Current weight is 100.4 kg at a height of 180 cm, with BMI 31.0 kg/m². He does not smoke. No medication use, allergies, or additional interval medical concerns are provided.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue and unintentional weight loss.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Endocrine: Denies increased thirst, frequent urination, and increased hunger.

Skin: Denies slow-healing wounds or frequent infections.

Neurologic: Denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 123/79 mmHg, pulse 79 beats/min, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 180 cm. Weight 100.4 kg. BMI 31.0 kg/m².

General: Obesity noted by BMI. He is clinically stable by the recorded vital signs and is not reporting symptoms suggestive of acute hyperglycemic decompensation.

Laboratory data: HbA1c 9.9%. Fasting glucose 245 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently markedly above usual outpatient glycemic targets based on HbA1c of 9.9% and fasting glucose of 245 mg/dL. Despite the degree of biochemical hyperglycemia, he denies polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, blurred vision, neuropathic symptoms, wounds, or recurrent infections. There is no reported evidence today of an acute metabolic complication.

Obesity, BMI 31.0 kg/m², longstanding and an important contributor to insulin resistance and diabetes control.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the increased risk of microvascular and cardiovascular complications if hyperglycemia persists. Reviewed nutrition goals emphasizing reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, increased intake of vegetables and fiber, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Weight reduction was encouraged as part of diabetes management.

Given the degree of hyperglycemia and the absence of an active treatment regimen in the provided history, pharmacologic therapy should be initiated promptly after review of contraindications and baseline renal function. Metformin would generally be an appropriate first-line option if renal function and gastrointestinal tolerance permit; additional therapy may be needed if glycemic response is inadequate. Medication risks, benefits, and adherence should be reviewed before prescribing.

Recommend assessment of renal status with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, along with a fasting lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment. Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensory testing, and arrange a dilated retinal examination. These evaluations are recommended for complication screening; no abnormal results are available from today’s visit.

Continue routine monitoring of home glucose if a glucose meter is available, with education regarding symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and when to seek urgent care, including vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, severe weakness, or inability to maintain hydration.

Return for close diabetes follow-up after initiation of therapy and review of recommended testing, rather than waiting for a routine annual visit.
