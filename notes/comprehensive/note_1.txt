Chief Complaint

Newly elevated blood sugar on recent laboratory testing.

Subjective

Diana Prince is a 71-year-old woman presenting for evaluation and management of newly identified type 2 diabetes mellitus. She states that routine laboratory testing obtained recently showed an elevated fasting glucose and hemoglobin A1c. She has not previously been diagnosed with diabetes and is not taking glucose-lowering medication.

She reports mild increased thirst and urination over the past several months, without dysuria, urgency, nocturia beyond once nightly, or hematuria. She denies increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, or significant fatigue. She has noticed occasional blurred vision when reading, which improves with her glasses. She denies eye pain, acute visual loss, or diplopia. She denies numbness, burning, or weakness in the extremities. There are no nonhealing wounds or recurrent skin or urinary infections.

Her diet includes frequent bread, pasta, and sweetened beverages, and she acknowledges limited regular exercise. She has had obesity for approximately four years. She has a history of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately seven years and depression for approximately 23 years. She reports that her mood is currently stable and denies suicidal or homicidal ideation. She does not smoke and reports no current tobacco use. Alcohol intake is occasional. No known history of cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, diabetic retinopathy, neuropathy, or peripheral vascular disease.

Assessment

Newly identified type 2 diabetes mellitus without current evidence of microvascular or macrovascular complication. Elevated fasting glucose and hemoglobin A1c are in the diabetic range and are consistent with the patient’s mild symptoms and risk factors, including age, obesity, limited physical activity, dietary pattern, and family history. Renal function and urine albumin excretion are currently normal. Foot examination is normal without neuropathy, ulceration, or impaired perfusion. She has no symptoms or examination findings concerning for hyperglycemic crisis.

Obesity, BMI 34.0 kg/m², contributing to insulin resistance and increasing cardiometabolic risk.

Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease with mild transaminase elevation, without reported symptoms of hepatic decompensation.

Depression, longstanding, currently clinically stable by history.

Plan

Discussed the diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus, expected disease course, treatment options, and the importance of preventing renal, retinal, neurologic, and cardiovascular complications. Patient expressed understanding and is agreeable to initiating treatment.

Begin metformin extended release 500 mg with the evening meal for one week, then increase to 500 mg twice daily if tolerated. Reviewed potential gastrointestinal adverse effects and advised her to contact the office for persistent diarrhea, vomiting, severe abdominal discomfort, or inability to maintain oral intake. Renal function is adequate for metformin use.

Recommend reducing sweetened beverages, desserts, and refined carbohydrates; increasing vegetables, fiber, lean protein, and portion control; and pursuing gradual weight loss. Encourage walking or other moderate-intensity activity for at least 30 minutes on most days, with gradual progression as tolerated.

Provide glucometer and education regarding fasting and occasional postprandial glucose monitoring. She may check fasting glucose several mornings per week and bring readings to follow-up. Reviewed symptoms of hypoglycemia, although risk is low with metformin monotherapy, and symptoms of significant hyperglycemia requiring medical attention.

Repeat hemoglobin A1c, comprehensive metabolic panel, fasting lipid profile, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio in approximately three months. Continue periodic renal monitoring.

Refer for a dilated diabetic eye examination, as this is a new diagnosis. Reinforced annual eye screening and prompt evaluation for acute visual changes.

Reviewed daily foot care, appropriate footwear, avoidance of barefoot walking, and the need to report any blister, wound, redness, drainage, or change in sensation. Foot examination performed today and was normal.

Discussed cardiovascular risk reduction, including dietary changes, physical activity, weight reduction, and review of lipid-lowering therapy at follow-up. Continue to monitor blood pressure.

Continue current depression management and follow clinically for mood changes. Patient denies suicidal or homicidal ideation.

Recommended influenza vaccination annually, current COVID vaccination as appropriate, pneumococcal vaccination, and completion of age-appropriate preventive care.

Return visit in approximately 4 to 6 weeks to assess medication tolerance and glucose readings, or sooner for worsening symptoms. Follow up immediately for persistent vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, inability to hydrate, chest pain, or shortness of breath.
