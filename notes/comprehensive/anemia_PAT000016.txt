Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for anemia.

Subjective

Isabella Parker is a 43-year-old woman, height 158 cm, weight 53.9 kg, BMI 21.6 kg/m², presenting for routine follow-up of anemia present for approximately 8 years. She reports no current fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, exertional intolerance, headache, palpitations, or shortness of breath. Pallor has been noted. No acute cardiopulmonary complaints today.

Her medical history is significant for inflammatory bowel disease for 8 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 4 years, and rheumatoid arthritis for 2 years. She currently smokes. There is no documented active anemia-directed management or established treatment regimen provided for review.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 117/82 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

General: Pallor noted. No documented acute distress.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic disease. The anemia is normocytic with reduced serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and reduced TIBC, a pattern more consistent with iron-restricted erythropoiesis in the setting of chronic inflammation than with uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Her longstanding inflammatory bowel disease and rheumatoid arthritis are likely contributors. Chronic kidney disease is not documented but should be assessed as another potentially reversible contributor. She is currently clinically stable without reported fatigue, dyspnea, dizziness, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance, although hemoglobin remains significantly reduced and pallor is present.

Inflammatory bowel disease, longstanding, with potential contribution to chronic inflammation and impaired iron availability.

Rheumatoid arthritis, present for approximately 2 years, also potentially contributing to inflammatory anemia.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 4 years. Glycemic control data are not provided; renal function is relevant to the anemia evaluation.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Obtain repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to confirm persistence and characterize marrow response.

Recommend renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers as part of the evaluation for contributing or alternative causes of anemia.

Review inflammatory bowel disease and rheumatoid arthritis activity with the treating clinicians, as improved control of chronic inflammation is central to correcting anemia of chronic disease.

Given the low transferrin saturation, assess for a concurrent component of absolute iron deficiency, particularly in the context of inflammatory bowel disease. Iron replacement should be guided by the complete clinical assessment and gastrointestinal disease activity; oral versus intravenous iron can be considered based on tolerance, absorption, and response.

If anemia persists, worsens, or fails to respond to treatment of underlying inflammation, consider hematology evaluation and discussion of additional therapy, including erythropoiesis-stimulating treatment only if clinically indicated after evaluation of renal function and other causes.

Reinforce smoking cessation, which is relevant to overall cardiopulmonary and inflammatory health.

Monitor for new fatigue, weakness, exertional dyspnea, dizziness, palpitations, chest discomfort, or worsening exercise tolerance. Arrange outpatient reassessment after the recommended evaluation and sooner for progression of symptoms.
