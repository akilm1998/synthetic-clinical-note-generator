Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, present for approximately 30 years. Current creatinine is 3.29 mg/dL, eGFR 18 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 60 mg/dL. She is currently asymptomatic without reported nausea, pruritus, fatigue, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, urinary frequency changes, or dyspnea. Despite the absence of symptoms, renal function is significantly impaired and requires active monitoring and renal-protective management.

Hypertension, longstanding for 52 years, currently poorly controlled with office blood pressure of 169/94 mmHg. This is an important modifiable risk factor for further renal and cardiovascular injury.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, diagnosed approximately 2 years ago, in the setting of chronic kidney disease. Current mineral and bone-related laboratory data are not available in the supplied record.

Overweight by BMI, 29.5 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed that renal function is significantly reduced despite the absence of current uremic or fluid-overload symptoms. Recommend prompt nephrology involvement for comprehensive CKD management, renal-protective planning, and discussion of options should kidney function decline further.

Repeat and trend kidney-related laboratory testing, including serum electrolytes, creatinine/eGFR, BUN, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously completed or if clinically indicated to assess renal structure and exclude obstruction.

Address blood pressure urgently. Recommend confirmation with repeat office measurement and home blood pressure monitoring, followed by individualized antihypertensive treatment and medication reconciliation. Avoid abrupt blood pressure reduction and tailor therapy to renal function, electrolytes, orthostasis risk, and overall goals of care.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal products at the next visit. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a treating clinician. Dose-adjust medications for renal function when applicable.

Counseled regarding moderation of dietary sodium, avoidance of excess processed foods, and maintaining appropriate hydration without forced fluid intake. Monitor for new swelling, shortness of breath, marked reduction or change in urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, progressive weakness, or chest pain.

Continue monitoring secondary hyperparathyroidism in coordination with nephrology; treatment decisions should be guided by calcium, phosphorus, alkaline phosphatase, and intact parathyroid hormone results.

Follow up promptly after repeat blood pressure assessment and laboratory testing, or sooner for any new symptoms. Seek urgent evaluation for severe shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, inability to maintain oral intake, or substantially decreased urine output.
