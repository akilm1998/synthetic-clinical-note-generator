Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Julian Holland is a 44-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, identified approximately 1 year ago. He has a 9-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, secondary hyperparathyroidism for approximately 1 year, and anemia of chronic disease for approximately 3 years. Chronic kidney disease has not been under active management.

He reports persistent fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. He does not smoke. No current medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 142/82 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 188 cm. Weight 94.4 kg. BMI 26.7 kg/m².

General: Outpatient follow-up evaluation; hemodynamically stable by vital signs.

Cardiopulmonary: No shortness of breath reported. Respiratory rate and oxygen saturation are normal.

Extremities: No peripheral edema reported.

Neurologic: No muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, or other uremic neurologic symptoms reported.

Laboratory data:
Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL.
Estimated glomerular filtration rate 6 mL/min/1.73 m².
BUN 68 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. Creatinine is 6.0 mg/dL, eGFR 6 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 68 mg/dL. He currently reports fatigue but no nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, urinary frequency change, or dyspnea. Although he is clinically stable by current vital signs and symptom report, the degree of renal impairment requires prompt specialty evaluation and active management. No formal disease stage is assigned today.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration approximately 9 years, a major risk factor for progressive kidney disease.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, present for approximately 1 year, associated with chronic kidney disease.

Anemia of chronic disease, present for approximately 3 years.

Mildly elevated blood pressure today at 142/82 mmHg.

Plan

Discussed the seriousness of the current kidney function results and the need for urgent nephrology evaluation. Recommend prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive chronic kidney disease management, assessment for complications, and discussion of renal replacement planning if clinically indicated.

Obtain repeat renal function and serum electrolytes promptly, along with CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Recommend renal ultrasound as part of evaluation of advanced chronic kidney disease if not previously completed.

Review diabetes control and cardiovascular risk factors at follow-up. Continue close blood pressure monitoring. Medication reconciliation is needed before making therapeutic recommendations; avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications unless specifically approved by a clinician.

Monitor for development of nausea, vomiting, worsening fatigue, confusion, pruritus, muscle cramps, markedly reduced urination, swelling, chest discomfort, or shortness of breath. Seek emergency care for severe shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, inability to keep fluids down, or significant reduction in urine output.

Follow up promptly after nephrology assessment and updated laboratory testing.
