Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Jordan Spencer is a 52-year-old male presenting for follow-up of hyperlipidemia first identified approximately 1 year ago. The condition has not been actively managed. He is not taking a documented lipid-lowering medication regimen.

He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain consistent with claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. He continues to smoke. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 11 years’ duration and longstanding obesity of approximately 24 years’ duration. Current height is 179 cm and weight is 100 kg.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 126/85 mmHg, pulse 80 beats/min, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Anthropometrics: BMI 31.2 kg/m², consistent with obesity.

Laboratory results: Total cholesterol 330 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 249 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 30 mg/dL, triglycerides 256 mg/dL.

Physical examination: No xanthomas or xanthelasma reported. No cardiopulmonary or peripheral vascular symptoms are reported at this visit.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol of 249 mg/dL, elevated triglycerides of 256 mg/dL, and low HDL cholesterol of 30 mg/dL. The LDL level represents severe primary hypercholesterolemia and warrants pharmacologic treatment independent of short-term calculated cardiovascular risk. The lipid pattern, obesity, active tobacco use, and chronic kidney disease confer additional cardiovascular risk. There are no currently reported symptoms suggestive of angina, claudication, or other symptomatic atherosclerotic disease.

Obesity, BMI 31.2 kg/m².

Chronic kidney disease, approximately 11-year duration; renal function values were not provided and should be considered when selecting and monitoring therapy.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and the need for active treatment. Recommend initiation of a high-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after reviewing contraindications, potential adverse effects, and patient preference. Given LDL cholesterol greater than 190 mg/dL, treatment should not be deferred pending coronary artery calcium scoring. If LDL reduction remains inadequate despite adherence and maximally tolerated statin therapy, addition of ezetimibe should be considered.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation and dose stabilization to assess response and adherence. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for additional risk characterization. Evaluate for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia, including thyroid, hepatic, renal, and metabolic causes, using clinically appropriate testing. Renal function should be reviewed before and during therapy because of the history of chronic kidney disease.

The LDL level warrants assessment for possible familial hypercholesterolemia, including a focused family history of premature cardiovascular disease and markedly elevated cholesterol. Encourage communication of relevant lipid and cardiovascular history to first-degree relatives as appropriate.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy dietary pattern emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, increased soluble fiber, vegetables, legumes, and whole grains. Recommend gradual, sustainable weight reduction and regular aerobic activity as tolerated. Address triglyceride elevation primarily through weight management, dietary modification, smoking cessation, and LDL-directed therapy; a fibrate is not indicated based on the currently provided triglyceride level alone.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered structured cessation support. Continue cardiovascular risk-factor monitoring. Follow up after repeat lipid assessment to review treatment response and finalize long-term lipid-lowering strategy.
