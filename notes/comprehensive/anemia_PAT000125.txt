Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for anemia.

Subjective

Harper Bennett is a 59-year-old woman, nonsmoker, presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding anemia, present for approximately 26 years and currently not under active management. She denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, palpitations, shortness of breath, reduced exercise tolerance, or other reported symptoms. She does not report pallor.

Relevant chronic conditions include rheumatoid arthritis for 29 years, chronic infection for 18 years, and heart failure for 7 years. No current medication regimen is provided.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue, weakness, or reduced exercise tolerance.
Cardiovascular: Denies palpitations.
Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.
Neurologic: Denies dizziness or headache.
Hematologic: No pallor reported.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 127/79 mmHg, pulse 85 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Anthropometrics: Height 180 cm, weight 73.5 kg, BMI 22.7 kg/m².

General: No acute symptoms reported during this visit.

Cardiovascular: Heart rate within normal range; cardiovascular examination is otherwise normal.

Respiratory: Respiratory rate mildly elevated at 21/min, with normal oxygen saturation and normal respiratory examination.

Laboratory Data:
Hemoglobin 8.3 g/dL
Hematocrit 24.4%
RBC 2.87 million/µL
MCV 85 fL
Serum iron 21 µg/dL
Ferritin 491 ng/mL
TIBC 235 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 8.9%

Assessment

Anemia consistent with chronic disease/inflammation, longstanding and currently untreated. The anemia is significant and normocytic, with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, low TIBC, and elevated ferritin, a pattern supporting inflammation-associated iron restriction in the setting of longstanding rheumatoid arthritis and chronic infection. Heart failure is also a relevant comorbidity. She is currently hemodynamically stable and denies cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms, but the hemoglobin of 8.3 g/dL warrants prompt evaluation and management.

Plan

Arrange prompt outpatient reassessment of the anemia and underlying inflammatory contributors. Obtain a repeat CBC with reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers. Review for evidence of ongoing blood loss or other contributors to anemia at follow-up.

Coordinate management of the underlying chronic inflammatory conditions. Consider iron replacement strategy only after completing clinical assessment and reviewing the overall cause of anemia; elevated ferritin with low transferrin saturation suggests limited iron availability rather than straightforward iron depletion.

Given the degree of anemia, seek urgent medical care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, marked weakness, or reduced exercise tolerance. Close follow-up is recommended rather than continued observation without management.
