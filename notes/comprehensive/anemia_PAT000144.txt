Chief Complaint

Initial evaluation of anemia and intermittent headache.

Subjective

John Mitchell is a 36-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of anemia documented over approximately 8 years. He has a known chronic infection of approximately 5 years’ duration. The anemia has not been actively managed previously.

He reports headache but denies fatigue, generalized weakness, reduced exercise tolerance, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or other cardiopulmonary symptoms. Pallor has been noted. He does not smoke. No medication use, allergies, surgical history, hospitalization history, or family history is provided.

Objective

Height 167 cm. Weight 68.3 kg. BMI 24.5 kg/m².

Blood pressure 122/79 mmHg. Pulse 79 bpm. Respiratory rate 16/min. Temperature 36.8°C. Oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

General: Alert and clinically stable appearing. Pallor noted.

Cardiovascular: Normal cardiovascular examination. No tachycardia documented.

Respiratory: Normal respiratory examination with nonlabored respirations.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL
Hematocrit 25.6%
RBC count 2.78 million/µL
MCV 92 fL
Serum iron 35 µg/dL
Ferritin 416 ng/mL
TIBC 193 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 18.1%

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of a chronic infection. The anemia is longstanding, with hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL, hematocrit 25.6%, and RBC count 2.78 million/µL. The normocytic indices, low serum iron and TIBC, and elevated ferritin support impaired iron utilization associated with inflammation rather than straightforward absolute iron deficiency. Transferrin saturation is reduced at 18.1%. The patient is currently hemodynamically stable without dyspnea, chest symptoms, tachycardia, dizziness, or exercise intolerance, though pallor and headache are present.

The degree and duration of anemia warrant confirmation of the pattern, assessment for additional reversible contributors, and evaluation of control and source of the chronic infection. There is no current evidence in the provided vital signs or symptoms suggesting acute cardiopulmonary compromise.

Plan

Obtain repeat complete blood count with red cell indices and reticulocyte count to confirm persistence and assess marrow response. Recommend peripheral blood smear, serum vitamin B12, serum folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as part of the evaluation for other contributing causes of chronic normocytic anemia.

Review the chronic infection in detail, including current activity, treatment status, and adequacy of control, because ongoing inflammation is likely contributing to the anemia. Management of the underlying infection should be coordinated with the appropriate treating clinician.

Do not begin empiric iron supplementation solely on the basis of the low serum iron, as the elevated ferritin and low TIBC favor inflammation-related iron sequestration. Iron therapy, including intravenous iron, should be considered only after confirmation of the overall iron status and clinical context.

Given the hemoglobin level, longstanding course, and incomplete prior evaluation, recommend outpatient hematology consultation for further assessment and treatment options, including consideration of disease-specific therapy if anemia persists after addressing the underlying inflammatory process. Erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy would not be initiated without clarifying the cause and reviewing renal function and other risk factors.

Discussed monitoring for worsening symptoms. He should seek urgent evaluation for new or progressive shortness of breath, chest pain, fainting, marked dizziness, palpitations, rapidly worsening weakness, or evidence of bleeding. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after the recommended evaluation to review results and establish an active management plan.
