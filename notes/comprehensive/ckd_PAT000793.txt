Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with significant reduction in estimated kidney function and elevated BUN and creatinine. She is currently without reported uremic symptoms, fluid-overload symptoms, or urinary changes. The prolonged history of hypertension and type 2 diabetes mellitus are important contributors and require close control. Given the degree of renal impairment, medication dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents are particularly important.

Hypertension, longstanding, with blood pressure 157/92 mmHg today and therefore not at an appropriate outpatient goal for a patient with chronic kidney disease. The current antihypertensive regimen and adherence need confirmation before assessing treatment effectiveness or making medication changes.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding. Glycemic control data were not provided today. Ongoing diabetes management should be coordinated with kidney-protective treatment and renal dosing considerations.

Medication review encounter. Adherence, therapeutic response, and medication tolerance require focused reconciliation. No medication-related symptoms are reported.

Plan

Complete medication reconciliation at the next available opportunity using the patient’s medication bottles and pharmacy dispensing history. Confirm each prescription, dose, frequency, indication, recent missed doses, cost or access barriers, and use of over-the-counter products. Assess effectiveness primarily through home and office blood pressure readings and diabetes monitoring. Review for dizziness, orthostasis, gastrointestinal effects, rash, muscle symptoms, or other intolerance before adjusting therapy.

Do not use nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs unless specifically directed by a clinician. Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications for renal dosing and avoid nephrotoxic supplements or medications. Encourage adequate but not excessive hydration and avoidance of unsupervised medication changes.

Because of the markedly reduced kidney function, recommend timely nephrology consultation for comprehensive CKD management and education regarding progression, renal replacement therapy planning if appropriate, and symptom monitoring. Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum electrolytes, complete blood count, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate to further characterize and monitor chronic kidney disease. These studies should be interpreted in conjunction with her primary care and kidney care teams.

Continue close blood pressure follow-up, with home blood pressure monitoring if available. Reassess antihypertensive therapy after medication reconciliation and review of adherence and tolerance. Continue coordinated diabetes management with attention to renal dosing and kidney-protective strategies.

Seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, rapidly increasing swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.

Follow up promptly after medication reconciliation and review of recommended kidney monitoring.
