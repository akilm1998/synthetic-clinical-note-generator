Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated diabetes measurements.

Recent laboratory testing shows HbA1c 9.5% and fasting glucose 206 mg/dL, consistent with substantially above-goal glycemic control. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, or unintentional weight loss. She also denies slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has a 16-year history of hypertension and a 43-year history of anxiety. She does not smoke. No current diabetes or antihypertensive medication regimen is documented in the supplied history.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with significantly elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose indicating inadequate glycemic control. Despite the degree of biochemical hyperglycemia, she currently denies polyuria, polydipsia, visual changes, constitutional symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, recurrent infections, or impaired wound healing. Her normal-weight BMI and 17-year disease duration warrant careful review of the current treatment history, adherence, nutritional intake, and possible progression of insulin deficiency before selecting or intensifying therapy.

Hypertension with blood pressure 155/83 mmHg today, above goal and requiring reassessment in the context of poorly controlled diabetes.

Anxiety, longstanding, which may affect self-management and follow-through; no acute anxiety-related concern reported today.

Plan

Discussed that the current HbA1c and fasting glucose reflect persistent hyperglycemia even in the absence of symptoms. Arrange prompt medication reconciliation and clinician-directed initiation or intensification of diabetes therapy based on prior treatment exposure, renal function, contraindications, dietary pattern, and ability to monitor glucose. Avoid assuming a regimen until this review is completed.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with fasting and, if directed, postprandial readings; review log at close follow-up. Provide diabetes nutrition counseling emphasizing consistent carbohydrate intake, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, and regular physical activity as tolerated while maintaining adequate caloric intake given BMI 20.6 kg/m².

Obtain or review renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before finalizing therapy, and assess fasting lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk management. Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot examination and routine dilated retinal evaluation given the 17-year duration of diabetes. Counsel regarding daily foot inspection and prompt evaluation of wounds or infection.

Repeat blood pressure measurement after rest and review home readings if available. Reassess antihypertensive treatment and adherence promptly; diabetes and hypertension should be managed together to reduce vascular and renal risk.

Seek urgent care for vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, inability to maintain oral intake, marked weakness, severe dehydration, or rapidly worsening hyperglycemic symptoms. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended after medication reconciliation and glucose review.
