Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Nicole Harper is a 49-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 3 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She has a history of coronary artery disease for approximately 3 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 24 months. She continues to smoke tobacco. No interval cardiovascular symptoms are reported.

Her weight is 66.7 kg at a height of 180 cm, with BMI 20.6 kg/m². The available lipid results remain markedly elevated: total cholesterol 330 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 235 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 45 mg/dL, and triglycerides 249 mg/dL. She is here to review disease control and update management.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: No constitutional symptoms reported.

Cardiovascular: Denies chest discomfort. No reported exercise-related leg pain or other claudication symptoms.

Neurologic: Denies dizziness.

Skin: Denies xanthomas or xanthelasma.

Respiratory, gastrointestinal, genitourinary, musculoskeletal, and psychiatric review is otherwise without concern reported today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 128/78 mmHg, pulse 80/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 180 cm. Weight 66.7 kg. BMI 20.6 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Total cholesterol 330 mg/dL. LDL cholesterol 235 mg/dL. HDL cholesterol 45 mg/dL. Triglycerides 249 mg/dL.

Physical examination: Patient is not reporting chest discomfort, claudication, dizziness, or lipid-related cutaneous findings at this visit. No xanthomas or xanthelasma are identified by history. Cardiopulmonary status is clinically stable based on the provided vital signs and absence of reported cardiopulmonary symptoms.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, currently untreated. The LDL level of 235 mg/dL represents severe elevation and, in the setting of known coronary artery disease and ongoing tobacco use, places her at substantial risk for further atherosclerotic cardiovascular events. HDL is 45 mg/dL. Overall control is not adequate.

Coronary artery disease, reported duration approximately 3 years, without current chest discomfort, dizziness, or claudication symptoms.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, reported duration approximately 24 months. The presence of fatty liver disease is not a reason to defer indicated lipid-lowering therapy, although liver-related laboratory monitoring should be incorporated into care.

Tobacco use, ongoing and clinically important cardiovascular risk factor.

Plan

Discussed the lipid results in detail, including the substantially elevated LDL cholesterol and the increased cardiovascular risk associated with coronary artery disease and continued smoking. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after medication reconciliation and shared decision-making. Given the LDL level and known coronary artery disease, additional therapy such as ezetimibe should be considered if LDL reduction is inadequate or if a statin is not tolerated. Treatment response and tolerance should be reassessed after initiation.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel approximately 4–12 weeks after starting or adjusting therapy. Recommend assessment of liver enzymes before or near initiation and with clinically indicated follow-up because of the history of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to severe dyslipidemia and measurement of lipoprotein(a) or apolipoprotein B to further refine cardiovascular risk. If treatment goals are not achieved despite maximally tolerated therapy, escalation to additional lipid-lowering treatment and coordination with cardiology should be considered.

Counseled on a heart-healthy diet emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, fish, and unsaturated fats while minimizing saturated and trans fats, processed foods, and excess refined carbohydrates. Encourage regular aerobic and resistance activity as tolerated. Her BMI is currently normal, so the focus is cardiovascular risk reduction rather than weight loss.

Strongly advised smoking cessation. Discussed that stopping tobacco is one of the most important modifiable interventions for reducing cardiovascular risk; cessation counseling and pharmacologic support options should be offered.

Reviewed symptoms requiring prompt medical evaluation, including new or persistent chest discomfort, shortness of breath, syncope, marked dizziness, or new exertional leg pain. Follow up after initiation of lipid-lowering therapy to review adherence, tolerance, and repeat lipid results.
