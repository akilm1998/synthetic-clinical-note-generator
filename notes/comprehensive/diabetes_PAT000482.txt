Chief Complaint

Annual review of type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic health conditions.

History of Present Illness

Hazel Fisher is a 55-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 14-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, currently without reported complications but with markedly elevated available glycemic measurements. Hemoglobin A1c is 10.0% and fasting glucose is 266 mg/dL. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. She also denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet.

She is not currently engaged in an active diabetes management regimen based on the history provided. There is no reported smoking history. Her weight is 60.1 kg at 162 cm, with BMI 22.9 kg/m².

Chronic comorbid conditions include dyslipidemia of approximately 2 years’ duration, obstructive sleep apnea of approximately 8 years’ duration, and anxiety of approximately 15 years’ duration. No medication list or allergy history was provided for review.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, longstanding for 14 years, with poor current glycemic control. HbA1c of 10.0% and fasting glucose of 266 mg/dL are substantially above usual treatment targets despite absence of current hyperglycemic symptoms. Her normal BMI does not lessen the significance of the laboratory abnormalities. There is no reported neuropathy, visual disturbance, wound problem, or recurrent infection at this visit.

Dyslipidemia, approximately 2 years’ duration. No lipid results or current treatment information were provided.

Obstructive sleep apnea, approximately 8 years’ duration.

Anxiety, approximately 15 years’ duration.

Preventive health maintenance review.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the need for prompt, structured diabetes treatment rather than continued observation alone. Recommend an early follow-up visit to establish a medication plan, with consideration of standard glucose-lowering therapy such as metformin when clinically appropriate and after review of renal function, contraindications, tolerability, and patient preferences. Given the degree of hyperglycemia, escalation beyond a single agent may be required if response is inadequate. Medication initiation should occur through shared decision-making after a complete medication and medical history review.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with education regarding technique, recording of results, recognition of symptomatic hyperglycemia, and urgent evaluation for vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, inability to maintain hydration, or rapidly worsening symptoms. Reinforced nutrition counseling focused on reducing concentrated sugars and refined carbohydrates, consistent carbohydrate intake, adequate hydration, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Her BMI is currently in the normal range; weight loss is not a treatment goal.

Recommend completion of diabetes complication surveillance, including urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive foot examination with protective sensation and pulses, and a dilated retinal examination. These should be used to guide treatment and risk reduction; no results from these evaluations are available in today’s information.

Review cardiovascular risk reduction in the context of diabetes and dyslipidemia. Continue to avoid tobacco. Review the lipid profile and overall cardiovascular risk before determining lipid-lowering therapy. Blood pressure is currently 124/76 mmHg.

Continue monitoring for symptoms of neuropathy, visual change, skin breakdown, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infection, excessive thirst, or frequent urination. Encourage daily foot self-inspection and prompt evaluation of any wound or color or temperature change.

Review ongoing management and treatment adherence for obstructive sleep apnea and anxiety at follow-up. Preventive care should include age-appropriate cancer screening, immunization review including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, and shingles vaccination as appropriate, and routine dental care.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after diabetes treatment is initiated or adjusted, with repeat HbA1c at an appropriate interval to assess response.
