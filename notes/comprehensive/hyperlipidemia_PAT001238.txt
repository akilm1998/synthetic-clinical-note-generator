Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Liam Matthews is a 63-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 28 years. The condition has not been actively managed. Recent lipid testing demonstrates markedly elevated cholesterol and triglycerides. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain consistent with claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 33 years, obesity for approximately 32 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 21 years. Current weight is 101.9 kg. He continues to smoke. No medication regimen is provided today, and no medication use is assumed.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 123/77 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 179 cm. Weight 101.9 kg. BMI 31.8 kg/m².

Laboratory Results:
Total cholesterol 368 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 253 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 42 mg/dL
Triglycerides 364 mg/dL

The available history is negative for chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. No additional physical examination findings are provided for this follow-up.

Assessment

Longstanding untreated hyperlipidemia with severe elevation of LDL cholesterol and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia. Total cholesterol is 368 mg/dL, LDL 253 mg/dL, HDL 42 mg/dL, and triglycerides 364 mg/dL. This pattern represents substantially increased atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of age, male sex, active tobacco use, long-standing type 2 diabetes, and obesity. He currently reports no cardiovascular or peripheral vascular symptoms.

Longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus, reported duration 33 years, contributing to overall cardiovascular risk.

Obesity with BMI 31.8 kg/m².

History of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. The markedly abnormal lipid profile warrants treatment planning while considering hepatic history.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the importance of active risk-factor management. Recommend initiation of guideline-directed lipid-lowering therapy, with a high-intensity statin discussed as the usual first-line approach for LDL cholesterol at this level and in the setting of diabetes and multiple cardiovascular risk factors. Medication selection and dosing should be reviewed after confirming current medications, allergies, prior statin exposure, and patient preferences. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of nonstatin therapy such as ezetimibe and consideration of specialty input would be appropriate.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment is initiated and stabilized to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia, including glycemic control, thyroid disease, renal disease, liver status, and dietary factors. Given the triglyceride level of 364 mg/dL, emphasize reduction of added sugars, refined carbohydrates, and alcohol if applicable; triglyceride-lowering medication can be reconsidered based on follow-up levels and overall cardiovascular risk.

Counseled on a heart-healthy, lower-saturated-fat diet emphasizing vegetables, whole grains, legumes, fish, and lean protein, along with gradual weight reduction and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage smoking cessation and offer structured cessation support at the next treatment discussion.

Continue routine monitoring of diabetes and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease in coordination with primary care management. Review for development of chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or other symptoms concerning for vascular disease and seek urgent evaluation for acute chest pain, dyspnea, or neurologic symptoms.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review medication decisions, tolerance, repeat lipid results, and progress with lifestyle and tobacco cessation efforts.
