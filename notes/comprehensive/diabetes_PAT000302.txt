Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Faith Hawkins is a 54-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 3 years ago. Current glycemic control is poor, with recent hemoglobin A1c of 10.0% and fasting glucose of 236 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, unintentional weight loss, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. She also denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet.

Diabetes is currently not being actively managed with a documented treatment regimen. No medication list was provided for review. She has a 5-year history of chronic kidney disease and a 16-year history of anxiety. She continues to smoke. No acute concerns are reported today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, diagnosed approximately 3 years ago, with marked hyperglycemia and inadequate current control. Hemoglobin A1c of 10.0% and fasting glucose of 236 mg/dL are consistent with significantly elevated average and fasting glucose levels despite the absence of current hyperglycemic symptoms. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of neuropathy, visual complications, impaired wound healing, or recurrent infection.

Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 5 years. The degree of renal impairment is not provided. This is clinically important when selecting and dosing glucose-lowering therapy and when assessing diabetic kidney involvement.

Anxiety, longstanding for approximately 16 years.

Tobacco use, ongoing. Smoking increases cardiovascular and renal risk in the setting of diabetes and chronic kidney disease.

BMI 25.2 kg/m², in the overweight range.

Plan

Discussed that the current A1c and fasting glucose indicate substantially uncontrolled diabetes and require active treatment rather than observation alone. Review and reconciliation of all current medications should be completed, including any prior diabetes therapies, adherence, cost or access barriers, and tolerance.

Recommend obtaining renal function assessment, including serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, before selecting or adjusting diabetes medication because of the history of chronic kidney disease. Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess for diabetic kidney involvement. Medication selection should be guided by renal function; metformin may be considered if appropriate for renal function and without contraindication, with additional glucose-lowering therapy considered given the degree of A1c elevation. Insulin therapy should be considered if clinically indicated based on repeat glucose assessment, symptoms, medication history, and overall risk.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with fasting and, when appropriate, postprandial checks while treatment is being established. Reviewed symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and advised prompt evaluation for persistent marked glucose elevation, vomiting, dehydration, confusion, or inability to maintain oral intake.

Counseled regarding reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, regular meals, and gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated. Nutrition counseling is recommended. Weight maintenance and prevention of further metabolic complications were discussed.

Recommend fasting lipid profile and assessment of cardiovascular risk as part of comprehensive diabetes care. Blood pressure is mildly elevated today at 138/85 mmHg; continue monitoring and reassess on follow-up rather than assigning a separate hypertension diagnosis from this single reading.

Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including skin integrity, pulses, and sensation, at follow-up. Recommend routine dilated retinal examination because of the duration and poor control of diabetes. Continue monitoring for neuropathy, visual changes, wounds, or infection.

Strongly advised smoking cessation. Discussed that cessation would reduce cardiovascular, renal, and overall diabetes-related risk. Offer cessation counseling and pharmacologic support if desired.

Anxiety should be reassessed at subsequent visits, including impact on self-care, sleep, and adherence. No medication or counseling regimen is documented today.

Follow up after completion of renal and diabetes monitoring studies to review results and establish or intensify a specific treatment regimen. Earlier evaluation is advised for symptomatic hyperglycemia or acute deterioration.
