Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Ava Mitchell is a 79-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 45 years ago. She is not currently described as having an active diabetes management regimen. Recent laboratory testing shows HbA1c 9.7% and fasting glucose 267 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimal glycemic control.

She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. She reports numbness in the hands or feet without associated tingling. No acute diabetic symptoms are reported today.

She has a longstanding history of depression, reported duration 59 years. No current depressive symptoms or safety concerns are provided for this visit. She does not smoke.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue and unintentional weight loss.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Endocrine: Denies increased thirst, frequent urination, and increased hunger.

Skin: Denies slow-healing wounds or frequent infections.

Neurologic: Reports numbness involving the hands or feet. Denies tingling.

Psychiatric: History of longstanding depression. No additional psychiatric concerns reported today.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 133/76 mmHg. Pulse 75 bpm. Respiratory rate 20/min. Temperature 36.8°C. Oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 176 cm. Weight 75.0 kg. BMI 24.2 kg/m².

General: No acute symptoms reported during this routine follow-up.

Laboratory Data: HbA1c 9.7%. Fasting glucose 267 mg/dL.

Physical examination today is notable by history for distal numbness. No diabetic wounds or other specific abnormal examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with markedly elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose indicating inadequate current control. Despite the absence of polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, blurred vision, or recurrent infections, the degree and apparent duration of hyperglycemia warrant prompt treatment reassessment. Distal numbness may represent diabetic peripheral sensory neuropathic symptoms, although formal neurologic and foot examination is needed to characterize this further.

Longstanding depression. No active symptoms are reported in the available interval history.

Normal-weight older adult with BMI 24.2 kg/m². Blood pressure is acceptable today at 133/76 mmHg. She is a nonsmoker.

Plan

Diabetes management was reviewed in detail. Discussed the significance of the HbA1c of 9.7% and fasting glucose of 267 mg/dL, including the risks of persistent hyperglycemia and the possibility of progression of neuropathic symptoms. A structured treatment plan should be established promptly rather than relying on observation alone. Medication selection should account for her age, renal function, nutritional status, hypoglycemia risk, and overall treatment goals. Renal function should be assessed before selecting or adjusting glucose-lowering therapy.

Recommend review of home glucose monitoring practices, dietary pattern, meal regularity, physical activity, medication barriers, and ability to manage a diabetes regimen safely. Encourage a consistent carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, and physical activity appropriate to her functional status and fall risk.

Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, and fasting lipid profile as part of diabetes risk assessment if not recently completed. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response.

Perform a comprehensive foot examination at follow-up, including skin inspection, pulses, and protective sensation testing. Counsel her to inspect her feet regularly and report wounds, color change, increasing numbness, or pain promptly. Recommend routine diabetic eye evaluation because of the prolonged disease duration and current hyperglycemia.

Given her age and reported numbness, avoid overly aggressive glucose lowering without consideration of hypoglycemia risk, but the current degree of hyperglycemia requires active intervention. Follow up soon after treatment is initiated or adjusted to review glucose readings, tolerance, adherence, and safety.

Depression

Continue to assess mood, sleep, interest, energy, cognition, and suicidal ideation at subsequent visits. Treatment and support options should be revisited based on her current symptoms, preferences, and existing care. Immediate evaluation is warranted if suicidal thoughts or significant functional decline emerge.

Follow-up

Arrange close outpatient follow-up for diabetes treatment initiation or adjustment and review of glucose monitoring. Return sooner for marked hyperglycemic symptoms, vomiting, inability to maintain oral intake, confusion, new visual changes, or a nonhealing foot wound.
