Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Eric Butler is an 85-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, reportedly present for approximately 38 years. Current renal indices remain markedly abnormal, with serum creatinine 4.12 mg/dL, BUN 91 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m².

He reports ongoing fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No interval fluid-overload symptoms are reported.

Relevant comorbidity includes gout for approximately 7 years. Smoking status is positive. Medication use, allergies, and prior renal imaging or specialist evaluations are not documented in the available history.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with longstanding duration of approximately 38 years and markedly reduced renal filtration. Creatinine 4.12 mg/dL, BUN 91 mg/dL, and eGFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m² indicate significant chronic renal dysfunction with azotemia. He currently reports fatigue but no nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, urinary frequency change, edema, or dyspnea to suggest prominent uremic or fluid-overload symptoms at this visit. Renal disease is not currently under an established management regimen based on the available history.

Blood pressure is elevated at 147/90 mmHg, relevant to ongoing renal risk.

Gout, present for approximately 7 years, is a clinically important comorbidity in the setting of advanced chronic renal dysfunction.

Current tobacco use, which increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease, assessment of renal trajectory, evaluation for complications, and discussion of renal replacement planning if appropriate.

Obtain repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, along with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to further characterize kidney disease and guide risk assessment. Check complete blood count and intact parathyroid hormone as part of evaluation for anemia and mineral-bone complications of chronic kidney disease.

Consider renal ultrasound to assess renal structure and exclude an obstructive or other structural contribution if not previously completed.

Review and reconcile all prescribed, over-the-counter, and herbal medications at the next contact, with particular attention to renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents. Avoid NSAIDs unless specifically directed by a clinician.

Continue close blood pressure monitoring and address persistent elevation in the context of kidney disease after medication reconciliation.

Review gout management with attention to renal dosing and avoidance of therapies that may worsen renal function.

Counsel regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of dehydration. Educate regarding urgent evaluation for new shortness of breath, progressive swelling, marked reduction in urine output, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or rapidly worsening weakness.

Follow up promptly after nephrology assessment and updated laboratory evaluation.
