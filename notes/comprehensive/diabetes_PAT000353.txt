Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Ian Lawson is a 37-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 11 years ago. Diabetes has not been actively managed recently. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Recent laboratory testing shows hemoglobin A1c of 9.6% and fasting glucose of 248 mg/dL, consistent with significantly elevated blood glucose despite the absence of reported hyperglycemic symptoms. No current diabetes medications were provided for review.

He also has a history of anxiety for approximately 3 years. No acute anxiety-related concerns are reported today. He is a current smoker. No other interval concerns are reported.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue and unintentional weight loss.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Endocrine: Denies increased thirst, frequent urination, or increased hunger.

Skin: Denies slow-healing wounds.

Neurologic: Denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet.

Infectious: Denies frequent infections.

Psychiatric: History of anxiety for 3 years; no acute concern reported.

No additional symptoms reported.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 115/78 mmHg. Pulse 72 bpm. Respiratory rate 14/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 180 cm. Weight 80.7 kg. BMI 24.9 kg/m².

Laboratory Results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.6%. Fasting glucose 248 mg/dL.

Physical Examination

No abnormal physical examination findings were provided for this visit. The patient reports no current foot symptoms, wounds, visual symptoms, or symptoms of volume depletion.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications

Eleven-year history of type 2 diabetes with current suboptimal control. Hemoglobin A1c of 9.6% and fasting glucose of 248 mg/dL indicate persistent hyperglycemia. He is currently asymptomatic and denies neuropathic symptoms, visual changes, recurrent infections, or poor wound healing. No complications are reported. BMI is 24.9 kg/m², and blood pressure is well controlled today.

2. Tobacco use

Current smoker. Smoking increases long-term cardiovascular and microvascular risk in the setting of diabetes.

3. Anxiety

History of anxiety for approximately 3 years without an acute concern reported today.

Plan

1. Discussed the significance of the elevated A1c and fasting glucose and the increased risk of future diabetic complications. Emphasized that lifestyle measures alone may not be sufficient at the current level of hyperglycemia.

2. Review current medication history and initiate evidence-based glucose-lowering therapy, with metformin a reasonable first-line option if no contraindication is identified. Renal function should be assessed before starting or adjusting metformin. If glycemic control remains markedly elevated, consider prompt escalation of therapy based on repeat assessment, comorbidities, cost, and patient preference.

3. Counsel regarding reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, regular physical activity, and consistent home glucose monitoring. Encourage recording fasting and occasional postprandial glucose values for review.

4. Recommend assessment of renal involvement with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, as well as a fasting lipid profile for comprehensive diabetes risk assessment.

5. Recommend comprehensive diabetic eye evaluation and routine foot examination, including inspection for skin breakdown and assessment of sensation and pulses. Reviewed the importance of daily foot inspection and prompt evaluation of any wound or infection.

6. Discussed smoking cessation and offered support with a quit plan and evidence-based cessation treatment. Continued smoking is particularly concerning given the elevated long-term vascular risk associated with diabetes.

7. Continue to monitor anxiety symptoms. Encourage discussion of sleep, stress, and functional impact at follow-up; treatment options can be addressed if symptoms are active or impairing.

8. Follow up after medication and glucose-monitoring review, with repeat glycemic assessment in approximately 3 months or sooner for worsening symptoms. Seek urgent care for persistent vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, confusion, severe weakness, or markedly worsening hyperglycemic symptoms.
