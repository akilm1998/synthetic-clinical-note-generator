Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Riley Benson is a 78-year-old male presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. Diabetes has not been actively managed. Recent laboratory testing shows HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 212 mg/dL, consistent with persistent hyperglycemia. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, unintentional weight loss, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has longstanding obesity of approximately 11 years’ duration and dyslipidemia of approximately 16 years’ duration. Anxiety has been present for approximately 13 years. Current medication regimen and allergies are not documented. He is an active smoker. No acute concerns are reported today.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue, fever, or unintentional weight loss.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Cardiovascular: No cardiovascular symptoms reported.

Respiratory: No respiratory symptoms reported.

Gastrointestinal: No gastrointestinal symptoms reported.

Genitourinary: Denies increased urinary frequency.

Endocrine: Denies polydipsia and polyphagia.

Musculoskeletal: No musculoskeletal symptoms reported.

Neurologic: Denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet.

Skin: Denies slow-healing wounds or frequent infections.

Psychiatric: History of anxiety. No acute psychiatric symptoms reported.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 128/76 mmHg, pulse 86 beats/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height: 184 cm.

Weight: 118.5 kg.

BMI: 35.0 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: HbA1c 9.3%. Fasting glucose 212 mg/dL.

Physical Examination: Patient is hemodynamically stable by recorded vital signs. No diabetic foot wounds, sensory complaints, or other diabetes-related symptoms are reported today. A focused foot and neurologic examination should be completed and documented at follow-up.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 212 mg/dL. Despite the degree of hyperglycemia, he denies polyuria, polydipsia, visual changes, weight loss, neuropathic symptoms, recurrent infections, or impaired wound healing. There is no reported evidence of an acute hyperglycemic syndrome.

Obesity, BMI 35.0 kg/m², longstanding and contributing to cardiometabolic risk.

Dyslipidemia, longstanding.

Anxiety, longstanding.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed that the current HbA1c and fasting glucose indicate inadequate glycemic control despite the absence of significant symptoms. Reviewed the importance of consistent diabetes management and the increased risk of vascular, renal, retinal, neurologic, and foot complications over time.

Recommend establishing a medication-based diabetes treatment plan after review of renal function and medication history. Metformin would be a reasonable initial option if renal function and other clinical factors permit; treatment should be individualized given his age and overall risk profile. Avoid overly aggressive glucose lowering or hypoglycemia, with treatment goals individualized through shared decision-making.

Obtain renal function, including serum creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate, before selecting or titrating therapy. Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and fasting lipid profile for diabetes risk assessment. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response.

Counseled regarding reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sweetened beverages, portion control, increased intake of non-starchy vegetables and fiber, and gradual regular physical activity as tolerated. Weight reduction was encouraged, with attention to safe activity and fall prevention.

Recommend home glucose monitoring if medication therapy with hypoglycemia risk is initiated or if clinically indicated. Reviewed symptoms requiring prompt evaluation, including marked thirst or urination, vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, or inability to maintain oral intake.

Perform a complete diabetic foot examination, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensation, at the next in-person assessment and advise daily self-inspection of the feet. Recommend a dilated retinal examination and routine dental care. Reinforced prompt evaluation of any wound, redness, drainage, or infection.

Smoking cessation counseling provided. Discussed the substantial cardiovascular and diabetes-related benefits of stopping tobacco use and offered assistance with a cessation plan.

Continue monitoring anxiety and address its effect on self-care and adherence. Follow up after initiation or adjustment of the diabetes management plan to review glucose readings, tolerance, lifestyle progress, and laboratory results.
