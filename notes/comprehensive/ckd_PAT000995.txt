Annual review and follow-up of longstanding chronic kidney disease.

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function

Longstanding chronic kidney disease of 29 years’ duration with creatinine 4.88 mg/dL, BUN 61 mg/dL, and eGFR 9 mL/min/1.73 m². She reports fatigue but currently has no nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. There is no reported evidence today of overt fluid overload or symptomatic uremia, although the degree of renal impairment places her at high risk for complications and requires prompt, structured renal care. Medication management and renal replacement planning are not documented.

2. Secondary hyperparathyroidism

Present for approximately 12 years in the setting of chronic kidney disease. Current parathyroid hormone, calcium, phosphorus, and vitamin D values are not available in the provided information.

3. Elevated blood pressure reading

Blood pressure today is 141/85 mmHg. In the setting of significant chronic kidney disease, blood pressure control should be reassessed carefully, with attention to volume status and kidney-protective treatment options. Current antihypertensive therapy is not documented.

4. Tobacco use

Current smoker. Tobacco exposure increases cardiovascular and renal risk. Smoking cessation counseling is indicated.

5. Preventive health and annual review

At age 71, preventive care should be individualized according to overall health, prior screening history, and preferences. Chronic kidney disease and tobacco use increase cardiovascular risk and make vaccination and cancer prevention particularly important.

Plan

1. Chronic kidney disease

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced renal dysfunction and discussion of renal replacement options, including dialysis education and, if appropriate, transplant evaluation. This should not be deferred given the eGFR of 9 mL/min/1.73 m², even in the absence of prominent uremic symptoms.

Obtain updated renal and complication assessment as clinically appropriate, including serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, albumin, intact parathyroid hormone, complete blood count, urinalysis, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. A renal ultrasound is also reasonable if not previously completed or if the etiology and structural status of the kidneys remain unclear.

Review all prescription medications, over-the-counter drugs, and supplements at the next contact; avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Any medication dosing should be adjusted for renal function. Encourage avoidance of dehydration and prompt evaluation for vomiting, diarrhea, or acute illness.

Discuss warning symptoms requiring urgent assessment, including worsening shortness of breath, new swelling, markedly reduced urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, severe weakness, or palpitations.

2. Secondary hyperparathyroidism

Coordinate evaluation with nephrology. Check intact parathyroid hormone, calcium, phosphorus, alkaline phosphatase, and vitamin D as part of CKD-mineral and bone disorder assessment. Treatment should be guided by these results and specialist recommendations; no specific phosphate binder, vitamin D preparation, or other therapy is assumed at this visit.

3. Blood pressure

Repeat blood pressure measurements and review home readings if available. Confirm current medication use before making changes. Reinforce a renal-appropriate, lower-sodium eating pattern and avoidance of excess processed foods. Antihypertensive therapy should be selected and titrated in coordination with renal function and nephrology input.

4. Fatigue

Fatigue may be related to advanced kidney disease, anemia, sleep, mood, or other medical contributors. Include CBC and metabolic assessment in the renal workup. Advise the patient to report worsening fatigue, weakness, dizziness, bleeding, or new exertional symptoms.

5. Tobacco use

Provide clear smoking-cessation counseling and assess readiness to quit, daily cigarette use, prior quit attempts, and interest in pharmacologic or behavioral support. Encourage cessation at every visit.

6. Preventive care

Review immunization status and recommend age- and risk-appropriate influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, shingles, and Tdap vaccination as indicated. Review breast, colorectal, and lung cancer screening history; given current smoking, assess eligibility for annual low-dose CT screening based on age and cumulative tobacco exposure. Continue cardiovascular risk assessment and encourage regular physical activity appropriate to functional status, balanced nutrition, and maintenance of a healthy weight.

Follow-up

Arrange expedited follow-up after renal evaluation and laboratory assessment, with routine primary care follow-up for blood pressure, smoking cessation, preventive care, and coordination of chronic kidney disease management. Seek urgent care for the warning symptoms reviewed above.
