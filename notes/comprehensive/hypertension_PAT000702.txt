Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure with dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue.

Subjective

Gavin Franklin is an 81-year-old man, height 188 cm, weight 85.9 kg, BMI 24.3 kg/m², presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding hypertension, reported duration approximately 24 years. His blood pressure is currently not being actively managed. He reports dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. No acute neurologic, respiratory, or ischemic symptoms are reported today.

Medical history is significant for coronary artery disease of approximately 13 years’ duration. He currently smokes. No current medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 153/95 mmHg. Pulse 79 bpm. Respiratory rate 16/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 94%.

General: Seen for outpatient follow-up; no acute distress is described.

Cardiovascular: Pulse is regular by recorded vital signs. No chest pain or palpitations are present at the time of documentation.

Respiratory: Respiratory rate is normal. No shortness of breath reported.

Neurologic: Dizziness reported without associated blurred vision or headache.

Laboratory data: Sodium 145 mmol/L. Potassium 4.2 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and associated dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. Hypertension has been present for approximately 24 years and is currently untreated or otherwise not actively managed. Given his advanced age and known coronary artery disease, blood pressure reduction should be approached deliberately with confirmation of readings and close monitoring for orthostatic symptoms.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding. He denies current chest pain or dyspnea, but the presence of palpitations and fatigue warrants clinical reassessment, particularly in the setting of uncontrolled blood pressure.

Current tobacco use, increasing cardiovascular risk.

Serum sodium and potassium are acceptable for consideration of antihypertensive therapy; additional baseline assessment is appropriate before selecting treatment.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure after seated rest and obtain home blood pressure readings, including occasional standing measurements because of reported dizziness. Maintain a written log for review.

Arrange prompt medication reconciliation and initiation of an evidence-based antihypertensive regimen, selected with consideration of age, coronary artery disease, renal function, orthostatic symptoms, and potential contraindications. Therapy should be started cautiously with close outpatient reassessment rather than rapid blood pressure reduction.

Recommend electrocardiogram for evaluation of palpitations and cardiovascular risk. Recommend lipid profile and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular and hypertension assessment. Obtain renal function testing before or soon after initiation of therapy as clinically appropriate.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, avoidance of excess dietary sodium, moderation of alcohol if applicable, and regular activity as tolerated. Continue a heart-healthy diet and maintain adequate hydration.

Seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, new or worsening shortness of breath, fainting, focal weakness, confusion, severe headache, acute visual change, or markedly worsening blood pressure with symptoms.

Arrange close follow-up within 1–2 weeks for blood pressure review, symptom reassessment, and treatment adjustment.
