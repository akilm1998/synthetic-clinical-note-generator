Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Evan Bryant is a 60-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. His hypertension is currently not under active treatment. Today’s blood pressure is 162/88 mmHg. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. No acute hypertensive symptoms are reported.

He has a history of coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, which is clinically relevant to blood pressure management. He reports no current chest discomfort or exertional dyspnea. He does not smoke. Height is 186 cm and weight is 82.3 kg, with BMI 23.8 kg/m².

Recent serum electrolytes show sodium 147 mmol/L and potassium 4.0 mmol/L. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Cardiovascular: Denies chest pain and palpitations.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.

Neurologic: Denies headache and dizziness.

Hematologic: Denies epistaxis.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 162/88 mmHg. Pulse 70 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Anthropometrics: Height 186 cm. Weight 82.3 kg. BMI 23.8 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: Sodium 147 mmol/L. Potassium 4.0 mmol/L.

Physical Examination: Patient is seen in the outpatient setting without reported acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms. Available objective findings are notable for persistently elevated blood pressure. No additional patient-specific examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension with current blood pressure of 162/88 mmHg, above goal and not currently managed with a documented treatment regimen. He is asymptomatic, without features reported to suggest acute hypertensive emergency. Given his coexisting coronary artery disease, sustained blood pressure elevation warrants timely initiation or re-establishment of antihypertensive therapy and close reassessment.

Mildly elevated serum sodium at 147 mmol/L with normal potassium of 4.0 mmol/L. This should be reassessed in clinical context, including hydration and dietary sodium intake, before selecting or adjusting therapy.

Coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, without current chest pain, dyspnea, or palpitations. Blood pressure control is an important component of cardiovascular risk reduction.

Nonsmoker. Normal BMI at 23.8 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated blood pressure and the increased cardiovascular risk associated with untreated hypertension in the setting of coronary artery disease. Recommend confirming the persistent elevation with properly performed repeat office measurements and home blood pressure monitoring, with a written log of morning and evening readings.

Recommend prompt clinician-directed initiation of antihypertensive therapy rather than continued observation alone. Medication selection should account for his coronary artery disease and be individualized after review of current medications, renal function, allergies, and prior treatment history. Potential options may include an ACE inhibitor or ARB, calcium-channel blocker, or thiazide-type diuretic as clinically appropriate; no medication is documented as started during this encounter.

Advise reduction of dietary sodium, avoidance of excessive alcohol intake, regular physical activity as tolerated, and continuation of tobacco avoidance. Maintain adequate hydration, particularly in view of the sodium of 147 mmol/L.

Recommend further evaluation appropriate for hypertension and cardiovascular risk, including renal function assessment, urinalysis, lipid profile, and ECG if not recently completed. Repeat serum electrolytes after treatment is initiated or adjusted, and reassess the mildly elevated sodium.

Because of the history of coronary artery disease, review cardiovascular preventive therapy and ongoing cardiology or primary care management as clinically indicated; no specialist recommendations or current preventive medications are documented here.

Reviewed urgent precautions. Seek emergency care for chest pain, new shortness of breath, neurologic deficit, severe headache, confusion, visual loss, or markedly higher blood pressure accompanied by symptoms.

Follow up promptly for blood pressure review and treatment planning, with earlier reassessment if home readings remain persistently elevated or symptoms develop.
