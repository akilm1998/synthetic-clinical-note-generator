Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Christian Cook is a 53-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding essential hypertension, present for approximately 15 years. Blood pressure control has not been actively managed recently. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. He has no acute cardiovascular or neurologic concerns today.

Medical history is significant for coronary artery disease for approximately 4 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 10 years, and chronic kidney disease for approximately 12 years. He currently smokes. No medication regimen, allergies, or recent home blood pressure readings are documented in the information available for today’s visit.

He is 173 cm tall and weighs 76.0 kg, with BMI 25.4 kg/m². Counseling focused on the importance of consistent blood pressure management given his coronary artery disease, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue.
Eyes: Denies blurred vision.
Cardiovascular: Denies chest pain or palpitations.
Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.
Neurologic: Denies headache or dizziness.
Hematologic: Denies epistaxis.
All other systems reviewed are without acute concerns reported today.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 149/91 mmHg. Pulse 80 bpm. Respiratory rate 14/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 99% on room air. Weight 76.0 kg. Height 173 cm. BMI 25.4 kg/m².

General: Alert, conversant, and in no apparent acute distress.

Cardiovascular: Pulse regular at 80 bpm. No acute circulatory symptoms reported.

Neurologic: No acute neurologic symptoms reported; patient is alert and appropriately responsive.

Laboratory Data: Serum sodium 139 mmol/L. Serum potassium 4.4 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding, with current office blood pressure of 149/91 mmHg. Control is suboptimal, and sustained management is particularly important in the setting of coronary artery disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and chronic kidney disease. He is asymptomatic today without features suggesting hypertensive emergency.

Coronary artery disease, reported duration 4 years, without current chest pain, dyspnea, or palpitations.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, reported duration 10 years.

Chronic kidney disease, reported duration 12 years. Current sodium and potassium are within a clinically acceptable range; renal function parameters were not provided.

Tobacco use, current smoker.

Plan

Discussed the need to reestablish active, consistent blood pressure management and the increased cardiovascular and renal risk associated with untreated hypertension in this patient. Recommend confirming blood pressure control with properly obtained home readings or repeat office measurements and reviewing the readings at follow-up.

Recommend initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy based on his complete medication history, renal function, diabetes, and coronary artery disease. Medication selection should account for kidney and cardiovascular comorbidities, with monitoring of renal function and potassium after any treatment change. No specific current regimen is documented today.

Continue dietary sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight maintenance, and avoidance of excessive alcohol intake. Strongly encouraged smoking cessation and offered cessation counseling.

Recommend review of a lipid profile, urinalysis, renal function testing, and electrocardiogram as part of cardiovascular and hypertension risk assessment; these are recommendations and were not completed or interpreted at this visit.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure accompanied by symptoms.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up to reassess blood pressure, review home readings, reconcile medications, and update management in the context of coronary artery disease, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease.
