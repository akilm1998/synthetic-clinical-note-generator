Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Owen Myers is an 82-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 44 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid results show markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with low HDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma.

He has a 16-year history of coronary artery disease, a 19-year history of chronic kidney disease, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 2 years. He continues to smoke. Weight is 82.0 kg at 167 cm, with BMI 29.4 kg/m². No interval cardiovascular symptoms are reported. He does not report new concerns related to his lipid disorder.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: No new constitutional concerns reported.

Cardiovascular: Denies chest discomfort. No intermittent claudication reported.

Neurologic: Denies dizziness.

Skin: Denies xanthomas or xanthelasma.

Respiratory, gastrointestinal, genitourinary, musculoskeletal, and psychiatric: No new concerns reported today.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with a markedly elevated mixed lipid profile, including total cholesterol 375 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 273 mg/dL, triglycerides 329 mg/dL, and HDL cholesterol 36 mg/dL. This represents substantial untreated atherosclerotic risk in the setting of established coronary artery disease, advanced age, active tobacco use, overweight status, chronic kidney disease, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. He is currently asymptomatic from a cardiovascular standpoint, without chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, without reported anginal symptoms today.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Active tobacco use.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Discussed the severity of the lipid abnormalities and the importance of cardiovascular risk reduction, particularly given his known coronary artery disease. Recommend initiation of guideline-directed lipid-lowering therapy after reviewing prior treatment history, potential adverse effects, renal and hepatic considerations, and the patient’s preferences. A high-intensity statin such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin would be clinically appropriate to discuss; additional therapy such as ezetimibe may be considered if LDL cholesterol remains substantially elevated or if statin therapy is not tolerated. Medication decisions should account for his chronic kidney disease and fatty liver disease.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, along with appropriate renal and hepatic monitoring. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to severe dyslipidemia and measurement of additional atherosclerotic risk markers such as apolipoprotein B or lipoprotein(a) if they would change management. Coronary artery calcium scanning is unlikely to add meaningful risk information given his established coronary artery disease.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy eating pattern with reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess dietary cholesterol; encouraged increased intake of vegetables, fiber, and unsaturated fats. Encourage weight reduction through gradual dietary and physical activity changes as tolerated.

Smoking cessation counseling provided. Advised that stopping tobacco is an essential component of reducing further cardiovascular risk; cessation support and pharmacologic options can be reviewed if he is ready.

Continue monitoring for new chest discomfort, exertional dyspnea, dizziness, or exertional leg pain. Seek urgent evaluation for persistent or severe chest symptoms, acute shortness of breath, or other concerning cardiovascular symptoms.

Follow up after review of treatment options and repeat lipid assessment to update the management plan.
