Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Cynthia Montgomery is a 76-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 46 years and currently not under active management. Blood pressure today is 158/95 mmHg. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. No symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive end-organ injury are reported.

She has a 39-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. She is a current smoker. Weight is 68.9 kg at 160 cm, with BMI 26.9 kg/m². Available serum electrolytes show sodium 135 mmol/L and potassium 3.6 mmol/L. No antihypertensive medications are documented.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Cardiovascular: Denies chest pain and palpitations.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.

Neurologic: Denies headache and dizziness.

Hematologic: Denies epistaxis.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 158/95 mmHg. Pulse 79 bpm. Respiratory rate 18/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Anthropometrics: Height 160 cm. Weight 68.9 kg. BMI 26.9 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: Sodium 135 mmol/L. Potassium 3.6 mmol/L.

Physical Examination: Focused examination is notable for the documented elevated blood pressure. No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms are reported during this encounter.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled, with blood pressure 158/95 mmHg. She is asymptomatic without reported features of hypertensive emergency. Given her age, prolonged duration of hypertension, active smoking, and longstanding type 2 diabetes, she is at increased cardiovascular and renal risk. Current potassium is low-normal at 3.6 mmol/L and sodium is borderline low at 135 mmol/L; these values should be considered when selecting and monitoring therapy.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding for 39 years, increasing overall cardiovascular and kidney disease risk.

Current tobacco use.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Discussed the significance of persistent blood pressure elevation and the importance of active treatment and regular monitoring. Recommend prompt primary care follow-up to confirm the blood pressure with repeat measurements and establish an antihypertensive regimen; medication selection should account for her diabetes, age, electrolyte values, renal function, and orthostatic risk. Do not make abrupt medication changes without clinical review.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring if available, with seated measurements after several minutes of rest and a written log for review. Counsel on reducing dietary sodium, avoiding excess alcohol, increasing physical activity as tolerated, weight maintenance, and smoking cessation.

Recommend evaluation of renal function and urine protein/albumin, repeat serum electrolytes after treatment is initiated or adjusted, urinalysis, lipid profile, and electrocardiogram as part of cardiovascular risk assessment. Continue comprehensive diabetes management and risk-factor reduction.

Seek emergency care immediately for severe or sudden headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic deficit, confusion, vision loss, or markedly higher blood pressure accompanied by symptoms. Arrange close outpatient reassessment for blood pressure management.
