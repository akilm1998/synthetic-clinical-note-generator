Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Nathan Jenkins is an 89-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 43 years ago. Diabetes has not been actively managed recently. Most recent HbA1c is 9.9% and fasting glucose is 243 mg/dL, consistent with inadequate glycemic control.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. He reports tingling in the hands or feet. No numbness is reported. No smoking history.

Comorbidity includes chronic kidney disease of approximately 4 years’ duration. Current diabetes medications, allergies, and home glucose readings are not documented in the information provided.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 243 mg/dL. Despite significant biochemical hyperglycemia, he denies polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual changes, or recurrent infections. Diabetes duration is approximately 43 years.

Paresthesia of the hands or feet, with tingling but no reported numbness. This warrants further clinical assessment for possible peripheral neuropathic symptoms, although no formal complication diagnosis is assigned today.

Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 4 years. Renal function should be considered carefully when selecting and dosing diabetes therapy.

BMI 29.8 kg/m², in the overweight range.

Blood pressure 142/87 mmHg today; mildly elevated reading noted, without assigning a separate hypertension diagnosis from this visit alone.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values indicate inadequate diabetes control and increased risk for microvascular and cardiovascular complications. Given his age and chronic kidney disease, treatment goals should be individualized, balancing improved glycemic control with avoidance of hypoglycemia.

Obtain a complete medication reconciliation and review any current or prior diabetes treatment before initiating or intensifying therapy. Consider renal function and hypoglycemia risk when selecting treatment. Metformin or other agents should only be used if appropriate for his renal function and overall clinical status; avoid aggressive treatment that could cause hypoglycemia.

Recommend home glucose monitoring as clinically appropriate, with review of fasting and symptomatic readings. Educated regarding symptoms of hypoglycemia and marked hyperglycemia and advised to seek prompt evaluation for confusion, vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, severe weakness, or persistently very high glucose readings.

Recommend dietary counseling focused on consistent carbohydrate intake, limiting concentrated sweets and sugar-sweetened beverages, and maintaining adequate nutrition. Encourage physical activity appropriate to his mobility and safety, with fall-risk awareness.

Recommend assessment of renal function, including serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, before finalizing medication choices. Recommend fasting lipid evaluation as part of diabetes risk assessment.

Perform or arrange a comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensory testing, given reported distal tingling. Encourage daily foot inspection and prompt attention to blisters, ulcers, redness, or wounds.

Recommend routine diabetic retinal evaluation and continued monitoring for visual symptoms.

Follow up after medication reconciliation and review of glucose monitoring and recommended renal evaluation to establish an individualized treatment plan.
