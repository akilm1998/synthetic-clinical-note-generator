Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Adam Ross is a 38-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 6 years ago. Current glycemic control is poor, with recent hemoglobin A1c of 9.1% and fasting glucose of 196 mg/dL. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has not reported a current diabetes treatment regimen. He does not smoke. Weight is 89.8 kg at a height of 181 cm, with BMI 27.4 kg/m². Relevant comorbidities include hypertension for approximately 3 years, depression for approximately 3 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 12 years.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue and unintentional weight loss.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Endocrine: Denies increased thirst, frequent urination, and increased hunger.

Cardiovascular: No cardiovascular symptoms reported.

Respiratory: No respiratory symptoms reported.

Gastrointestinal: No gastrointestinal symptoms reported.

Genitourinary: Denies frequent urination.

Skin: Denies slow-healing wounds and frequent infections.

Neurologic: Denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet.

Psychiatric: History of depression. No acute psychiatric symptoms reported today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 134/83 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Height 181 cm. Weight 89.8 kg. BMI 27.4 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Hemoglobin A1c 9.1%. Fasting glucose 196 mg/dL.

No diabetes-related complications are documented on today’s available information. No abnormal physical examination findings were provided beyond the recorded vital signs and anthropometric measurements.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, present for approximately 6 years, currently inadequately controlled based on hemoglobin A1c of 9.1% and fasting glucose of 196 mg/dL. Despite the absence of symptomatic hyperglycemia, the laboratory values indicate clinically significant persistent hyperglycemia. He is overweight but not obese. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of neuropathy, recurrent infection, visual complications, or catabolic weight loss.

Hypertension, approximately 3 years in duration. Blood pressure today is 134/83 mmHg.

Depression, approximately 3 years in duration. No acute psychiatric concerns reported today.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, approximately 12 years in duration. Weight management and improved glycemic control are particularly important given this comorbidity.

Plan

Discussed that current glycemic control is above target despite the absence of overt hyperglycemic symptoms. Emphasized nutrition counseling focused on reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, increased intake of vegetables and fiber, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encouraged gradual weight reduction and avoidance of further weight gain.

Because the A1c is 9.1%, pharmacologic treatment should be initiated or intensified rather than relying on lifestyle measures alone. Review of prior treatment history, medication reconciliation, contraindications, renal function, and patient preferences is recommended before selecting therapy. Metformin would generally be considered first-line if renal and hepatic considerations permit; additional therapy may be needed if glycemic response is inadequate.

Recommend home glucose monitoring, particularly while treatment is being established, with review of fasting and postprandial readings at follow-up. Reviewed symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and advised prompt evaluation for vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, marked weakness, inability to maintain oral intake, or rapidly worsening polyuria and polydipsia.

Recommend obtaining renal function with serum creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and a fasting lipid profile as part of diabetes risk assessment and treatment planning. Recommend periodic repeat hemoglobin A1c to assess response to therapy.

Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot evaluation, including skin integrity, pulses, and protective sensation, at a future visit. Recommend diabetic retinal examination and routine dental and skin care. He should promptly report any foot wounds, visual changes, or persistent sensory symptoms.

Continue blood pressure monitoring and reinforce a lower-sodium, heart-healthy eating pattern. Diabetes control and weight management may also benefit his hypertension and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Depression should be reassessed at follow-up, including mood, functioning, sleep, and safety. Treatment decisions should be coordinated with his current symptoms and preferences.

Follow up after initiation or adjustment of diabetes therapy to review glucose readings, tolerance, adherence, and repeat glycemic assessment.
