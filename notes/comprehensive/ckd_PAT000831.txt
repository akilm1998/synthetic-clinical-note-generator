Past medical history is significant for gout for 8 years and coronary artery disease for 2 years. Chronic kidney disease has been present for approximately 12 years.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal filtration by current laboratory results, including creatinine 4.94 mg/dL, eGFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 85 mg/dL. Despite the degree of renal impairment, he currently denies fatigue, pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, change in urination, and dyspnea. The absence of symptoms does not lessen the significance of the laboratory abnormalities. Medication management appears insufficiently defined at this encounter, and the current regimen, adherence, effectiveness, and tolerance cannot be meaningfully evaluated without an active medication list.

Blood pressure is 139/90 mmHg, with mildly elevated diastolic pressure in the context of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease.

Gout, longstanding, with renal impairment requiring careful selection and renal dosing of any urate-lowering or flare therapy. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs should generally be avoided unless specifically directed by a clinician familiar with his renal function.

Coronary artery disease, present for 2 years. Cardiovascular risk is increased in the setting of advanced chronic kidney disease and elevated blood pressure.

Plan

Medication review and safety: Establish and reconcile the complete current medication list, including prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal products. Assess adherence, therapeutic response, and adverse effects at the next medication reconciliation. All medications should be reviewed for renal dosing. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other nephrotoxic agents unless specifically approved by the treating clinician. Do not start, stop, or adjust cardiovascular, gout, diuretic, or kidney-related medications without review of renal function and the complete medication list.

Renal disease: Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation given eGFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m² and BUN 85 mg/dL. Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, and urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to further characterize current renal status and identify immediately actionable abnormalities. Recommend assessment for anemia and mineral-bone complications with CBC and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Renal ultrasound may be considered to evaluate kidney structure and obstruction if not previously performed.

Blood pressure and cardiovascular risk: Continue close blood pressure monitoring. Review the complete cardiac and antihypertensive regimen once available, with attention to renal dosing, blood pressure control, and secondary cardiovascular prevention. Avoid excessive dietary sodium and maintain appropriate cardiovascular risk-factor management in coordination with the treating clinician.

Gout: Review current gout treatment and frequency of flares once the medication list is reconciled. Any urate-lowering or acute flare therapy should be selected and dosed for severe renal impairment. Avoid self-treatment with over-the-counter anti-inflammatory medications.

Monitoring and precautions: Seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, marked weakness, rapidly progressive swelling, or a significant decrease in urine output. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended after medication reconciliation and repeat renal evaluation.
