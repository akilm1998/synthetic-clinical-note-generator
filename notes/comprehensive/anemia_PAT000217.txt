Chief Complaint

Initial outpatient consultation for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Avery Ward is an 87-year-old woman, 151 cm in height and weighing 51.1 kg (BMI 22.4 kg/m²), presenting for evaluation of longstanding anemia. Anemia has been present for approximately 29 years in the setting of chronic kidney disease of 31 years’ duration, chronic infection of approximately 50 years’ duration, and rheumatoid arthritis of approximately 29 years’ duration.

She denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, exertional limitation, shortness of breath, palpitations, or headache. Pallor has been noted. She reports current tobacco use. No medication history, allergy history, bleeding history, or prior anemia-directed treatment is provided for this encounter.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 145/86 mmHg, pulse 84 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

General: Pallor is present. No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms are reported.

Cardiovascular: Normal cardiovascular examination; pulse 84 bpm.

Respiratory: Normal respiratory examination. Oxygen saturation is 97% on room air.

Anthropometrics: Height 151 cm, weight 51.1 kg, BMI 22.4 kg/m².

Review of Systems: Positive for pallor. Negative for fatigue, weakness, reduced exercise tolerance, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, and headache.

Laboratory Data:
Hemoglobin 9.3 g/dL
Hematocrit 27.8%
RBC count 3.35 million/µL
MCV 83 fL
Serum iron 31 µg/dL
Ferritin 450 ng/mL
TIBC 210 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 14.8%

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, clinically stable and presently without cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms. The hemoglobin of 9.3 g/dL, hematocrit of 27.8%, and RBC count of 3.35 million/µL confirm clinically meaningful anemia. The MCV is within the normocytic range.

The iron profile demonstrates low serum iron and reduced transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and low TIBC. In the context of longstanding rheumatoid arthritis, chronic infection, and chronic kidney disease, this pattern is most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic disease, with impaired iron availability for erythropoiesis. Chronic kidney disease may additionally contribute through reduced erythropoietin production. The low transferrin saturation raises the possibility of concomitant functional iron restriction; the elevated ferritin argues against assuming uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency based on serum iron alone.

She is hemodynamically stable, with normal oxygen saturation and no reported dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, or exercise intolerance. Pallor is present. There is no current indication from the provided information for emergent intervention or transfusion.

Plan

Obtain a complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to further characterize erythropoietic response and exclude an alternative hematologic pattern.

Recommend serum vitamin B12 and folate levels, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as part of the evaluation of chronic normocytic anemia.

Repeat iron studies, including ferritin, serum iron, TIBC, and transferrin saturation, when clinically appropriate to assess the persistence and severity of iron-restricted erythropoiesis.

Review for potential sources of chronic blood loss, nutritional inadequacy, and other contributors to anemia as part of the broader clinical history and examination. Further evaluation should be guided by symptoms, examination findings, and the results of the recommended studies.

Management should focus on the underlying chronic inflammatory, infectious, rheumatologic, and renal conditions. If anemia remains persistent or worsens, consider coordinated evaluation with the clinician managing her chronic kidney disease regarding renal anemia and whether erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy is appropriate. Iron replacement should not be initiated solely on the basis of the low serum iron; the decision between oral or intravenous iron should follow reassessment of iron availability, renal status, inflammatory activity, tolerance, and the overall clinical context.

Monitor for new fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, chest discomfort, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, or evidence of bleeding. Seek urgent evaluation for rapidly worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, or clinically significant bleeding.

Counseled regarding tobacco cessation, given ongoing smoking and the potential adverse effects of tobacco use on cardiopulmonary and overall health. Arrange outpatient follow-up after completion of the recommended evaluation to review the anemia trajectory and determine further management.
