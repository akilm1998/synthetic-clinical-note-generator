Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Savannah Bell is a 51-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 5 years and currently not under active management. She has no chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 19 years, chronic kidney disease for 7 years, and hypertension for 2 years. Current weight is 72.0 kg at a height of 168 cm, with BMI 25.5 kg/m².

Cardiovascular: Denies chest discomfort and intermittent claudication.
Neurologic: Denies dizziness.
Skin: Denies fatty skin deposits or yellow eyelid deposits.
Respiratory, gastrointestinal, genitourinary, musculoskeletal, and constitutional symptoms were not reported today.

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 142/92 mmHg. Pulse 78 bpm. Respiratory rate 15/min. Temperature 36.6°C. Oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Anthropometrics: Height 168 cm. Weight 72.0 kg. BMI 25.5 kg/m².

Total cholesterol 333 mg/dL

No xanthomas or xanthelasma are reported. No cardiovascular or neurologic symptoms are reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with marked elevation of total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol and concurrent hypertriglyceridemia. Current lipid values are substantially above goal and indicate poor control in the setting of long-standing disease that has not been actively managed. She is asymptomatic without reported chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness. Her type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, and hypertension substantially increase overall cardiovascular risk. Nonsmoking status is favorable.

Hypertension with blood pressure above goal today at 142/92 mmHg.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, duration 19 years.

Chronic kidney disease, duration 7 years.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the increased cardiovascular risk associated with diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and hypertension. Recommend initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy, generally a high-intensity statin if no contraindication, after review of contraindications, drug interactions, prior intolerance, and patient preferences. If LDL reduction remains inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe or other lipid-lowering therapy can be considered.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, whole grains, legumes, fish, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess sugar. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated and continued avoidance of tobacco.

Obtain a follow-up fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and additional cardiovascular risk assessment, including apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a), as clinically appropriate. Medication selection and dosing should account for chronic kidney disease.

Reassess blood pressure outside the office and review the current hypertension management plan at follow-up. Continue coordinated management of diabetes and chronic kidney disease, with attention to therapies that reduce cardiovascular and renal risk.

Return for follow-up after lipid-management initiation or adjustment to review tolerance, adherence, blood pressure, and repeat lipid results. Seek urgent evaluation for new chest pain, significant shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or acute limb symptoms.
