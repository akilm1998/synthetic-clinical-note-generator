Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, with markedly elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose after approximately 15 years of disease and no active management currently documented. Although he denies symptomatic hyperglycemia, current laboratory values indicate inadequate glycemic control. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of peripheral neuropathy, recurrent infection, impaired wound healing, or acute metabolic decompensation.

Hypertension with blood pressure today of 141/91 mmHg. This is above the usual outpatient treatment goal and requires confirmation and active management, particularly in the setting of diabetes.

Obstructive sleep apnea, diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. Current treatment adherence and symptom control were not provided.

Current tobacco use.

Preventive health maintenance and diabetes complication screening require attention given the long duration of diabetes and current glycemic values.

Plan

Diabetes: Discussed the significance of the HbA1c of 9.8% and fasting glucose of 221 mg/dL, even in the absence of classic hyperglycemic symptoms. Arrange prompt diabetes-focused follow-up to establish an active treatment plan. Medication options should be reviewed after confirming renal function, current medications, contraindications, preferences, and ability to monitor glucose. Lifestyle counseling should include reduction of sugar-sweetened beverages and refined carbohydrates, portion awareness, regular physical activity as tolerated, and maintenance of a consistent eating pattern. Home glucose monitoring should be considered as appropriate to the treatment selected, with education regarding symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia.

Obtain recommended diabetes monitoring studies, including serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and fasting lipid profile. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval to assess response to treatment. Perform a comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensation, at follow-up. Recommend a dilated retinal examination because of the long duration of diabetes and elevated HbA1c. Review dental care and immunization status as part of preventive diabetes care.

Hypertension: Recheck blood pressure with proper technique and review home readings if available. Discuss sodium reduction, regular physical activity, tobacco cessation, and weight maintenance. Given coexisting diabetes and today’s elevated reading, initiate or adjust antihypertensive therapy after medication reconciliation and assessment of renal function and urine albumin excretion.

Obstructive sleep apnea: Review current sleep symptoms, use and effectiveness of any prescribed therapy, and daytime sleepiness at follow-up. Reinforce adherence to established sleep-apnea treatment if applicable and consider reassessment if symptoms remain uncontrolled.

Tobacco use: Strongly advised smoking cessation. Discuss readiness to quit and offer counseling and evidence-based cessation treatment at the next dedicated discussion, including nicotine replacement or prescription therapy if appropriate after medication and medical history review.

Preventive care: Review immunization history, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, hepatitis B, and other age- and risk-appropriate vaccines. Review cardiovascular risk factors, diet, physical activity, mental health, dental care, and age-appropriate cancer screening. At age 34, routine colorectal cancer screening would not generally be indicated unless additional risk factors are identified.

Follow-up: Close outpatient follow-up recommended to establish diabetes and blood pressure management, review renal, lipid, and urine albumin testing, complete foot assessment, and address sleep apnea and smoking cessation. Seek urgent care for vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, severe weakness, inability to keep fluids down, or rapidly worsening symptoms.
