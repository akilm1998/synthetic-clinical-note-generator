Assessment

1. Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides

The lipid profile demonstrates severe LDL elevation with low HDL and moderate hypertriglyceridemia. The LDL level of 232 mg/dL, long duration of disease, xanthomas, and history of coronary artery disease are concerning for longstanding atherogenic dyslipidemia and possible inherited lipid disorder. His normal BMI and absence of tobacco use do not eliminate the substantial cardiovascular risk associated with these lipid levels. There are no current symptoms suggestive of angina, claudication, or neurologic ischemia.

2. Coronary artery disease

Known coronary artery disease of approximately 10 years’ duration. He is currently free of reported chest discomfort, dizziness, or exertional leg symptoms. The presence of established coronary disease makes aggressive lipid reduction particularly important.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the relationship between elevated LDL cholesterol, triglycerides, and progression of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. Given the LDL cholesterol above 190 mg/dL and established coronary artery disease, recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy unless contraindicated, with medication selection and dosing reviewed after confirming medication history, allergies, prior intolerance, and patient preference.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel to confirm the baseline profile and assess response to treatment. Recommend evaluation for secondary contributors to marked dyslipidemia, including thyroid disease, diabetes, kidney disease, liver disease, dietary contributors, and other relevant causes. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for additional risk characterization. Coronary artery calcium scoring would not generally be necessary to establish treatment need in a patient with known coronary artery disease and this degree of LDL elevation.

If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe and consideration of further nonstatin therapy may be appropriate. Triglyceride management will primarily focus on LDL reduction, dietary measures, and limiting refined carbohydrates and alcohol; additional triglyceride-specific therapy can be considered based on follow-up levels and overall cardiovascular risk.

Counseled regarding a Mediterranean-style eating pattern, reduction of saturated and trans fats, increased soluble fiber, regular physical activity as tolerated, and continued avoidance of tobacco. Because of his coronary artery disease, he should seek urgent evaluation for new or persistent chest pressure, shortness of breath, syncope, or other acute cardiovascular symptoms.

Follow-up after repeat laboratory evaluation to review results, confirm treatment plan, and monitor lipid response and tolerance.
