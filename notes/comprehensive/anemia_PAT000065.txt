Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Erica Chapman is a 73-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 10 years. Her relevant comorbidities include malignancy of approximately 33 years’ duration and heart failure of approximately 3 years’ duration. She is not currently receiving documented management for the anemia.

She reports headache and appears pale. She denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, and reduced exercise tolerance. She does not smoke. No medication use, allergies, bleeding history, or recent procedures are documented in the available history.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue, weakness, or reduced exercise tolerance.

Cardiovascular: Denies palpitations.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.

Neurologic: Reports headache. Denies dizziness.

Hematologic: Pallor is noted. No other hematologic symptoms are reported.

Objective

Height 152 cm. Weight 52.4 kg. BMI 22.7 kg/m².

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 132/76 mmHg, pulse 90 beats/min, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Physical Examination: Pallor is present. Cardiovascular examination is normal. Respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory Data: Hemoglobin 9.1 g/dL, hematocrit 28.0%, RBC count 3.33 million/µL, MCV 84 fL. Serum iron 36 µg/dL, ferritin 486 ng/mL, TIBC 235 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 15.3%.

Assessment

1. Anemia of chronic disease/inflammation

Chronic anemia with hemoglobin 9.1 g/dL and hematocrit 28.0%, associated with a low RBC count and normocytic indices. The iron profile demonstrates low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and reduced TIBC, a pattern more consistent with iron sequestration in chronic inflammation than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Her longstanding malignancy and heart failure are clinically relevant potential contributors. She is hemodynamically stable, oxygenating normally, and denies cardiopulmonary symptoms, although pallor and headache persist. The anemia has not been actively managed based on the current history.

2. Headache

Headache is present without reported dizziness, weakness, dyspnea, or focal cardiopulmonary symptoms. Anemia may contribute, although the symptom is not specific. Clinical monitoring is appropriate, with escalation for new neurologic symptoms, abrupt or severe headache, visual changes, confusion, or worsening functional status.

3. Heart failure, established comorbidity

History of heart failure for approximately 3 years. No current shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance is reported, and oxygen saturation is 98% with a normal respiratory examination. The condition remains relevant to interpretation and management of the chronic anemia.

4. Malignancy, established comorbidity

History of malignancy for approximately 33 years. Chronic inflammation or malignancy-associated processes may be contributing to the anemia. No additional malignancy-related clinical information is provided in this encounter.

Plan

For the anemia, obtain interval CBC with red-cell indices and reticulocyte count to assess persistence and marrow response. Recommend peripheral blood smear, vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as part of an etiologic evaluation. Reassess iron studies in clinical context rather than treating the elevated ferritin as evidence of adequate usable iron; the low transferrin saturation may reflect functional iron restriction.

Review for occult blood loss, nutritional factors, and the current status of the known malignancy and heart failure at follow-up. Treatment should be directed at the underlying chronic disease. Depending on the subsequent evaluation and overall goals of care, consider hematology input regarding iron replacement or an erythropoietin-stimulating agent; no specific medication regimen is initiated or documented today.

Monitor headache and pallor. Seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest discomfort, syncope, sustained palpitations, marked weakness, confusion, neurologic deficits, or significant bleeding.

Continue routine outpatient follow-up after repeat assessment of the anemia and associated chronic conditions.
