Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Sydney Fuller is a 32-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. She reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No interval uremic or volume-overload symptoms are reported.

She has a 1-year history of gout. Current medication use, allergies, and prior renal workup are not documented in the available history. She does not smoke. Height is 154 cm and weight is 54.8 kg, with BMI 23.1 kg/m².

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.

Cardiovascular: Denies peripheral swelling.

Gastrointestinal: Denies nausea.

Genitourinary: Denies change in urinary frequency.

Skin: Denies pruritus.

Musculoskeletal: Denies muscle cramps. History of gout.

Neurologic: No sleep disturbance reported.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 128/85 mmHg, pulse 68/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Anthropometrics: Height 154 cm; weight 54.8 kg; BMI 23.1 kg/m².

Laboratory Data:
Serum creatinine 2.72 mg/dL.
BUN 90 mg/dL.
Estimated GFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m².

Physical Examination

General: Evaluation performed in the outpatient setting; patient reports no acute constitutional or cardiopulmonary symptoms.

Cardiovascular: Blood pressure and pulse as above.

Respiratory: Respiratory rate and oxygen saturation are within the provided measurements; no dyspnea reported.

Extremities: No peripheral edema reported by the patient.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced estimated GFR and significant azotemia. Creatinine is 2.72 mg/dL, eGFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 90 mg/dL. Although she is currently asymptomatic and normotensive by today’s measurement, the degree of renal impairment warrants prompt nephrology assessment and comprehensive evaluation. There is no current evidence from the interval history of symptomatic fluid overload or uremic prodrome.

Gout, present for approximately 1 year, is clinically relevant in the setting of substantial renal dysfunction. Medication selection and dosing will require renal adjustment, and nephrotoxic agents should be avoided.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology referral for evaluation and longitudinal management of chronic kidney disease, including assessment of etiology and renal replacement therapy education as clinically appropriate.

Obtain recommended renal evaluation with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum electrolytes, complete blood count, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound. Repeat renal function testing to assess current trajectory.

Perform a complete medication and supplement reconciliation at the next contact, with renal dosing of all therapies. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications unless specifically approved by a clinician. Gout treatment should be selected with attention to renal function; avoid empiric NSAID use.

Counsel regarding maintenance of appropriate hydration, avoidance of high-sodium dietary intake, and adherence to an individualized renal diet pending specialist evaluation. Monitor blood pressure and report new swelling, reduced urination, shortness of breath, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, or progressive pruritus promptly; seek urgent evaluation for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms.

Follow up promptly after nephrology evaluation and review of repeat laboratory testing.
