Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly abnormal cholesterol levels.

Subjective

John Jacob is a 53-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 23 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid panel shows total cholesterol 318 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 224 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 31 mg/dL, and triglycerides 316 mg/dL.

He denies current chest discomfort, intermittent leg pain with walking, or dizziness. He reports fatty skin deposits consistent with xanthomas. No xanthelasma is reported. Relevant cardiovascular risk factors include active tobacco use, obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 11 years, and known coronary artery disease for approximately 8 years. Weight is 96.0 kg at 174 cm, with BMI 31.7 kg/m².

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 120/78 mmHg, pulse 88 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

General: 53-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up; no acute cardiopulmonary symptoms reported.

Skin: Xanthomas reported/present. No xanthelasma reported.

Cardiopulmonary: No chest discomfort or dizziness reported. No additional examination findings provided.

Laboratory Data: Total cholesterol 318 mg/dL. LDL cholesterol 224 mg/dL. HDL cholesterol 31 mg/dL. Triglycerides 316 mg/dL.

Assessment

Severely uncontrolled mixed hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, elevated triglycerides, and low HDL cholesterol. The degree and duration of elevation, presence of xanthomas, active smoking, diabetes, obesity, and established coronary artery disease place him at very high risk for further atherosclerotic cardiovascular events. He has no reported symptoms suggestive of an acute coronary or limb ischemic event today.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Obesity, BMI 31.7 kg/m².

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the need for prompt initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy for secondary cardiovascular prevention. Recommend urgent medication reconciliation and clinician-directed initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, with consideration of additional therapy such as ezetimibe or a PCSK9 inhibitor if LDL cholesterol remains substantially above goal or statin therapy is not tolerated. Treatment decisions should account for his established coronary artery disease and diabetes.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation of secondary contributors and additional risk assessment, including lipoprotein(a) or apolipoprotein B, as clinically appropriate.

Counseled on a heart-healthy diet emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, avoidance of excess refined carbohydrates, weight reduction, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Strongly advised complete tobacco cessation.

Reviewed emergency precautions. He should seek immediate emergency care for new or persistent chest discomfort, shortness of breath, diaphoresis, fainting, sudden neurologic symptoms, or new exertional leg pain. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended to ensure treatment is started and lipid response is reassessed.
