Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal filtration and significant azotemia. Creatinine is 6.0 mg/dL, eGFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 70 mg/dL. Despite the absence of reported fatigue, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, urinary change, or dyspnea, the degree of renal dysfunction warrants prompt specialist evaluation and active management. There is no current evidence from the provided history of symptomatic fluid overload or overt uremic prodrome.

Blood pressure is elevated at 149/88 mmHg in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Longstanding secondary hyperparathyroidism.

Longstanding gout.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation to reassess the chronic kidney disease, review renal replacement therapy options, and determine whether urgent preparation or initiation of dialysis is indicated based on the full clinical assessment, electrolyte and acid-base status, volume status, nutrition, and evolving symptoms.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, albumin, CBC, intact PTH, urinalysis, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio as clinically appropriate. Renal ultrasound may be considered to assess renal structure and chronicity if not previously documented.

Review blood pressure management with attention to renal protection and avoidance of hypotension. Medication reconciliation is needed before selecting therapy; avoid nephrotoxic medications and adjust all prescriptions for the degree of renal impairment.

Continue monitoring for new nausea, vomiting, worsening fatigue, confusion, pruritus, muscle cramps, reduced urine output, edema, dyspnea, or chest discomfort. Immediate emergency evaluation is appropriate for development of severe shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, or rapidly worsening weakness.

Follow up promptly after nephrology assessment and updated laboratory evaluation.
