Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Nicholas Powell is a 79-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 8 years. He reports no current fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or other cardiopulmonary symptoms. Pallor has not been reported. He does not smoke.

His relevant chronic conditions include inflammatory bowel disease of approximately 43 years’ duration, rheumatoid arthritis for 10 years, and heart failure for 9 years. The anemia has not been under active management. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Assessment

Chronic significant normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of longstanding inflammatory bowel disease and rheumatoid arthritis, with heart failure as an additional chronic comorbidity. Hemoglobin is 8.3 g/dL with hematocrit 24.9% and RBC count 2.65 million/µL. The normal MCV of 94 fL supports a normocytic pattern. Low serum iron, low TIBC, ferritin 250 ng/mL, and transferrin saturation 8.4% are compatible with inflammation-associated iron sequestration and limited iron availability. The markedly low transferrin saturation also warrants evaluation for a concurrent iron-restricted component, particularly given the history of inflammatory bowel disease; this should not be assumed to represent uncomplicated nutritional iron deficiency.

He is currently without reported fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, headache, or reduced exercise tolerance. Blood pressure and oxygen saturation are stable, although the respiratory rate is mildly increased at 23/min and the hemoglobin level is sufficiently low to require timely reassessment and management. There is no provided evidence of acute hemodynamic instability.

Longstanding inflammatory bowel disease, rheumatoid arthritis, and heart failure may all contribute to chronic inflammation and anemia. Additional potentially reversible contributors, including occult blood loss, renal dysfunction, vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, and an inadequate marrow response, should be assessed before selecting therapy.

Overweight by BMI, 29.8 kg/m². Nonsmoker.

Plan

Arrange prompt repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count to confirm the current degree of anemia and assess marrow response. Recommend peripheral blood smear review, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers as part of the anemia evaluation.

Reassess iron availability in the context of chronic inflammation, including repeat iron studies as clinically indicated. Given the low transferrin saturation and history of inflammatory bowel disease, evaluate for gastrointestinal blood loss or active intestinal disease when clinically appropriate. Treatment should be guided by the repeat assessment and the activity of his inflammatory conditions; oral or intravenous iron may be considered depending on absorption, tolerance, disease activity, and response.

Coordinate management of the underlying inflammatory bowel disease and rheumatoid arthritis, as control of chronic inflammation is central to improving anemia of inflammation. Heart failure should be considered when assessing exertional tolerance, volume status, and the safety of any anemia-directed therapy.

No anemia-directed medication is documented at this visit. An erythropoiesis-stimulating agent should not be initiated without further evaluation of the anemia and contributing conditions; renal function and overall risk-benefit considerations would be required.

Provide strict precautions to seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, marked dizziness, palpitations, active bleeding, confusion, or abrupt decline in exercise tolerance.

Follow up promptly after repeat laboratory evaluation to review results and establish an active treatment plan.
