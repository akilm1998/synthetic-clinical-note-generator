Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Adriana Wells is a 53-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 7 years ago. Current disease control is poor, with recent hemoglobin A1c of 10.0% and fasting glucose of 226 mg/dL. She is not currently described as receiving active diabetes management.

She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No acute hyperglycemic symptoms are reported.

She has a 13-year history of obesity and a 16-year history of anxiety. She does not smoke. No medication regimen is provided for review.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue and unintentional weight loss.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Endocrine: Denies increased thirst, frequent urination, and increased hunger.

Skin: Denies slow-healing wounds or recurrent infections.

Neurologic: Denies numbness or tingling in the hands or feet.

Psychiatric: History of anxiety. No acute psychiatric symptoms reported.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 124/81 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Anthropometrics: Height 159 cm, weight 99.6 kg, BMI 39.4 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: Hemoglobin A1c 10.0%. Fasting glucose 226 mg/dL.

Physical Examination: The available examination data consist of the recorded vital signs and anthropometric measurements. No diabetes-related wound, sensory, visual, or vascular abnormality is reported in the supplied encounter information.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently markedly above glycemic targets based on hemoglobin A1c of 10.0% and fasting glucose of 226 mg/dL. Although she denies classic hyperglycemic symptoms and neuropathic or infectious complications, the degree of biochemical hyperglycemia requires prompt, structured treatment rather than observation alone. Duration of diabetes is approximately 7 years.

Severe obesity, BMI 39.4 kg/m², longstanding and clinically relevant to insulin resistance and diabetes management.

Anxiety, longstanding. No acute symptoms reported today.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated A1c and fasting glucose, including the risk of progressive microvascular and macrovascular complications even in the absence of symptoms. Diabetes care should be actively re-established with individualized nutrition counseling, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, and gradual progression toward regular physical activity as tolerated.

Prompt pharmacologic treatment is indicated. Metformin would generally be considered first-line therapy if renal function and other contraindications permit; given the A1c of 10.0%, additional glucose-lowering therapy may also be appropriate depending on treatment response, comorbid risk, affordability, and patient preference. Medication selection should follow review of renal function, cardiovascular risk, adverse-effect considerations, and ability to adhere to the regimen. No medication is documented as started at this encounter.

Recommend assessment of serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio for diabetes-related renal risk stratification, along with a fasting lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment. Recommend comprehensive dilated eye evaluation and a complete diabetic foot examination, including skin integrity, pulses, and sensation. These evaluations are particularly important given the duration of diabetes and current degree of hyperglycemia.

Encouraged home glucose monitoring if a glucometer is available, with documentation of fasting and occasional postprandial readings to guide treatment adjustment. Reviewed symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including persistent vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, confusion, severe weakness, rapidly worsening polyuria or polydipsia, or markedly elevated glucose readings.

Weight reduction was strongly recommended as part of diabetes treatment. Anxiety should continue to be addressed as part of comprehensive chronic disease care, with reassessment of its effect on sleep, eating patterns, activity, and adherence.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after initiation of an active treatment plan and review of recommended laboratory and preventive evaluations; repeat hemoglobin A1c after an appropriate interval to assess response.
