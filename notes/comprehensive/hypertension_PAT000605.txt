Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Penelope Long is a 69-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 21 years ago. Her blood pressure has not been actively managed recently. She reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or nosebleeds. No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic complaint is reported today.

She also has type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 12 years. Current diabetes treatment and home glucose readings were not provided. She continues to smoke. Weight is stable by current measurement, with BMI in the normal range. Dietary sodium intake, alcohol use, physical activity, medication history, allergies, and family history were not discussed today.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 153/94 mmHg. Pulse 76 bpm. Respiratory rate 14/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 168 cm. Weight 60.4 kg. BMI 21.4 kg/m².

General: Outpatient vital signs reviewed. No acute symptom requiring emergency intervention is reported.

Cardiovascular: Pulse 76 bpm. She reports intermittent palpitations; no associated chest pain or dyspnea.

Neurologic: Reports intermittent dizziness. Denies headache or blurred vision.

Laboratory data reviewed: Sodium 137 mmol/L and potassium 4.5 mmol/L, both within the provided reference context.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding, with current office blood pressure of 153/94 mmHg. Control is suboptimal, and she is not currently receiving documented active management. Intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue may occur with a variety of conditions and warrant reassessment, although she has no reported chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, visual change, or epistaxis to suggest an acute hypertensive emergency.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 12 years. Diabetes increases her long-term cardiovascular and renal risk and is relevant to antihypertensive selection and monitoring.

Current tobacco use, contributing to cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given the degree and longstanding nature of the elevation, as well as the coexistence of diabetes and tobacco use. Recommend confirming the outpatient blood pressure pattern with properly performed home readings, including seated rest for several minutes before measurement and recording readings at consistent times. Bring the log to follow-up.

Recommend initiation of antihypertensive therapy after medication reconciliation and review of renal function and urine albumin status. An ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker may be particularly appropriate to consider in a patient with diabetes, depending on kidney evaluation, contraindications, and shared decision-making. Her provided sodium and potassium values are acceptable for consideration of standard therapy, but potassium and renal function should be monitored after treatment is selected.

Recommend a low-sodium eating pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of tobacco, and moderation of alcohol if applicable. Smoking cessation was strongly encouraged; offer cessation counseling and pharmacologic support at follow-up.

Recommend evaluation for cardiovascular and hypertensive end-organ risk, including renal function testing, urinalysis or urine albumin assessment, lipid profile, and an ECG. These are recommendations and have not been completed as part of the information reviewed today.

Because of the reported palpitations and dizziness, advise prompt reassessment if symptoms become persistent or worsen. Seek emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, new neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or acute vision changes.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up within 2–4 weeks to review home blood pressure readings, complete medication reconciliation, reassess symptoms, and update the treatment plan.
