Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Abigail Reed is a 67-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 25 years. Her condition has not been actively managed recently. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and nosebleeds. No acute hypertensive symptoms are reported today.

Medical history is significant for coronary artery disease of approximately 17 years’ duration and chronic kidney disease of approximately 25 years’ duration. She does not smoke. Height is 175 cm, weight 73.8 kg, and BMI 24.1 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 170/95 mmHg; pulse 75 bpm; respiratory rate 14/min; temperature 36.9°C; oxygen saturation 98%.

General: No acute symptoms reported.

Cardiovascular: Pulse 75 bpm. No chest pain or palpitations reported.

Respiratory: Respiratory rate 14/min with oxygen saturation 98%. No shortness of breath reported.

Neurologic/visual: No headache, dizziness, or blurred vision reported.

Laboratory data: Sodium 139 mmol/L and potassium 3.8 mmol/L.

Assessment

Longstanding essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure of 170/95 mmHg and inadequate current control. She is asymptomatic today, without reported chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, visual changes, or epistaxis. Given the prolonged history of hypertension and coexisting chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease, she is at increased cardiovascular and renal risk. Current electrolytes are within expected ranges, but the available values alone are insufficient to assess renal function or medication safety comprehensively.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current blood pressure and the need for prompt re-establishment of active hypertension management. Confirm blood pressure with repeat measurement using appropriate cuff size and review home readings if available. Recommend initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy at this visit, selected with consideration of her chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease; medication choice and dosing should be individualized after review of her current medication list, renal function, and prior tolerance.

Recommend obtaining renal function testing and repeat serum electrolytes before or shortly after starting or changing therapy. Urinalysis, ECG, and lipid profile are appropriate for cardiovascular and hypertension risk assessment. Continue monitoring for hypotension or electrolyte abnormalities after treatment changes.

Counseled on limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, avoiding excess alcohol, and continuing tobacco avoidance. Encourage home blood pressure monitoring with a written log.

She should seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, new weakness or numbness, confusion, vision loss, or other acute neurologic symptoms. Arrange close outpatient reassessment, preferably within days, to review repeat blood pressure measurements and confirm that treatment has been initiated and is being tolerated.
