Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Charles Bennett is a 98-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension, present for approximately 59 years. Blood pressure control is currently suboptimal, and no active antihypertensive regimen is documented. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. He reports no current symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive end-organ involvement.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease of approximately 10 years’ duration, hyperlipidemia for approximately 21 years, and chronic kidney disease for approximately 21 years. He does not smoke. Height is 161 cm, weight 56.5 kg, and BMI 21.8 kg/m².

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue. No fever reported.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Cardiovascular: Denies chest pain or palpitations.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.

Neurologic: Denies headache or dizziness.

HEENT: Denies epistaxis.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 156/100 mmHg. Pulse 80 beats/minute. Respiratory rate 15/minute. Temperature 36.6°C. Oxygen saturation 96%.

Anthropometrics: Height 161 cm. Weight 56.5 kg. BMI 21.8 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Sodium 135 mmol/L. Potassium 3.7 mmol/L.

Physical examination: No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms are reported today. No additional examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Longstanding essential hypertension with current office blood pressure of 156/100 mmHg, without reported symptoms of hypertensive emergency. Given the duration of hypertension and comorbid coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease, persistent elevation warrants confirmation and careful treatment planning. The absence of headache, visual change, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, neurologic symptoms, or epistaxis is reassuring but does not eliminate chronic cardiovascular and renal risk.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, clinically asymptomatic by today’s review.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Current sodium is 135 mmol/L and potassium is 3.7 mmol/L; renal function results are not provided. Any antihypertensive selection and dose adjustment should account for kidney disease and advanced age.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement during the visit using appropriate technique and obtain home blood pressure readings if feasible, with attention to symptoms such as dizziness or orthostasis. Review the pattern of readings before determining the intensity of therapy.

Discuss initiation or re-initiation of antihypertensive treatment, using a cautious individualized approach because of advanced age, coronary artery disease, and chronic kidney disease. Potential options include an ACE inhibitor or ARB, calcium-channel blocker, or low-dose thiazide-type diuretic, selected after review of the complete medication history, renal function, volume status, and tolerability. No specific medication is documented as currently being taken, and no regimen is started in this note.

Obtain or review renal function and repeat serum electrolytes if antihypertensive therapy is initiated or adjusted. Monitor potassium and sodium closely, particularly if a renin-angiotensin system blocker or diuretic is used.

Recommend a heart-healthy, lower-sodium dietary pattern as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol if applicable, and regular activity appropriate to functional status and fall risk. Avoid overly aggressive blood pressure reduction if it produces dizziness, weakness, orthostasis, or reduced perfusion.

For cardiovascular risk assessment, consider ECG, lipid profile review, and urinalysis as clinically appropriate, with interpretation in the context of known coronary artery disease, hyperlipidemia, and chronic kidney disease.

Seek urgent medical attention for chest pain, new shortness of breath, severe headache, acute visual change, focal neurologic symptoms, confusion, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up after blood pressure confirmation and medication reconciliation to reassess control, tolerance, and renal/electrolyte status.
