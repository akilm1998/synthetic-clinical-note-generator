Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Leo Davidson is a 73-year-old man, height 176 cm, weight 70.9 kg, BMI 22.9 kg/m², presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding anemia. The anemia has been present for approximately 29 years and has not been actively managed. He currently denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or awareness of pallor. No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms are reported.

Relevant chronic conditions include rheumatoid arthritis for 19 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 22 years. He does not smoke. No current medication regimen, allergy history, bleeding history, or additional social history is provided.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of longstanding rheumatoid arthritis. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are reduced, with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and low TIBC, a pattern favoring impaired iron utilization associated with chronic disease rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. He is currently asymptomatic, with normal blood pressure, heart rate, and oxygen saturation. The degree and longstanding nature of anemia warrant further characterization and active outpatient management.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, without acute concern addressed today.

Rheumatoid arthritis, longstanding and clinically relevant as a likely contributor to chronic inflammatory anemia.

Plan

Obtain a repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to confirm persistence and evaluate marrow response. Check vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate to assess other contributors to the anemia. Review for occult blood loss and nutritional or gastrointestinal contributors during follow-up.

Coordinate management of the underlying inflammatory disease with the treating clinician. If anemia persists or worsens, consider hematology evaluation for further assessment and discussion of iron replacement or erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy when clinically indicated, particularly after renal function and other reversible causes are clarified. Do not begin empiric iron therapy solely on the current ferritin value without further clinical assessment.

Monitor for new fatigue, weakness, dizziness, exertional intolerance, dyspnea, palpitations, chest pain, syncope, or visible bleeding. Seek urgent evaluation for chest pain, significant shortness of breath, syncope, or rapidly worsening symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after repeat evaluation.
