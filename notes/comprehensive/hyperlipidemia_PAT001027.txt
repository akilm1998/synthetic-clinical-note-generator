Chief Complaint

Medication review and management of markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Emily Cooper is a 55-year-old woman, 156 cm tall and weighing 70.3 kg, with a 1-year history of hyperlipidemia and a 10-year history of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. She presents for review of lipid management. No active lipid-lowering medication regimen is documented for review. Accordingly, medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be assessed against a current pharmacologic regimen.

Recent lipid profile demonstrates total cholesterol 315 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 212 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 43 mg/dL, and triglycerides 301 mg/dL. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain consistent with claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She currently smokes. BMI is 28.9 kg/m².

No medication-related adverse effects are reported. No history of statin intolerance is provided.

Review of Systems

Cardiovascular: Denies chest discomfort and intermittent claudication.

Neurologic: Denies dizziness.

Skin: Denies xanthomas and xanthelasma.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 126/81 mmHg. Pulse 75 bpm. Respiratory rate 14/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Anthropometrics: Height 156 cm. Weight 70.3 kg. BMI 28.9 kg/m².

Laboratory Data:
Total cholesterol: 315 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol: 212 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol: 43 mg/dL
Triglycerides: 301 mg/dL

Physical Examination: No xanthomas or xanthelasma are reported. No other examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe elevation of LDL cholesterol and concomitant moderate hypertriglyceridemia. LDL of 212 mg/dL places her in a markedly elevated range warranting prompt pharmacologic treatment for primary prevention, independent of calculated short-term risk. Total cholesterol is 315 mg/dL, HDL is 43 mg/dL, and triglycerides are 301 mg/dL. She has additional risk-enhancing factors including active tobacco use, overweight status, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

The condition has not been actively treated with a documented lipid-lowering regimen. Medication effectiveness and tolerance therefore remain unassessed. The degree of LDL elevation raises consideration of a primary lipid disorder, although secondary contributors should also be evaluated. There are no reported symptoms suggesting current angina, peripheral arterial disease, or cerebrovascular prodrome.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding. This does not generally preclude statin therapy; treatment should be coordinated with assessment of hepatic safety.

Tobacco use, clinically relevant to overall atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discuss initiation of a high-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after shared decision-making and review of contraindications, potential adverse effects, and pregnancy-related considerations as clinically applicable. The therapeutic goal is a substantial reduction in LDL cholesterol, with reassessment of response and tolerance after treatment initiation.

Obtain baseline hepatic assessment before or at the time of starting therapy, particularly given the history of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Review for potential secondary causes of severe dyslipidemia, including thyroid disease, diabetes or dysglycemia, renal disease, dietary contributors, and other medication-related causes as clinically indicated.

Repeat a fasting lipid panel approximately 4–12 weeks after starting or adjusting therapy to assess adherence and treatment response, then periodically thereafter. If LDL reduction is inadequate despite confirmed adherence and maximally tolerated statin therapy, consider addition of ezetimibe and further risk-based lipid management.

Address triglycerides of 301 mg/dL primarily through reduction of saturated fat, refined carbohydrates, and alcohol if applicable, weight reduction, smoking cessation, and improved physical activity. A fibrate or prescription omega-3 therapy is not routinely first-line at this triglyceride level unless persistent elevation or additional clinical indications emerge.

Provide counseling regarding a Mediterranean-style eating pattern, increased soluble fiber, replacement of saturated fats with unsaturated fats, and gradual aerobic and resistance exercise as tolerated. Recommend smoking cessation with discussion of behavioral and pharmacologic support options.

At follow-up, specifically review medication adherence, missed doses, muscle symptoms, weakness, gastrointestinal symptoms, and any other perceived adverse effects. Reassess the lipid profile and adjust therapy based on response and tolerance.
