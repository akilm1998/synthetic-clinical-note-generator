Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up of anemia.

Subjective

Thomas Foster is an 87-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of anemia identified approximately 3 years ago. He has a 27-year history of rheumatoid arthritis, a chronic inflammatory condition. He is not currently documented as receiving treatment or monitoring for the anemia. He denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, or noted pallor. No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms are reported today.

He continues to smoke. Height is 171 cm, weight 63.5 kg, BMI 21.7 kg/m².

Objective

Vital Signs: BP 128/81 mmHg, pulse 88 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

General: No pallor reported. No tachycardia by vital signs.

Cardiovascular: Examination consistent with normal cardiovascular findings; pulse regular by recorded vital signs.

Respiratory: Respiratory examination without abnormality documented; oxygen saturation 95% on room air.

Laboratory Results:
Hemoglobin 8.5 g/dL
Hematocrit 25.7%
RBC 3.21 million/µL
MCV 80 fL
Serum iron 34 µg/dL
Ferritin 237 ng/mL
TIBC 203 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 16.7%

Review of systems is negative for fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, and pallor.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation associated with longstanding rheumatoid arthritis. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are significantly reduced, with a borderline normocytic pattern, low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated/retained ferritin, and low TIBC. This pattern favors inflammation-related iron sequestration rather than isolated absolute iron deficiency, although concurrent iron deficiency cannot be excluded on the available data. He is currently clinically stable and denies symptoms of tissue hypoxia, but the degree of anemia warrants prompt evaluation and active management.

Plan

Arrange prompt repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to confirm persistence and assess marrow response.

Obtain vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as part of the anemia evaluation.

Review rheumatoid arthritis activity and coordinate management of the underlying inflammatory disease. Consider hematology and/or rheumatology consultation if anemia persists, worsens, or the diagnostic evaluation is unrevealing.

Assess for occult blood loss and other sources of anemia as clinically indicated, particularly given his age and low hemoglobin.

Discuss treatment options after repeat evaluation, including iron replacement if absolute iron deficiency is demonstrated and consideration of other anemia-directed therapy in the setting of chronic inflammatory disease.

He was instructed to seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, palpitations, syncope, marked weakness, confusion, or evidence of bleeding. Smoking cessation was advised. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended after repeat laboratory evaluation.
