Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Melanie Knight is a 35-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. Blood pressure remains above goal today at 143/88 mmHg. She is currently not under an active antihypertensive management regimen. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus, each present for approximately 1 year. She does not smoke. Weight is stable at 54.5 kg with BMI 22.7 kg/m². No medication regimen or allergies are provided for review.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 143/88 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air. Height 155 cm; weight 54.5 kg; BMI 22.7 kg/m².

General: Afebrile with normal oxygen saturation and heart rate.

Cardiovascular: Pulse regular by vital-sign assessment. No additional examination findings documented.

Respiratory: Respiratory rate normal; oxygen saturation preserved.

Laboratory Data: Serum sodium 137 mmol/L. Serum potassium 4.5 mmol/L.

Review of Systems: Negative for headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and nosebleeds.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent elevation at today’s visit. She is asymptomatic, with blood pressure of 143/88 mmHg. The absence of acute neurologic, cardiopulmonary, or visual symptoms does not suggest a hypertensive emergency. Given coexisting coronary artery disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus, consistent blood pressure control is particularly important. Electrolytes are currently within the provided reference ranges, with sodium 137 mmol/L and potassium 4.5 mmol/L.

Coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago; clinically stable by current symptom review.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. No diabetes-related laboratory data are available in the current record.

Normal BMI and no tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management, including a low-sodium dietary pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, moderation of alcohol intake, and continued avoidance of tobacco.

Recommend confirming the degree of blood pressure elevation with standardized repeat measurements and home blood pressure monitoring, with a written log for review. Given persistent elevation and the presence of coronary artery disease and diabetes, pharmacologic treatment should be addressed at this visit using an agent appropriate for her comorbidities after medication reconciliation and shared decision-making.

Review current medications, allergies, renal function, urine albumin assessment, lipid profile, and diabetes control at follow-up before or during initiation and titration of therapy. Consider baseline ECG and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular and target-organ risk assessment.

She should seek urgent medical attention for new chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, focal neurologic symptoms, acute visual changes, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up after a short interval with home blood pressure readings to reassess control and adjust management.
