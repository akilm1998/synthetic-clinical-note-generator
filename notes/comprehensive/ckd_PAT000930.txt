Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced estimated kidney function, creatinine 3.64 mg/dL, eGFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 63 mg/dL. The patient is currently asymptomatic without reported nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, fatigue, edema, urinary changes, sleep disturbance, or dyspnea. Longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus is a significant risk factor. Blood pressure is mildly elevated at 141/86 mmHg. Given the prolonged history and current laboratory values, this requires active evaluation and coordinated renal care despite the absence of uremic or volume-overload symptoms.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for 23 years, with current renal disease requiring assessment of glycemic and renal risk-factor control.

Tobacco use.

Overweight by BMI, 29.3 kg/m².

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for comprehensive evaluation and management of advanced chronic kidney disease, including discussion of renal-protective care and preparation for possible future renal replacement needs if clinically indicated.

Repeat renal function assessment and obtain serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, complete blood count, intact parathyroid hormone, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess complications and etiology of the chronic renal impairment. Consider renal ultrasound as part of structural evaluation.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications at the next contact. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a treating clinician. Ensure medication dosing is adjusted for the current degree of renal impairment.

Address blood pressure and diabetes management with a kidney-protective approach. Encourage home blood pressure monitoring, avoidance of excess dietary sodium, moderation of dietary protein as clinically appropriate, and maintenance of adequate hydration without excessive fluid intake.

Counsel regarding smoking cessation and offer cessation support.

Monitor for new edema, reduced urine output, worsening shortness of breath, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, chest pain, or other acute changes. Seek urgent medical attention if these occur.

Follow up promptly after nephrology evaluation and updated testing, or sooner for any new symptoms.
