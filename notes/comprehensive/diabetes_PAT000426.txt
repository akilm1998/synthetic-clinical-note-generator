Chief Complaint

Annual review of longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Colin Graham is a 93-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 43 years ago. Diabetes is currently not under active management. Most recent laboratory results show HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 226 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimal glycemic control.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet. No smoking history; he does not smoke.

Weight is 100.6 kg at 179 cm, with BMI 31.4 kg/m², consistent with obesity of approximately 29 years’ duration. No medication list, allergies, or additional medical, surgical, family, or social history was provided for review today.

Physical examination today is notable for obesity by measured BMI. He reports no current distal sensory symptoms, skin wounds, or visual complaints. A complete diabetic foot and retinal assessment should be incorporated into ongoing preventive care.

Assessment

Longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, approximately 43 years in duration, with elevated HbA1c of 9.3% and fasting glucose of 226 mg/dL. Although currently asymptomatic, these values indicate clinically significant hyperglycemia and inadequate control. Given his age, treatment decisions should be individualized with attention to hypoglycemia risk, renal function, functional status, nutritional intake, and overall goals of care.

Obesity, BMI 31.4 kg/m², longstanding.

Preventive and chronic disease care requires review, including assessment for renal, retinal, neurologic, and cardiovascular complications of diabetes.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose despite absence of polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, or other hyperglycemic symptoms. Recommend confirming the current medication history and treatment goals with the patient and caregivers, if involved, before initiating or intensifying therapy.

Recommend renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before selecting or adjusting glucose-lowering medication. If appropriate after review of renal function, oral therapy such as metformin may be considered, with cautious dosing and monitoring given his advanced age. Avoid overly aggressive glucose lowering and prioritize avoidance of hypoglycemia. If oral therapy is unsuitable or inadequate, discuss other individualized options, including a simplified regimen appropriate to his functional status and ability to self-manage.

Recommend nutrition counseling focused on consistent carbohydrate intake, limiting concentrated sweets and sugar-sweetened beverages, and maintaining adequate nutrition without restrictive dieting. Encourage safe, regular physical activity as tolerated, with attention to balance and fall risk.

Recommend fasting lipid profile and cardiovascular risk review as part of diabetes care. Blood pressure is currently 136/80 mmHg; continue periodic monitoring.

Recommend comprehensive dilated eye examination and annual diabetic retinal screening. Perform a complete foot examination at follow-up, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensory testing, and reinforce daily foot inspection and well-fitting footwear. Advise prompt evaluation for any wound, redness, drainage, or skin breakdown.

Review immunizations and age-appropriate preventive care, including influenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19, and shingles vaccination as appropriate. Continue routine screening and preventive decisions based on life expectancy, functional status, and patient preferences.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after medication and renal assessment to review glucose management and tolerance. Repeat HbA1c at an clinically appropriate interval after treatment changes, with earlier review for symptoms of marked hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia.
