Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure with dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue.

Height is 185 cm and weight is 77.3 kg, with BMI 22.6 kg/m².

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, inadequately controlled. Blood pressure is significantly elevated at 158/98 mmHg in the setting of approximately 1 year of hypertension without active management. Dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue are present, but he denies symptoms concerning for hypertensive emergency, including chest pain, dyspnea, severe headache, visual disturbance, or epistaxis. His diabetes and chronic kidney disease increase his cardiovascular and renal risk and warrant prompt outpatient treatment and close monitoring.

2. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed 3 years ago. Comorbidity increases overall cardiovascular risk and influences blood pressure treatment goals and medication selection.

3. Chronic kidney disease, diagnosed 1 year ago. Current sodium and potassium are within the supplied reference context; renal function data are not provided. Blood pressure control is particularly important to reduce further renal risk.

4. Hyperlipidemia, diagnosed 1 year ago. This contributes to elevated long-term cardiovascular risk.

5. Tobacco use. Active smoking further increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

6. Dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. These may occur in the setting of uncontrolled blood pressure but require reassessment if persistent, worsening, or associated with cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms.

Plan

Discussed the need for prompt initiation or re-establishment of a structured hypertension treatment plan with medication selection tailored to his diabetes and chronic kidney disease. Confirm current medications, adherence, allergies, and prior treatment response before prescribing. Avoid self-adjustment of any medication.

Begin home blood pressure monitoring if available, with readings recorded at consistent times and reviewed at follow-up. Reinforced reduced sodium intake, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and continued attention to healthy weight; BMI is currently 22.6 kg/m².

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered discussion of cessation supports at follow-up.

Recommend evaluation with renal function testing, urinalysis, lipid profile, and ECG as clinically appropriate for hypertension with chronic kidney disease, diabetes, palpitations, and hyperlipidemia. Continue monitoring serum electrolytes after any antihypertensive therapy affecting renal function or potassium.

Seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe or abrupt headache, new visual or neurologic symptoms, syncope, or markedly higher blood pressure with concerning symptoms.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment initiation or adjustment.
