Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and abnormal renal function.

Subjective

Amelia Foster is a 91-year-old woman, 153 cm tall and 69.5 kg (BMI 29.7 kg/m²), presenting for urgent outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease reportedly present for approximately 50 years. Current management has not been established. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath.

Her medical history is also significant for secondary hyperparathyroidism for approximately 30 years, hyperlipidemia for approximately 50 years, and coronary artery disease for approximately 30 years. She currently smokes. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 146/86 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Anthropometrics: Height 153 cm, weight 69.5 kg, BMI 29.7 kg/m².

Laboratory Results:
Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL.
BUN 62 mg/dL.
Estimated GFR 6 mL/min/1.73 m².

Review of systems is positive for fatigue. She specifically denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, edema, urinary frequency changes, sleep disturbance, and dyspnea.

Physical examination is limited to the documented vital signs and symptom-directed assessment. She is afebrile with normal respiratory rate and oxygen saturation. No peripheral swelling or respiratory symptoms are reported.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, longstanding by history, with creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 62 mg/dL, and eGFR 6 mL/min/1.73 m². Although she reports only fatigue and no current symptoms suggestive of fluid overload or overt uremic syndrome, these results represent severe renal impairment requiring urgent specialist and laboratory assessment. Current renal management is not established.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Elevated blood pressure reading in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Recommend same-day emergency department evaluation or immediate contact with nephrology for assessment of the markedly abnormal renal function, particularly given her age and eGFR of 6. She should seek emergency care immediately for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent nausea or vomiting, weakness, reduced urine output, or swelling.

Recommend urgent repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, and CBC, as well as urinalysis and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. These are recommendations for evaluation and have not been completed today.

Recommend urgent nephrology consultation to clarify the cause and chronicity of the renal impairment, review goals of care and treatment options, and assess whether renal replacement therapy may be indicated based on symptoms, examination, and additional laboratory findings. Renal ultrasound may be considered as part of the evaluation if not previously performed.

Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter products unless specifically approved by a clinician. Medication reconciliation is needed before initiating or adjusting therapy. Blood pressure management should be reviewed in the context of renal function and coronary artery disease.

Continue monitoring for edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, urinary changes, and worsening fatigue. Smoking cessation was advised. Close follow-up is required after urgent renal evaluation.
