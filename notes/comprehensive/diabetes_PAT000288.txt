Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Aria Patterson is a 37-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 11 years ago. Diabetes control is currently poor, with recent hemoglobin A1c of 10.0% and fasting glucose of 251 mg/dL. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has not reported an established diabetes treatment regimen at this visit. No medication use or medication-related adverse effects are provided. The importance of active management was reviewed given the degree of hyperglycemia.

Past medical history is notable for obesity for approximately 19 years, hypertension for approximately 1 year, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 7 years. She is a nonsmoker.

Review of systems is negative for constitutional symptoms, including fatigue and unintentional weight loss. No visual complaints are reported. No polyuria, polydipsia, or polyphagia. No skin wounds or recurrent infections. No peripheral numbness or tingling. No additional acute concerns are reported.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 138/87 mmHg. Pulse 81 bpm. Respiratory rate 18/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 161 cm. Weight 101.4 kg. BMI 39.1 kg/m².

Laboratory Results: Hemoglobin A1c 10.0%. Fasting glucose 251 mg/dL.

General: Adult woman with severe obesity, presenting for outpatient follow-up. No acute symptoms reported.

Cardiopulmonary: Respiratory rate and oxygen saturation are within normal limits. No respiratory distress is evident from the available assessment.

Neurologic: No numbness or tingling reported in the extremities.

Skin: No slow-healing wounds or recurrent infections reported.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently markedly above glycemic goal based on hemoglobin A1c of 10.0% and fasting glucose of 251 mg/dL. Although she denies classic hyperglycemic symptoms, the laboratory values indicate significant persistent hyperglycemia. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of neuropathy, infection, or constitutional complications.

Severe obesity with BMI 39.1 kg/m², a significant contributor to insulin resistance and diabetes burden.

Hypertension with blood pressure today of 138/87 mmHg.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding by history.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated A1c and fasting glucose and the need for prompt, consistent diabetes treatment rather than observation alone. Reviewed lifestyle measures including reduction of sugar-sweetened beverages and refined carbohydrates, portion control, increased intake of vegetables and fiber, and gradual progression toward regular physical activity as tolerated. Weight reduction was emphasized as an important component of glycemic, blood pressure, and fatty liver management.

Recommend initiation of evidence-based glucose-lowering pharmacotherapy, with metformin commonly considered first-line if renal function and tolerability permit. Given the A1c of 10.0%, additional therapy should be considered promptly if glycemic response is inadequate or if repeat assessment remains substantially elevated. Medication selection should account for obesity, cardiovascular and renal risk, tolerability, and liver history.

Recommend obtaining renal function with serum creatinine/eGFR before and during metformin therapy, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio for diabetic kidney disease screening, and a fasting lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment. Recommend a comprehensive dilated eye examination and complete diabetic foot examination. Continue monitoring for polyuria, polydipsia, blurred vision, unexplained weight loss, recurrent infections, wounds, or new sensory changes.

Blood pressure is mildly elevated today. Continue lifestyle measures directed at sodium reduction, weight loss, and physical activity, with reassessment at follow-up and consideration of antihypertensive therapy based on persistent readings and overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

Continue management of obesity and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease through gradual weight loss, improved nutrition, and increased activity. Avoid excess alcohol and unnecessary hepatotoxic medications.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after treatment initiation and recommended laboratory evaluation to review glucose response, adherence, adverse effects, blood pressure, and need for medication intensification. Advise urgent evaluation for vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, severe weakness, inability to maintain oral intake, or rapidly worsening hyperglycemic symptoms.
