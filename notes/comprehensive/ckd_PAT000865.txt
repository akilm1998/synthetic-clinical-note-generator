Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced estimated GFR and substantial azotemia, present for approximately 7 years. Creatinine is 5.07 mg/dL, eGFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 86 mg/dL. Although she currently denies symptoms suggestive of uremia or volume overload, these laboratory findings represent advanced renal dysfunction requiring prompt specialist evaluation and active management planning. A formal disease stage is not assigned here.

Hypertension, currently above goal at 160/93 mmHg. Poor blood-pressure control may accelerate renal disease progression and increases cardiovascular risk. The effectiveness and adherence of antihypertensive therapy cannot be determined without a documented medication regimen.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding, with current lipid-lowering treatment and response not documented.

Medication review: A specific regimen is not provided, so medication efficacy, adherence, tolerability, drug interactions, and renal dose appropriateness remain to be established. Particular attention is warranted to avoid nephrotoxic or renally inappropriate medications.

Plan

1. Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for advanced chronic kidney disease, assessment of uremic risk, and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if clinically indicated. The absence of current symptoms does not lessen the significance of the laboratory abnormalities.

2. Obtain/review a complete medication reconciliation, including all prescription medications, over-the-counter products, supplements, and herbal preparations. Assess adherence, therapeutic effectiveness, and adverse effects. Review all medications for renal dosing and avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a treating clinician. Do not initiate or adjust medications without confirming the current regimen and relevant electrolyte and renal data.

3. Reassess blood pressure promptly with confirmation of home or office measurements and review of antihypertensive adherence and tolerance. Optimize blood-pressure management using agents appropriate for her renal function after medication reconciliation and laboratory review.

4. Recommend renal evaluation including serum electrolytes, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, renal ultrasound, and intact parathyroid hormone level. These studies would help characterize complications and guide treatment; no results from these evaluations are available in today’s data.

5. Counsel regarding urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, progressive edema, markedly reduced or absent urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, or other acute deterioration.

6. Continue close outpatient follow-up after medication reconciliation and nephrology assessment.
