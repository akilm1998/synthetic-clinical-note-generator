Chief Complaint

Annual review of hypertension and chronic cardiovascular risk factors.

Subjective

Michelle Cross is an 81-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 7-year history of essential hypertension, currently not under active management. She reports intermittent dizziness and palpitations. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, and epistaxis. No symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency are reported today.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease present for approximately 34 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 6 years, and hyperlipidemia for 2 years. She does not smoke. Height is 167 cm and weight is 71.1 kg, with BMI 25.5 kg/m².

Review of systems is otherwise notable for no reported headache, visual disturbance, chest pain, dyspnea, fatigue, or nosebleeds. Cardiovascular review is positive for palpitations. Neurologic review is positive for dizziness.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 157/95 mmHg. Pulse 73 beats/minute. Respiratory rate 17/minute. Temperature 36.8°C. Oxygen saturation 96%.

Anthropometrics: Height 167 cm. Weight 71.1 kg. BMI 25.5 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Sodium 146 mmol/L. Potassium 4.4 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure at 157/95 mmHg in an 81-year-old woman with a 7-year history of hypertension that is not currently being actively managed. The presence of dizziness and palpitations warrants attention, although she has no chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic visual symptoms, severe headache, or epistaxis to suggest hypertensive crisis at this visit.

Her coronary artery disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and hyperlipidemia substantially increase overall cardiovascular risk and strengthen the indication for consistent blood pressure control. Sodium is mildly elevated at 146 mmol/L; potassium is within the supplied reference context and does not raise an immediate electrolyte safety concern. Renal function values are not provided and should be considered before selecting or titrating antihypertensive therapy.

Palpitations with a normal resting pulse of 73 beats/minute require further characterization, particularly given her age and established coronary artery disease. Dizziness may be related to blood pressure fluctuation or another cardiovascular or neurologic process and should not be attributed to hypertension alone without evaluation.

Plan

Blood pressure management was reviewed in detail. Recommend confirming the blood pressure pattern with repeat seated measurements using an appropriately sized cuff and home blood pressure monitoring, including a symptom and pulse log. Assessment should include seated and standing measurements because of her dizziness and advanced age.

Recommend initiation or re-establishment of a structured antihypertensive treatment plan after medication reconciliation, review of prior tolerance, and assessment of renal function. Given coexisting coronary artery disease and diabetes, therapy should be selected with attention to cardiovascular and renal protection while avoiding excessive blood pressure reduction or orthostatic symptoms. She should not self-start or stop prescription therapy without clinician guidance.

Recommend serum creatinine/eGFR, repeat electrolytes, urinalysis, lipid profile, and diabetes monitoring as clinically appropriate before and during treatment adjustment. An ECG is recommended to evaluate palpitations and assess for ischemic or rhythm-related abnormalities. Further cardiac evaluation should be guided by ECG findings and symptom pattern.

Advise a heart-healthy, reduced-sodium eating pattern; avoid adding salt to food and limit highly processed foods. Continue avoidance of tobacco. Encourage regular, moderate physical activity as tolerated, with attention to balance and fall prevention. Maintain adequate hydration unless otherwise restricted by a treating clinician.

Because of her coronary artery disease, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and hypertension, preventive cardiovascular care should include review of lipid-lowering therapy, glycemic control, antiplatelet indications if applicable, vaccination status, renal screening, diabetic retinal and foot care, osteoporosis screening, and age-appropriate cancer screening. These should be individualized to her overall health, prior results, treatment goals, and preferences.

She should seek urgent medical attention for chest pain, new or worsening shortness of breath, fainting, persistent severe palpitations, new focal weakness, confusion, acute visual loss, severe headache, or markedly higher blood pressure accompanied by symptoms.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure review, symptom assessment, and treatment planning rather than routine annual follow-up alone.
