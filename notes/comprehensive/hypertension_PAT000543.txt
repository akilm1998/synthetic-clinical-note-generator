Chief Complaint

Annual review of longstanding hypertension and chronic cardiovascular risk factors.

Subjective

Brooke Snyder is a 79-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review and assessment of longstanding hypertension, reportedly present for approximately 40 years and currently without active treatment. She reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. No acute cardiopulmonary complaint is reported today.

Relevant history includes coronary artery disease of approximately 27 years’ duration and hyperlipidemia of approximately 37 years’ duration. She currently smokes. Weight is 83.5 kg at a height of 173 cm, with BMI 27.9 kg/m².

Current medication list and allergies were not provided for this review. No medication use is assumed.

Assessment

1. Longstanding essential hypertension, currently above goal at 152/92 mmHg and not actively managed. She has a 40-year history of hypertension with current intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency, including chest pain, dyspnea, severe headache, visual change, or epistaxis. Sodium and potassium are currently within expected ranges.

2. Coronary artery disease, longstanding, approximately 27 years. Hypertension and active smoking increase ongoing cardiovascular risk. Palpitations warrant further assessment, particularly in the setting of known coronary disease.

3. Hyperlipidemia, longstanding, approximately 37 years. Current lipid values and treatment status are not provided.

4. Active tobacco use, contributing substantially to cardiovascular risk.

5. Overweight, BMI 27.9 kg/m².

Plan

Hypertension:
Discussed the importance of resuming active blood pressure management given persistent elevation, advanced age, coronary artery disease, and prolonged duration of untreated hypertension. Recommend confirming the blood pressure pattern with properly seated office measurements and home blood pressure monitoring, including a log of readings and associated symptoms. Review orthostatic symptoms carefully because of reported dizziness and age.

Recommend initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy after medication reconciliation and assessment of renal function, with attention to tolerability and avoidance of excessive blood pressure reduction. Potential outpatient options include an ACE inhibitor or ARB, calcium-channel blocker, or thiazide-type diuretic, selected based on current medications, renal function, orthostatic measurements, and coronary disease history. Do not make abrupt medication changes without confirming the current medication list.

Recommend renal function testing and urinalysis as part of hypertension assessment. Repeat serum electrolytes after starting or changing therapy, particularly if an ACE inhibitor, ARB, or diuretic is selected. Recommend ECG for evaluation of longstanding hypertension, coronary disease, and palpitations.

Coronary artery disease and palpitations:
Recommend prompt ECG and review of prior cardiovascular treatment history. Further cardiac evaluation should be considered if palpitations persist, become more frequent, or are associated with presyncope, syncope, chest discomfort, or dyspnea. She should seek emergency care for chest pain, new or worsening shortness of breath, fainting, focal neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or acute visual change.

Hyperlipidemia and cardiovascular prevention:
Recommend obtaining a current lipid profile and reviewing whether lipid-lowering therapy is indicated or already prescribed. Reinforce a heart-healthy, lower-sodium diet, regular activity as tolerated, weight control, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Dietary sodium reduction is particularly important for blood pressure control.

Tobacco use:
Strongly advised smoking cessation. Discuss cessation counseling and pharmacologic support at follow-up, taking cardiovascular history and patient preferences into account.

Preventive care:
Annual preventive review should include medication and allergy reconciliation, immunization review, fall-risk assessment, screening for cognitive and mood concerns, vision and hearing review, and age-appropriate cancer screening based on prior results and patient preferences. Encourage regular physical activity appropriate to functional status and balance, with gradual progression.

Follow-up:
Arrange close outpatient follow-up after home blood pressure readings, medication reconciliation, ECG, and recommended laboratory evaluation are completed. Reassess dizziness and palpitations promptly rather than waiting for the next annual visit.
