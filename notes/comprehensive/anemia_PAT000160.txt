Chief Complaint

Annual review with evaluation of longstanding anemia.

Subjective

Christian Cook is a 67-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease assessment. He has a 7-year history of anemia in the setting of chronic kidney disease of approximately 23 years’ duration and heart failure of approximately 6 years’ duration. The anemia has not been actively managed.

He denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, headache, and reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor is present. He continues to smoke. No medication list, allergies, or additional social or family history are provided.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue, weakness, or reduced exercise tolerance.

Cardiopulmonary: Denies shortness of breath, palpitations, or dizziness. Smoking history is positive.

Neurologic: Denies headache or dizziness.

Hematologic: Pallor reported and observed. No other bleeding-related symptoms are provided.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 147/82 mmHg, pulse 98 bpm, respiratory rate 24/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Anthropometrics: Height 163 cm, weight 71.5 kg, BMI 26.9 kg/m².

Physical Examination: Patient appears pale. Cardiovascular examination is otherwise normal. Respiratory examination is normal, with oxygen saturation preserved at 98%. No additional abnormal examination findings are documented.

Laboratory Data: Hemoglobin 8.7 g/dL, hematocrit 26.3%, RBC count 3.09 million/µL, MCV 85 fL, serum iron 49 µg/dL, ferritin 469 ng/mL, TIBC 260 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 18.8%.

Assessment

1. Anemia of chronic disease/inflammation

Seven-year history of significant, persistent anemia with hemoglobin 8.7 g/dL and hematocrit 26.3%. The anemia is normocytic, with MCV 85 fL. Low serum iron and relatively low TIBC in the setting of elevated ferritin are most consistent with iron-restricted erythropoiesis associated with chronic inflammation and chronic kidney disease rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. The reduced transferrin saturation of 18.8% suggests limited iron availability for erythropoiesis. Chronic kidney disease is likely contributing through impaired erythropoietin production, although current renal function is not provided. Heart failure and chronic systemic disease may further reduce physiologic reserve.

He is currently without reported symptomatic cardiopulmonary effects, but the degree and duration of anemia warrant active evaluation and management. Pallor is present, with borderline tachycardia and increased respiratory rate despite normal oxygen saturation.

2. Chronic kidney disease

Chronic kidney disease has been present for approximately 23 years and is a major potential contributor to the anemia. Renal function, proteinuria status, and current nephrology management are not included in the available information. Anemia management should be coordinated with assessment of renal function and overall cardiovascular status.

3. Heart failure

Heart failure has been present for approximately 6 years. The patient denies dyspnea, palpitations, and reduced exercise tolerance today, and oxygen saturation is normal. Anemia may worsen myocardial workload and heart failure tolerance even in the absence of current symptoms.

4. Tobacco use

Patient continues to smoke. Smoking increases cardiovascular risk and may adversely affect chronic kidney disease and heart failure outcomes.

5. Preventive health and annual review

At age 67, preventive care should be reviewed in the context of his cardiovascular, renal, and hematologic comorbidity. BMI is 26.9 kg/m², consistent with overweight range.

Plan

1. Anemia

Repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count to confirm persistence and assess marrow response. Obtain peripheral blood smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate. Review for occult blood loss and other contributors to chronic anemia, particularly if the degree of anemia is disproportionate to renal disease or if the pattern changes.

Review the anemia and iron indices with the clinician managing his chronic kidney disease. Consider iron replacement only after confirming the overall iron status and clinical context; the elevated ferritin argues against empiric routine oral iron without further assessment. If anemia remains significant after reversible contributors are addressed, consideration of an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent or intravenous iron should be guided by renal function, iron availability, cardiovascular risk, and specialist or nephrology recommendations.

Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs when possible because of potential renal and cardiovascular effects. Counsel regarding urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, sustained palpitations, marked weakness, signs of bleeding, or rapidly worsening exercise tolerance.

2. Chronic kidney disease

Obtain current renal function and review kidney-related monitoring, blood pressure control, urine protein assessment, and renal-protective management. Coordinate anemia treatment with renal disease management. Avoid nephrotoxic medications and ensure medication dosing is reviewed for renal function once the medication list is available.

3. Heart failure

Continue clinical surveillance for fluid retention, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, chest discomfort, and declining exercise tolerance. The respiratory rate of 24/min and pulse of 98 bpm merit reassessment, particularly given the hemoglobin of 8.7 g/dL, even though oxygen saturation is normal and the patient denies dyspnea. Any new cardiopulmonary symptoms should prompt expedited evaluation.

4. Tobacco use

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and its importance in reducing cardiovascular, renal, and heart failure risk. Offer structured cessation support and pharmacologic options after reviewing current medications, contraindications, and patient preference.

5. Preventive care

Review immunization status, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, shingles, and tetanus vaccination as appropriate. Confirm age-appropriate colorectal cancer screening and other indicated cancer screening based on prior results and shared decision-making. Review fall risk, nutrition, physical activity, dental care, vision, hearing, and advance care planning.

Follow-up

Arrange timely follow-up after repeat blood count and renal evaluation to review results and establish an active anemia management plan. Earlier assessment is indicated for any new cardiopulmonary symptoms, syncope, bleeding, or worsening functional status.
