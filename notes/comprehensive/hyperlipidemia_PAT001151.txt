Chief Complaint

Initial outpatient consultation for markedly elevated cholesterol and triglycerides.

Subjective

Samuel Wood is a 98-year-old man, 178 cm tall and weighing 86.8 kg, with a BMI of 27.4 kg/m², presenting for initial evaluation of longstanding hyperlipidemia. The lipid disorder has been present for approximately 55 years and is currently not under active management. Recent lipid measurements show total cholesterol 297 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 198 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 32 mg/dL, and triglycerides 337 mg/dL.

He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. He reports no tobacco use.

Relevant medical history includes coronary artery disease of approximately 22 years’ duration, hypertension of approximately 59 years’ duration, and type 2 diabetes mellitus of approximately 20 years’ duration. These conditions substantially increase his baseline atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk in the setting of severe LDL elevation and low HDL cholesterol. No current medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 149/90 mmHg. Pulse 74 beats/minute. Respiratory rate 19/minute. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 96%.

General: 98-year-old man presenting for outpatient evaluation. No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms reported.

Anthropometrics: Height 178 cm. Weight 86.8 kg. BMI 27.4 kg/m².

Cardiovascular and vascular review: No chest discomfort, dizziness, or intermittent claudication reported. No xanthomas or xanthelasma reported.

Laboratory Data: Total cholesterol 297 mg/dL. LDL cholesterol 198 mg/dL. HDL cholesterol 32 mg/dL. Triglycerides 337 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and hypertriglyceridemia. The LDL of 198 mg/dL represents a substantial atherogenic burden, while triglycerides of 337 mg/dL and HDL of 32 mg/dL indicate additional adverse lipid risk. The pattern is clinically significant in this patient with established coronary artery disease, longstanding hypertension, and type 2 diabetes mellitus. Although he does not report angina, claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma, absence of symptoms does not reduce the cardiovascular risk associated with these lipid values. The disorder has been present for approximately 55 years and is not currently being actively managed.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, without chest discomfort reported today. Lipid reduction is particularly important given the established atherosclerotic disease history.

Hypertension with current blood pressure of 149/90 mmHg. This is above typical outpatient treatment targets, particularly in the context of coronary artery disease and diabetes, although treatment decisions should be individualized given his age, overall health, tolerance, and goals of care.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, increasing cardiovascular risk in combination with severe dyslipidemia and coronary artery disease.

Overweight by BMI, 27.4 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the markedly abnormal lipid profile and the increased risk associated with LDL cholesterol of 198 mg/dL in the setting of coronary artery disease and diabetes. Given his age of 98 years, treatment should be individualized through shared decision-making, taking into account functional status, frailty, life expectancy, prior treatment tolerance, comorbidities, and personal goals. A lipid-lowering medication, typically a statin if consistent with his goals and clinically tolerated, should be considered after review of his complete medication history and prior adverse effects. If adequate LDL reduction is not achieved or a statin is not tolerated, addition of nonstatin therapy such as ezetimibe may be considered.

Recommend confirming the fasting lipid profile if the available specimen was not fasting and assessing the response to any subsequently selected therapy. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for additional risk characterization, particularly given the degree and duration of LDL elevation and known coronary artery disease. Evaluation for potentially reversible contributors to dyslipidemia, including thyroid, hepatic, and renal abnormalities, is also reasonable before or at initiation of therapy. No additional laboratory results are available for interpretation today.

Recommend dietary reduction of saturated fats, trans fats, processed carbohydrates, and excess dietary sugars, with emphasis on vegetables, legumes, whole grains, and appropriately portioned lean proteins. Because triglycerides are elevated, avoidance of concentrated sugars and refined carbohydrates is particularly relevant. Encourage physical activity appropriate to his functional capacity and fall risk, without provoking chest discomfort or significant exertional symptoms.

Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management in coordination with the clinicians managing his coronary artery disease, hypertension, and diabetes. Reassess blood pressure and review the full medication list at follow-up before making changes. He should seek urgent evaluation for new chest discomfort, shortness of breath, syncope, focal neurologic symptoms, or new exertional leg pain.

Arrange outpatient follow-up after medication and risk-benefit review, with repeat lipid testing after any therapy is initiated or adjusted to assess tolerability and response.
