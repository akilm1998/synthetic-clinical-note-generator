Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Ryan Hughes is a 95-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension, diagnosed approximately 36 months ago. Blood pressure control has not been adequately addressed. He reports intermittent dizziness and palpitations. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, and epistaxis. He does not smoke. Current weight is 56.2 kg at 162 cm, with BMI 21.4 kg/m².

His medical history is also notable for hyperlipidemia for approximately 36 months and chronic kidney disease for approximately 52 months. No antihypertensive or other medication regimen is provided for review today. Recent serum electrolytes show sodium 142 mmol/L and potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 174/102 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Weight 56.2 kg; height 162 cm; BMI 21.4 kg/m².

The patient reports dizziness and palpitations but no chest pain, dyspnea, severe headache, visual disturbance, or epistaxis. No additional physical examination findings are documented in the supplied encounter information.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure of 174/102 mmHg in the setting of approximately three years of inadequately addressed disease. The associated dizziness and palpitations warrant prompt reassessment rather than routine observation alone. He does not report symptoms suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency at this time, including chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, or visual change; however, his advanced age and chronic kidney disease increase the potential consequences of sustained uncontrolled blood pressure.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Sodium and potassium are currently within an acceptable range for interpretation, although potassium is toward the upper end of normal and renal function should be considered when selecting and monitoring antihypertensive therapy.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding, with cardiovascular risk amplified by age, hypertension, and chronic kidney disease.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure after a period of quiet rest using an appropriately sized cuff and confirm the reading manually if possible. Given the degree of elevation and associated dizziness and palpitations, arrange prompt clinician reassessment and do not defer evaluation to a routine distant follow-up. He should seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe or new headache, neurologic symptoms, new visual change, confusion, fainting, or persistent markedly elevated blood pressure with worsening symptoms.

Discuss initiation of an individualized antihypertensive regimen, taking into account his age, chronic kidney disease, orthostatic risk, pulse, and current potassium level. Medication reconciliation is needed before treatment selection; no current regimen is documented. Blood pressure response and symptoms should be monitored closely after any therapy is started or adjusted, with attention to dizziness and falls.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine protein as clinically appropriate before and during antihypertensive treatment, as well as repeat serum electrolytes after treatment changes. An ECG is appropriate for evaluation of palpitations and hypertensive cardiovascular effects. Lipid profile and urinalysis should be obtained or reviewed as part of cardiovascular and kidney risk assessment if not recently available.

Counsel regarding avoidance of excess dietary sodium, maintenance of appropriate hydration, slow position changes, and home blood pressure monitoring if he can do so safely with assistance. Continue avoidance of tobacco. Follow up promptly for blood pressure reassessment and review of the above evaluation.
