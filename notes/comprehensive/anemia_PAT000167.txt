Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Owen Myers is a 95-year-old man, 172 cm tall and weighing 88.2 kg, with BMI 29.8 kg/m², presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding anemia, reported duration approximately 36, in the setting of malignancy of 6 years’ duration and heart failure of 41 years’ duration. He is not a smoker.

He currently denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, and visible pallor. No acute anemia-related symptoms are reported today.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 131/76 mmHg, pulse 89 beats/min, respiratory rate 23/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Physical Examination:
Cardiovascular: Normal cardiovascular examination; no tachycardia.
Respiratory: Normal respiratory examination with oxygen saturation maintained at 97%.
General/skin: No pallor observed.

Laboratory Data:
Hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL
Hematocrit 27.2%
RBC count 3.2 million/µL
MCV 85 fL
Serum iron 45 µg/dL
Ferritin 500 ng/mL
TIBC 241 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 18.7%

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia with hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL and hematocrit 27.2%. The accompanying low serum iron, reduced TIBC, and elevated ferritin are most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/disease, particularly given the history of malignancy and longstanding heart failure. The anemia is currently clinically compensated: he denies cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms, blood pressure and oxygenation are stable, and pulse is not tachycardic. Respiratory rate is mildly elevated at 23/min and should be reassessed in the context of his heart failure.

No evidence from today’s presentation of acute symptomatic decompensation. The degree of anemia remains clinically significant and warrants structured evaluation, particularly because the current anemia-directed management has not been established.

Plan

Repeat CBC with reticulocyte count to assess severity and marrow response, with peripheral blood smear review.

Recommend evaluation of vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers to assess contributing factors and exclude additional reversible causes of normocytic anemia.

Review malignancy status and chronic inflammatory burden with the treating oncology team. Heart failure status should also be reassessed, including screening for worsening dyspnea, edema, orthopnea, or declining exercise tolerance.

Do not initiate empiric oral iron solely on the basis of the low serum iron, as the elevated ferritin and low TIBC support iron sequestration related to chronic disease rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Iron replacement, including intravenous iron, should be considered only after review of the complete clinical context and iron availability.

Consider hematology consultation for persistent hemoglobin below 9 g/dL, inadequate reticulocyte response, progressive decline, or uncertainty regarding the contribution of malignancy, inflammation, renal dysfunction, or marrow disease. An erythropoiesis-stimulating agent would require individualized risk-benefit assessment in the setting of advanced age, malignancy, heart failure, and thrombotic risk.

Seek urgent medical attention for new dyspnea at rest, chest pain, syncope, sustained palpitations, acute confusion, active bleeding, or marked functional decline. Close outpatient reassessment is recommended after repeat laboratory evaluation.
