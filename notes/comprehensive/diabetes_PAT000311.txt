Assessment and Plan

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications

Longstanding diabetes of approximately 49 years with HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 238 mg/dL, indicating inadequate glycemic control despite the absence of reported polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, fatigue, visual symptoms, wounds, infections, or neuropathic complaints. At age 90, treatment goals should be individualized with attention to renal function, frailty, hypoglycemia risk, functional status, and overall priorities. The current degree of hyperglycemia nevertheless warrants active review rather than continued observation alone.

Obtain a complete medication reconciliation and clarify prior diabetes therapies, adherence, dietary pattern, glucose-monitoring practices, and history of hypoglycemia before selecting treatment. Management should be conservative and individualized, with avoidance of rapid correction or agents likely to cause hypoglycemia unless there is a compelling indication. Renal function must be reviewed before initiating or adjusting glucose-lowering therapy because of longstanding chronic kidney disease. Consider nutrition counseling emphasizing consistent carbohydrate intake, adequate hydration, and avoidance of concentrated sugars while maintaining adequate caloric intake and avoiding unintended weight loss.

Recommend home glucose monitoring if feasible and appropriate, with documentation of fasting and occasional postprandial readings. Repeat HbA1c after an interval appropriate to the treatment plan to assess response.
