Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Lucas Patterson is an 83-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes, present for approximately 36 years. He is not currently reporting increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, unintentional weight loss, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. He also denies numbness or tingling in the hands or feet. Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin A1c of 9.6% and fasting glucose of 227 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimal glycemic control.

He has a 14-year history of anxiety noted in the medical history. No additional anxiety symptoms are reported today. He does not smoke. Current medication use, allergies, and home glucose readings were not provided for review.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, present for approximately 36 years, with current hyperglycemia and hemoglobin A1c of 9.6%. The patient denies osmotic symptoms, constitutional symptoms, visual complaints, neuropathic symptoms, and wound or infection concerns, but laboratory values indicate that glycemic control is above the desired range. No documented diabetic complications are reported today.

History of anxiety, approximately 14 years, without acute concerns reported at this visit.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated fasting glucose and hemoglobin A1c and the importance of active diabetes management despite the absence of symptoms. Given his age and longstanding disease, treatment goals should be individualized with attention to avoiding hypoglycemia while improving persistent hyperglycemia.

Medication reconciliation is needed before selecting therapy. Discuss initiation or optimization of glucose-lowering medication, with metformin considered if renal function and overall tolerance permit. Renal function should be assessed before prescribing or adjusting metformin or other agents. If glucose remains substantially elevated, additional therapy can be considered based on renal function, hypoglycemia risk, functional status, cost, and patient preferences. No medication regimen is documented today.

Recommend home glucose monitoring as appropriate, with review of readings at the next visit. Counsel regarding a consistent carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, and regular physical activity appropriate for his abilities and fall risk.

Recommend diabetes complication surveillance, including urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive foot examination, and routine dilated retinal evaluation. These are recommendations for ongoing evaluation; no results from these assessments are provided today.

Reviewed the importance of daily foot inspection and prompt evaluation of sores, redness, drainage, or wounds that do not heal. Continue routine monitoring of blood pressure and cardiovascular risk factors.

Follow up after medication reconciliation and recommended laboratory evaluation to reassess glycemic control and finalize treatment. Earlier evaluation is appropriate for marked worsening of thirst or urination, vomiting, confusion, weakness, visual changes, or other acute concerns.
