Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of approximately 7 years’ duration with substantially reduced estimated GFR, creatinine 2.67 mg/dL, and BUN 80 mg/dL. The elevated BUN and creatinine indicate significant chronic renal impairment. She currently reports fatigue but no nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, urinary change, or dyspnea to suggest clinically overt uremic or volume-overload symptoms. The absence of these symptoms does not lessen the need for prompt renal evaluation given the degree of impairment and the lack of active disease management.

Blood pressure is elevated at 142/89 mmHg in the setting of chronic kidney disease. Current antihypertensive treatment is not provided, so no medication adjustment can be made from the available information.

Anemia of chronic disease, present for approximately 1 year, may contribute to her fatigue. The supplied information does not include a current blood count or iron indices, so the severity and specific treatment needs cannot be determined from today’s data.

Plan

Recommend prompt referral to nephrology for comprehensive evaluation and ongoing management of advanced chronic kidney disease, including clarification of the underlying cause, assessment of progression, renal-protective treatment planning, and counseling regarding future renal replacement options if clinically appropriate.

Obtain or arrange repeat renal function assessment with serum electrolytes, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level. A renal ultrasound is also appropriate to evaluate renal anatomy and exclude obstructive or structural disease. These studies should be interpreted in conjunction with prior renal results to establish the trajectory of disease.

Review blood pressure measurements over time and address blood pressure control with a kidney-protective regimen after medication reconciliation and assessment of potassium and other electrolytes. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. All medications, including over-the-counter products and supplements, should be reviewed for renal dosing and safety.

For anemia, obtain a complete blood count and evaluation directed at chronic kidney disease-associated anemia before considering therapy. Treatment decisions should be guided by hemoglobin level, iron status, symptoms, and nephrology input.

Counseled regarding prompt medical evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, peripheral edema, markedly decreased urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, or chest symptoms. Continue close outpatient follow-up while arranging nephrology care.
