Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Hayden Johnston is a 95-year-old man, height 178 cm, weight 71.9 kg, BMI 22.7 kg/m², presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 50 years. He reports no interval fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of an acute uremic syndrome or clinically apparent fluid overload.

Chronic kidney disease has not been actively managed recently. He has a history of secondary hyperparathyroidism of approximately 29 years’ duration. No medication list is provided, and no medication changes are reported. He does not smoke.

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, longstanding for approximately 50 years. Current creatinine is 3.96 mg/dL, eGFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 73 mg/dL. He is clinically stable by history without nausea, pruritus, cramps, sleep disturbance, change in urination, edema, dyspnea, or other reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms. The condition has not been actively managed, and the degree of renal impairment warrants structured nephrologic evaluation and close monitoring.

2. Secondary hyperparathyroidism, longstanding for approximately 29 years, in the setting of chronic kidney disease. Current mineral metabolism studies are not available for review.

3. Elevated blood pressure reading, 144/88 mmHg. Chronic hypertension is not otherwise documented in the supplied history, and no antihypertensive regimen is provided. Blood pressure should be followed in the context of renal protection and overall goals of care.

4. Advanced age, 95 years, requiring individualized decisions regarding intensity of evaluation and treatment, symptom burden, functional status, and patient preferences.

Plan

Obtain or update renal monitoring with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, repeat creatinine/eGFR and BUN, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone. These studies will help assess complications of chronic kidney disease, including anemia and mineral-bone disorder, and establish current disease trajectory.

Recommend referral to nephrology for comprehensive management of longstanding chronic kidney disease and secondary hyperparathyroidism. Renal ultrasound may be considered as part of structural evaluation if not previously performed.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications at the next visit, with renal dose adjustment as appropriate. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Dietary and fluid recommendations should be individualized rather than overly restrictive, particularly given his age and absence of reported edema or dyspnea.

Monitor blood pressure with repeat measurements. Dietary sodium moderation is reasonable. Antihypertensive therapy should be considered or adjusted only after medication reconciliation and discussion of treatment goals, tolerability, and renal status.

Discuss warning symptoms requiring prompt medical attention, including new or worsening shortness of breath, rapidly increasing edema, markedly reduced urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, or chest pain.

Continue routine follow-up after updated laboratory evaluation, with earlier reassessment for any new symptoms or clinical decline. Management should incorporate his advanced age, symptom burden, functional priorities, and preferences regarding intensive renal replacement planning versus conservative supportive care.
