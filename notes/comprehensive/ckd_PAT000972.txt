Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Grant Porter is a 60-year-old man, 169 cm tall and 76.8 kg (BMI 26.9 kg/m²), presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease reportedly present for approximately 18 years. He is not currently reporting fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, or shortness of breath.

Relevant comorbidities include anemia of chronic disease for 17 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 3 years, and coronary artery disease for 2 years. He continues to smoke. No medication list, allergy history, or interval specialist care is provided.

No additional examination findings are documented today. The patient reports no edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, or urinary frequency change.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of approximately 18 years’ duration with markedly reduced estimated renal function, creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, eGFR 6 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 74 mg/dL. Although currently without reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms, the degree of renal impairment requires prompt comprehensive evaluation and active renal replacement planning. Disease control is inadequate based on the available laboratory profile, and the condition has not been actively managed.

Hypertension, with blood pressure 151/87 mmHg, requiring reassessment and treatment optimization in the context of advanced chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus of 3 years’ duration; diabetes remains an important contributor to renal and cardiovascular risk. No glycemic data are available for review today.

Anemia of chronic disease, present for 17 years, requiring reassessment in the setting of advanced kidney disease.

Coronary artery disease, present for 2 years, increasing overall cardiovascular risk.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for advanced chronic kidney disease, assessment for indications for renal replacement therapy, and preparation for dialysis or other appropriate renal replacement options. Absence of current symptoms does not lessen the significance of the eGFR of 6 mL/min/1.73 m².

Repeat renal function and obtain a serum electrolytes panel to assess current metabolic status. Obtain urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone as part of comprehensive CKD evaluation. Consider renal ultrasound to assess renal structure and chronicity if not previously performed.

Review urgently for warning symptoms, including new or worsening shortness of breath, progressive swelling, reduced urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, chest pain, or worsening pruritus. These symptoms should prompt immediate medical evaluation.

Reassess blood pressure promptly and address hypertension with a kidney- and cardiovascular-conscious treatment plan after medication reconciliation. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents where possible; review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications.

Reassess anemia with CBC and guide treatment according to results and nephrology recommendations. Evaluate mineral and bone complications with intact parathyroid hormone and appropriate mineral testing.

Continue coordinated management of diabetes and coronary artery disease, with attention to cardiovascular risk reduction. Counsel regarding smoking cessation; continued tobacco use substantially increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

Follow up promptly after renal evaluation and repeat testing, or sooner for any warning symptoms.
