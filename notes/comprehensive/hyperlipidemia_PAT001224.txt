Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Sophia Turner is a 78-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 12 years and currently without an established treatment regimen. Recent lipid results are markedly abnormal, with total cholesterol 376 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 263 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 40 mg/dL, and triglycerides 363 mg/dL.

She denies chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, and dizziness. She reports fatty skin deposits and fatty deposits around the eyelids, consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

Weight is 116.3 kg at a height of 179 cm, with BMI 36.3 kg/m². Obesity has been present for approximately 44 years. No medication use, allergies, family history, or prior cardiovascular events are provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 128/78 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 179 cm. Weight 116.3 kg. BMI 36.3 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Total cholesterol 376 mg/dL. LDL cholesterol 263 mg/dL. HDL cholesterol 40 mg/dL. Triglycerides 363 mg/dL.

Skin findings reported: xanthomas and xanthelasma. No chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness reported today.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, present for approximately 12 years and not currently being actively treated. The LDL of 263 mg/dL represents a substantial atherogenic burden, while triglycerides of 363 mg/dL are also significantly elevated. The presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma further supports longstanding lipid excess. She currently denies symptoms suggestive of active angina, claudication, or neurologic hypoperfusion, but her age and degree and duration of dyslipidemia place her at high risk for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.

Obesity, BMI 36.3 kg/m², longstanding. This likely contributes to the adverse lipid profile and increases overall cardiometabolic risk.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated LDL and triglycerides and the importance of active risk reduction. Recommend initiation of lipid-lowering therapy after shared decision-making, with a high-intensity statin such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin unless contraindications, intolerance, or patient preference indicate otherwise. Given the degree of LDL elevation, anticipate the need to assess response and consider addition of ezetimibe if adequate reduction is not achieved or if statin therapy is not tolerated. Medication selection and dosing should account for age, renal and hepatic status, potential drug interactions, and patient preference.

Recommend evaluation for potentially reversible contributors to dyslipidemia, including thyroid disease, diabetes or other metabolic abnormalities, renal disease, and liver disease, before or at the time of treatment initiation. Recommend baseline safety testing appropriate to the selected therapy and repeat a fasting lipid panel approximately 4 to 12 weeks after starting or adjusting treatment, followed by periodic monitoring.

Counseled regarding reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess calories; increased intake of vegetables, legumes, whole grains, and fiber; and avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages and excess alcohol if applicable. Encourage gradual, low-impact physical activity as tolerated and a structured weight-reduction approach, with attention to safety and functional status at age 78.

Because of the longstanding severe LDL elevation and physical findings, consider additional risk assessment with apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a) measurement if results would influence management. Further cardiovascular evaluation should be guided by development of exertional chest discomfort, unexplained dyspnea, claudication, dizziness, or other concerning symptoms. She was advised to seek urgent evaluation for persistent chest pain, acute shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or sudden exercise-limiting leg pain.

Follow up after initiation or adjustment of lipid-lowering therapy to review tolerance, adherence, interval symptoms, weight trajectory, and repeat lipid results.
