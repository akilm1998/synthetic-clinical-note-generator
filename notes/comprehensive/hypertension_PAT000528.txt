Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension and review of blood pressure control.

Subjective

Maria Armstrong is a 42-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension, present for approximately 12 years and currently without an active treatment regimen documented. She also has type 2 diabetes mellitus of approximately 11 years’ duration. She reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or nosebleeds. No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms are reported today.

She continues to smoke. Her height is 178 cm and weight is 78.3 kg, with a BMI of 24.7 kg/m². The importance of blood pressure control in the setting of diabetes and tobacco use was reviewed. Dietary sodium intake, physical activity, and alcohol use should be clarified at follow-up as part of cardiovascular risk assessment.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 144/93 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 178 cm, weight 78.3 kg, BMI 24.7 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 145 mmol/L, potassium 3.8 mmol/L.

Physical examination is notable for an elevated blood pressure reading. No additional examination findings are provided for this encounter.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal at 144/93 mmHg. The elevated diastolic pressure and duration of disease warrant active treatment and confirmation of the out-of-office blood pressure pattern. Intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue may occur in the setting of inadequately controlled blood pressure but are nonspecific; she has no current symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 11 years, increasing her cardiovascular and renal risk and making consistent blood pressure control particularly important.

Current tobacco use, an additional modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

Serum sodium and potassium are within acceptable ranges for consideration of antihypertensive therapy, although renal function has not been provided and should be assessed before or shortly after treatment initiation.

Plan

Discussed the chronic nature of hypertension and the increased risk of cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, and renal complications, particularly in the setting of diabetes and smoking. Recommend confirming the average blood pressure with a validated home monitor, obtaining readings twice daily for 7 days, and bringing the log and device to the next visit. She should seek urgent medical attention for severe or rapidly worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, confusion, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Recommend initiation of an evidence-based antihypertensive medication after review of renal function and shared decision-making. An ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker would be reasonable to consider given her diabetes, with monitoring of renal function and potassium after initiation; other appropriate options include amlodipine or a thiazide-type diuretic depending on the complete clinical evaluation and laboratory results. No current antihypertensive medication use is documented.

Recommend a low-sodium, heart-healthy eating pattern, regular aerobic physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and maintenance of her current healthy weight. Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered counseling and evidence-based cessation support.

Recommend evaluation of renal function and urine albumin excretion, along with diabetes-related cardiovascular risk assessment including lipid testing. Urinalysis and an ECG are also appropriate baseline investigations in the evaluation of longstanding hypertension. Continue diabetes care and coordinate blood pressure goals with her diabetes management.

Follow-up after home blood pressure monitoring and medication/laboratory review, with earlier reassessment if dizziness or palpitations become persistent, severe, or associated with syncope, chest discomfort, or shortness of breath.
