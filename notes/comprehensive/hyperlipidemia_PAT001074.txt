Chief Complaint

Annual health review and follow-up of untreated hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Caroline Rivera is a 77-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. Hyperlipidemia has been present for approximately 6 years and is currently not under active treatment. Recent lipid results show markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with elevated triglycerides and low HDL cholesterol. She reports fatty deposits around the eyelids and skin consistent with xanthelasma and xanthomas.

She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, and dizziness. She does not smoke. Weight is 98.2 kg at a height of 172 cm, with BMI 33.2 kg/m²; obesity has been present for approximately 3 years. No medication use, allergies, family history, or additional symptoms are provided for today’s review.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia, unspecified. Lipid profile demonstrates severe elevation of total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with concomitant hypertriglyceridemia and low HDL cholesterol. The presence of xanthelasma and xanthomas suggests a prolonged lipid burden. She currently has no reported chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness. Given her age and substantially elevated LDL cholesterol, her untreated lipid profile represents a significant long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk. Secondary contributors and a possible inherited lipid disorder should be considered clinically, particularly in view of the markedly elevated LDL level.

Obesity, BMI 33.2 kg/m², present for approximately 3 years.

Preventive health review completed in the context of annual examination.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the benefits and potential risks of lipid-lowering therapy. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of a statin, with atorvastatin or rosuvastatin being reasonable options if no contraindication is identified. Medication choice and dose should account for age, comorbidities, preferences, and potential interactions. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, additional therapy such as ezetimibe may be considered.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or lifestyle intervention to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary causes of dyslipidemia and additional risk assessment with ApoB and lipoprotein(a) when clinically appropriate. Coronary artery calcium imaging may be considered if treatment decisions remain uncertain after discussion.

Counseled regarding a Mediterranean-style eating pattern, reduction of saturated and trans fats, avoidance of excess refined carbohydrates and alcohol, increased dietary fiber, portion control, and gradual regular physical activity as tolerated. Weight reduction is recommended, with an initial goal of sustainable modest loss rather than rapid dieting.

Continue avoidance of tobacco. Seek urgent evaluation for new chest pressure, shortness of breath, neurologic deficits, or acute limb pain.

Review age-appropriate preventive care, including immunizations, cancer screening, osteoporosis screening, fall-risk assessment, vision and hearing care, and advance care planning at the annual health-maintenance review.

Follow up after lipid management is initiated or within approximately 6–12 weeks to review tolerance and repeat lipid results, with ongoing chronic disease follow-up thereafter.
