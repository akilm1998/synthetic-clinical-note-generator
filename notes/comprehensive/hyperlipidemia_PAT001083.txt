Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Benjamin Murphy is a 54-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 17 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. He has known coronary artery disease of approximately 9 years’ duration. Recent lipid results show marked elevation of total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with low HDL and elevated triglycerides.

He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. No current lipid-lowering medications are documented. He continues to smoke. He has longstanding obesity, present for approximately 33 years. No medication adverse effects are reported, as no active lipid medication use is documented.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: No specific constitutional symptoms reported.

Cardiovascular: Denies chest discomfort. No intermittent claudication reported.

Neurologic: Denies dizziness.

Skin: Denies fatty skin deposits or yellow plaques around the eyelids.

Respiratory, gastrointestinal, genitourinary, musculoskeletal, and psychiatric review is otherwise without specific complaint reported today.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 122/82 mmHg, pulse 89 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 162 cm. Weight 96.1 kg. BMI 36.6 kg/m².

Laboratory Results:
Total cholesterol 301 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 202 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 34 mg/dL
Triglycerides 327 mg/dL

Physical examination: Patient is seen for routine follow-up and is not reporting acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms. No xanthomas or xanthelasma are reported. Available vital signs are stable. A more detailed cardiovascular and vascular examination should specifically assess for carotid bruits and other evidence of atherosclerotic disease given his coronary artery disease and markedly abnormal lipid profile.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe LDL elevation, low HDL, and moderate hypertriglyceridemia. Current values are significantly above recommended targets, particularly in the setting of established coronary artery disease. The longstanding duration and lack of active management increase his cardiovascular risk. There are no currently reported symptoms suggestive of acute ischemia, claudication, or neurologic compromise.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, without reported anginal symptoms today.

Class II obesity with BMI 36.6 kg/m².

Current tobacco use, which further increases cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the substantially increased cardiovascular risk associated with LDL cholesterol of 202 mg/dL in the setting of coronary artery disease and ongoing tobacco use.

Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after shared decision-making and review of contraindications, potential adverse effects, and adherence expectations. Given the degree of LDL elevation and known coronary artery disease, additional therapy such as ezetimibe may be considered if LDL reduction remains inadequate or if a statin is not tolerated. Treatment should be selected and monitored through primary care, with consideration of cardiology input if treatment goals are not achieved.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel after starting or adjusting therapy to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and measurement of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) as clinically appropriate. A coronary artery calcium scan is not expected to change management substantially in a patient with known coronary artery disease and this degree of lipid abnormality.

Counseled on a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, lean proteins, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated fats, trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol. Encourage gradual increase in regular physical activity as tolerated and weight reduction, with attention to cardiovascular symptoms during exertion.

Strongly advised smoking cessation. Discussed that stopping tobacco is one of the most important modifiable interventions for reducing further cardiovascular risk; cessation medications and counseling can be offered when he is ready.

Continue routine follow-up for coronary artery disease and risk-factor modification. Seek urgent medical attention for new or persistent chest pressure, shortness of breath, syncope, focal neurologic symptoms, or significant exertional leg pain. Follow up after initiation or adjustment of lipid therapy to review tolerance, adherence, and repeat lipid results.
