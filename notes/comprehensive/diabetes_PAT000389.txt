Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Henry Walsh is a 93-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 60 years. Current disease control is suboptimal based on recent laboratory testing. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. He reports numbness and tingling involving the hands and feet. No acute change in these symptoms is reported.

He has a 33-year history of hypertension. He currently smokes. No medication regimen, allergies, or home glucose readings are provided for review today.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue and unintentional weight loss.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Endocrine: Denies increased thirst, frequent urination, or increased hunger.

Skin: Denies slow-healing wounds.

Neurologic: Reports numbness and tingling of the hands and feet.

Infectious disease: Denies frequent infections.

The remainder of the review of systems is not contributory to today’s diabetes follow-up.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 143/88 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 185 cm. Weight 93.1 kg. BMI 27.2 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.8%. Fasting glucose 228 mg/dL.

Physical examination: Patient is seen for outpatient follow-up and is hemodynamically stable by recorded vital signs. No acute hyperglycemic symptoms are reported. He endorses distal numbness and tingling of the hands and feet; a detailed sensory and vascular foot examination should be performed and documented at follow-up.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented acute complication, currently poorly controlled. Hemoglobin A1c of 9.8% and fasting glucose of 228 mg/dL indicate persistent hyperglycemia despite the absence of polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, or other constitutional symptoms. Given his age of 93 years and long duration of diabetes, treatment goals should be individualized with emphasis on avoiding symptomatic hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia.

Paresthesias of the hands and feet, concerning for possible diabetic peripheral neuropathic symptoms. No ulcers, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections are reported.

Hypertension by history for 33 years. Blood pressure today is mildly elevated at 143/88 mmHg.

Overweight, BMI 27.2 kg/m².

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Review the patient’s current medication bottles, adherence, access to medications, dietary pattern, and home glucose monitoring before selecting or intensifying therapy. Discuss pharmacologic treatment appropriate for his renal function, nutritional status, functional status, and hypoglycemia risk; avoid overly aggressive glucose lowering given advanced age. If treatment is initiated or adjusted, provide clear glucose-monitoring and hypoglycemia precautions.

Counsel regarding a consistent carbohydrate-conscious diet, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, and regular activity appropriate to his mobility and safety. Encourage adequate hydration.

Recommend obtaining renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio as part of diabetes monitoring, along with a fasting lipid profile if not recently assessed. Continue periodic HbA1c monitoring after treatment changes, with an individualized goal based on overall health and risk of hypoglycemia.

Perform a comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including skin inspection, pulses, and protective sensation, at the next visit or sooner if symptoms worsen. Reinforce daily foot inspection, appropriate footwear, and prompt evaluation of any blister, ulcer, color change, or infection.

Recommend routine diabetic eye evaluation, particularly given the prolonged duration of diabetes and poor glycemic control.

Review blood pressure management and home readings at follow-up. Reinforce smoking cessation; offer counseling and cessation support.

Return for close outpatient follow-up after medication reconciliation and treatment planning, or sooner for marked hyperglycemic symptoms, confusion, weakness, inability to maintain hydration, new visual changes, or foot wounds.
