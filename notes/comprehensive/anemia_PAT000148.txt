Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Arthur Nichols is a 70-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of longstanding anemia, reported for approximately 39 years. He currently denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or noticeable pallor. No interval anemia-related complaints are reported.

Relevant chronic conditions include rheumatoid arthritis for approximately 30 years, malignancy for approximately 23 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 9 years. These conditions provide significant potential inflammatory and chronic disease contributors to his anemia. He is a current smoker. No anemia-directed medication or supplementation is documented.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 127/82 mmHg, pulse 86 beats/min, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Anthropometrics: Height 166 cm, weight 75.0 kg, BMI 27.2 kg/m².

General: No anemia-related symptoms reported at today’s visit. Pallor is not reported.

Cardiovascular: Pulse 86 beats/min without tachycardia by measured vital signs.

Respiratory: Oxygen saturation 96% on the recorded vital signs; no shortness of breath reported.

Laboratory Results:
Hemoglobin 8.2 g/dL
Hematocrit 24.7%
RBC count 2.94 million/µL
MCV 84 fL
Serum iron 36 µg/dL
Ferritin 168 ng/mL
TIBC 228 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 15.8%

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation in the setting of longstanding rheumatoid arthritis and malignancy. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are significantly reduced, with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, ferritin in a preserved range, and low TIBC. This pattern favors impaired iron availability related to chronic inflammation rather than straightforward absolute iron depletion, although combined etiologies remain possible. The anemia has reportedly been present for approximately 39 years and is currently not associated with fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, headache, or reduced exercise tolerance.

The patient is hemodynamically stable by today’s vital signs, without documented tachycardia or hypoxemia. Given the hemoglobin of 8.2 g/dL and underlying malignancy and rheumatoid arthritis, the degree of anemia warrants active reassessment despite minimal symptoms.

Rheumatoid arthritis, longstanding, relevant as a chronic inflammatory contributor.

Malignancy, longstanding, relevant as a potential contributor to chronic inflammation and anemia.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding; relevant comorbidity in overall medical risk assessment.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Review the chronic anemia with the patient and discuss that the laboratory pattern is compatible with anemia of chronic inflammation, while recognizing that mixed anemia, nutritional deficiency, renal contribution, occult blood loss, or disease-related marrow suppression may also need consideration.

Recommend updated complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to reassess severity and marrow response. Recommend vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as part of the anemia evaluation. Review the clinical status of the underlying rheumatoid arthritis and malignancy and coordinate management with the clinicians caring for those conditions.

Do not begin empiric iron replacement solely on the current iron indices without clarifying the overall cause; ferritin is preserved and TIBC is low, while transferrin saturation is reduced. Iron replacement, including oral or intravenous therapy, should be considered only after review of the complete evaluation and clinical context.

Because hemoglobin is 8.2 g/dL, arrange timely outpatient reassessment of the blood count and treatment plan. Advise prompt medical evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, marked weakness, bleeding, or reduced exercise tolerance.

Encourage smoking cessation as part of overall chronic disease care. Continue routine follow-up for diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, and malignancy, with anemia reassessed in conjunction with those conditions.
