Chief Complaint

Initial outpatient consultation for chronic kidney disease and fatigue.

Subjective

Eric Butler is a 79-year-old male, 181 cm tall and weighing 88.1 kg, presenting for initial evaluation of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 7 years ago. Current laboratory results show serum creatinine 3.59 mg/dL, BUN 92 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 18 mL/min/1.73 m². He reports fatigue.

He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported today. He has a history of hyperlipidemia for approximately 38 years. He currently smokes. Current medication and allergy reconciliation should be completed before treatment decisions are made.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Positive for fatigue. No other constitutional symptoms reported.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.

Cardiovascular: Denies peripheral edema.

Gastrointestinal: Denies nausea.

Genitourinary: Denies change in urinary frequency.

Skin: Denies pruritus.

Neurologic/Musculoskeletal: Denies muscle cramps.

Psychiatric/Sleep: Denies sleep disturbance.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 151/89 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Anthropometrics: Height 181 cm, weight 88.1 kg, BMI 26.9 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: Serum creatinine 3.59 mg/dL. BUN 92 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 18 mL/min/1.73 m².

Physical examination is limited to the documented vital signs and reported symptom review. There is no reported peripheral edema, dyspnea, or other evidence of fluid overload at this visit.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function and significant azotemia. The patient reports approximately 7 years of known disease, with current creatinine 3.59 mg/dL, BUN 92 mg/dL, and eGFR 18 mL/min/1.73 m². He is not reporting nausea, pruritus, confusion, muscle cramps, reduced urination, edema, or shortness of breath, but fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction or associated anemia/metabolic complications. Electrolyte status, acid-base status, urine protein burden, blood counts, mineral metabolism, and renal structural findings require assessment. No formal disease stage is assigned today.

Elevated blood pressure at today’s visit, 151/89 mmHg, in the setting of chronic kidney disease. Long-term blood pressure control is important to reduce further renal and cardiovascular risk.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding by history.

Active tobacco use, contributing to cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for comprehensive evaluation and longitudinal management of markedly reduced renal function. Discussed that the current kidney function and BUN elevation warrant timely specialist assessment even in the absence of significant uremic symptoms.

Recommend repeat renal function testing with a serum electrolytes panel and assessment of acid-base status. Obtain a complete blood count to evaluate fatigue and possible anemia associated with chronic kidney disease. Obtain urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to quantify proteinuria. Consider renal ultrasound to evaluate kidney size and structural abnormalities, and intact parathyroid hormone testing to assess for chronic kidney disease-related mineral and bone disorder.

Perform complete medication reconciliation before initiating or adjusting therapy. Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal products. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a clinician. Avoid unnecessary iodinated contrast exposure and ensure all medication doses are adjusted for renal function.

Recheck blood pressure and obtain home readings if feasible. Review sodium restriction, avoidance of excessive processed foods, and individualized fluid and dietary guidance pending renal and electrolyte assessment. Do not make empiric fluid changes without review of kidney function and volume status.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and offered assistance with cessation planning. Address cardiovascular risk reduction, including reassessment of hyperlipidemia management after medication reconciliation.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including new or worsening shortness of breath, rapidly increasing swelling, markedly decreased urine output, persistent vomiting, chest pain, severe weakness, confusion, or inability to maintain oral intake.

Follow up promptly after recommended testing and nephrology evaluation, with earlier reassessment for any change in symptoms.
