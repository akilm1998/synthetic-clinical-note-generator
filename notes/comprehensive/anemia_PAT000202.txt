Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Shannon Douglas is a 72-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 9 years. She reports headache but denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor. No interval cardiopulmonary symptoms are reported.

Her medical history is notable for malignancy of approximately 13 years’ duration and rheumatoid arthritis of approximately 4 years’ duration, both of which may contribute to chronic inflammation and impaired erythropoiesis. Anemia has not been under active treatment. Smoking status is documented as current smoker. Height is 150 cm and weight is 64.6 kg, with BMI 28.7 kg/m².

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue, weakness, or reduced exercise tolerance.

Neurologic: Reports headache. Denies dizziness.

Cardiopulmonary: Denies shortness of breath or palpitations.

Hematologic: Denies awareness of pallor.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 124/80 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

General: No pallor is noted.

Cardiovascular: Normal cardiovascular examination. Heart rate is not tachycardic.

Respiratory: Normal respiratory examination. Oxygenation is adequate on room air.

Laboratory Results:
Hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL
Hematocrit 26.1%
RBC count 3.07 million/µL
MCV 85 fL
Serum iron 43 µg/dL
Ferritin 189 ng/mL
TIBC 229 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 18.8%

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, present for approximately 9 years, with hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL and hematocrit 26.1%. Iron studies demonstrate low serum iron and TIBC with ferritin not reduced, a pattern most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic disease rather than isolated absolute iron deficiency. Her longstanding malignancy and rheumatoid arthritis are clinically relevant potential contributors. The anemia is currently not under active management.

She is hemodynamically stable today without tachycardia, hypoxemia, dyspnea, chest symptoms, dizziness, or reduced exercise tolerance. Headache is present but is not accompanied by other reported neurologic or cardiopulmonary symptoms. No pallor is observed.

Current smoking status noted. Overweight by BMI at 28.7 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the laboratory pattern and likely contribution of chronic inflammatory disease and malignancy to impaired red blood cell production. Recommend reassessment with a complete blood count including reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the anemia and assess for additional reversible contributors.

Recommend repeat iron studies and review for any evidence of blood loss or nutritional deficiency before initiating iron therapy. Given the low hemoglobin and chronicity, coordinate management with the clinicians overseeing her malignancy and rheumatoid arthritis. Consideration of oral or intravenous iron, or an erythropoietin-stimulating agent, should be based on the underlying disease activity, renal function, complete anemia evaluation, and specialist input; no such therapy is initiated today.

Monitor headache and anemia-related symptoms. Seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, palpitations, syncope, severe dizziness, marked weakness, or acute neurologic change. Encourage smoking cessation as part of overall health management.

Return for follow-up after the recommended anemia evaluation to review results and update treatment plan.
