Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension and cardiovascular risk-factor management.

Subjective

Frank Montgomery is a 45-year-old man, height 187 cm, weight 75.9 kg, BMI 21.7 kg/m², presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. Blood pressure has not been actively managed. He reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. No hypertensive-crisis symptoms are reported today.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 14 years, chronic kidney disease for 4 years, and hyperlipidemia for 12 years. He does not smoke. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 160/93 mmHg. Pulse 69 bpm. Respiratory rate 15/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

General: Adult male with elevated office blood pressure; no acute cardiopulmonary symptoms reported.

Cardiovascular: Pulse regular at 69 bpm by vital-sign measurement. No palpitations observed during the recorded examination.

Respiratory: Respiratory rate and oxygen saturation are normal. No dyspnea reported.

Neurologic: Intermittent dizziness reported. No headache, blurred vision, or other acute neurologic symptoms reported.

Height and weight as above; BMI is within the normal range.

Laboratory data: Sodium 140 mmol/L. Potassium 4.7 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent office blood pressure elevation at 160/93 mmHg in the setting of approximately 4 years of insufficient active management. The patient is currently symptomatic with intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue, but has no chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, visual disturbance, or epistaxis to suggest an acute hypertensive emergency. His normal pulse, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation are reassuring. Sodium and potassium are currently within expected ranges; however, his chronic kidney disease and diabetes substantially increase the importance of consistent blood pressure control and careful medication selection and monitoring.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for 14 years, increasing overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

Chronic kidney disease, present for 4 years, requiring renal-protective blood pressure management and monitoring of renal function and electrolytes.

Hyperlipidemia, present for 12 years, contributing to elevated long-term cardiovascular risk.

Intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. These may occur with uncontrolled blood pressure but warrant reassessment if persistent or worsening. There are no reported acute ischemic or heart-failure symptoms.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current blood pressure and the increased cardiovascular and renal risk associated with coexisting diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and hyperlipidemia. Recommend prompt clinician-directed initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy rather than continued observation alone. Medication selection should account for diabetes, chronic kidney disease, renal function, potassium level, and potential cardiovascular benefit. The current potassium is 4.7 mmol/L, so repeat renal function and electrolyte monitoring should accompany any therapy affecting renal perfusion or potassium balance.

Begin home blood pressure monitoring with a validated upper-arm cuff. Check blood pressure after several minutes of seated rest, with a log of readings, pulse, symptoms, and timing for review. Confirm the elevated office measurement with repeat measurements and assess for an orthostatic component if dizziness continues.

Recommend review of renal function, urine protein assessment, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of comprehensive hypertension and diabetic kidney-risk evaluation. Recommend an ECG given palpitations and longstanding hypertension. These should be obtained as outpatient evaluations unless symptoms become acute.

Counseled on limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, avoiding excess alcohol, and continuing tobacco avoidance. His BMI is 21.7 kg/m², with no indication for weight-loss intervention.

Continue coordinated management of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and hyperlipidemia, with attention to cardiovascular risk reduction and renal protection.

Seek emergency evaluation for severe or sudden headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic deficit, marked visual change, confusion, fainting, or persistently extreme blood pressure readings accompanied by symptoms.

Arrange close outpatient reassessment after home readings and treatment initiation or adjustment to evaluate blood pressure response, dizziness, palpitations, and laboratory safety monitoring.
