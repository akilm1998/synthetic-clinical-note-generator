Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Brooklyn Barnes is a 33-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of anemia present for approximately 14 years. Her anemia has not been under active management. She reports headache but denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or other reported anemia-related cardiopulmonary symptoms. Pallor has been noted.

Her medical history is notable for inflammatory bowel disease for approximately 10 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 3 years, and malignancy diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. No current medication regimen is provided. She does not smoke.

Objective

Height is 155 cm, weight 56.0 kg, and BMI 23.3 kg/m². Blood pressure is 115/79 mmHg, pulse 75 beats/min, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, and oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

General examination is notable for pallor. She is not tachycardic. Cardiovascular examination is normal. Respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory results show hemoglobin 9.4 g/dL, hematocrit 29.1%, RBC count 3.51 million/µL, and MCV 83 fL, consistent with a chronic normocytic anemia. Serum iron is 47 µg/dL, ferritin 455 ng/mL, TIBC 200 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 23.5%. The combination of reduced circulating iron, elevated ferritin, and low TIBC is more consistent with iron-restricted erythropoiesis in the setting of chronic inflammation than with uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency.

Assessment

Anemia of chronic disease/chronic inflammation, longstanding and currently not actively managed. The anemia is normocytic with hemoglobin 9.4 g/dL and hematocrit 29.1%. Iron indices demonstrate low serum iron and low TIBC with preserved-to-elevated ferritin, supporting inflammatory sequestration of iron. Her longstanding inflammatory bowel disease and more recent malignancy are clinically relevant potential drivers of chronic inflammation. There is no current evidence of hemodynamic instability or symptomatic cardiopulmonary compromise; pulse, blood pressure, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation are normal. Headache may be associated with the anemia but is not accompanied by dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance.

Inflammatory bowel disease, approximately 10-year duration, a significant potential contributor to chronic inflammatory anemia. The current degree of disease activity is not provided.

Malignancy, approximately 1-year duration, also potentially contributing to an inflammatory anemia. No treatment or disease-status information is provided.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 3-year duration. This is a relevant comorbidity in overall medical management, although the available data do not establish a specific contribution to the current anemia.

Plan

Obtain updated hematologic evaluation with complete blood count and indices, reticulocyte count, and peripheral blood smear to characterize erythropoietic response and confirm stability of the anemia. Check vitamin B12, folate, and renal function, and obtain inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate to assess additional or overlapping contributors.

Continue evaluation and treatment of the underlying inflammatory conditions in coordination with the clinicians managing her inflammatory bowel disease and malignancy. Control of the underlying inflammatory process is central to improving anemia of chronic disease.

The current iron profile does not support empiric escalation of oral iron solely on the basis of the low serum iron, given the ferritin of 455 ng/mL and low TIBC. Iron replacement, including consideration of intravenous iron, should be individualized based on repeat studies, evidence of true iron deficiency or ongoing gastrointestinal losses, inflammatory bowel disease activity, and overall clinical context.

An erythropoiesis-stimulating agent is not indicated solely from the information currently available and would require clarification of the underlying cause, renal function, malignancy status, treatment intent, thrombotic risk, and response to management of inflammation. Hematology input is appropriate if the anemia worsens, the reticulocyte response is inadequate, additional cytopenias develop, or the diagnosis remains uncertain.

Monitor for worsening fatigue, dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, or declining exercise tolerance. Reassess after the recommended evaluation and review of the underlying inflammatory disease activity.
