Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced estimated renal function and significantly elevated creatinine and BUN. Although she denies fatigue, pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, urinary changes, edema, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath, the current renal indices require prompt specialist assessment and active management. The duration of disease is approximately 8 years.

Hypertension, longstanding for 28 years, currently above goal at 165/91 mmHg and an important risk factor for progression of renal disease.

Hyperlipidemia, duration approximately 2 years.

Current tobacco use, which increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the creatinine of 6.0 mg/dL, BUN of 60 mg/dL, and eGFR of 6 mL/min/1.73 m², even in the absence of symptoms. Recommend urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management, assessment for complications, and discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate.

Recommend prompt review of blood pressure management and home blood pressure monitoring. Medication reconciliation and selection of kidney-appropriate antihypertensive therapy should be completed urgently; no current medication regimen is documented today.

Recommend serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as part of evaluation and complication assessment if not recently completed. These should be coordinated promptly with nephrology or the treating clinician.

Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications unless specifically directed by a clinician. Review all over-the-counter medications and supplements before use. Encourage appropriate hydration without excessive fluid intake; individualized dietary counseling for advanced CKD is recommended.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and cardiovascular risk reduction. Continue follow-up for hypertension and hyperlipidemia.

Seek emergency care immediately for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, marked weakness, persistent nausea or vomiting, rapidly worsening swelling, substantially decreased urination, or other acute deterioration.

Return for close primary care follow-up after urgent renal evaluation, or sooner for any new symptoms.
