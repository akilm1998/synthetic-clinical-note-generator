Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up of abnormal renal function.

Subjective

Victoria Richardson is a 36-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of chronic kidney disease, reported to have been present for approximately 5 years and currently without active disease management. She has type 2 diabetes mellitus for 2 years and hyperlipidemia for 5 years. She reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, change in urination frequency, peripheral edema, or shortness of breath. No acute uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported today.

She smokes. Current medication list and allergies are not provided.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 143/83 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 152 cm; weight 54.3 kg; BMI 23.5 kg/m².

Laboratory Results:
Serum creatinine 2.93 mg/dL.
BUN 81 mg/dL.
Estimated GFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m².

Physical examination: Patient is clinically stable by available vital signs and denies symptoms suggestive of acute volume overload or uremia. No additional examination findings are documented.

Review of Systems: Constitutional negative for fatigue. Skin negative for pruritus. Gastrointestinal negative for nausea. Musculoskeletal negative for muscle cramps. Genitourinary negative for urinary frequency change. Cardiopulmonary negative for shortness of breath. Peripheral vascular negative for edema. Sleep negative for sleep disturbance.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function and markedly elevated BUN and creatinine. The patient has a 5-year history of CKD and is currently asymptomatic, without reported edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, or urinary change. The degree of renal impairment warrants prompt nephrology evaluation and assessment for complications. No evidence from the available history or vital signs of acute respiratory or hemodynamic instability.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 2-year history, relevant risk factor for progressive kidney disease.

Hyperlipidemia, 5-year history.

Elevated blood pressure reading in the setting of CKD.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the seriousness of the current renal function results and the need for prompt medical evaluation. Recommend urgent nephrology referral and expedited repeat assessment of renal function, including serum electrolytes, creatinine, BUN, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and complete blood count. Recommend renal ultrasound and evaluation of calcium-phosphate balance with intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate.

Review and reconcile all current medications, including over-the-counter agents and supplements, at the next contact. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications unless specifically directed by a clinician. Medication dosing should be adjusted for renal function.

Recommend close blood pressure and diabetes management with attention to kidney protection. Encourage smoking cessation. Dietary counseling should focus on individualized sodium and renal nutrition guidance pending specialist evaluation; avoid unapproved supplements or restrictive diets.

Proceed to the emergency department immediately for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, chest pain, or other acute deterioration.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after repeat evaluation and nephrology consultation.
