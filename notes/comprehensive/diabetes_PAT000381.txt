Chief Complaint

Annual review of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Brooke Snyder is a 58-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease follow-up. She has a 26-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. Current laboratory results show hemoglobin A1c 9.6% and fasting glucose 258 mg/dL. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. She denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet.

She is a nonsmoker. Weight is 55.0 kg at 153 cm, with BMI 23.5 kg/m². Her medical history is also notable for non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 16 years. No current diabetes medications, allergies, or other treatment regimen are provided for review.

Review of systems is otherwise negative based on the symptoms reviewed above. No acute constitutional, visual, genitourinary, skin, or neurologic concerns are reported.

Objective

Vital signs: blood pressure 117/75 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 153 cm. Weight 55.0 kg. BMI 23.5 kg/m².

Laboratory results: hemoglobin A1c 9.6%. Fasting glucose 258 mg/dL.

No diabetic complications are reported by history. No abnormal physical examination findings are supplied for today’s visit.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently significantly above individualized glycemic targets based on hemoglobin A1c of 9.6% and fasting glucose of 258 mg/dL. Despite the degree of hyperglycemia, she is not reporting classic hyperglycemic symptoms, weight loss, recurrent infections, visual symptoms, or peripheral neuropathic symptoms. Her 26-year disease duration increases the importance of formal screening for renal, retinal, and foot complications.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding, approximately 16 years. This should be considered when selecting and monitoring diabetes therapy and when reviewing metabolic risk factors.

Normal-range BMI and normal blood pressure today. Nonsmoker.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values indicate inadequate diabetes control and warrant active treatment rather than observation alone. Review prior diabetes therapy, adherence, home glucose monitoring, medication tolerance, and contraindications at the next medication reconciliation. If not contraindicated and not already prescribed, initiation of metformin would be a reasonable first-line option; treatment selection should account for renal function, hepatic history, cost, preferences, and risk of hypoglycemia. Additional therapy should be considered if glycemic response remains inadequate.

Recommend updated renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before or during medication planning. Recommend fasting lipid profile and review of cardiovascular risk factors as part of diabetes care. Repeat hemoglobin A1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response.

Encouraged a consistent carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, regular physical activity as tolerated, and maintenance of her current healthy weight. Recommend home glucose monitoring if medication therapy requiring monitoring is initiated, with instruction regarding symptoms of hypoglycemia and marked hyperglycemia.

Recommend comprehensive dilated eye examination because of the long duration of diabetes. Perform a complete diabetic foot examination, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensation, at follow-up and annually; advised daily self-inspection and prompt evaluation of any wound, color change, or infection. Continue routine dental care.

Review age-appropriate preventive care, including breast, cervical, and colorectal cancer screening according to prior results and applicable guidelines. Review immunization status, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, hepatitis B, and shingles vaccination as appropriate.

Follow up promptly for medication and glucose-management planning, with reassessment of glycemic control after treatment is established. Seek urgent care for vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, inability to maintain hydration, severe weakness, or persistently very high glucose readings.
