Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Samuel Wood is a 45-year-old man presenting for urgent outpatient follow-up of hypertension, diagnosed approximately 3 years ago and currently without active management. Blood pressure today is 169/94 mmHg. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. No symptoms suggestive of acute end-organ involvement are reported.

Past medical history is significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 15 years, hyperlipidemia for 8 years, and chronic kidney disease for 3 years. He currently smokes. Height is 184 cm and weight is 85.7 kg, with BMI 25.3 kg/m².

Review of systems: Constitutional review is negative for fatigue. Neurologic review is negative for headache or dizziness. Eyes: no blurred vision. Cardiovascular: no chest pain or palpitations. Respiratory: no shortness of breath. ENT: no nosebleeds. No other acute concerns reported.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 169/94 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 184 cm; weight 85.7 kg; BMI 25.3 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Sodium 147 mmol/L; potassium 3.6 mmol/L.

Physical examination is limited to the documented vital signs and symptom assessment. The patient reports no symptoms concerning for hypertensive emergency.

Assessment

Markedly elevated blood pressure in a patient with approximately 3 years of untreated or unmanaged essential hypertension. He is currently asymptomatic, with no reported chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, visual disturbance, or epistaxis to suggest hypertensive emergency; however, the degree of elevation and his substantial cardiovascular and renal risk warrant prompt treatment and close reassessment.

Comorbid type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, and hyperlipidemia increase his risk for cardiovascular and renal complications. Mild hypernatremia is present at 147 mmol/L, with potassium at the low-normal range of 3.6 mmol/L. Current smoking further increases vascular risk.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement during the visit using appropriate cuff size and technique, with assessment of readings in both arms if clinically indicated. Arrange prompt outpatient initiation of antihypertensive therapy through the treating clinician, with medication selection individualized to his diabetes and chronic kidney disease. Renal function and urinary protein assessment should be reviewed or obtained before and after initiating or adjusting therapy, particularly if a renin-angiotensin system agent is selected. Monitor electrolytes after treatment initiation or dose changes.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, reduced dietary sodium intake, avoidance of excess alcohol, regular physical activity as tolerated, and smoking cessation counseling. Review adherence barriers and ensure that hypertension is not left untreated.

Continue coordinated management of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and chronic kidney disease, including cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend ECG, lipid profile review, and urinalysis as part of hypertension and end-organ risk assessment if not recently completed.

Seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, new neurologic deficits, confusion, acute visual change, or persistent markedly worsening blood pressure. Arrange close follow-up within days to reassess blood pressure, symptoms, treatment initiation, and laboratory monitoring.
