Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Caroline Rivera is an 88-year-old woman with a 32-year history of chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 29 years, coronary artery disease for 18 years, and gout for 37 years. She reports ongoing fatigue. Denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. She continues to smoke.

Objective

Height 167 cm, weight 61.4 kg, BMI 22.0 kg/m². BP 145/81 mmHg, pulse 82, respirations 19, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: creatinine 3.88 mg/dL, BUN 60 mg/dL, eGFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and fatigue. Disease has not been actively managed and requires prompt reassessment and specialty involvement. No current symptoms of fluid overload or overt uremic symptoms reported.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease and gout, longstanding comorbidities.

Elevated blood pressure reading.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange expedited nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management and discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate.

Obtain repeat renal function and serum electrolytes, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone. Consider renal ultrasound if not recently performed.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications for renal dosing and avoid NSAIDs and other nephrotoxins. Encourage adequate but not excessive hydration and monitoring for reduced urine output, worsening fatigue, nausea, dyspnea, or edema.

Address blood pressure and diabetes control at follow-up, with treatment decisions guided by renal function and comorbid coronary artery disease.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation. Follow up promptly after laboratory testing or sooner for worsening symptoms.
