Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective
Aria Patterson is a 69-year-old woman with an approximately 8-year history of hypertension, currently not actively managed. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. Known comorbidities include coronary artery disease of 18 years’ duration and hyperlipidemia diagnosed 1 year ago. She does not smoke.

Objective
Height 167 cm; weight 70.0 kg; BMI 25.1 kg/m².
Blood pressure 165/90 mmHg; pulse 77 bpm; respiratory rate 14/min; temperature 36.9°C; oxygen saturation 96%.
Serum electrolytes: sodium 136 mmol/L, potassium 4.7 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure, currently without reported symptoms of acute end-organ involvement. Longstanding hypertension and coexisting coronary artery disease increase cardiovascular risk. Electrolytes are within expected ranges.

Plan
Confirm blood pressure with repeat measurement and home monitoring, documenting a log for review. Discuss initiation of antihypertensive therapy with consideration of her coronary artery disease and baseline electrolytes; no regimen is currently documented. Counsel on sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight maintenance, and moderation of alcohol intake. Recommend ECG, urinalysis, and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk assessment. Review promptly for medication initiation and blood pressure response. Seek urgent evaluation for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, or visual changes.
