Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Isaac Henderson is a 52-year-old man with approximately 15 years of hypertension, currently without documented active management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 9 years, chronic kidney disease for 2 years, and hyperlipidemia for 19 years. He does not smoke.

Objective

Blood pressure 155/93 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%. Height 186 cm, weight 100 kg, BMI 28.9 kg/m². Serum sodium 137 mmol/L and potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently uncontrolled. Blood pressure is elevated but without symptoms or findings provided to suggest hypertensive emergency.

2. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic, increasing cardiovascular and renal risk.

3. Chronic kidney disease, present for 2 years; renal-protective antihypertensive selection is important.

4. Hyperlipidemia, longstanding cardiovascular risk factor.

Plan

Confirm blood pressure with repeat office measurement and initiate an active antihypertensive treatment plan after medication reconciliation. Given diabetes and chronic kidney disease, an ACE inhibitor or ARB should be considered if clinically appropriate, with monitoring of renal function and potassium after initiation or dose changes. Obtain home blood pressure readings and review at close follow-up.

Counseled on sodium reduction, weight management, regular physical activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management in the context of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and hyperlipidemia.

Seek emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly worsening blood pressure. Close outpatient follow-up recommended.
