Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

55-year-old male with a 9-year history of essential hypertension, currently not under active treatment. Denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. History is also significant for coronary artery disease for 10 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 23 years. Continues to smoke. No medication regimen provided for review.

Objective

Blood pressure 144/93 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air. Height 160 cm, weight 73.7 kg, BMI 28.8 kg/m². Serum sodium 146 mmol/L and potassium 4.7 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and above goal today without reported symptoms or evidence of acute hypertensive symptoms. Cardiovascular risk is increased by coexisting coronary artery disease, diabetes, overweight status, and active tobacco use.

2. Coronary artery disease, history of 10 years.

3. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, history of 23 years.

4. Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed importance of consistent blood pressure management given coronary artery disease and diabetes. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log and review of readings at follow-up. Discuss initiation of antihypertensive therapy after medication reconciliation, with selection guided by comorbid diabetes and coronary disease; do not start or adjust medication until current medications and contraindications are confirmed. Recommend reduced sodium intake, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Counselled regarding smoking cessation.

Recommend ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular and hypertension risk assessment, with repeat electrolytes and renal function monitoring if antihypertensive therapy is initiated. Continue routine management of diabetes and coronary artery disease. Seek urgent care for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly elevated blood pressure. Follow up after home readings and medication reconciliation.
