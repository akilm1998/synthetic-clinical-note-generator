Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Sean Palmer is a 79-year-old man with a 40-year history of type 2 diabetes, obesity, obstructive sleep apnea, and anxiety, presenting for routine follow-up. Diabetes control is currently poor. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling. Current medication regimen was not provided. He continues to smoke.

Vital signs: BP 134/82 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respirations 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 94%.

Height 175 cm, weight 106.9 kg, BMI 34.9 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.9%; fasting glucose 233 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, with markedly elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose indicating inadequate glycemic control. No hyperglycemic, neuropathic, or infectious symptoms reported.

Obesity, BMI 34.9 kg/m².

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding.

Anxiety, longstanding.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Review and reconcile all current medications and adherence; discuss initiation or intensification of diabetes therapy based on renal function, hypoglycemia risk, comorbidities, and patient preferences.

Counseled on reducing concentrated carbohydrates, portion control, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and home glucose monitoring.

Recommend serum creatinine/eGFR, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetic eye and foot evaluation.

Discussed smoking cessation.

Continue management of obstructive sleep apnea and anxiety. Reassess glycemic control and treatment response at follow-up.
