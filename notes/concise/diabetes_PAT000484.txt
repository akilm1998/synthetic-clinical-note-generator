Chief Complaint
Medication review for type 2 diabetes.

Subjective
Sophia Turner is a 91-year-old woman with an 11-year history of type 2 diabetes, currently not receiving documented active management. She denies polydipsia, polyuria, increased hunger, weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, and recurrent infections. Reports numbness and tingling in the hands and feet. Diabetes medication regimen, adherence, and tolerance require reconciliation today; no medication-related adverse effects are reported. She smokes. Comorbidities include obstructive sleep apnea for 45 years, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease for 36 years, and depression for 45 years.

Objective
BP 138/78 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respirations 17/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96%. Height 175 cm, weight 82.7 kg, BMI 27.0 kg/m². HbA1c 9.6%; fasting glucose 257 mg/dL.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complication, poorly controlled based on elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose. Distal numbness and tingling reported. No symptomatic hyperglycemia or evidence of infection by history.

Plan
Reconcile all current medications, including any diabetes therapy, and assess adherence, affordability, administration, and tolerance. Discuss initiation or intensification of glucose-lowering therapy with an individualized goal appropriate for age and comorbidities; review renal function before selecting therapy. Encourage consistent nutrition, physical activity as tolerated, hydration, and home glucose monitoring. Review hypoglycemia precautions and foot-care measures. Recommend renal and cardiovascular risk assessment, including urine albumin screening and lipid evaluation, and routine diabetic eye and foot examinations. Counsel regarding smoking cessation. Follow up promptly after medication reconciliation to finalize treatment plan and reassess glucose control.
