Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. Chronic comorbidities include hypertension for 41 years, hyperlipidemia for 7 years, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 8 years. He currently smokes. No current medication regimen is provided.

Objective
Height 171 cm, weight 76.6 kg, BMI 26.2 kg/m². Blood pressure 160/94 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%. Recent laboratory results show creatinine 5.77 mg/dL, BUN 61 mg/dL, and eGFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. Despite absence of uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, the current renal impairment requires prompt specialty management. Hypertension is above goal today. History of secondary hyperparathyroidism and hyperlipidemia. Ongoing tobacco use.

Plan
Recommend prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive CKD management and discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate. Obtain updated renal monitoring, including serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact PTH; consider renal ultrasound if not previously performed. Review blood pressure management and avoid nephrotoxic medications, including NSAIDs. Counsel regarding smoking cessation and prompt evaluation for new nausea, vomiting, confusion, progressive weakness, reduced urine output, swelling, or shortness of breath. Close outpatient follow-up after laboratory and nephrology evaluation.
