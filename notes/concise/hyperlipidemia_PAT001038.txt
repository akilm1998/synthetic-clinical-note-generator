Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective
Alexandra Boyd is a 39-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of hyperlipidemia present for approximately 2 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 1 year, chronic kidney disease for 1 year, and hypertension for 3 years. She does not smoke.

Objective
Height 151 cm; weight 60.9 kg; BMI 26.7 kg/m². Blood pressure 146/87 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 350 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 230 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 50 mg/dL, triglycerides 351 mg/dL.

Assessment
Severe hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, not currently managed. No reported cardiovascular or cutaneous manifestations. Cardiovascular risk is increased by concomitant diabetes, chronic kidney disease, hypertension, and overweight status. Blood pressure is above goal today.

Plan
Discussed heart-healthy eating pattern with reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess calories, along with regular physical activity and weight management.

Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after review of contraindications and shared decision-making, with consideration of additional lipid-lowering therapy if LDL remains substantially elevated. Obtain a repeat fasting lipid panel and assess for secondary contributors, including glycemic, renal, hepatic, and thyroid factors, as clinically appropriate. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk assessment.

Address hypertension and diabetes control at follow-up, given their contribution to overall cardiovascular risk. Return after treatment initiation and repeat lipid assessment to review response and tolerability.
