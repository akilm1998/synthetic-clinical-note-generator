Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

James Carter is an 84-year-old man seen for follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 38 years. Management has not been active. He has known coronary artery disease, reported duration 39 years. Denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain/claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. He does not smoke.

Objective

Blood pressure 131/82 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respirations 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%. Height 161 cm, weight 59.6 kg, BMI 23.0 kg/m².

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 380 mg/dL, LDL-C 269 mg/dL, HDL-C 38 mg/dL, triglycerides 366 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Severe mixed hyperlipidemia, longstanding and currently inadequately managed, with markedly elevated LDL-C and triglycerides and low HDL-C.
2. Coronary artery disease, longstanding. No current anginal or claudication symptoms reported.

Plan

Discussed substantially increased cardiovascular risk associated with current lipid levels and underlying coronary artery disease. Recommend initiation of lipid-lowering therapy, preferably a high-intensity statin if tolerated, with treatment choice individualized to age, comorbidities, and patient preference. Consider addition of ezetimibe if LDL-C remains significantly elevated despite statin therapy. Counsel on reducing saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates, and maintaining regular activity as tolerated. Obtain a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment is established to assess response. Continue monitoring for chest discomfort, exertional dyspnea, or other ischemic symptoms and seek urgent care for acute symptoms.
