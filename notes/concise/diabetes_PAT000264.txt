Chief Complaint
Initial consultation for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective
Damian Boyd is a 51-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 20 years ago. Current diabetes management has been inadequate. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the extremities. No diabetes medications are documented for this visit. He does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 9 years’ duration, anxiety for 19 years, and depression for 30 years.

Objective
Height 186 cm; weight 102.8 kg; BMI 29.7 kg/m². Blood pressure 140/80 mmHg, pulse 68 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Hemoglobin A1c 9.2%. Fasting glucose 230 mg/dL.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled, based on HbA1c and fasting hyperglycemia. Despite marked biochemical hyperglycemia, he reports no osmotic, constitutional, infectious, visual, or neuropathic symptoms.

Chronic kidney disease, duration approximately 9 years; renal function parameters are important in determining safe diabetes therapy. Comorbid anxiety and depression. Overweight status. Blood pressure is mildly elevated today without sufficient information to establish a separate diagnosis.

Plan
Discussed the significance of sustained hyperglycemia and risks for microvascular and cardiovascular complications. Perform medication reconciliation and establish an individualized diabetes treatment regimen, with therapy selected in consideration of chronic kidney disease.

Recommend serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and fasting lipid profile. Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot examination and referral for dilated retinal evaluation. Counsel regarding nutrition focused on carbohydrate quality and portion control, weight reduction, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage home glucose monitoring with review of readings at follow-up.

Reassess glycemic control and treatment response after initiation or adjustment of therapy. Coordinate ongoing management of chronic kidney disease, anxiety, and depression.
