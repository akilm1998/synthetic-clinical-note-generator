Chief Complaint
Annual review and follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective
Nicholas Powell is a 61-year-old man, 187 cm and 87.8 kg, BMI 25.1 kg/m², presenting for annual review. Hyperlipidemia has been present for approximately 22 years and is currently untreated. Recent lipid values: total cholesterol 341 mg/dL, LDL-C 227 mg/dL, HDL-C 40 mg/dL, and triglycerides 370 mg/dL. Reports xanthelasma. Denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness. Continues to smoke.

History also notable for hypertension for 25 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 6 years.

Objective
BP 149/82 mmHg, pulse 80/min, respirations 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%. Xanthelasma reported. No other examination findings provided.

Assessment
Severe mixed hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and triglycerides, longstanding and not currently treated. Xanthelasma supports chronic lipid elevation. No reported symptoms suggestive of active ischemia or claudication.

Hypertension with above-goal office blood pressure.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Active tobacco use.

Plan
Discussed substantially increased cardiovascular risk associated with severe lipid elevation, hypertension, and smoking. Recommend initiation of high-intensity lipid-lowering therapy after shared decision-making, with consideration of additional therapy if LDL-C remains markedly elevated. Repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation and assess adherence and response.

Recommend evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and review of hepatic status in the context of known fatty liver disease. Encourage a Mediterranean-style diet, reduction of saturated fats and refined carbohydrates, regular aerobic activity, and maintenance of current healthy weight.

Recommend smoking cessation counseling and cessation support.

Reassess blood pressure with home readings and review antihypertensive management at follow-up.

Consider assessment of overall atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk and coronary artery calcium imaging if treatment decision remains uncertain. Return for lipid and blood pressure follow-up after initiating management, or sooner for chest discomfort, dyspnea, or exertional leg symptoms.
