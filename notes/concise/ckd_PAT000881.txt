Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective
Patrick Bell is a 48-year-old man with approximately 5 years of chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 14 years, and coronary artery disease for 3 years. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. He does not smoke. CKD is not currently under active management.

Objective
Height 167 cm; weight 74.7 kg; BMI 26.8 kg/m².
BP 137/88 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, SpO2 97%.
Recent creatinine 4.5 mg/dL, BUN 81 mg/dL, eGFR 11 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. Currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms. Major risk factors include longstanding type 2 diabetes and coronary artery disease. Requires prompt renal evaluation and active management.

Plan
Arrange urgent nephrology referral for comprehensive CKD evaluation and discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically indicated.
Obtain updated serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and renal ultrasound as part of evaluation.
Review diabetes and blood pressure control; avoid NSAIDs and other potential nephrotoxins.
Educated regarding urgent evaluation for new shortness of breath, edema, markedly decreased urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, or chest pain.
Follow up promptly after nephrology evaluation and repeat laboratory testing.
