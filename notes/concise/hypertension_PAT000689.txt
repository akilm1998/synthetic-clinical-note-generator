Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Lauren Lawson is a 74-year-old woman with a 22-year history of hypertension and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 14 years. She is not currently receiving documented antihypertensive management. She reports intermittent dizziness and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or epistaxis. She does not smoke.

Objective

Height 155 cm; weight 64.4 kg; BMI 26.8 kg/m². Blood pressure 150/93 mmHg, pulse 70 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 99%. Serum sodium 137 mmol/L and potassium 3.7 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled based on office blood pressure of 150/93 mmHg, with associated intermittent dizziness and fatigue. No symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency.

2. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 14 years, increasing overall cardiovascular and renal risk in the setting of uncontrolled hypertension.

3. Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given longstanding hypertension and diabetes. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with review of readings and confirmation of persistent elevation. Discuss initiation of antihypertensive therapy after review of renal function and urine albumin status; an ACE inhibitor or ARB would be reasonable to consider in the setting of diabetes if clinically appropriate. Recommend limiting dietary sodium, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight control, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Obtain renal function, urine albumin assessment, lipid profile, and ECG as part of cardiovascular risk evaluation. Follow up promptly for persistently elevated readings or worsening dizziness; seek urgent care for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, or vision changes.
