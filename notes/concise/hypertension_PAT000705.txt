Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Audrey Jenkins is a 54-year-old woman with a 3-year history of essential hypertension and coronary artery disease for approximately 1 year. Hypertension is currently not being actively managed. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. Nonsmoker. Height 159 cm, weight 69.8 kg, BMI 27.6 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure 161/90 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Serum sodium 147 mmol/L and potassium 4.7 mmol/L. No abnormal physical examination findings provided.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and no associated symptoms. Control is suboptimal and treatment is not currently established. Coronary artery disease is a significant cardiovascular comorbidity. Mild overweight status.

Plan

Discussed importance of active blood pressure management, sodium reduction, regular physical activity, weight reduction, and limiting alcohol if applicable. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring and prompt reassessment to confirm persistent elevation and initiate an appropriate antihypertensive regimen, with consideration of cardiovascular comorbidity. Review medication tolerance and adherence at follow-up. Recommend ECG, lipid profile, urinalysis, and monitoring of renal function and electrolytes as clinically appropriate when treatment is initiated. Seek urgent care for chest pain, severe headache, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly worsening blood pressure.
