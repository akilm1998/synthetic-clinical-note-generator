Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure and dizziness.

Subjective
David Parker is a 61-year-old man with a 16-year history of essential hypertension, currently without an established management plan, presenting with dizziness and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or epistaxis. He does not smoke. Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease and hyperlipidemia for 16 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 13 years.

Objective
Blood pressure 158/99 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%. Height 184 cm, weight 77.9 kg, BMI 23.0 kg/m². Serum sodium 147 mmol/L and potassium 3.5 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with persistently elevated blood pressure and associated dizziness/fatigue. No reported symptoms suggesting hypertensive emergency today. Longstanding cardiovascular risk due to coronary artery disease, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia.

Plan
Discussed need for prompt initiation of a structured antihypertensive treatment plan and close outpatient monitoring, with medication selection guided by diabetes, coronary artery disease, and electrolyte results. Begin home blood pressure monitoring and bring readings to follow-up. Counsel on limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity, and avoiding excess alcohol.

Repeat serum electrolytes and assess renal function before or shortly after treatment initiation; obtain ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as clinically appropriate. Continue follow-up for diabetes, coronary artery disease, and hyperlipidemia. Seek emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, vision changes, or markedly higher blood pressure readings. Recheck in the clinic within 1–2 weeks, sooner for worsening dizziness or new symptoms.
