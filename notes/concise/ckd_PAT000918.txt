Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Frank Montgomery is a 32-year-old male, nonsmoker, 185 cm tall and 99.3 kg (BMI 29.0 kg/m²), presenting for follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No interval disease-related concerns reported.

Relevant comorbidities include hyperlipidemia for 2 years and gout for 1 year.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 136/86 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respirations 14/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Recent laboratory results:
Creatinine 3.33 mg/dL
BUN 94 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 15 mL/min/1.73 m²

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, with markedly reduced renal function on current laboratory testing. Patient is currently asymptomatic without reported fluid overload or uremic symptoms. Disease requires prompt evaluation and active outpatient management.

2. Hyperlipidemia, 2-year history.

3. Gout, 1-year history.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation. Obtain/review urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum electrolytes, CBC, intact PTH, and renal ultrasound as part of CKD evaluation. Repeat renal function testing as clinically indicated.

Review avoidance of NSAIDs and other nephrotoxic agents, medication renal dosing, blood pressure monitoring, and kidney-protective dietary measures. Instruct patient to seek urgent care for worsening shortness of breath, edema, markedly decreased urination, persistent nausea/vomiting, confusion, or other acute change.

Continue outpatient management of hyperlipidemia and gout with attention to renal dosing and avoidance of medications that may worsen kidney function.
