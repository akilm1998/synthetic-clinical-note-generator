Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Henry Walsh is a 53-year-old man with approximately 15 years of chronic kidney disease and a 12-year history of gout. He does not smoke. He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance were reviewed; no active CKD medication regimen is documented for adjustment today.

Objective

Height 163 cm, weight 79.2 kg, BMI 29.8 kg/m². BP 150/85 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respirations 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Creatinine 3.68 mg/dL, eGFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 69 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms. Medication regimen and adherence require reconciliation before assessing effectiveness or making changes.

Gout, longstanding.

Elevated blood pressure reading in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Plan

Complete medication reconciliation, including any gout and over-the-counter medications, with attention to renal dosing, adherence, and adverse effects. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications unless specifically directed.

Recommend close renal follow-up and monitoring with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum electrolytes, CBC, and intact PTH as clinically indicated. Review renal replacement planning if kidney function remains at this level or declines.

Continue dietary sodium moderation, avoid dehydration, and monitor for edema, dyspnea, nausea, worsening fatigue, or changes in urination. Reassess blood pressure and medication needs after reconciliation.
