Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Arthur Nichols is a 76-year-old man with approximately 40 years of hypertension, currently without an established management regimen. He reports intermittent palpitations and fatigue but denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. He continues to smoke. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for 13 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 7 years.

Objective

Blood pressure is 166/105 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, and oxygen saturation 95% on room air. Height is 173 cm, weight 86.2 kg, and BMI 28.8 kg/m². Serum sodium is 147 mmol/L and potassium 3.8 mmol/L.

Assessment

Long-standing essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure and inadequate current management. He has no symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive end-organ injury today, but his chronic kidney disease and diabetes increase cardiovascular and renal risk. Palpitations and fatigue are noted without associated chest pain, dyspnea, or syncope.

Plan

Confirm blood pressure with repeat measurements and home monitoring. Promptly establish an antihypertensive treatment plan appropriate for his chronic kidney disease and diabetes, with monitoring of renal function and serum electrolytes after initiation or adjustment. Recommend ECG for evaluation of palpitations and cardiovascular assessment; lipid profile and urinalysis are also appropriate for risk and end-organ evaluation. Counsel on sodium restriction, weight management, regular activity as tolerated, and smoking cessation. He should seek urgent care for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or visual change. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended for blood pressure reassessment and treatment titration.
