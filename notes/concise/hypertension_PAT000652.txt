Chief Complaint

Initial evaluation of hypertension.

Subjective

Brooke Snyder is a 34-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of hypertension, present for approximately 4 years and currently not under active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, or epistaxis. Reports palpitations. No tobacco use. Weight 89.1 kg, height 180 cm, BMI 27.5 kg/m².

Medical history is significant for coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease, each documented for approximately 1 year. Current medications, allergies, and family history were not provided.

Objective

Vital Signs: BP 164/98 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respirations 17/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory Data: Sodium 137 mmol/L, potassium 4.0 mmol/L.

General examination and cardiopulmonary findings not documented today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and no current management. No symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency at this visit.

Palpitations without associated chest pain, dyspnea, dizziness, or syncope reported.

Known coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease, which increase the importance of prompt blood pressure control and influence medication selection.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Discussed cardiovascular and renal risks of untreated hypertension. Recommend initiation of antihypertensive therapy after medication reconciliation and review of renal function; an ACE inhibitor or ARB may be appropriate given the history of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease if renal function and contraindications permit. Avoid empiric medication changes without confirming current medication and allergy history.

Obtain home blood pressure readings with proper technique and bring log to follow-up. Counsel on reduced sodium intake, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and avoidance of excess alcohol.

Obtain renal function and urine assessment, lipid profile, and ECG as part of hypertension and cardiovascular risk evaluation. ECG is also recommended for assessment of palpitations.

Follow up promptly, preferably within 1–2 weeks, for blood pressure reassessment and treatment adjustment. Seek emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly worsening palpitations.
