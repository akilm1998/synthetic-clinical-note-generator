Chief Complaint

Medication review for type 2 diabetes.

Subjective

Ashley Murray is a 92-year-old woman with a 3-year history of type 2 diabetes, obesity, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, and longstanding anxiety. She is not on a documented glucose-lowering regimen. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling. Medication adherence and tolerance were reviewed; there are no reported medication-related concerns. She continues to smoke.

Objective

Height 163 cm, weight 86.3 kg, BMI 32.5 kg/m². Blood pressure 131/78 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%. HbA1c 9.7%; fasting glucose 258 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently inadequately controlled based on markedly elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose. She is clinically asymptomatic but appears to be without effective active pharmacologic management. Obesity, smoking, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, and advanced age should be considered when selecting therapy and glycemic targets.

Plan

Discussed results and the need for active diabetes treatment. Recommend initiation of an individualized glucose-lowering regimen after review of renal function and overall goals of care, with cautious titration given age. Reinforced diabetic nutrition, portion control, and activity as tolerated. Recommend serum creatinine/eGFR, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and fasting lipid profile to guide treatment and risk reduction. Encourage smoking cessation. Follow up promptly to reassess glucose control, adherence, and treatment tolerance.
