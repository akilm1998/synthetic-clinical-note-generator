Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for uncontrolled blood pressure with dizziness and palpitations.

Subjective

Chase Fuller is a 47-year-old man with a 12-year history of essential hypertension, currently without documented active management. He reports dizziness and intermittent palpitations. Denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, or epistaxis. Known chronic kidney disease for approximately 2 years. Nonsmoker.

Objective

Blood pressure 149/94 mmHg, pulse 68 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%. Height 180 cm, weight 87.2 kg, BMI 26.9 kg/m².

Serum sodium 146 mmol/L; potassium 5.0 mmol/L.

Assessment

Persistent essential hypertension with above-goal office blood pressure and associated dizziness/palpitations. No current symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Chronic kidney disease increases cardiovascular and renal risk. Potassium is borderline high and should be considered when selecting antihypertensive therapy.

Plan

Confirm blood pressure with repeat measurements and home monitoring, including symptom correlation. Recommend prompt initiation or re-establishment of antihypertensive therapy, with agent selection guided by chronic kidney disease and potassium level; avoid empiric escalation of medications that may further increase potassium until renal function and repeat electrolytes are reviewed. Obtain renal function testing and repeat serum electrolytes. Recommend ECG for evaluation of palpitations. Counsel on sodium reduction, regular physical activity, weight control, and limiting alcohol. Seek emergency care for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly worsening blood pressure. Close outpatient follow-up after monitoring and laboratory review.
