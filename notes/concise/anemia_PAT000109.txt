Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Joshua Ward is a 53-year-old man with a 14-year history of anemia, chronic kidney disease for 8 years, malignancy for 5 years, and chronic infection for 16 years. He reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or pallor. Nonsmoker. Current weight 69.2 kg, height 177 cm, BMI 22.1 kg/m². Anemia has not been actively managed.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 132/82 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respirations 19/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Recent laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 9.5 g/dL; hematocrit 27.8%; RBC 3.16 million/µL; MCV 88 fL. Serum iron 39 µg/dL, ferritin 263 ng/mL, TIBC 195 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 20%.

Assessment

Chronic, asymptomatic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation in the setting of chronic kidney disease, malignancy, and chronic infection. Low serum iron and TIBC with preserved ferritin support inflammatory iron restriction. Hemoglobin is reduced but patient is hemodynamically stable without cardiopulmonary symptoms.

Plan

Arrange prompt outpatient evaluation with repeat CBC, reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, renal function testing, inflammatory markers, vitamin B12, and folate levels.

Review anemia management in the context of chronic kidney disease, malignancy, and chronic infection; coordinate care with the clinicians managing these conditions. Consider iron assessment and treatment strategy based on the complete evaluation rather than empiric iron alone.

Provide urgent precautions for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, bleeding, or marked weakness. Close follow-up after laboratory review.
