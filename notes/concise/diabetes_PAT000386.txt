Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective
Justin Richardson is an 80-year-old man with a 10-year history of type 2 diabetes, currently without reported diabetes-related symptoms. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the extremities. Diabetes control has not been actively managed. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for 37 years and dyslipidemia for 21 years. He does not smoke.

Objective
Height 161 cm; weight 67.7 kg; BMI 26.1 kg/m².
BP 132/87 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.
HbA1c 9.2%; fasting glucose 196 mg/dL.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently suboptimally controlled based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. Despite biochemical hyperglycemia, he reports no polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual symptoms, infectious complications, or neuropathic symptoms. Chronic kidney disease and dyslipidemia are important comorbidities when selecting and intensifying therapy.

Plan
Review current diabetes treatment history, adherence, diet, and home glucose monitoring with the patient.
Recommend individualized intensification of glucose-lowering therapy, taking age and chronic kidney disease into account; verify renal function before selecting or adjusting medications.
Counsel regarding carbohydrate-conscious nutrition, regular physical activity as tolerated, and hypoglycemia precautions if therapy is initiated or intensified.
Recommend monitoring renal status, including serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and ongoing lipid and cardiovascular risk management.
Continue routine diabetic foot and eye surveillance.
Follow up after treatment review and repeat glycemic assessment.
