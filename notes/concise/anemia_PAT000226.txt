Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective
Connor Walters is a 72-year-old man with a 26-year history of anemia in the setting of longstanding inflammatory bowel disease and malignancy. He reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, pallor, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. Anemia has not been actively managed. He continues to smoke. Height 184 cm, weight 96.5 kg, BMI 28.5 kg/m².

Objective
Vital signs: BP 127/81 mmHg, pulse 92 bpm, respirations 19/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 9.4 g/dL, hematocrit 27.5%, RBC 3.16 million/µL, MCV 87 fL. Serum iron 49 µg/dL, ferritin 313 ng/mL, TIBC 269 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 18.2%.

Assessment
Chronic normocytic anemia, consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the context of longstanding inflammatory bowel disease and malignancy. Hemoglobin remains significantly reduced, although he is currently asymptomatic and hemodynamically stable. Elevated ferritin with low serum iron and relatively low TIBC supports inflammatory iron restriction rather than straightforward iron deficiency.

Plan
Obtain repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers to reassess contributors to the anemia. Review current inflammatory bowel disease and malignancy activity with the treating clinicians. Consider hematology referral for persistent hemoglobin around 9 g/dL and guidance regarding iron therapy or erythropoiesis-stimulating treatment if indicated after evaluation. Counsel regarding smoking cessation. Advise prompt evaluation for new dyspnea, chest pain, palpitations, dizziness, bleeding, or worsening exercise tolerance. Continue outpatient follow-up after reassessment.
