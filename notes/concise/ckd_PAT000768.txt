Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective
Hazel Fisher is an 87-year-old woman with an 18-year history of chronic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 44 years, presenting for routine follow-up. She reports persistent fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. She continues to smoke. No current medication regimen is documented.

Objective
Height 163 cm, weight 76.5 kg, BMI 28.8 kg/m². BP 147/85 mmHg, pulse 80/min, respirations 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air. Creatinine 4.39 mg/dL, BUN 72 mg/dL, estimated GFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine, currently with fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, volume overload, dyspnea, or urinary change. Longstanding diabetes and suboptimally controlled blood pressure are important risk factors. Renal disease appears inadequately managed and requires expedited reassessment.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, with renal disease.

Tobacco use.

Plan
Arrange expedited nephrology referral for evaluation of advanced chronic kidney disease, renal replacement therapy planning, and goals-of-care discussion appropriate to age and comorbidity burden.

Recommend serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as part of CKD reassessment. Review renal dosing and avoid NSAIDs and other nephrotoxins.

Perform medication reconciliation and optimize blood pressure and diabetes management with attention to renal function. Counsel on smoking cessation and avoidance of excess dietary sodium.

Educated regarding urgent evaluation for worsening fatigue, nausea/vomiting, confusion, chest pain, increasing shortness of breath, edema, or markedly decreased urine output.
