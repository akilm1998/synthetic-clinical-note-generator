Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective
Anthony Cooper is a 64-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 23 years. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. History is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 21 years, secondary hyperparathyroidism for 3 years, and anemia of chronic disease for 12 years. He does not smoke.

Objective
Height 170 cm, weight 82.4 kg, BMI 28.5 kg/m². Blood pressure 146/89 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respirations 19/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, eGFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 75 mg/dL.

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. Despite absence of uremic or volume-overload symptoms today, renal disease is not currently under active management. Comorbid type 2 diabetes, secondary hyperparathyroidism, and anemia of chronic disease increase renal risk. Blood pressure remains above goal.

Plan
Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning. Obtain/review serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate. Monitor blood pressure and renal symptoms closely. Counsel on avoiding NSAIDs and other potential nephrotoxins, maintaining appropriate dietary sodium and renal diet measures, and seeking urgent care for new shortness of breath, swelling, persistent nausea/vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or markedly decreased urination. Continue close primary care follow-up.
