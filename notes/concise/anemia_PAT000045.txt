Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of longstanding anemia.

Subjective

Matthew Reed is a 69-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. Anemia has been present for approximately 29 years and is currently untreated. He denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, pallor, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. Relevant chronic conditions include chronic kidney disease for 8 years and chronic infection for 5 years. He does not smoke.

Objective

Height 176 cm, weight 76.2 kg, BMI 24.6 kg/m². BP 142/77 mmHg, pulse 87 bpm, respirations 17/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL, hematocrit 25.6%, RBC 2.88 million/µL, MCV 89 fL. Serum iron 39 µg/dL, ferritin 466 ng/mL, TIBC 238 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 16.4%.

Assessment

Longstanding normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic kidney disease. Hemoglobin is significantly reduced, with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and reduced TIBC supporting an inflammatory pattern rather than uncomplicated iron deficiency. Patient is currently asymptomatic and hemodynamically stable.

Chronic kidney disease and chronic infection require ongoing monitoring and may be contributing to the anemia.

Annual preventive health review.

Plan

Obtain updated CBC with reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, renal function testing, inflammatory markers, vitamin B12, and folate to further characterize the anemia. Reassess iron indices and evaluate for additional contributors if anemia worsens or becomes symptomatic.

Review management of chronic kidney disease and chronic infection. Consider nephrology involvement regarding anemia-directed therapy, including erythropoiesis-stimulating treatment or intravenous iron, based on renal function, repeat studies, and overall clinical assessment. Avoid empiric iron treatment without reassessing the iron profile.

Repeat blood count after evaluation and arrange closer follow-up given hemoglobin of 8.6 g/dL. Seek urgent care for new dyspnea, chest pain, syncope, palpitations, or marked weakness.

Continue routine age-appropriate preventive care, including immunization review, cancer screening, blood pressure monitoring, and fall-risk assessment.
