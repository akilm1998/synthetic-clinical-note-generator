Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Nora Kelly is a 97-year-old woman with a 29-year history of essential hypertension, currently without documented active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 19 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 3 years. She does not smoke.

Objective

Blood pressure 163/89 mmHg; pulse 76 bpm; respiratory rate 18/min; temperature 36.8°C; oxygen saturation 95%. Height 180 cm, weight 68.4 kg, BMI 21.1 kg/m². Serum sodium 139 mmol/L and potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension with persistently elevated reading, asymptomatic. Given prolonged duration, lack of active management, advanced age, diabetes, and coronary artery disease, prompt treatment assessment is indicated. No features of hypertensive emergency are reported today.

2. Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

3. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 3-year duration.

Plan

Arrange prompt clinician-directed initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy, with consideration of age, coronary disease, diabetes, renal function, orthostasis, and fall risk. Confirm blood pressure with repeat office or home measurements and monitor for postural symptoms. Review adherence and lifestyle contributors, including sodium intake and physical activity as tolerated. Recommend ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular risk and hypertension assessment. Recheck serum electrolytes and renal function after treatment changes. Seek emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or visual changes.
