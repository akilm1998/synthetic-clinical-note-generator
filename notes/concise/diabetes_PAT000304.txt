Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Ruby Marshall is an 86-year-old woman with a 46-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, currently not under active management. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. Reports tingling in the hands or feet. She also has an 11-year history of dyslipidemia. She continues to smoke.

Objective

Height 180 cm; weight 82.6 kg; BMI 25.5 kg/m².
Blood pressure 131/78 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.
HbA1c 9.2%; fasting glucose 247 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, poorly controlled based on HbA1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 247 mg/dL. Although she has no osmotic or constitutional symptoms, she reports distal tingling concerning for possible early peripheral neuropathy.

2. Dyslipidemia, longstanding, requiring cardiovascular risk reduction in the setting of diabetes and tobacco use.

3. Tobacco use. Continued smoking increases vascular and diabetes-related risk.

Plan

Discussed elevated glucose values and the importance of active diabetes management. Review and initiate an individualized glucose-lowering regimen, with attention to renal function, hypoglycemia risk, frailty, and treatment goals appropriate for her age. Encourage consistent nutrition, avoidance of concentrated sugars, and regular activity as tolerated.

Obtain renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before finalizing medication selection; monitor HbA1c and fasting glucose after treatment is established. Recommend routine diabetic foot examination and dilated eye examination. Counsel regarding daily foot care and prompt evaluation of wounds or new sensory changes.

Review lipid management and cardiovascular risk reduction. Strongly encourage smoking cessation. Follow up after treatment initiation to reassess glycemic control and tolerance.
