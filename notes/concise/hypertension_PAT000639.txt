Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Matthew Reed is a 78-year-old man with a 10-year history of essential hypertension, currently without documented antihypertensive management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. He is a nonsmoker. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for 29 years, hyperlipidemia for 22 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 16 years.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 172/98 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Height 167 cm, weight 73.3 kg, BMI 26.3 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 140 mmol/L, potassium 3.7 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, inadequately controlled. Blood pressure is markedly elevated today, without reported symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive complications. Longstanding hypertension in the setting of chronic kidney disease and diabetes confers increased cardiovascular and renal risk.

2. Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, with need for renal-conscious blood pressure management.

3. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

4. Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure after rest and confirm with home readings if feasible. Arrange prompt outpatient reassessment for initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy, with consideration of renal and diabetic comorbidities. Review dietary sodium intake, physical activity, and adherence barriers. Obtain renal function assessment and urinalysis as clinically appropriate before finalizing therapy. Counsel to seek urgent care for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, or visual changes. Continue coordinated management of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and hyperlipidemia.
