Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Michelle Cross is a 48-year-old woman with a 17-year history of chronic kidney disease and hyperlipidemia for 6 years, presenting for routine follow-up. She reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. She smokes. No current CKD management regimen is documented.

Objective

Height 160 cm, weight 59.6 kg, BMI 23.3 kg/m². Blood pressure 139/91 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Serum creatinine 4.99 mg/dL, BUN 81 mg/dL, estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function after a prolonged disease course. Despite absence of reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, current renal indices are concerning and require prompt specialist evaluation. Blood pressure is mildly elevated. Hyperlipidemia and active tobacco use increase cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology referral for evaluation and management planning. Recommend serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as part of CKD assessment. Review renal-protective measures, including avoidance of NSAIDs and other nephrotoxins, appropriate dietary sodium and protein intake, and monitoring for reduced urine output, worsening edema, dyspnea, persistent nausea, confusion, or severe weakness; seek urgent care if these develop. Address blood pressure and hyperlipidemia at follow-up. Strongly counsel smoking cessation. Close outpatient follow-up after nephrology evaluation.
