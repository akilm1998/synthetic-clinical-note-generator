Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly reduced kidney function.

Subjective

Ryan Hughes is a 45-year-old man with approximately 6 years of chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 14 years, gout for 3 years, and coronary artery disease for 1 year. He reports fatigue. Denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, leg swelling, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. He smokes. No current medication regimen provided.

Objective

Height 171 cm, weight 79.2 kg, BMI 27.1 kg/m². BP 142/84 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Creatinine 3.53 mg/dL, eGFR 15 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 62 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and azotemia, currently associated with fatigue but without reported volume-overload or overt uremic symptoms. Etiologies likely include longstanding diabetes and hypertension; gout and coronary artery disease are additional comorbidities. Blood pressure remains above goal. CKD is not currently under active management.

Plan

Urgent nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation and management planning.

Obtain repeat serum electrolytes, renal function, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone; consider renal ultrasound as part of CKD evaluation.

Review and optimize diabetes and blood pressure control while avoiding nephrotoxic medications, including NSAIDs. Medication reconciliation is needed before initiating or adjusting therapy.

Counseled to seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, edema, markedly decreased urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness. Smoking cessation strongly encouraged.
