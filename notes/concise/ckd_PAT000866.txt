Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and abnormal renal function.

Subjective
Richard Lawson is a 94-year-old man with approximately 41-year history of chronic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 44 years. He is a nonsmoker. Denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath.

Objective
Height 169 cm, weight 83.4 kg, BMI 29.2 kg/m². BP 144/89 mmHg, pulse 71/min, respirations 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: creatinine 5.6 mg/dL, BUN 86 mg/dL, eGFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function in the setting of longstanding diabetes. Currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms. Blood pressure mildly elevated. Renal disease is not currently under active management.

Plan
Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for assessment and discussion of renal replacement planning. Recommend prompt repeat renal function and electrolyte testing, with CBC and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio as clinically indicated. Consider renal ultrasound as part of evaluation. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents; review all prescription and over-the-counter medications with a clinician. Seek emergency care for new shortness of breath, swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or worsening weakness. Close outpatient follow-up after nephrology assessment.
