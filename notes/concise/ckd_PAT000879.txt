Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Sophia Turner is a 44-year-old woman, 180 cm, 83.3 kg, BMI 25.7 kg/m², presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 12 years ago. She reports fatigue. Denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. Current medication regimen was not provided. She smokes. Comorbidities include anemia of chronic disease for 4 years, gout for 1 year, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 1 year.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 140/81 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respirations 19/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Laboratory results: serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, eGFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 67 mg/dL.

No edema or other abnormal physical findings were provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction and known anemia of chronic disease. Although she currently denies nausea, pruritus, fluid overload symptoms, or urinary changes, the renal indices require urgent specialist assessment. Associated gout and secondary hyperparathyroidism noted.

Plan

Recommend same-day urgent evaluation, preferably through nephrology or the emergency department, given creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 67 mg/dL, and eGFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m². Assess for electrolyte or acid-base complications, volume status, uremic symptoms, and need for renal replacement therapy.

Recommend serum electrolytes, repeat renal function testing, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone testing. Renal ultrasound is also recommended if not previously completed.

Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications; review all prescription and over-the-counter medications with a clinician before use. Counsel regarding smoking cessation and blood pressure control. Seek emergency care immediately for worsening fatigue, nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, shortness of breath, new swelling, or markedly decreased urination.
