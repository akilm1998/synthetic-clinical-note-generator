Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly abnormal cholesterol levels.

Subjective

Madelyn Stewart is a 78-year-old woman with a 23-year history of hyperlipidemia, currently without active lipid-lowering management. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She continues to smoke. Comorbidities include hypertension for 21 years, chronic kidney disease for 11 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 5 years.

Objective

Height 160 cm, weight 59.4 kg, BMI 23.2 kg/m². Blood pressure 160/89 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 339 mg/dL, LDL-C 231 mg/dL, HDL-C 37 mg/dL, triglycerides 356 mg/dL.

Assessment

Severe mixed dyslipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and triglycerides and low HDL-C, untreated. Her age, active tobacco use, hypertension, chronic kidney disease, and prolonged duration of dyslipidemia confer high atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk despite absence of current ischemic symptoms.

Hypertension with elevated office blood pressure today.

Chronic kidney disease and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, relevant to lipid-lowering treatment selection and monitoring.

Plan

Discuss initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy, with statin therapy strongly indicated unless contraindicated or not tolerated. Review prior treatment history, potential adverse effects, renal and hepatic considerations, and adherence before selecting and titrating therapy; consider addition of nonstatin therapy if LDL-C remains substantially elevated.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid assessment after treatment initiation or adjustment, with appropriate renal and hepatic monitoring. Consider ApoB and Lp(a) measurement for risk refinement. Further cardiovascular risk assessment, including consideration of coronary artery calcium imaging, may be discussed if treatment decisions remain uncertain.

Reinforce smoking cessation, reduction of saturated and trans fats, increased dietary fiber, and regular physical activity as tolerated.

Address blood pressure promptly with primary care follow-up and home monitoring. Advise urgent evaluation for new chest discomfort, dyspnea, focal neurologic symptoms, or symptomatic severe hypertension.
