Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective
Brooklyn Barnes is a 41-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 5 years ago. She reports no headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Hypertension is not currently under active management, and no medication regimen is documented. She is a nonsmoker. History is also notable for hyperlipidemia for 11 years.

Objective
Blood pressure 154/92 mmHg; pulse 74 bpm; respiratory rate 17/min; temperature 36.9°C; oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Height 178 cm, weight 91.9 kg, BMI 29.0 kg/m². Serum sodium 147 mmol/L and potassium 4.5 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure, currently asymptomatic and not adequately controlled. Mildly elevated serum sodium. Hyperlipidemia. Overweight by BMI.

Plan
Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with documented readings and review of technique. Counsel on sodium reduction, heart-healthy diet, regular physical activity, weight reduction, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Recommend prompt follow-up to review out-of-office readings and discuss initiation of antihypertensive therapy. Consider evaluation with renal function, urinalysis, ECG, and lipid profile as clinically appropriate. Continue cardiovascular risk-factor modification for hyperlipidemia. Return sooner for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure.
