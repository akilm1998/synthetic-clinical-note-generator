Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for poorly controlled type 2 diabetes.

Subjective

Bob Hill is a 66-year-old man with a 23-year history of type 2 diabetes that is currently not actively managed. Recent HbA1c is 9.4% and fasting glucose is 216 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, fatigue, weight loss, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. Reports numbness and tingling in the hands and feet. Nonsmoker. History also includes anxiety for 42 years and depression for 13 years.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 130/75 mmHg, pulse 68 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 160 cm, weight 58.1 kg, BMI 22.7 kg/m².

Laboratory data: HbA1c 9.4%; fasting glucose 216 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus with significant hyperglycemia. Long-standing disease with inadequate current management. No reported polyuria, polydipsia, visual symptoms, constitutional symptoms, or infectious complications.

2. Distal numbness and tingling of hands and feet, concerning for diabetes-associated peripheral sensory symptoms. No documented examination findings to establish a complication.

3. Chronic anxiety and depression.

Plan

Discussed prompt re-engagement in diabetes care and initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy based on comprehensive medication and renal review. Recommend home glucose monitoring with documentation of fasting and postprandial values, diabetic nutrition counseling, and regular physical activity as tolerated.

Obtain renal function with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and fasting lipid profile as part of diabetes risk assessment. Perform comprehensive foot examination and arrange retinal screening. Counsel regarding daily foot inspection and prompt evaluation of wounds or infection.

Follow up promptly for review of glucose readings and treatment response; seek urgent care for vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, inability to maintain hydration, or rapidly worsening hyperglycemic symptoms.
