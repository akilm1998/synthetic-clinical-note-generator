Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective
Vanessa Warren is a 90-year-old woman with a 51-year history of hyperlipidemia, coronary artery disease, hypertension, and type 2 diabetes mellitus. She does not smoke. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, or reported xanthomas. Xanthelasma is present. No lipid-lowering treatment is documented.

Objective
Height 168 cm; weight 65.8 kg; BMI 23.3 kg/m².
BP 153/88 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.
Lipid profile: total cholesterol 350 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 239 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 48 mg/dL, triglycerides 315 mg/dL.

Assessment
Severe untreated mixed hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and hypertriglyceridemia in the setting of established coronary artery disease, diabetes, and hypertension. Xanthelasma supports longstanding dyslipidemia. She is currently without reported ischemic or claudication symptoms.

Plan
Discuss prompt initiation of lipid-lowering therapy, balancing expected cardiovascular benefit, tolerability, comorbidity burden, and patient preferences at age 90; statin therapy is strongly indicated given coronary artery disease and LDL 239 mg/dL, with consideration of additional therapy if response is inadequate or statin intolerance occurs.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid evaluation after treatment initiation or adjustment and assessment for treatment-related adverse effects. Reinforce a heart-healthy diet low in saturated and trans fats, regular activity as tolerated, and continued tobacco avoidance. Coordinate management of concomitant hypertension and diabetes. Advise urgent evaluation for new chest discomfort, dyspnea, syncope, or exertional leg symptoms.
