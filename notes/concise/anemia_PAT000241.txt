Chief Complaint
Initial consultation for chronic anemia.

Subjective
Nathaniel Gibson is a 36-year-old man, 188 cm, 86.6 kg (BMI 24.5 kg/m²), nonsmoker, presenting for evaluation of anemia reportedly present for approximately 11 years. He denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, palpitations, shortness of breath, reduced exercise tolerance, or other reported anemia-related symptoms. No current anemia-directed treatment is documented.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for approximately 1 year and heart failure for approximately 1 year. No additional medication, allergy, surgical, hospitalization, family, or social history is provided.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 128/82 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 24/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.2 g/dL
Hematocrit 24.8%
RBC 2.82 million/µL
MCV 88 fL
Serum iron 24 µg/dL
Ferritin 464 ng/mL
TIBC 266 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 9.0%

Assessment
Chronic normocytic anemia with significant reduction in hemoglobin and hematocrit. Low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC are most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation, likely contributed to by chronic kidney disease and/or heart failure. Iron-restricted erythropoiesis is present despite preserved iron stores. He is currently hemodynamically stable and denies cardiopulmonary symptoms, although respiratory rate is mildly elevated at 24/min.

Plan
Obtain CBC with reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the anemia and assess contributing chronic disease.

Review kidney function and heart failure status, including current disease-directed treatment, at follow-up. Consider nephrology and/or hematology consultation given hemoglobin of 8.2 g/dL and the long duration of anemia.

Discuss iron replacement strategy after reviewing the additional evaluation; oral or intravenous iron may be considered based on renal function, inflammatory burden, tolerance, and response. An erythropoiesis-stimulating agent may be considered in coordination with renal specialists if anemia is attributable to chronic kidney disease and other reversible causes are excluded.

Advised to seek urgent care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, palpitations, syncope, marked weakness, or bleeding. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review results and establish a coordinated treatment plan.
