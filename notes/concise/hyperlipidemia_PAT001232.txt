Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia.

Subjective
Nicole Harper is a 94-year-old woman seen for follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 24 years and currently untreated. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. She continues to smoke. Comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 25 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 68 years. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective
Height 152 cm, weight 47.4 kg, BMI 20.5 kg/m². BP 131/77 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent lipid panel: total cholesterol 313 mg/dL, LDL-C 209 mg/dL, HDL-C 41 mg/dL, triglycerides 314 mg/dL.

Assessment
Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and elevated triglycerides, currently untreated. No reported cardiovascular or cutaneous symptoms. Advanced age, diabetes, and ongoing tobacco use increase overall cardiovascular risk. History of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease noted.

Plan
Discussed lipid results and cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of statin therapy, taking into account age, diabetes, comorbid liver disease, functional status, and patient preferences. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors and repeat fasting lipid panel after any treatment or lifestyle change. Counsel on reducing saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates, maintaining activity as tolerated, and smoking cessation. Continue routine management of diabetes and fatty liver disease with the patient’s care team. Follow up after treatment decision and updated lipid assessment.
