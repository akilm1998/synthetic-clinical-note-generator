Chief Complaint

Initial evaluation of hypertension.

Subjective

Scott Douglas is a 75-year-old man presenting for new-patient evaluation of hypertension, present for approximately 8 years and currently untreated. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Relevant history includes chronic kidney disease for 19 years, hyperlipidemia for 45 years, and coronary artery disease for 8 years. He currently smokes. No medication list or allergy history was provided.

Objective

Blood pressure 162/95 mmHg. Pulse 78 bpm. Respiratory rate 14/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 97%. Height 161 cm, weight 71.8 kg, BMI 27.7 kg/m².

Serum sodium 147 mmol/L and potassium 3.9 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently untreated, with markedly elevated office blood pressure today. He is asymptomatic without features suggesting acute hypertensive emergency. Chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease increase his cardiovascular and renal risk.

2. Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Potassium is 3.9 mmol/L; sodium is mildly elevated at 147 mmol/L. Antihypertensive selection should account for renal function and coronary disease.

3. Coronary artery disease, present for approximately 8 years, increasing the importance of sustained blood pressure control.

4. Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

5. Tobacco use.

6. Overweight, BMI 27.7 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the need for prompt treatment of persistently elevated blood pressure and reduction of cardiovascular and renal risk. Confirm home readings with a validated upper-arm cuff, maintain a blood pressure log, and bring readings to follow-up. Recommend a low-sodium diet, avoidance of excess alcohol, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and smoking cessation.

Review prior records and reconcile medications before initiating an antihypertensive regimen; renal function and electrolytes should be reviewed for safe agent selection and monitoring. Recommend ECG, urinalysis, lipid profile, and renal assessment as part of cardiovascular and hypertension evaluation. Continue risk-factor management for coronary artery disease and hyperlipidemia.

Seek emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or acute visual change. Follow up promptly, within 1–2 weeks, for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning.
