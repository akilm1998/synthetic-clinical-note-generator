Chief Complaint

Annual comprehensive review and chronic kidney disease follow-up.

Subjective

Margaret Walsh is an 89-year-old woman, 165 cm, 62.1 kg (BMI 22.8 kg/m²), nonsmoker, with a 16-year history of chronic kidney disease. She reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. Comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 32 years, coronary artery disease for 31 years, and anemia of chronic disease for 21 years. No medication list is provided.

Objective

Blood pressure 151/87 mmHg, pulse 82/min, respirations 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Creatinine 4.88 mg/dL, BUN 76 mg/dL, eGFR 11 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and substantially elevated BUN/creatinine. Despite absence of reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, renal function warrants prompt specialist assessment and active management. Hypertension is above goal today. Longstanding diabetes, coronary artery disease, and anemia of chronic disease increase overall risk.

Preventive care and chronic disease review completed; smoking remains absent.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for evaluation of advanced CKD, treatment planning, and discussion of renal replacement therapy versus conservative goals of care in the context of age and comorbidity.

Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum electrolytes, CBC, intact PTH, and renal ultrasound as part of CKD assessment. Review renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic medications when the medication list is available.

Reassess blood pressure and coordinate management of diabetes, coronary artery disease, and anemia with the treating clinicians.

Review immunizations, fall prevention, nutrition, bone health, and individualized age-appropriate cancer screening. Discuss advance care planning and health-care preferences with the patient and family.

Follow up promptly after nephrology evaluation or sooner for dyspnea, edema, vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, or significant change in urination.
