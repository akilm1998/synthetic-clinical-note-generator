Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective
Vanessa Warren is a 77-year-old woman seen for follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 11 years. She reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. Chronic anemia of disease is also noted. She currently smokes. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective
Height 170 cm; weight 75.1 kg; BMI 26.0 kg/m². Blood pressure 149/90 mmHg, pulse 87 bpm, respirations 19/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 93 mg/dL, eGFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significantly elevated creatinine and BUN. Although currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, disease is not under active management and requires urgent specialist evaluation. Elevated blood pressure today. Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding. Tobacco use.

Plan
Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning. Obtain repeat renal function and serum electrolytes promptly, along with CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate. Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications when available; avoid NSAIDs and other potential nephrotoxins. Counsel on smoking cessation and blood pressure monitoring. Seek emergency care for new shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, severe weakness, rapidly worsening swelling, or markedly decreased urine output. Close follow-up after laboratory reassessment and nephrology consultation.
