Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective
Sarah Griffin is a 66-year-old woman seen for follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 9 years ago. She is not currently receiving documented management for blood pressure. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Comorbid conditions include coronary artery disease for 11 years and hyperlipidemia for 9 years. She does not smoke.

Objective
Blood pressure 167/93 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%. Height 151 cm, weight 64.3 kg, BMI 28.2 kg/m². Serum sodium 136 mmol/L and potassium 3.8 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with significantly elevated office reading and inadequate current management, without reported symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency. Coronary artery disease and hyperlipidemia increase overall cardiovascular risk. Overweight by BMI.

Plan
Discussed importance of blood pressure control, reduced sodium intake, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log and confirmation of readings. Given the degree of elevation and history of coronary artery disease, recommend initiation of antihypertensive therapy after medication and contraindication review. Consider ECG, urinalysis, lipid profile, and repeat serum electrolytes as part of cardiovascular risk and treatment assessment. Follow up promptly for blood pressure review and medication management; seek emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly worsening blood pressure.
