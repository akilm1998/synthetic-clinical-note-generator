Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective
Harper Bennett is a 37-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 14 years and not currently actively managed. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. She smokes. Medical history is significant for coronary artery disease for 1 year, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 3 years, and chronic kidney disease for 1 year.

Objective
Height 179 cm; weight 69.8 kg; BMI 21.8 kg/m². Blood pressure 151/97 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%. Serum electrolytes: sodium 137 mmol/L, potassium 4.1 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure, currently asymptomatic and not actively managed. Increased cardiovascular and renal risk in the setting of coronary artery disease, type 2 diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and tobacco use.

Plan
Repeat blood pressure measurement and begin home blood pressure monitoring with a written log. Arrange prompt follow-up to establish antihypertensive therapy appropriate for her coronary artery disease, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease; review renal function and urine testing before or during treatment planning. Counsel on sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and smoking cessation. Continue coordination of care for diabetes, coronary artery disease, and chronic kidney disease. Seek emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly worsening blood pressure.
