Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Grant Porter is a 75-year-old man with a 33-year history of chronic kidney disease, longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus for 31 years, hypertension for 13 years, and gout for 33 years. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. Current medication regimen is not provided. He continues to smoke.

Objective

Height 160 cm, weight 60.4 kg, BMI 23.6 kg/m². Blood pressure 156/91 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Recent laboratory results: creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, eGFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 67 mg/dL. No additional examination findings documented.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. Despite absence of reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms, disease control is poor and renal function requires prompt specialist assessment.

Hypertension, currently above goal at today’s visit.

Longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Chronic gout.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for advanced kidney disease assessment and discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate.

Obtain serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as part of CKD evaluation.

Review all medications and supplements for renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents; do not use NSAIDs unless specifically directed.

Reassess and optimize blood pressure and diabetes management with renal considerations. Counsel on sodium restriction, avoidance of dehydration, and smoking cessation.

Educate regarding urgent evaluation for nausea or vomiting, worsening fatigue, confusion, progressive edema, shortness of breath, chest pain, or significant change in urination.
