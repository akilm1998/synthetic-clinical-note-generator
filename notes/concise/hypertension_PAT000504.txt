Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Savannah Bell is a 42-year-old woman, 152 cm, 57.1 kg (BMI 24.7 kg/m²), with a 3-year history of hypertension, chronic kidney disease for 2 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 9 years. She reports intermittent dizziness and fatigue. Denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or epistaxis. She does not smoke. Hypertension has not been actively managed.

Objective

BP 165/101 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respirations 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 147 mmol/L, potassium 5.0 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and associated dizziness/fatigue; currently inadequately controlled. No symptoms reported to suggest acute hypertensive emergency.

Chronic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus increase cardiovascular and renal risk. Potassium is borderline elevated at 5.0 mmol/L, which is relevant to antihypertensive selection.

Plan

Promptly initiate or reestablish antihypertensive management after review of renal function and current medication history; avoid empiric medication selection without accounting for potassium and chronic kidney disease. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log and dietary sodium restriction.

Obtain/monitor renal function and repeat electrolytes; consider ECG, urinalysis, and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular and renal risk assessment. Reinforce diabetes and blood pressure control, regular physical activity, and avoidance of tobacco. Seek urgent care for chest pain, dyspnea, severe headache, acute visual changes, neurologic symptoms, or markedly worsening blood pressure. Close outpatient follow-up recommended.
