Chief Complaint
Initial consultation for elevated cholesterol.

Subjective
Connor Walters is a 43-year-old man presenting for evaluation of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 3 years and currently untreated. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 310 mg/dL, LDL-C 216 mg/dL, HDL-C 38 mg/dL, and triglycerides 278 mg/dL. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. He does not smoke. History is also notable for hypertension for 4 years.

Objective
Height 168 cm, weight 72.5 kg, BMI 25.7 kg/m². Blood pressure 135/92 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air. Lipid panel as above. No additional examination findings documented.

Assessment
Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia and low HDL-C. No reported cardiovascular or cutaneous symptoms. LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL warrants pharmacologic risk reduction independent of calculated 10-year risk. Hypertension with mildly elevated diastolic pressure.

Plan
Discussed cardiovascular risk associated with untreated dyslipidemia and lifestyle measures, including reduction of saturated and trans fats, increased dietary fiber, regular aerobic activity, weight maintenance, and limiting refined carbohydrates and alcohol.

Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after shared decision-making, with review of potential adverse effects and adherence. Repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors and risk refinement with ApoB and lipoprotein(a), as clinically appropriate; assess family history of premature cardiovascular disease.

Continue outpatient blood pressure monitoring and reinforce dietary sodium reduction and regular exercise. Follow up after repeat lipid testing to review response and finalize ongoing management.
