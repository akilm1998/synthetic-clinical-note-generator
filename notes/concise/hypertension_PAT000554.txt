Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective
Aria Patterson is a 97-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of essential hypertension, diagnosed approximately 2 years ago and not currently under active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. She does not smoke. Medical history is notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 23 years, hyperlipidemia for 23 years, and chronic kidney disease for 46 years.

Objective
Height 152 cm; weight 64.0 kg; BMI 27.7 kg/m². Blood pressure 174/101 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96%. Serum sodium 143 mmol/L and potassium 4.2 mmol/L. No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure and inadequate current management, currently asymptomatic without features suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Advanced age, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and hyperlipidemia confer increased cardiovascular and renal risk. Electrolytes are within the provided reference context.

Plan
Repeat blood pressure after seated rest and obtain additional readings, including consideration of orthostatic measurements. Discuss prompt initiation of an individualized antihypertensive regimen, with cautious titration given age and chronic kidney disease. Recommend review of renal function and urinalysis, ECG, and lipid profile as clinically appropriate. Counsel on sodium reduction, weight management, and regular activity as tolerated. Advise immediate emergency evaluation for chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic deficits, severe headache, visual change, or persistent substantially worsening blood pressure. Close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment adjustment.
