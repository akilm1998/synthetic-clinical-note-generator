Chief Complaint
Initial evaluation of elevated blood pressure.

Subjective
Lauren Lawson is a 46-year-old woman presenting as a new patient for evaluation of hypertension, present for approximately 3 years and not currently under active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. Past medical history is notable for coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She does not smoke.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 159/90 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respirations 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.
Height 152 cm; weight 62.8 kg; BMI 27.2 kg/m².
Serum electrolytes: sodium 135 mmol/L, potassium 4.8 mmol/L.
No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms reported.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with above-goal office blood pressure, currently untreated/not actively managed. No symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency today.
Coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago.
Overweight by BMI.

Plan
Discussed importance of blood pressure control, particularly in the setting of coronary artery disease. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log and review of technique. Recheck blood pressure promptly and discuss initiation of antihypertensive therapy; medication selection should account for her coronary artery disease and electrolyte results.

Counseled on reduced-sodium diet, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Recommend ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular risk and hypertension evaluation. Continue routine management of coronary artery disease.

Seek emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Follow up in clinic within 2–4 weeks for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning.
