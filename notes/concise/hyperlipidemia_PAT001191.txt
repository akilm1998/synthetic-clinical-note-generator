Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Christopher Perry is an 87-year-old man, 188 cm and 134.3 kg, with a 50-year history of hyperlipidemia and longstanding obesity. His lipid disorder has not been actively managed. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, and dizziness. He has xanthelasma but no reported xanthomas. He continues to smoke.

Objective

Blood pressure 129/76 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 95%. BMI 38.0 kg/m². Recent lipid panel: total cholesterol 301 mg/dL, LDL 202 mg/dL, HDL 44 mg/dL, and triglycerides 275 mg/dL.

Assessment

Severe, inadequately controlled hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL and elevated triglycerides. Xanthelasma is present. Additional cardiovascular risk factors include advanced age, active tobacco use, and obesity.

Plan

Discussed the substantially increased cardiovascular risk associated with his lipid levels and smoking. Recommend initiation of lipid-lowering therapy after reviewing goals, potential adverse effects, and patient preferences; a statin would generally be first-line, with consideration of additional therapy if LDL remains elevated. Counsel on reducing saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates, weight reduction, and activity as tolerated. Strongly advised smoking cessation. Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment and assessment for secondary contributors as clinically appropriate. Follow up to review treatment decision and lipid response.
