Chief Complaint

Medication review for hypertension.

Subjective

Catherine Nichols is a 38-year-old woman with a 5-year history of essential hypertension, hyperlipidemia for 4 years, and coronary artery disease for 1 year. She is not documented as taking antihypertensive medication currently. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. She smokes. Medication adherence and treatment tolerance are not assessable because no active antihypertensive regimen is listed.

Objective

Height 167 cm, weight 76.4 kg, BMI 27.4 kg/m². Blood pressure 146/93 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air. Serum sodium 147 mmol/L and potassium 4.1 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, persistently elevated and currently without documented pharmacologic management. Patient is asymptomatic without findings suggestive of hypertensive emergency.

Hyperlipidemia and coronary artery disease, requiring consideration in overall cardiovascular risk reduction.

Tobacco use and overweight status.

Plan

Discussed blood pressure control, medication adherence, and treatment tolerance. Recommend initiating an appropriate antihypertensive regimen after shared decision-making, with medication selection mindful of her coronary artery disease and baseline electrolytes. Encourage home blood pressure monitoring and keeping a written log.

Counseled on reduced sodium intake, regular physical activity, weight reduction, limiting alcohol if applicable, and smoking cessation. Recommend review of cardiovascular risk therapy, including lipid management, at follow-up. Consider ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of hypertension and cardiovascular risk assessment.

Follow up in 2–4 weeks for blood pressure reassessment and medication response; seek urgent care for chest pain, severe headache, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure.
