Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective
Chelsea Benson is an 87-year-old woman with an approximately 8-year history of untreated hyperlipidemia, coronary artery disease, chronic kidney disease, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. She does not smoke. No current lipid-lowering medication is documented.

Objective
Height 176 cm; weight 63.8 kg; BMI 20.6 kg/m².
BP 137/82 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.
Total cholesterol 293 mg/dL, LDL-C 212 mg/dL, HDL-C 36 mg/dL, triglycerides 223 mg/dL.

Assessment
Severe mixed dyslipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and elevated triglycerides, currently untreated. Given established coronary artery disease, this represents high cardiovascular risk despite absence of current ischemic or claudication symptoms. Chronic kidney disease and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease are important comorbidities when selecting and monitoring therapy.

Plan
Discuss prompt initiation of lipid-lowering therapy, favoring a statin-based regimen if tolerated, with adjustment for age, renal function, liver history, and patient preferences. Consider addition of ezetimibe if LDL-C remains substantially elevated or statin therapy is insufficient or not tolerated. Repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment; obtain appropriate renal and hepatic monitoring before and during therapy. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk characterization. Reinforce heart-healthy diet, avoidance of saturated/trans fats, and activity as safely tolerated. Continue monitoring for new chest discomfort, dyspnea, dizziness, or exertional leg pain; seek urgent care for acute cardiopulmonary symptoms.
