Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Isaac Henderson is an 88-year-old man with an 11-year history of untreated hyperlipidemia and chronic kidney disease for approximately 12 years. He continues to smoke. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness. He reports fatty skin deposits consistent with xanthomas; no xanthelasma reported. No current lipid-lowering medication is documented.

Objective

Height 185 cm, weight 83.9 kg, BMI 24.5 kg/m². Blood pressure 133/88 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respirations 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Recent lipid panel: total cholesterol 363 mg/dL, LDL-C 273 mg/dL, HDL-C 39 mg/dL, triglycerides 256 mg/dL.

Assessment

Severe untreated hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and associated xanthomas. No current symptoms suggestive of acute coronary or peripheral arterial ischemia. Chronic kidney disease and active tobacco use increase overall cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the significantly elevated cholesterol and cardiovascular risk. Recommend prompt initiation of lipid-lowering therapy after reviewing goals, risks, renal function, medication history, and patient preference; a statin would generally be first-line, with consideration of additional therapy if response is inadequate or treatment is not tolerated. Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel and evaluation for secondary contributors, including ApoB and Lp(a) as clinically appropriate. Counselled on smoking cessation, heart-healthy diet, and regular activity as tolerated. Reviewed emergency precautions for new chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or acute limb pain. Close outpatient follow-up arranged to reassess treatment and lipid response.
