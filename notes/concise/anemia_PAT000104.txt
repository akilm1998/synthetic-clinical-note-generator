Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective
Alice Freeman is a 37-year-old woman seen for follow-up of anemia present for approximately 9 years. She reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor remains noted. Anemia has not been actively managed. History is also notable for heart failure diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She smokes. Height 178 cm, weight 92.5 kg, BMI 29.2 kg/m².

Objective
Vital signs: BP 124/78 mmHg, pulse 94 bpm, respirations 20/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Pale appearance. Cardiovascular examination otherwise normal. Respiratory examination normal.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 9.1 g/dL, hematocrit 28.0%, RBC 3.04 million/µL, MCV 92 fL, serum iron 38 µg/dL, ferritin 344 ng/mL, TIBC 264 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 14.4%.

Assessment
Chronic normocytic anemia with pallor. Low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC are consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic disease. No current cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms. Heart failure may contribute to the chronic inflammatory anemia pattern.

Plan
Review and address potential underlying contributors, including heart failure and other chronic inflammatory, renal, infectious, autoimmune, or malignant processes as clinically indicated.

Recommend repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with renal function, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers. Consider hematology consultation if anemia persists, worsens, or remains unexplained.

Discuss iron management based on the overall evaluation; avoid empiric iron escalation without clarification of iron status given elevated ferritin. Encourage smoking cessation.

Monitor for worsening fatigue, dyspnea, chest pain, palpitations, dizziness, or syncope and seek urgent care if these occur. Arrange outpatient follow-up after reassessment.
