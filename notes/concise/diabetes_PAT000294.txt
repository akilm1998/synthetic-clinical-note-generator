Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective
81-year-old male with 21-year history of type 2 diabetes, currently without reported symptoms of hyperglycemia or complications. Denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. Diabetes control has been suboptimal.

Comorbid conditions include chronic kidney disease for approximately 37 years, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 10 years, and anxiety for 60 years. Patient currently smokes. No medication regimen provided for review.

Objective
Height 168 cm; weight 72.5 kg; BMI 25.7 kg/m².
BP 152/83 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98%.
HbA1c 9.9%. Fasting glucose 223 mg/dL.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, poorly controlled based on elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose. Patient denies osmotic, constitutional, visual, neuropathic, or wound-related symptoms.
Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.
Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.
Anxiety.
Tobacco use.
Elevated office blood pressure reading.

Plan
Discussed significant hyperglycemia and need for active diabetes management. Review current medications and adherence at next medication reconciliation; recommend prompt initiation or intensification of glucose-lowering therapy as clinically appropriate, with consideration of renal and hepatic comorbidities. Reinforced diabetic nutrition, portion control, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, regular physical activity as tolerated, and home glucose monitoring.

Recommend renal monitoring with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine/eGFR, and medication adjustment based on kidney function. Recommend fasting lipid profile and routine diabetic foot and eye evaluations. Counsel regarding smoking cessation.

Repeat blood pressure measurement outside the office or at a subsequent visit and address persistent elevation. Follow up promptly for worsening polyuria, polydipsia, vomiting, confusion, vision changes, or other acute concerns. Close outpatient follow-up for diabetes management.
