Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Matthew Reed is a 78-year-old man, 164 cm and 76.9 kg (BMI 28.6), presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 45 years. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. Chronic comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 14 years, hyperlipidemia for 37 years, and anemia of chronic disease for 24 years. He continues to smoke.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 138/87 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Recent laboratory results: creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, eGFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 66 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significantly elevated creatinine and BUN. Despite absence of reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, current laboratory findings warrant prompt specialist assessment and close monitoring.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Anemia of chronic disease.

Tobacco use.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for advanced chronic kidney disease, including discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate. Obtain updated renal function and electrolyte panel, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, intact PTH, and renal ultrasound as part of disease assessment. Review blood pressure and diabetes management at follow-up, with medication dosing adjusted for renal function. Avoid NSAIDs and other potential nephrotoxins. Counsel regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of excess dietary sodium. Seek urgent care for new shortness of breath, swelling, markedly reduced urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, or chest pain. Close outpatient follow-up after nephrology assessment.
