Chief Complaint
Initial consultation for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective
Kayla Pierce is a 98-year-old woman, 180 cm, 83.3 kg, BMI 25.7 kg/m², presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 15 years. CKD has not been actively managed. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. She is a current smoker. History is also notable for gout for 3 years. Medication, allergy, family, and surgical histories were not provided.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 153/85 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respirations 20/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Laboratory data: creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 73 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings provided.

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and azotemia, present for approximately 15 years and currently without active management. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction; no current symptoms of overt fluid overload or significant uremic prodrome reported. Elevated blood pressure. Comorbid gout. Current tobacco use.

Plan
Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for evaluation of renal replacement therapy candidacy, dialysis indications, prognosis, and goals of care, taking into account her advanced age and current symptom burden.

Obtain/review serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, CBC, intact PTH, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate. Repeat renal function testing promptly.

Perform complete medication reconciliation when available; avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Review dietary sodium, renal-appropriate nutrition, and blood pressure management. Address gout treatment cautiously in the context of severe renal impairment.

Recommend smoking cessation. Educate patient and family to seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, edema, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, marked weakness, or substantially reduced urine output. Close follow-up after nephrology evaluation and laboratory assessment.
