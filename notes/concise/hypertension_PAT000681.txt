Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Michelle Cross is an 87-year-old woman with a 19-year history of essential hypertension and coronary artery disease of approximately 35 years’ duration. She reports no headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Hypertension is currently not under active management. She continues to smoke.

Objective

Height 179 cm; weight 87.2 kg; BMI 27.2 kg/m². Blood pressure 166/92 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96%. Serum sodium 146 mmol/L and potassium 4.2 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and no reported symptoms of acute end-organ involvement. Long duration of disease and coexisting coronary artery disease increase cardiovascular risk. Mildly elevated sodium noted; potassium is normal. Tobacco use and overweight status are additional modifiable risk factors.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management and cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend confirmation with repeat and home blood pressure measurements and prompt initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy through shared decision-making, with consideration of her age and coronary artery disease. Recommend dietary sodium reduction, regular activity as tolerated, weight management, and smoking cessation. Review medication options and monitor renal function and electrolytes after therapy is established. Recommend ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular and hypertension assessment. Advise urgent evaluation for chest pain, new shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly worsening blood pressure. Follow up soon for blood pressure reassessment.
