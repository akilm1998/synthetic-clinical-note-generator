Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia.

Subjective
Angela Holland is an 80-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia present for approximately 29 years and currently not under active treatment. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. She does not smoke. Comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 2 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 38 years.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 131/80 mmHg, pulse 68 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.
Height 174 cm; weight 75.4 kg; BMI 24.9 kg/m².
Lipid panel: total cholesterol 339 mg/dL, LDL-C 246 mg/dL, HDL-C 42 mg/dL, triglycerides 253 mg/dL.

Assessment
Severe longstanding hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and elevated triglycerides, currently untreated. No reported symptoms suggestive of active cardiovascular ischemia or peripheral arterial disease. Type 2 diabetes and advanced age increase overall cardiovascular risk. History of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease noted.

Plan
Discussed substantially increased cardiovascular risk associated with LDL-C 246 mg/dL and diabetes. Recommend initiation of statin therapy, with choice and intensity individualized through shared decision-making given age and comorbidities. Review treatment goals, potential adverse effects, and adherence.
Counseled on a heart-healthy diet low in saturated and trans fats, regular physical activity as tolerated, and continued tobacco avoidance.
Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors and additional risk assessment, including ApoB and lipoprotein(a), if clinically useful.
Continue routine management of diabetes and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Follow up after treatment decision and lipid reassessment.
