Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective
Preston Oliver is a 95-year-old man here for follow-up of anemia present for approximately 20 years. He reports no fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, headache, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor persists. History is notable for inflammatory bowel disease for 28 years and chronic infection for 3 years. He does not smoke. No current anemia-directed treatment is documented.

Objective
Height 163 cm, weight 78.1 kg, BMI 29.4 kg/m². BP 124/75 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respirations 21/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Appears clinically stable with pallor. Cardiovascular and respiratory examinations are unremarkable.

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin 9.4 g/dL, hematocrit 27.4%, RBC 3.3 million/µL, MCV 83 fL, serum iron 22 µg/dL, ferritin 290 ng/mL, TIBC 244 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 9%.

Assessment
Chronic anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation, with normocytic indices and low serum iron/transferrin saturation in the setting of longstanding inflammatory bowel disease and chronic infection. Clinically stable and largely asymptomatic aside from pallor. No evidence of current hemodynamic compromise.

Plan
Obtain interval CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the anemia. Review for ongoing inflammatory activity and any evidence of gastrointestinal blood loss in the context of inflammatory bowel disease. Discuss iron replacement, including oral versus intravenous therapy, after reassessment and consideration of tolerance and absorption. Continue outpatient monitoring; seek urgent care for new dyspnea, chest pain, syncope, palpitations, or worsening weakness.
