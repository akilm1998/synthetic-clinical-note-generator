Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective
Nicole Harper is a 62-year-old woman with a 9-year history of hyperlipidemia, currently without active lipid-lowering management. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. No tobacco use. Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 8 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 21 years.

Objective
BP 124/77 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respirations 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%. Height 155 cm, weight 54.1 kg, BMI 22.5 kg/m².

Total cholesterol 312 mg/dL, LDL-C 225 mg/dL, HDL-C 38 mg/dL, triglycerides 244 mg/dL.

Assessment
Severe untreated hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C, low HDL-C, and elevated triglycerides. Established coronary artery disease places her at high cardiovascular risk. Coexisting non-alcoholic fatty liver disease noted. No current ischemic or peripheral vascular symptoms reported.

Plan
Discussed the importance of prompt lipid-lowering therapy for secondary cardiovascular prevention. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after reviewing contraindications and shared decision-making, with consideration of additional therapy such as ezetimibe if LDL-C remains above goal. Obtain a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation and assess adherence and response. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for risk refinement. Counsel on a Mediterranean-style diet, reduction of saturated and trans fats, regular physical activity as tolerated, and continued tobacco avoidance. Monitor liver-related safety clinically given history of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Follow up after repeat lipid testing to adjust therapy.
