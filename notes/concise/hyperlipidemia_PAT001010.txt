Chief Complaint

Annual review of dyslipidemia and chronic disease management.

Subjective

Trevor Warren is a 62-year-old man with a 26-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and untreated dyslipidemia. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness. Reports fatty skin deposits and yellow eyelid deposits consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma. He does not smoke.

Objective

Height 160 cm; weight 71.9 kg; BMI 28.1 kg/m². BP 128/78 mmHg, pulse 68 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 337 mg/dL, LDL-C 223 mg/dL, HDL-C 36 mg/dL, triglycerides 391 mg/dL.

Assessment

Severe mixed dyslipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and triglycerides and low HDL-C, currently untreated. Xanthomas and xanthelasma are present. Longstanding type 2 diabetes and overweight status confer substantially increased cardiovascular risk, although he currently reports no anginal, claudication, or neurologic symptoms.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 26 years; ongoing chronic disease review indicated.

Plan

Recommend initiation of high-intensity lipid-lowering therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after shared decision-making and review of contraindications. Repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation; consider addition of ezetimibe if LDL-C remains substantially above goal. Address triglyceride elevation with reduction of refined carbohydrates, saturated fats, and alcohol if applicable, along with weight reduction and regular aerobic activity.

Assess for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and review diabetes control as part of comprehensive chronic disease management. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for risk refinement. Reinforce heart-healthy nutrition, physical activity, and continued tobacco avoidance.

Continue routine diabetes preventive care, including periodic retinal, renal, and foot assessment and age-appropriate immunizations. Follow up after treatment initiation and repeat lipid testing.
