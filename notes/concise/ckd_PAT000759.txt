Chief Complaint

Initial outpatient consultation for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Monica Shaw is an 83-year-old woman, 170 cm, 74.3 kg (BMI 25.7 kg/m²), with chronic kidney disease reported for approximately 49 years. She has not had active disease-directed management documented. She denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath. She does not smoke. Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 4 years and gout for 3 years.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 151/86 mmHg, pulse 78/min, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Available laboratory results: creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 82 mg/dL, estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function. Creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 82 mg/dL, and eGFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m² indicate advanced renal impairment despite absence of reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms. Given the prolonged history and lack of established management, she requires prompt nephrology-directed evaluation and renal replacement therapy planning.

2. Hypertension. Blood pressure is elevated at 151/86 mmHg in the setting of severe chronic kidney disease.

3. Coronary artery disease, reported duration 4 years.

4. Gout, reported duration 3 years.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology consultation for comprehensive CKD evaluation, assessment for indications for renal replacement therapy, and discussion of dialysis access and conservative management options as appropriate.

Recommend serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as part of the CKD evaluation.

Review renal-adjusted medication dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxins, including NSAIDs. Medication reconciliation is needed before establishing a treatment regimen.

Address blood pressure control in the context of kidney disease and coronary artery disease after medication review.

Advise immediate emergency evaluation for new dyspnea, edema, chest pain, confusion, persistent nausea or vomiting, marked weakness, or substantial change in urine output.
