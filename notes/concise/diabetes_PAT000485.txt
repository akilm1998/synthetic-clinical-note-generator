Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

William Turner is a 36-year-old male with a 12-year history of type 2 diabetes, currently without active diabetes management. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling. History also notable for depression for approximately 1 year. He currently smokes.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 118/82 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 186 cm, weight 78.2 kg, BMI 22.6 kg/m².

HbA1c 9.6%. Fasting glucose 240 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, poorly controlled based on elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose. Patient is currently asymptomatic despite significant hyperglycemia. No reported neuropathic, visual, wound, or infectious symptoms. Depression and active tobacco use noted.

Plan

Discussed laboratory results and risks of persistent hyperglycemia. Recommend re-establishing active diabetes treatment, including prompt discussion of pharmacologic therapy and individualized glucose monitoring.

Counseled on carbohydrate-conscious nutrition, regular physical activity, and avoidance of tobacco; smoking cessation encouraged.

Recommend diabetes preventive care, including foot examination, dilated retinal examination, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, renal function testing, and fasting lipid profile. Repeat HbA1c after treatment is established to assess response.

Follow up promptly for diabetes management and sooner for polyuria, polydipsia, vomiting, marked weakness, or other worsening symptoms.
