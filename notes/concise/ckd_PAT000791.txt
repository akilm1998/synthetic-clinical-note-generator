Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Paige Palmer is a 94-year-old woman with a 34-year history of chronic kidney disease, hypertension for 44 years, coronary artery disease for 37 years, and gout for 37 years. She reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. Smoking status: current smoker. No active medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Height 157 cm; weight 72.5 kg; BMI 29.4 kg/m². Blood pressure 165/93 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Serum creatinine 3.88 mg/dL, eGFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 93 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine, currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms. Disease control appears suboptimal. Hypertension is above goal and is an important comorbidity. Coronary artery disease and gout noted.

Plan

Recommend expedited nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management and discussion of future renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate. Obtain repeat renal function and serum electrolytes, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact PTH; consider renal ultrasound if not previously completed. Review and optimize blood pressure management while accounting for renal function and coronary artery disease. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications; review all prescription and over-the-counter medications. Counsel regarding smoking cessation and monitoring for edema, worsening fatigue, nausea, pruritus, confusion, reduced urine output, or shortness of breath. Seek urgent evaluation for severe dyspnea, chest pain, confusion, or markedly reduced urination.
