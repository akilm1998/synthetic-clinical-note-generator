Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective
Blake Hunter is an 89-year-old man with a 27-year history of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease for 12 years, presenting for routine follow-up. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, edema, shortness of breath, or change in urination. He does not smoke.

Objective
Height 174 cm, weight 75.4 kg, BMI 24.9 kg/m². BP 143/86 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air. Recent laboratory results: creatinine 3.91 mg/dL, eGFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 75 mg/dL.

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. Currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms. Blood pressure is mildly elevated. Longstanding disease and concurrent coronary artery disease increase overall risk. No established management regimen is documented.

Plan
Discussed the importance of active renal follow-up and avoidance of nephrotoxic medications, including NSAIDs. Recommend nephrology evaluation and review of renal-protective and blood pressure treatment options, with medication dosing adjusted for renal function. Obtain/review urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum electrolytes, CBC, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate. Monitor blood pressure, weight, urine output, edema, dyspnea, nausea, confusion, and worsening fatigue. Seek urgent care for new shortness of breath, markedly decreased urination, chest pain, confusion, or progressive swelling. Return for close outpatient follow-up after laboratory and renal evaluation.
