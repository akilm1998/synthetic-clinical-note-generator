Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective
Teresa Cunningham is a 77-year-old woman with a 24-year history of anemia, followed in the setting of malignancy, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 19 years, and chronic infection for 2 years. She reports no fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, headache, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor is present. She continues to smoke. Weight is 81.4 kg at 167 cm (BMI 29.2 kg/m²).

Objective
Vital signs: BP 131/79 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respirations 19/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Laboratory data: Hemoglobin 8.0 g/dL, hematocrit 24.3%, RBC 2.61 million/µL, MCV 93 fL. Serum iron 24 µg/dL, ferritin 404 ng/mL, TIBC 236 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 10.2%.

Assessment
Anemia consistent with chronic disease/inflammation: significant chronic normocytic anemia with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and low TIBC. This pattern is more consistent with iron-restricted erythropoiesis related to chronic inflammation than uncomplicated iron deficiency. She is currently clinically stable without cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms, though pallor persists. Malignancy and chronic infection are relevant contributing conditions. Management requires reassessment, as the anemia has not been actively addressed.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan
Obtain repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with renal function, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the anemia. Review for ongoing blood loss and assess the activity of the underlying malignancy and chronic infection. Consider iron replacement only after confirming the overall iron profile and source of inflammation; hematology/oncology input is appropriate regarding anemia-directed therapy, including whether erythropoiesis-stimulating treatment or intravenous iron is indicated. Continue monitoring for worsening fatigue, dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, or bleeding; seek urgent care if these develop. Continue routine management of diabetes and reinforce smoking cessation.
