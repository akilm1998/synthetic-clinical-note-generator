Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and abnormal renal function.

Subjective
Caroline Rivera is an 86-year-old woman with a 35-year history of chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease for 3 years, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 8 years. She reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. She currently smokes. No active CKD management is documented.

Objective
BP 144/87 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%. Height 165 cm, weight 79.5 kg, BMI 29.2 kg/m².

Serum creatinine 3.91 mg/dL, BUN 62 mg/dL, eGFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated nitrogenous waste markers. Although she denies current uremic or volume-overload symptoms, the degree of renal impairment requires urgent specialist assessment. Blood pressure is mildly elevated. Comorbid coronary artery disease and secondary hyperparathyroidism increase clinical complexity.

Plan
Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management and discussion of renal replacement planning. Recommend serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone testing, with renal ultrasound as clinically indicated. Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications for renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxins; medication regimen is not specified today. Counsel smoking cessation and avoidance of NSAIDs. Proceed to the emergency department for new or worsening dyspnea, edema, persistent nausea/vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or marked reduction in urine output.
