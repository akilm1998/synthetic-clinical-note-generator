Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Hunter Ford is a 76-year-old man, nonsmoker, with a 42-year history of chronic kidney disease and longstanding hypertension for 16 years and gout for 18 years. Reports ongoing fatigue. Denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. CKD has not been actively managed. No medication regimen provided.

Objective

Height 174 cm, weight 70.5 kg, BMI 23.3 kg/m². BP 167/92 mmHg, pulse 69, respirations 16, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 95%. Recent labs: creatinine 3.23 mg/dL, eGFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 64 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with significantly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine, currently associated with fatigue but no reported fluid overload or uremic symptoms. Renal disease is not currently under active management.

Hypertension, currently above goal.

History of gout.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation and active CKD management. Repeat renal function and serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact PTH as clinically appropriate. Obtain renal ultrasound if not recently completed. Address blood pressure with a renal-appropriate treatment plan and home BP monitoring. Avoid NSAIDs and other potential nephrotoxins; review all over-the-counter medications and supplements. Counsel on appropriate renal diet and to seek urgent care for worsening shortness of breath, edema, markedly decreased urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, or chest pain. Follow up closely after laboratory and specialty evaluation.
