Chief Complaint

Annual review and chronic kidney disease follow-up.

Subjective

Bob Hill is a 40-year-old male, 188 cm, 87.7 kg (BMI 24.8), presenting for comprehensive annual review. Chronic kidney disease has been present for approximately 9 years and has not been under active management. He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. Smoking status: current smoker.

Comorbidities include gout for 1 year, coronary artery disease for 1 year, and hyperlipidemia for 2 years. No current medication regimen provided.

Objective

BP 132/89 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Serum creatinine 3.33 mg/dL. BUN 81 mg/dL. eGFR 18 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of approximately 9 years’ duration with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. Currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms; management needs to be established.

Coronary artery disease and hyperlipidemia, requiring cardiovascular risk-factor optimization.

Gout, with renal impairment relevant to medication selection.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Refer promptly to nephrology for comprehensive CKD evaluation and ongoing management, including discussion of renal replacement planning if appropriate.

Obtain urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum electrolytes, CBC, intact PTH, and renal ultrasound as part of evaluation. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents; review all prescription and over-the-counter medications for renal dosing.

Monitor blood pressure and renal function closely. Review dietary sodium and protein intake with renal dietitian if available.

Address cardiovascular risk reduction, including lipid management and smoking cessation. Counsel regarding cessation resources.

Review gout treatment with attention to renal dosing and avoidance of NSAIDs.

Update age- and risk-appropriate preventive care, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, and tetanus vaccination status, and routine cancer screening. Close primary care follow-up after testing and nephrology evaluation.
