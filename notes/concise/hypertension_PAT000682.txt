Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective
Brooke Snyder is a 58-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of approximately 6-year history of hypertension, currently without active treatment. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. She does not smoke. History also includes hyperlipidemia for 11 years.

Objective
Blood pressure 151/96 mmHg, pulse 70 bpm, respirations 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%. Height 177 cm, weight 87.1 kg, BMI 27.8 kg/m². Serum sodium 142 mmol/L and potassium 3.9 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension, persistently above goal and currently untreated. Patient is asymptomatic without features suggesting acute hypertensive emergency. Electrolytes are within normal limits.
Hyperlipidemia.

Plan
Discussed blood pressure risks and importance of treatment adherence and monitoring. Recommend initiation of pharmacologic antihypertensive therapy after shared decision-making, with medication selection guided by renal function and cardiovascular risk assessment. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring and recording readings for review.

Counseled on reduced sodium intake, regular physical activity, weight management, and limiting alcohol if applicable. Recommend evaluation of renal function and urinalysis before or shortly after starting therapy, as well as updated lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment. Follow up in 2–4 weeks for blood pressure review and treatment adjustment. Seek urgent care for chest pain, severe headache, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure.
