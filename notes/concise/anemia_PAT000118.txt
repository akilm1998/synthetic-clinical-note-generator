Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective
Catherine Nichols is a 51-year-old woman, BMI 24.8 kg/m², nonsmoker, with a 9-year history of anemia in the setting of inflammatory bowel disease for 4 years and malignancy for 5 years. She reports headache and appears pale. She denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, and reduced exercise tolerance. No current anemia-directed treatment is documented.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 122/78 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respirations 20/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air. Height 180 cm; weight 80.4 kg.

Pallor noted. No tachycardia or hypoxemia.

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin 9.3 g/dL, hematocrit 27.2%, RBC 3.32 million/µL, MCV 82 fL, serum iron 40 µg/dL, ferritin 286 ng/mL, TIBC 200 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 20%.

Assessment
Chronic normocytic anemia with low serum iron and TIBC and preserved/elevated ferritin, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of inflammatory bowel disease and malignancy. Clinically stable today, with headache and pallor but no cardiopulmonary symptoms or hemodynamic instability.

Plan
Obtain/update CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the anemia. Continue evaluation and control of the underlying inflammatory bowel disease and malignancy in coordination with the treating specialists. Review iron requirements in the context of inflammatory disease; consider intravenous iron or an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent only if clinically indicated after assessment of the underlying conditions. Monitor hemoglobin and symptoms closely. Advise prompt evaluation for new dyspnea, chest pain, syncope, palpitations, or worsening weakness.
