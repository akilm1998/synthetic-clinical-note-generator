Chief Complaint
Initial evaluation of elevated blood pressure.

Subjective
Teresa Cunningham is a 62-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of hypertension, present for approximately 5 years and currently untreated. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. History is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 2 years. She does not smoke.

Objective
Height 172 cm; weight 82.5 kg; BMI 27.9 kg/m².
Blood pressure 157/93 mmHg; pulse 75 bpm; respirations 16/min; temperature 36.9°C; oxygen saturation 99%.
Serum sodium 144 mmol/L; potassium 5.0 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and no reported symptoms concerning for hypertensive emergency. Hypertension has been untreated for approximately 5 years. Type 2 diabetes mellitus is an important cardiovascular risk factor. Potassium is at the upper end of normal and should be reassessed before selecting therapy affecting potassium balance.

Plan
Discussed diagnosis, cardiovascular risk reduction, and importance of treatment adherence.
Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a validated cuff and recording readings for review.
Recommend a lower-sodium, heart-healthy diet, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and moderation of alcohol intake.
Repeat blood pressure and serum electrolytes; obtain renal function assessment and urinalysis as part of hypertension and diabetes evaluation.
Discuss initiation of antihypertensive therapy after review of repeat potassium and renal function; avoid empiric potassium-raising therapy until these results are clarified.
Recommend ECG and lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment.
Follow up promptly for blood pressure review and treatment initiation. Seek emergency care for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or acute vision changes.
