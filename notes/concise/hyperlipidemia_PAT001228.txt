Chief Complaint
Annual review and evaluation of elevated cholesterol.

Subjective
Victoria Richardson is a 60-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. Hyperlipidemia has been present for approximately 2 years and is currently untreated. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 300 mg/dL, LDL-C 201 mg/dL, HDL-C 43 mg/dL, and triglycerides 280 mg/dL. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

Relevant chronic conditions include hypertension for 5 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 19 years. No current lipid-lowering medications are documented.

Objective
Height 166 cm, weight 61.7 kg, BMI 22.4 kg/m². Blood pressure 139/89 mmHg, pulse 70 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Assessment
Hyperlipidemia with marked elevation of LDL-C and concurrent hypertriglyceridemia, untreated. No reported cardiovascular or cutaneous symptoms.

Hypertension, mildly elevated blood pressure today.

Longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Plan
Discussed cardiovascular risk associated with LDL-C of 201 mg/dL and triglycerides of 280 mg/dL. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after shared decision-making, with review of potential adverse effects and adherence. Emphasize Mediterranean-style eating pattern, reduction of saturated fats and refined carbohydrates, regular physical activity, and continued avoidance of tobacco.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation to assess response. Consider baseline and follow-up liver testing given known fatty liver disease and planned lipid therapy. Consider additional cardiovascular risk assessment, including lipoprotein(a) or coronary artery calcium scoring, if treatment decision remains uncertain.

Continue lifestyle measures and routine blood pressure monitoring; reassess hypertension management at follow-up.

Review age-appropriate preventive care, including immunizations and recommended cancer screening, during the annual health maintenance visit.

Follow up in approximately 6–12 weeks for lipid and blood pressure review, or sooner for new chest discomfort, exertional leg symptoms, or medication concerns.
