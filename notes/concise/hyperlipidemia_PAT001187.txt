Chief Complaint

Initial evaluation of elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

James Carter is a 73-year-old male presenting for initial outpatient consultation regarding hyperlipidemia, identified approximately 2 years ago and currently untreated. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. He is an active smoker. Relevant history includes hypertension for 37 years and coronary artery disease for 28 years. No current lipid-lowering medication regimen was provided.

Objective

Height 188 cm; weight 84.1 kg; BMI 23.8 kg/m². Blood pressure 140/89 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 294 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 191 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 34 mg/dL, triglycerides 345 mg/dL.

Assessment

Severe mixed dyslipidemia with markedly elevated LDL and triglycerides and low HDL. Significant atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk given known coronary artery disease, hypertension, advanced age, and ongoing tobacco use. No current symptoms suggestive of acute ischemia or claudication.

Plan

Discussed substantially increased cardiovascular risk and the importance of treatment. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy for secondary prevention, after reviewing contraindications, potential adverse effects, and patient preference. Consider addition of ezetimibe if LDL remains above goal despite maximally tolerated statin therapy.

Recommend heart-healthy diet emphasizing reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and alcohol, with regular physical activity as tolerated. Strongly encouraged smoking cessation.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, with consideration of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk assessment. Continue management of hypertension and coronary artery disease with the patient’s established care team. Follow up after repeat lipid testing or sooner for medication concerns or new chest symptoms.
