Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Tristan Burke is a 35-year-old man with a 15-year history of hypertension, currently without active treatment. He reports no headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. He does not smoke. History also includes type 2 diabetes mellitus and coronary artery disease, each diagnosed approximately 1 year ago.

Objective

Height 181 cm, weight 70.8 kg, BMI 21.6 kg/m². Blood pressure 144/96 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Serum sodium 135 mmol/L and potassium 4.7 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension remains above goal after a prolonged period without active management. No symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency today. Coexisting diabetes and coronary artery disease increase cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed need for active blood pressure treatment and home BP monitoring with a written log. Recommend initiation of antihypertensive therapy, with selection guided by diabetes, coronary artery disease, renal function, medication history, and tolerability. Reinforced low-sodium diet, regular physical activity, weight maintenance, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management in the context of diabetes and coronary artery disease. Obtain baseline renal function and repeat electrolytes after therapy initiation; consider ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular risk assessment. Return for blood pressure review after treatment initiation or sooner for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or severe headache.
