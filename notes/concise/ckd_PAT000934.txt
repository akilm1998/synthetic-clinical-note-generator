Chief Complaint

Annual review and chronic kidney disease follow-up.

Subjective

Kayla Pierce is a 45-year-old woman presenting for annual review. She has an 11-year history of chronic kidney disease, 14-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, and anemia of chronic disease for approximately 4 years. Reports fatigue. Denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. She currently smokes.

Objective

Height 156 cm, weight 61.8 kg, BMI 25.4 kg/m². BP 144/89 mmHg, pulse 86/min, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: creatinine 2.88 mg/dL, eGFR 18 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 61 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with significant reduction in renal function and azotemia. Fatigue may be related to chronic kidney disease and known anemia of chronic disease. No current symptoms of fluid overload or uremic prodrome reported.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Elevated blood pressure in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Tobacco use.

Annual preventive health review.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation given eGFR 18 mL/min/1.73 m² and longstanding, currently untreated chronic kidney disease. Recommend repeat renal function and serum electrolytes, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone assessment; consider renal ultrasound if not previously completed. Review renal-protective management and medication safety, including avoidance of NSAIDs and other nephrotoxins. Do not start or adjust medications without reviewing current regimen and renal function.

Address diabetes control with updated diabetes monitoring and individualized kidney-safe treatment planning. Reassess blood pressure and discuss lifestyle measures, including sodium reduction, regular activity as tolerated, and weight maintenance.

Discuss smoking cessation and offer evidence-based cessation support. Review routine age-appropriate immunizations and preventive screening, including cervical, breast, colorectal, and cardiovascular risk screening as indicated. Follow up after laboratory assessment or sooner for worsening fatigue, nausea, confusion, reduced urine output, swelling, or shortness of breath.
