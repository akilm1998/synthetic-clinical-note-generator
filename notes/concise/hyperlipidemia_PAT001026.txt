Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective
Brooke Snyder is a 93-year-old woman with a 13-year history of hyperlipidemia, currently not receiving documented lipid-lowering treatment. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. Continues to smoke. History also includes hypertension for 6 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 60 years.

Objective
Height 160 cm; weight 61.4 kg; BMI 24.0 kg/m². BP 144/86 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respirations 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 334 mg/dL, LDL-C 211 mg/dL, HDL-C 45 mg/dL, triglycerides 391 mg/dL.

Assessment
Hyperlipidemia with marked elevation of LDL cholesterol and triglycerides, without reported ischemic or peripheral vascular symptoms. Risk is increased by advanced age, active tobacco use, and hypertension. Coexisting non-alcoholic fatty liver disease should be considered when selecting and monitoring therapy.

Plan
Discussed substantially elevated cardiovascular risk and benefits versus potential risks of lipid-lowering therapy in the context of advanced age and overall goals of care. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of a statin, with consideration of a moderate-intensity regimen if consistent with patient preference and tolerance. Consider repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or lifestyle intervention to assess response.

Counseled on reduction of saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates and alcohol, and maintaining regular activity as tolerated. Strongly advised smoking cessation. Continue blood pressure follow-up and management. Monitor for new chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or other cardiovascular symptoms.
