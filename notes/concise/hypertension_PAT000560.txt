Chief Complaint

New patient evaluation for hypertension.

Subjective

Patricia Greene is a 32-year-old woman, nonsmoker, presenting for initial outpatient consultation. She reports a 7-year history of hypertension that has not been actively managed. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. No current antihypertensive medications are reported.

Past medical history is notable for hyperlipidemia and chronic kidney disease, each diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. No additional medication or surgical history is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 160/96 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 171 cm; weight 82.5 kg; BMI 28.2 kg/m². Nonsmoker.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 147 mmol/L, potassium 4.4 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently uncontrolled. Blood pressure is markedly elevated at 160/96 mmHg without symptoms concerning for acute end-organ injury. Chronic kidney disease increases the importance of prompt treatment and monitoring.

2. Chronic kidney disease, approximately 1 year since diagnosis. Renal function values are not provided. Hypertension may contribute to progression; treatment selection should account for kidney function and potassium level.

3. Hyperlipidemia, approximately 1 year since diagnosis. Cardiovascular risk assessment and lipid-directed management are indicated in the context of longstanding hypertension and chronic kidney disease.

4. Overweight, BMI 28.2 kg/m².

Plan

Confirm blood pressure with repeat office measurement and home monitoring, with proper cuff size and technique. Begin structured hypertension treatment after review of renal function and baseline kidney assessment; an ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker would be reasonable to consider in the setting of chronic kidney disease if renal function and potassium permit. Avoid NSAID use when possible and review sodium intake.

Obtain renal function testing, repeat electrolytes, urinalysis, and urine albumin assessment; monitor potassium and creatinine after initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy. Recommended baseline ECG and fasting lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment.

Counseled on sodium reduction, weight loss, regular physical activity, and limiting alcohol intake. Return promptly for blood pressure reassessment, ideally within 1–2 weeks. Seek emergency care for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or visual changes.
