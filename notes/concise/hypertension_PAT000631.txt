Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective
Jane Watson is a 48-year-old woman with a 5-year history of essential hypertension, currently without active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, and hyperlipidemia, each present for approximately 7 years. She does not smoke. BMI is 24.9 kg/m².

Objective
Blood pressure 153/102 mmHg; pulse 77 bpm; respirations 17/min; temperature 36.9°C; oxygen saturation 99%. Height 170 cm, weight 72.0 kg. Serum sodium 144 mmol/L and potassium 5.2 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with significantly elevated office blood pressure, currently untreated or without documented active management. No symptoms concerning for hypertensive emergency today. Mild hyperkalemia in the setting of chronic kidney disease; this is clinically relevant when selecting antihypertensive therapy.

Plan
Repeat blood pressure after appropriate rest and obtain home readings if available. Arrange prompt primary care follow-up to initiate or adjust antihypertensive therapy, with consideration of kidney disease and potassium level. Repeat serum electrolytes and renal function promptly to reassess potassium. Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use before treatment decisions. Counsel on sodium restriction, regular physical activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Continue coordinated management of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and hyperlipidemia. Seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, visual changes, or other acute deterioration.
