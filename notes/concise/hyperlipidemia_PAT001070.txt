Chief Complaint
Initial consultation for longstanding elevated cholesterol.

Subjective
Caleb Freeman is a 71-year-old man, 190 cm, 99.6 kg, BMI 27.6 kg/m², presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of hyperlipidemia present for approximately 17 years and currently untreated. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 334 mg/dL, LDL-C 227 mg/dL, HDL-C 33 mg/dL, and triglycerides 368 mg/dL. Reports xanthomas and xanthelasma. Denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness.

Relevant history includes coronary artery disease for 22 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 24 years, and chronic kidney disease for 9 years. Current smoker. No medication list provided.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 142/86 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respirations 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Weight 99.6 kg; height 190 cm; BMI 27.6 kg/m². Xanthomas and xanthelasma reported. No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms.

Assessment
Severe mixed hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and triglycerides and low HDL-C, longstanding and presently without active treatment. Cutaneous lipid deposits support prolonged significant dyslipidemia. Cardiovascular risk is very high given established coronary artery disease, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, age, hypertension-range blood pressure, and ongoing tobacco use. No current anginal or claudication symptoms reported.

Plan
Discussed need for prompt lipid-lowering therapy; recommend initiation of a high-intensity statin after reviewing contraindications, prior intolerance, and baseline safety considerations. If LDL-C remains above goal or therapy is not tolerated, consider addition of ezetimibe and further lipid-specialist-directed therapy.

Obtain a fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation and consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for risk assessment. Evaluate for secondary contributors to marked dyslipidemia as clinically appropriate and monitor renal and hepatic function with therapy.

Counseled on Mediterranean-style diet, reduction of saturated and trans fats and refined carbohydrates, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight maintenance, and smoking cessation. Continue cardiovascular risk reduction in coordination with his established care team. Follow up promptly to review treatment initiation and lipid response.
