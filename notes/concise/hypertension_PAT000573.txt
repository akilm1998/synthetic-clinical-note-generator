Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective
Preston Oliver is a 49-year-old man with a 10-year history of hypertension, currently without active management. Reports intermittent dizziness and fatigue. Denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, or epistaxis. Nonsmoker. Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 14 years, chronic kidney disease for 4 years, and coronary artery disease for 1 year.

Objective
BP 164/101 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respirations 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Height 188 cm, weight 100.7 kg, BMI 28.5 kg/m². Serum sodium 143 mmol/L; potassium 5.0 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with persistently elevated blood pressure and symptoms of dizziness/fatigue. No reported symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency. Management is currently inadequate. Coexisting diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease increase cardiovascular and renal risk. Potassium is at the upper end of normal, relevant to antihypertensive selection.

Plan
Discussed need for prompt initiation or re-establishment of antihypertensive treatment and close blood pressure monitoring; medication reconciliation required before prescribing. Recommend home BP log and dietary sodium reduction, weight management, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Obtain ECG, urinalysis, and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk assessment; monitor renal function and electrolytes with treatment adjustments. Avoid excess dietary potassium and nephrotoxic medications unless specifically directed. Seek emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, or acute visual changes. Close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment.
