Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Cody Warren is a 74-year-old male presenting for follow-up of hypertension, present for approximately 8 years and not currently actively managed. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. He has type 2 diabetes mellitus for 4 years and chronic kidney disease for 14 years. He currently smokes. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Blood pressure 157/100 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%. Height 185 cm, weight 95.8 kg, BMI 28.0 kg/m². Serum sodium 137 mmol/L and potassium 5.3 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent elevation and no associated symptoms today. Chronic kidney disease and diabetes increase cardiovascular and renal risk. Mild hyperkalemia is present and should be considered before selecting antihypertensive therapy.

Plan

Discussed importance of active blood pressure management, low-sodium diet, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight control, and smoking cessation. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring and review of readings at follow-up. Repeat serum electrolytes and obtain renal function testing before initiating or adjusting therapy, given chronic kidney disease and potassium of 5.3 mmol/L. Select antihypertensive treatment based on repeat potassium and renal function; avoid empiric therapy that could further increase potassium until reviewed. Recommend ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular and renal risk assessment. Follow up promptly for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, or vision changes. Return to clinic within 1–2 weeks for blood pressure and laboratory review.
