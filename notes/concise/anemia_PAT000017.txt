Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective
Adriana Wells is a 64-year-old woman with a 19-year history of anemia, chronic inflammatory bowel disease for 9 years, rheumatoid arthritis for 26 years, and heart failure for 2 years. She is a nonsmoker. She reports headache and pallor but denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, and reduced exercise tolerance. No interval anemia-related cardiopulmonary symptoms reported.

Objective
Height 163 cm, weight 78.9 kg, BMI 29.7 kg/m². Blood pressure 131/76 mmHg, pulse 90 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air. Pallor noted. Cardiovascular and respiratory examinations are otherwise unremarkable.

Laboratory data: Hemoglobin 9.3 g/dL, hematocrit 27.8%, RBC 3.27 million/µL, MCV 85 fL, serum iron 35 µg/dL, ferritin 404 ng/mL, TIBC 203 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 17.2%.

Assessment
Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of longstanding rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory bowel disease. Low serum iron and TIBC with elevated ferritin support impaired iron availability related to chronic inflammation. She is currently hemodynamically stable without significant fatigue or cardiopulmonary limitation. Heart failure is a relevant comorbidity requiring avoidance of worsening anemia-related physiologic stress.

Plan
Obtain interval CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with renal function, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers to further characterize persistent anemia. Continue evaluation and optimization of underlying inflammatory conditions with attention to gastrointestinal blood loss if clinically indicated. Consider iron replacement strategy based on repeat indices and inflammatory disease activity; hematology consultation may be appropriate if anemia persists or worsens. Advise prompt evaluation for increasing dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, or symptomatic decline.
