Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective
Isabella Parker is a 44-year-old woman with a 9-year history of essential hypertension, currently not under active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. Medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 3 years, hyperlipidemia for 11 years, and coronary artery disease for 1 year. She does not smoke.

Objective
Height 158 cm; weight 70.1 kg; BMI 28.1 kg/m². Blood pressure 149/94 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%. Serum sodium 145 mmol/L and potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with blood pressure above goal on today’s reading. No symptoms concerning for hypertensive emergency. Cardiovascular risk is increased by diabetes, hyperlipidemia, coronary artery disease, and overweight status.

Plan
Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log and repeat office assessment in 2–4 weeks. Discuss initiation of antihypertensive therapy given persistent hypertension and cardiovascular comorbidities; medication selection should account for diabetes, coronary artery disease, and renal function. Recommend reduced sodium intake, weight reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Obtain/review renal function and urine testing before or during treatment initiation; consider ECG and updated lipid assessment as part of cardiovascular risk evaluation. Continue routine management of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and coronary artery disease. Seek urgent care for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly elevated blood pressure.
