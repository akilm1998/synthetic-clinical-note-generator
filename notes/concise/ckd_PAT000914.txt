Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective
Catherine Nichols is a 78-year-old woman, nonsmoker, with a 33-year history of chronic kidney disease, hypertension for 16 years, and gout for 13 years. She reports persistent fatigue. She denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No current medication regimen is documented.

Objective
BP 168/98 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respirations 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%. Height 178 cm, weight 66.2 kg, BMI 20.9 kg/m².
Creatinine 3.3 mg/dL, BUN 68 mg/dL, eGFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with significantly reduced renal function and fatigue, currently requiring renewed evaluation and active management. Hypertension is markedly elevated today and is an important modifiable contributor to renal disease progression. History of gout.

Plan
Recommend prompt medication reconciliation and initiation or adjustment of an individualized blood pressure regimen, with home BP monitoring and close reassessment.
Obtain repeat renal function and serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact PTH as clinically appropriate.
Recommend renal ultrasound and nephrology referral for evaluation of advanced chronic kidney disease and planning of ongoing renal care.
Review renal dosing and avoidance of NSAIDs and other nephrotoxic agents; reinforce appropriate hydration and blood pressure control.
Follow up closely for worsening fatigue, nausea, pruritus, edema, dyspnea, or changes in urine output.
