Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Edward Holland is a 74-year-old man with a 10-year history of chronic kidney disease presenting for outpatient follow-up. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. He does not smoke. Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 13 years, secondary hyperparathyroidism for 25 years, and hyperlipidemia for 38 years. No current medication regimen is documented.

Objective

Height 178 cm, weight 91.2 kg, BMI 28.8 kg/m². Blood pressure 143/88 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air. Serum creatinine 4.03 mg/dL, BUN 72 mg/dL, and eGFR 15 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. Although currently without uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, disease control requires prompt reassessment and active outpatient management. Blood pressure is mildly elevated. History is also notable for secondary hyperparathyroidism, coronary artery disease, and hyperlipidemia.

Plan

Discussed the importance of re-engaging in regular kidney disease care. Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation given current renal function and consideration of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate. Obtain repeat renal function and serum electrolytes, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone; consider renal ultrasound if not recently performed. Review blood pressure, kidney-protective therapy, medication dosing, and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents at follow-up. Counsel to seek urgent care for new shortness of breath, progressive swelling, markedly reduced urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, or chest pain. Close outpatient follow-up recommended.
