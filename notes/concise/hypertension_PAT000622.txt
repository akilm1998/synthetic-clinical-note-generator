Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective
Arthur Nichols is a 31-year-old man with approximately one year of essential hypertension and known coronary artery disease. He is not currently receiving documented antihypertensive management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. He does not smoke.

Objective
Blood pressure is 155/90 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, and oxygen saturation 98% on room air. Height is 190 cm, weight 100.4 kg, BMI 27.8 kg/m². Serum sodium is 137 mmol/L and potassium is 3.5 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with persistent elevation at today’s visit and suboptimal current disease control. No symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Mild hypokalemia is not present; potassium is low-normal. Coronary artery disease is a relevant comorbidity.

Plan
Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management, home blood pressure monitoring, reduced sodium intake, regular physical activity, weight optimization, and moderation of alcohol if applicable. Recommend confirming persistent elevation with home or repeat office measurements and initiating an appropriate antihypertensive regimen, with medication selection guided by his coronary artery disease and repeat electrolyte assessment. Review cardiovascular risk factors and obtain appropriate baseline evaluation, including ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis, if not recently completed. Advised urgent evaluation for new chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly elevated blood pressure. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure review and treatment adjustment.
