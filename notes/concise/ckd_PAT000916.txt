Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease and hypertension.

Subjective

Alice Mathew is a 38-year-old woman with a 14-year history of chronic kidney disease, hypertension for 6 years, anemia of chronic disease, and secondary hyperparathyroidism. She reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. She does not smoke. No current medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Height 166 cm, weight 81.6 kg, BMI 29.6 kg/m². Blood pressure 151/92 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%. Recent laboratory results show creatinine 5.93 mg/dL, BUN 66 mg/dL, and eGFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine, currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms. Hypertension is above goal. Comorbid anemia of chronic disease and secondary hyperparathyroidism require ongoing renal-focused management.

Plan

Discussed the seriousness of the current renal function and need for prompt nephrology evaluation, including assessment for renal replacement therapy and dialysis planning as clinically indicated. Recommend repeat renal function and electrolyte panel, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone; consider renal ultrasound as part of updated evaluation. Review and address blood pressure management with renal-appropriate therapy. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications, and maintain appropriate dietary sodium and fluid guidance pending nephrology assessment. Seek emergency care for new shortness of breath, worsening swelling, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or markedly decreased urination. Close follow-up arranged after urgent renal evaluation.
