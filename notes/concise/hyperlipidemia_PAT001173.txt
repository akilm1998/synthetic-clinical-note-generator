Chief Complaint

Annual review of longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Leo Davidson is a 68-year-old man with approximately 35 years of untreated hyperlipidemia. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. He does not smoke. Comorbidities include obesity for approximately 50 years, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 8 years, and chronic kidney disease for 3 years. No current lipid-lowering medication is provided.

Objective

Height 175 cm; weight 101.7 kg; BMI 33.2 kg/m². Blood pressure 133/86 mmHg, pulse 72/min, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Reported lipid profile: total cholesterol 302 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 197 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 44 mg/dL, triglycerides 303 mg/dL.

Assessment

Marked mixed dyslipidemia with severe LDL elevation and moderate hypertriglyceridemia, longstanding and currently untreated. No reported cardiovascular or cutaneous symptoms. Risk is increased by age, obesity, chronic kidney disease, and fatty liver disease.

Plan

Discussed substantially increased lifetime atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk and the importance of active treatment. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after review of contraindications, medication interactions, and patient preference. If LDL response is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, consider addition of ezetimibe and specialist lipid management.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation, with consideration of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for risk refinement. Review for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and monitor hepatic and renal status clinically.

Counseled on a Mediterranean-style diet with reduced saturated fat, refined carbohydrates, and alcohol; gradual increase in aerobic activity as tolerated; and weight reduction. Continue tobacco avoidance. Preventive care should include age-appropriate immunizations and recommended colorectal, prostate, and other cardiovascular risk screening through primary care. Follow up after repeat lipid testing to assess response and adherence.
