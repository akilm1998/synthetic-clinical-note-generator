Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Penelope Long is a 98-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of longstanding anemia, documented for approximately 54 years. She reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or noticeable pallor. She has a history of malignancy of approximately 45 years’ duration and continues to smoke. No anemia-directed treatment is documented.

Objective

Height 175 cm; weight 89.7 kg; BMI 29.3 kg/m².

Vital signs: BP 129/80 mmHg, pulse 90 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Cardiopulmonary examination is stable, with normal cardiovascular and respiratory findings. No pallor noted.

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin 8.9 g/dL, hematocrit 25.9%, RBC 2.82 million/µL, MCV 92 fL. Serum iron 39 µg/dL, ferritin 368 ng/mL, TIBC 269 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 14.5%.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation associated with longstanding malignancy. Hemoglobin remains significantly reduced, although she is currently clinically asymptomatic and hemodynamically stable. Iron indices show low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin, supporting functional iron restriction rather than straightforward iron depletion.

Malignancy, longstanding. This remains the major relevant chronic comorbidity in the context of her anemia.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Obtain interval CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers to further characterize contributors to the chronic anemia.

Review anemia and malignancy management with the treating oncology team. Consider iron replacement strategy, including intravenous iron if clinically appropriate, and erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy only after confirming the underlying indication and treatment goals.

Monitor for new fatigue, dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, bleeding, or reduced functional capacity. Continue routine outpatient follow-up.
