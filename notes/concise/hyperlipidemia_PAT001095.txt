Annual review; follow-up of hyperlipidemia and chronic conditions.

Total cholesterol 319 mg/dL, LDL-C 215 mg/dL, HDL-C 32 mg/dL, triglycerides 362 mg/dL.

Assessment:
Severe mixed dyslipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and triglycerides and low HDL-C, untreated for approximately 8 years. Given established coronary artery disease, lipid lowering is strongly indicated despite absence of current ischemic symptoms.
Obesity, BMI 30.8 kg/m².
Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.
Coronary artery disease, currently without reported chest discomfort or exertional claudication.

Plan:
Discussed substantially increased cardiovascular risk and importance of active lipid management. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, with treatment selection after medication and contraindication review. Consider addition of ezetimibe if LDL-C remains above goal; specialist-directed therapy may be needed if response is inadequate.
Repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation and assess liver enzymes as clinically appropriate, given NAFLD. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and measurement of ApoB and lipoprotein(a), particularly in the setting of premature coronary artery disease.
Counseled on Mediterranean-style diet, reduction of saturated fats and refined carbohydrates, weight reduction, and regular aerobic/resistance exercise as tolerated. Continue tobacco avoidance.
Recommend ongoing coronary artery disease risk-factor management and prompt evaluation for new chest pain, dyspnea, or exercise intolerance.
Address preventive care during annual visits, including diabetes screening, immunization review, and age-appropriate cancer screening. Follow up after lipid reassessment or sooner for cardiovascular symptoms.
