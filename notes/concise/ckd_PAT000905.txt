Chief Complaint

Annual review and chronic kidney disease follow-up.

Subjective

Kevin Peterson is a 64-year-old man, 170 cm, 72.2 kg, BMI 25.0 kg/m², presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 26-year history of chronic kidney disease, currently not under active management. Reports fatigue. Denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 2 years, gout for 12 years, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 9 years. He currently smokes.

Objective

Blood pressure 154/87 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL. BUN 78 mg/dL. eGFR 9 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and biochemical evidence of significant azotemia. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction. Although he currently denies uremic, fluid-overload, or respiratory symptoms, the degree of renal impairment requires prompt specialist assessment and active management.

Hypertension, suboptimally controlled today at 154/87 mmHg.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 2-year history; glycemic control data are not provided.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, 9-year history, in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Gout, 12-year history.

Active tobacco use.

Preventive health maintenance reviewed as part of annual assessment.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management, assessment of renal replacement therapy needs, and timely planning for dialysis access or other appropriate modality if indicated. Obtain updated serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact PTH, and consider renal ultrasound as part of renal assessment.

Review and address blood pressure control and kidney-protective diabetic management with the treating clinician; avoid NSAIDs and other nephrotoxins. Monitor for worsening fatigue, nausea, vomiting, pruritus, confusion, progressive weakness, reduced urine output, edema, or shortness of breath; seek urgent care if these develop.

Continue coordinated management of diabetes, gout, and secondary hyperparathyroidism with renal specialists. Smoking cessation strongly advised. Review age-appropriate immunizations, cancer screening, cardiovascular risk reduction, nutrition, and exercise at preventive follow-up.

Follow up promptly after nephrology assessment and updated investigations.
