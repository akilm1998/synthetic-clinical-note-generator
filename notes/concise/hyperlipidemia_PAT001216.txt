Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Liam Matthews is a 97-year-old man seen for follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 60 years and not currently under active lipid-lowering management. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. Continues to smoke. History is also notable for hypertension for 61 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 18 years. No interval cardiovascular symptoms reported.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 148/91 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Height 178 cm, weight 67.5 kg, BMI 21.3 kg/m².

Recent lipid panel:
Total cholesterol 366 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 278 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 35 mg/dL
Triglycerides 267 mg/dL

Assessment

Severely elevated mixed lipid profile with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, currently inadequately controlled. No reported anginal symptoms, claudication, dizziness, or cutaneous lipid deposits. Ongoing tobacco use and hypertension increase cardiovascular risk. History of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease noted.

Plan

Discussed substantially increased cardiovascular risk associated with current lipid levels and smoking. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of lipid-lowering therapy, such as a statin, with consideration of age, overall goals of care, potential benefits, and tolerability. Consider repeat fasting lipid panel and assessment for secondary contributors before or after treatment initiation as clinically appropriate.

Counseled on reducing saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates, maintaining regular activity as tolerated, and avoiding tobacco; smoking cessation encouraged.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and coordinate management of hypertension. Follow up after treatment discussion and updated lipid assessment, or sooner for chest discomfort, new exertional leg pain, or other concerning symptoms.
