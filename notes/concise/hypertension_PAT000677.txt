Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Lauren Lawson is a 93-year-old woman with a 16-year history of essential hypertension, currently without active treatment documented. She reports fatigue but denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or epistaxis. History is also notable for coronary artery disease for approximately 15 years and hyperlipidemia for approximately 45 years. She continues to smoke. No interval cardiovascular or neurologic symptoms reported.

Objective

Height 180 cm; weight 76.1 kg; BMI 23.5 kg/m².
BP 170/95 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.
Serum sodium 137 mmol/L; potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, inadequately controlled based on office blood pressure of 170/95 mmHg. No reported symptoms suggestive of acute end-organ involvement. Longstanding disease and coexisting coronary artery disease increase cardiovascular risk.
2. Coronary artery disease, longstanding; currently without chest pain or dyspnea.
3. Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.
4. Tobacco use.
5. Fatigue.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated blood pressure and need for active treatment and close monitoring. Repeat blood pressure measurement and home blood pressure log recommended. Discuss initiation of antihypertensive therapy, with regimen selected cautiously given advanced age and coronary artery disease; review renal function and electrolytes before and after treatment as clinically appropriate. Counsel on reduced sodium intake, regular activity as tolerated, and smoking cessation. Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management. Seek urgent care for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, focal neurologic symptoms, or visual changes. Close outpatient follow-up recommended for blood pressure reassessment.
