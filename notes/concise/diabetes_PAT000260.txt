Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood glucose.

Subjective

Hannah Simmons is a 78-year-old woman with an 8-year history of type 2 diabetes, currently without active management. Recent laboratory testing showed HbA1c 9.5% and fasting glucose 221 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, fatigue, unintentional weight loss, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling. No acute symptoms reported.

Relevant comorbidities include long-standing depression and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. She currently smokes. Weight is stable at 50.6 kg; BMI 21.6 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 140/76 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respirations 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95% on room air. Height 153 cm, weight 50.6 kg, BMI 21.6 kg/m².

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without complications, currently markedly above glycemic goal based on HbA1c 9.5% and fasting glucose 221 mg/dL. She is asymptomatic without reported infectious, neurologic, visual, or constitutional complications. Given her age and normal BMI, treatment should be individualized with attention to hypoglycemia risk, nutrition, functional status, and comorbid depression and fatty liver disease.

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Depression and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding; relevant to adherence and overall care planning.

Plan

Arrange prompt outpatient diabetes management visit to review treatment options and establish a safe glucose-lowering regimen; no current medication regimen is documented. Encourage consistent meals, reduction of concentrated carbohydrates, hydration, and home glucose monitoring if feasible.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin, fasting lipid profile, and diabetes-related eye and foot screening as part of re-engagement in care. Review hypoglycemia precautions if pharmacologic treatment is initiated.

Discuss smoking cessation and assess mood, medication adherence barriers, and available support at follow-up.

Seek urgent evaluation for vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, confusion, marked weakness, worsening polyuria/polydipsia, or persistently very high home glucose readings.
