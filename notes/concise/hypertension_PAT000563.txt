Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective
Hazel Fisher is a 45-year-old woman with a 7-year history of essential hypertension, currently not under active management. She reports intermittent dizziness and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, or epistaxis. She does not smoke. Comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 8 years and hyperlipidemia for 11 years.

Objective
Height 175 cm, weight 91.0 kg, BMI 29.7 kg/m². Blood pressure 141/99 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air. Serum sodium 144 mmol/L and potassium 5.2 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension remains above goal, particularly with elevated diastolic pressure, in the setting of limited disease management. Current dizziness and fatigue are mild and without symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Potassium is mildly elevated at 5.2 mmol/L and should be reassessed before initiating therapies that may further increase potassium. Additional cardiovascular risk is conferred by diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and excess weight.

Plan
Discussed sodium reduction, regular physical activity, weight reduction, and moderation of alcohol intake. Begin home blood pressure monitoring with a written log. Repeat serum potassium and obtain renal function testing before selecting antihypertensive therapy; review treatment options at close follow-up. Recommend ECG, urinalysis, and updated lipid assessment as part of cardiovascular risk evaluation. Continue coordinated management of diabetes and hyperlipidemia. Follow up within 2–4 weeks, or sooner for persistent worsening symptoms. Seek urgent care for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure.
