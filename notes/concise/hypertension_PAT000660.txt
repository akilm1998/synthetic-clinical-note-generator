Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Ian Lawson is a 75-year-old man with a 10-year history of hypertension, currently without active management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. He does not smoke. History is also notable for hyperlipidemia for 25 years and coronary artery disease for 5 years.

Objective

Blood pressure 159/99 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%. Height 188 cm, weight 86.6 kg, BMI 24.5 kg/m². Serum sodium 144 mmol/L and potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently uncontrolled based on today’s elevated office reading. Patient is asymptomatic without features suggesting acute hypertensive emergency.

2. Coronary artery disease, established history, increasing the importance of consistent blood pressure control.

3. Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the risks of persistent uncontrolled blood pressure, particularly in the setting of coronary artery disease. Recommend confirming blood pressure with repeat office measurements and home monitoring, along with initiation of an appropriate antihypertensive treatment plan after review of the patient’s preferences and full medication history. Encourage a low-sodium diet, regular physical activity as tolerated, and moderation of alcohol intake. Recommend ECG, urinalysis, lipid profile, and periodic renal function/electrolyte monitoring as part of cardiovascular risk assessment and hypertension management. Reviewed emergency precautions for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly worsening blood pressure. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment.
