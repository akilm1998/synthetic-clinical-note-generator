Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Peter Greene is a 91-year-old man seen for follow-up of longstanding essential hypertension, present for approximately 22 years and not currently under active management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. Reports intermittent palpitations and fatigue. He does not smoke. Comorbid coronary artery disease and hyperlipidemia have been present for approximately 39 and 44 years, respectively.

Objective

Blood pressure 157/96 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%. Height 188 cm, weight 82.4 kg, BMI 23.3 kg/m².

Serum sodium 142 mmol/L. Serum potassium 4.2 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure in the setting of long duration and no current active management. No symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency. Intermittent palpitations and fatigue noted. Coronary artery disease and hyperlipidemia are important cardiovascular comorbidities.

Plan

Discussed blood pressure control and cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log and review of readings at follow-up. Discuss initiation of an age-appropriate antihypertensive regimen after confirming out-of-office readings and reviewing overall cardiovascular status. Recommend ECG evaluation for palpitations, particularly given known coronary artery disease. Continue lifestyle measures including limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular activity as tolerated, and avoiding excess alcohol. Seek urgent care for chest pain, new shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly elevated blood pressure. Follow up promptly for treatment planning and reassessment.
