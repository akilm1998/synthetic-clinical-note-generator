Chief Complaint

Initial evaluation of elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Mia Collins is a 44-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient consultation regarding hypertension. She has had hypertension for approximately 4 years and is not currently receiving active management. Blood pressure today is 144/98 mmHg. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis.

Her medical history is notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 5 years ago and hyperlipidemia diagnosed approximately 6 years ago. She reports current tobacco use. No antihypertensive or other medications are documented for this visit. Dietary and physical activity history is not otherwise provided.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 144/98 mmHg; pulse 80 bpm; respiratory rate 16/min; temperature 36.7°C; oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Height 171 cm; weight 86.0 kg; BMI 29.4 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: Serum sodium 136 mmol/L; potassium 4.9 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and approximately 4 years without active management. She is currently asymptomatic, without features suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Her cardiovascular risk is increased by concomitant type 2 diabetes, hyperlipidemia, active tobacco use, and elevated BMI. Serum sodium is within the expected range; potassium is at the upper end of the reference range and should be considered when selecting or monitoring therapy that may further increase potassium.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 5-year duration.

Hyperlipidemia, approximately 6-year duration.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Confirm blood pressure control with repeat office measurements using appropriate technique and recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log. Review readings at follow-up to distinguish sustained hypertension from isolated office elevation and to guide treatment intensity.

Discuss initiation of pharmacologic antihypertensive therapy given the degree and duration of elevation, diabetes, and overall cardiovascular risk. Selection should account for renal status, potassium level, comorbid diabetes, tolerability, and potential contraindications. If a renin-angiotensin system agent is considered, monitor serum potassium and renal function after initiation or dose changes.

Counsel regarding sodium reduction, weight reduction, regular aerobic physical activity as tolerated, moderation of alcohol intake, and smoking cessation. Encourage a diet emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and reduced processed foods.

Recommend baseline cardiovascular and hypertension evaluation including electrocardiogram, lipid profile, and urinalysis. Continued diabetes and lipid risk-factor management should be coordinated as part of comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction.

Provide education regarding urgent evaluation for severe headache, chest pain, acute shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up after a period of home blood pressure monitoring, sooner if readings are persistently markedly elevated or symptoms develop.
