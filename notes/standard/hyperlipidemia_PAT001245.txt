Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Michael Brooks is a 52-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 10 years and currently without active pharmacologic management. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. He does not smoke.

He has a history of type 2 diabetes mellitus for 18 years. No interval cardiovascular symptoms are reported. Current lipid results were reviewed: total cholesterol 341 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 220 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 41 mg/dL, and triglycerides 399 mg/dL.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 121/75 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 183 cm, weight 89.1 kg, BMI 26.6 kg/m².

No abnormal physical examination findings were provided. Patient is without reported symptoms suggestive of active ischemic or peripheral arterial disease.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding and presently untreated. Lipid profile demonstrates marked elevation of total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with significant hypertriglyceridemia and low-normal HDL cholesterol. The LDL level of 220 mg/dL is substantially elevated and, together with longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus, confers increased lifetime atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk despite the absence of current symptoms. No reported xanthomas, xanthelasma, chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding for 18 years; current glycemic control and treatment regimen were not reviewed today.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the importance of active treatment for cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after reviewing contraindications, potential adverse effects, and patient preferences. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, consider addition of ezetimibe and further lipid-lowering evaluation.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, limitation of refined carbohydrates and alcohol given the triglyceride elevation, increased dietary fiber, and regular aerobic and resistance exercise as tolerated. Continue avoidance of tobacco. Weight is mildly above ideal range; modest weight reduction may improve overall cardiometabolic risk.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after initiation or adjustment of therapy to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to severe dyslipidemia and measurement of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) for additional risk assessment. Reinforce coordinated management of diabetes, including optimization of glycemic control, as this may also improve triglycerides and reduce cardiovascular risk.

Return for lipid and treatment-response follow-up after medication initiation and repeat testing, or sooner for new chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dyspnea, or other concerning cardiovascular symptoms.
