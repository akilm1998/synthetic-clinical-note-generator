Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Allison Hamilton is an 80-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 34 years. She has not been receiving active management for her blood pressure. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. No acute hypertensive symptoms are reported.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 47 years and chronic kidney disease for 16 years. She does not smoke. Current weight is 87.2 kg at a height of 179 cm, with BMI 27.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 165/99 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory data: Sodium 145 mmol/L, potassium 4.0 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension with persistent marked elevation in an asymptomatic 80-year-old woman and no current active management. Long duration of disease, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease increase her cardiovascular and renal risk. Available sodium and potassium values are within acceptable ranges for consideration of antihypertensive therapy, although additional renal and metabolic assessment is needed before finalizing treatment.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

BMI 27.2 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed that the current blood pressure is above goal and warrants active treatment rather than observation alone. Recommend confirming the elevation with repeat office measurements and home blood pressure monitoring, including a written log of readings and technique review.

Recommend initiation of antihypertensive therapy after review of current renal function, medication history, contraindications, and patient preferences. Given the history of diabetes and chronic kidney disease, an ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker may be considered if clinically appropriate, with monitoring of renal function and potassium after initiation or dose changes. Additional therapy may be required if blood pressure remains elevated.

Recommend dietary sodium reduction, regular activity as tolerated, and maintenance of a healthy weight. Review diabetes and kidney disease management in conjunction with blood pressure control.

Recommend urinalysis, lipid profile, and renal function assessment as part of cardiovascular and kidney risk evaluation. An ECG is also appropriate for assessment of longstanding hypertension.

Reviewed symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, acute visual changes, neurologic symptoms, or markedly higher blood pressure readings. Close outpatient reassessment is recommended after home readings are obtained and treatment is initiated or adjusted.
