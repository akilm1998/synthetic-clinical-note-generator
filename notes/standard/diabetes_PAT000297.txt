Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for diabetes management.

Subjective

Ruby Marshall is a 41-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 14 years ago. Diabetes has not been actively managed recently. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, unintentional weight loss, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has a history of hypertension for approximately 5 years and obstructive sleep apnea for approximately 11 years. She currently smokes. No diabetes medications or other active medication regimen are provided for review today.

Objective

Height 163 cm; weight 60.6 kg; BMI 22.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 129/89 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Recent laboratory results:
Hemoglobin A1c 9.2%.
Fasting glucose 204 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications

Diabetes is substantially above goal based on HbA1c of 9.2% and fasting glucose of 204 mg/dL, despite the absence of current hyperglycemic, neuropathic, or infectious symptoms. The duration of disease is approximately 14 years, and the lack of active management places her at increased risk for future microvascular and cardiovascular complications. Her BMI is 22.8 kg/m², without current obesity.

2. Hypertension

Blood pressure today is 129/89 mmHg, with mildly elevated diastolic pressure. Diabetes increases her long-term cardiovascular and renal risk.

3. Obstructive sleep apnea

Longstanding history, approximately 11 years. Ongoing management is important given the associated cardiometabolic risk.

4. Tobacco use

Current smoking is an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor, particularly in the setting of diabetes and hypertension.

Plan

1. Diabetes
Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the need for active treatment rather than observation alone. Recommend prompt initiation or re-establishment of a structured diabetes treatment plan, including pharmacologic therapy after review of contraindications and renal function. Metformin would be a reasonable first-line option if clinically appropriate; additional therapy may be needed depending on response and overall risk profile.

Counseled regarding consistent carbohydrate-controlled nutrition, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, regular physical activity, and home glucose monitoring as clinically appropriate. Reviewed symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and advised earlier evaluation for worsening thirst, urination, vomiting, dehydration, visual changes, or marked weakness.

Recommend assessment of renal status with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and completion of routine diabetes eye and foot evaluations. Continue monitoring for neuropathic symptoms, skin breakdown, and visual changes. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval to assess response to treatment.

2. Hypertension
Continue close blood pressure monitoring and reinforce dietary sodium reduction, regular activity, smoking cessation, and diabetes risk-factor control. Review antihypertensive management at follow-up in the context of overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

3. Obstructive sleep apnea
Continue attention to effective OSA management and reassess adherence to prescribed therapy if applicable, given the longstanding history and coexistence of hypertension and diabetes.

4. Tobacco use
Strongly advised smoking cessation. Discussed that cessation would substantially reduce cardiovascular and microvascular risk. Offer behavioral and pharmacologic cessation support at a subsequent visit.

Follow-up

Arrange close outpatient follow-up to establish diabetes treatment, review recommended renal and cardiovascular risk assessment, and reassess glycemic control and blood pressure.
