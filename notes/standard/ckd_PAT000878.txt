Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Autumn West is a 76-year-old woman, height 159 cm, weight 75.3 kg, BMI 29.8 kg/m², presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 33 years. She reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath.

Her history is also notable for longstanding hypertension of approximately 40 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 7 years, and anemia of chronic disease for 4 years. She continues to smoke. No interval uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 162/94 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory data:
Serum creatinine 4.07 mg/dL
BUN 81 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m²

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal filtration and significant azotemia. Although she is currently asymptomatic and reports no edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, or urinary change, the creatinine, BUN, and eGFR indicate advanced renal dysfunction requiring close medical and nephrology oversight. The longstanding hypertension and diabetes are important potential contributors. Current blood pressure is above goal and represents an ongoing risk for further renal and cardiovascular injury.

Hypertension, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled based on today’s office measurement.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 7 years; current glycemic control data are not available in the information provided.

Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding; no current blood count is available for assessment today.

Overweight by BMI. Active tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease, assessment of renal replacement therapy needs, and individualized education regarding future treatment options. This recommendation is based on the markedly reduced eGFR and elevated BUN, despite the absence of current uremic symptoms.

Repeat renal function and serum electrolyte testing, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate to assess complications of chronic kidney disease. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously performed or if the underlying renal anatomy has not been evaluated.

Review and reconcile all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplemental medications at the next contact. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a treating clinician. Medication dosing should be adjusted for renal function.

Address persistent hypertension with confirmation of blood pressure measurements and development or adjustment of a renal-appropriate antihypertensive regimen. Encourage home blood pressure monitoring if feasible.

Continue coordinated management of diabetes and cardiovascular risk factors, with attention to renal-appropriate dietary counseling, sodium restriction, and avoidance of excess protein intake unless otherwise advised by nephrology or a renal dietitian.

Monitor for new nausea, vomiting, worsening fatigue, confusion, generalized pruritus, muscle cramps, progressive reduction in urine output, edema, or shortness of breath. Urgent evaluation is warranted if these symptoms develop, particularly if accompanied by chest pain or severe weakness.

Counsel regarding smoking cessation, given the adverse renal and cardiovascular effects of ongoing tobacco use.
