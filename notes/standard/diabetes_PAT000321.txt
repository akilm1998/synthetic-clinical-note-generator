Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Mia Collins is a 63-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. Glycemic control is currently suboptimal. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has a 21-year history of hypertension. She currently smokes. No diabetes treatment regimen is documented for review today.

Objective

Height 162 cm; weight 59.8 kg; BMI 22.8 kg/m². Blood pressure 137/83 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Hemoglobin A1c 9.7%. Fasting glucose 267 mg/dL.

She is without reported hyperglycemic, neuropathic, or infectious symptoms. No diabetes-related complications are reported.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with marked hyperglycemia and inadequate glycemic control. Hemoglobin A1c of 9.7% and fasting glucose of 267 mg/dL indicate need for active treatment intensification despite the absence of symptoms. Current BMI is 22.8 kg/m², without obesity.

Hypertension, longstanding, with current blood pressure 137/83 mmHg.

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated A1c and fasting glucose, risks of progressive microvascular and cardiovascular complications, and the importance of consistent diabetes management. Recommend initiation of an evidence-based glucose-lowering regimen, with medication selection guided by renal function, contraindications, tolerability, cost, and patient preference. Given the degree of hyperglycemia, combination therapy or escalation to injectable therapy should be considered if adequate improvement is not achieved promptly.

Obtain renal function, including serum creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess kidney involvement and guide medication selection. Recommend fasting lipid evaluation and routine diabetes complication surveillance, including a comprehensive foot examination and dilated retinal examination.

Counseled regarding carbohydrate-conscious nutrition, regular physical activity as tolerated, home glucose monitoring, recognition of symptoms of significant hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia, and avoidance of tobacco. Strongly encouraged smoking cessation; cessation support and pharmacologic options can be discussed at follow-up.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and address cardiovascular risk reduction in the context of diabetes and longstanding hypertension. Follow up after initiation or adjustment of therapy with repeat glycemic assessment in approximately 3 months, or sooner for symptomatic hyperglycemia or persistently markedly elevated home glucose readings.
