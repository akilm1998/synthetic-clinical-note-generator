Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Riley Benson is a 66-year-old man, 186 cm tall and 99.3 kg (BMI 28.7 kg/m²), with a 22-year history of anemia in the setting of chronic infection for 25 years and chronic kidney disease for 13 years. He is a current smoker. He reports headache and appears clinically pale. He denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance.

No active anemia-directed medication regimen is documented for review today; therefore, treatment effectiveness, adherence, and medication tolerance cannot be assessed. There is no reported history in the provided information of oral iron, intravenous iron, or erythropoiesis-stimulating agent use.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 139/79 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Physical Examination: Pallor noted. Cardiovascular examination is normal. Respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory Data: Hemoglobin 9.3 g/dL, hematocrit 28.4%, RBC 3.3 million/µL, MCV 86 fL. Serum iron 22 µg/dL, ferritin 153 ng/mL, TIBC 223 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 9.9%.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation, with likely contributions from chronic kidney disease and longstanding chronic infection. Low serum iron and transferrin saturation with preserved ferritin and low TIBC support iron-restricted erythropoiesis in the setting of chronic inflammation rather than a straightforward iron-deficiency pattern. Hemoglobin is 9.3 g/dL with associated pallor and headache, although he denies cardiopulmonary or significant constitutional symptoms. No tachycardia or hypoxemia is present.

Plan

Review and reconcile all current medications at the next contact, specifically confirming whether any oral or intravenous iron or erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy has been started. If treatment is initiated, monitor adherence, clinical response, and tolerance, including gastrointestinal effects with oral iron and infusion-related reactions with intravenous therapy.

Obtain or trend a complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, and assess vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the chronic anemia. Continue evaluation and management of the underlying chronic kidney disease and chronic infection, as control of these conditions is central to improving anemia.

Consider iron replacement strategy based on the overall clinical context and repeat indices, recognizing the markedly low transferrin saturation despite ferritin of 153 ng/mL. Counsel regarding smoking cessation given its adverse impact on overall cardiopulmonary and vascular health.

Follow up after medication reconciliation and repeat laboratory assessment, or sooner for worsening headache, new shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, or marked weakness.
