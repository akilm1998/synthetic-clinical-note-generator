Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with significant hyperglycemia based on HbA1c of 9.5% and fasting glucose of 252 mg/dL. The patient is currently asymptomatic, but glycemic control is inadequate for a 21-year history of diabetes.

Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 7 years; renal function should be considered when selecting or adjusting diabetes therapy.

Dyslipidemia, present for approximately 14 years.

Plan

Review and reconcile all current prescription, over-the-counter, and supplemental medications at the next medication discussion, including any prior diabetes therapies, dosing, adherence, and adverse effects. Given the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose, prompt initiation or intensification of glucose-lowering therapy is recommended, with medication selection and dosing guided by kidney function and treatment tolerance.

Recommend monitoring fasting and postprandial glucose as appropriate and bringing glucose records to follow-up. Reinforce consistent carbohydrate intake, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, and regular physical activity as tolerated. His BMI is 27.1 kg/m²; modest weight reduction may improve glycemic control.

Recommend obtaining renal function assessment, including serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, before finalizing medication adjustments. Continue routine diabetes complication surveillance, including foot assessment and retinal evaluation. Follow up promptly after medication reconciliation and laboratory review to reassess glycemic control and treatment tolerance.
