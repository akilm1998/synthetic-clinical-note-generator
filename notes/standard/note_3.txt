Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Joseph Bailey is a 93-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 13 years ago. He reports overall stable control and denies symptomatic hyperglycemia, including excessive thirst, polyuria, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, blurred vision, or unusual fatigue. He denies hypoglycemic symptoms such as shakiness, diaphoresis, confusion, or syncope. No recent falls or hospitalizations.

He denies chest pain, dyspnea, abdominal pain, nausea, or lower-extremity swelling. No foot sores, pain, numbness, or tingling reported. He does not smoke. History is also notable for obstructive sleep apnea and depression, both longstanding; no acute mood or sleep-related concerns reported today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 132/68 mmHg, pulse 72 beats/min, respirations 16/min, temperature 97.7°F, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 183 cm, weight 78.4 kg, BMI 23.4 kg/m².

General: Alert, pleasant elderly man in no acute distress. Ambulates independently.

HEENT: Oral mucous membranes moist. No acute visual complaints.

Cardiovascular: Regular rate and rhythm. No murmur appreciated. Peripheral pulses palpable.

Respiratory: Lungs clear to auscultation bilaterally. No respiratory distress.

Abdomen: Soft and nontender.

Extremities: No edema. Feet warm and well perfused. Skin intact without ulcers, calluses, or diabetic dermopathy.

Neurologic: Alert and oriented. Light-touch sensation grossly intact in both feet. No focal weakness.

Laboratory Results: Hemoglobin A1c 7.1%. Fasting glucose 138 mg/dL. Renal function and urine albumin screening are due for routine monitoring; results are not available for review today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without complications. Glycemic control is reasonable for age and clinical context, with A1c 7.1% and fasting glucose 138 mg/dL. No reported hypoglycemia, neuropathic symptoms, foot ulceration, or other current diabetic complications.

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding, without acute concern reported today.

Depression, longstanding, currently without reported exacerbation.

Plan

Continue current diabetes management pending medication reconciliation. Review adherence, dietary intake, and home glucose readings at the next visit. Avoid overly aggressive glycemic treatment given advanced age and risk of hypoglycemia.

Encourage a balanced diet with consistent carbohydrate intake, adequate hydration, and physical activity as tolerated. Continue routine daily foot inspection and report any skin breakdown, redness, drainage, or new sensory changes.

Obtain comprehensive metabolic panel, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and fasting lipid profile. Continue periodic hemoglobin A1c monitoring; repeat in approximately 3 months.

Recommend annual dilated eye examination and routine dental care. Review immunization status, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, and shingles vaccines.

Medication list and home glucose monitoring supplies to be reconciled at follow-up. Return in 3 months, or sooner for symptomatic hyperglycemia, hypoglycemia, new visual changes, or foot concerns.
