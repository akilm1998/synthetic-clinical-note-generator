Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of anemia.

Subjective

Gavin Holland is an 84-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding anemia, recorded as present for 54. He reports no fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, or noticeable pallor. Anemia has not been actively managed. He has type 2 diabetes mellitus of 26 years’ duration. He currently smokes. No medication regimen, allergies, bleeding history, or other associated history is provided.

Objective

Height 168 cm, weight 74.2 kg, BMI 26.3 kg/m². Blood pressure 130/79 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent laboratory results: hemoglobin 8.4 g/dL, hematocrit 26.0%, RBC count 2.8 million/µL, MCV 93 fL, serum iron 24 µg/dL, ferritin 167 ng/mL, TIBC 269 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 8.9%.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation, currently without reported symptomatic limitations. The anemia is significant and normocytic. Low serum iron and markedly reduced transferrin saturation with ferritin in the normal-to-elevated range support impaired iron availability, although concurrent iron deficiency cannot be excluded without further evaluation. The underlying chronic inflammatory, renal, or other systemic contributor has not been established in the information available. Given his age and degree of anemia, additional evaluation is warranted despite the absence of symptoms.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, with 26-year duration. No current glycemic data or treatment regimen is provided.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Obtain or repeat a complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to confirm persistence and characterize marrow response. Check vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate. Review for chronic inflammatory disease, chronic infection, renal impairment, nutritional deficiency, and occult blood loss based on history and examination.

Given the transferrin saturation of 8.9%, assess for iron-restricted erythropoiesis and consider iron replacement only after clarification of the underlying cause and renal function. Intravenous or oral iron selection should be guided by tolerance, response, and the final evaluation. An erythropoiesis-stimulating agent would be considered only if an appropriate indication, such as anemia related to renal disease, is established.

Coordinate ongoing management of his diabetes and reinforce smoking cessation, as both may contribute to long-term vascular and systemic risk. Advise prompt evaluation for new dyspnea, chest discomfort, syncope, palpitations, bleeding, or worsening weakness. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review the anemia evaluation and response to management.
