Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Patrick Harper is a 55-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 8 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 354 mg/dL, LDL-C 266 mg/dL, HDL-C 39 mg/dL, and triglycerides 246 mg/dL. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness. He reports fatty deposits involving the skin and eyelids consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma. He currently smokes. Comorbidity includes hypertension diagnosed approximately 2 years ago.

Objective

Height 186 cm, weight 97.9 kg, BMI 28.3 kg/m². Blood pressure 139/83 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Reported xanthomas and xanthelasma. No other examination findings provided.

Assessment

Severe hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C of 266 mg/dL, elevated triglycerides, and low HDL-C. The degree and duration of LDL elevation, together with reported xanthomas and xanthelasma, raise concern for a significant underlying lipid disorder and confer substantial long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk despite the absence of current ischemic symptoms.

Hypertension, approximately 2 years’ duration, with today’s blood pressure 139/83 mmHg.

Tobacco use, an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Recommend prompt initiation of high-intensity lipid-lowering therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after discussion of risks, benefits, and contraindications. Given the LDL-C level, if response is inadequate or treatment is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe and consideration of specialist-directed advanced therapy would be appropriate.

Obtain a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation to assess response, along with evaluation for potentially reversible secondary contributors to severe dyslipidemia. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk characterization. A coronary artery calcium scan may be considered if it would assist with risk discussion and treatment decisions; it should not delay therapy.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy diet low in saturated and trans fats, weight reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, and complete tobacco cessation. Reviewed the importance of urgent evaluation for new chest discomfort, unexplained shortness of breath, or exertional leg pain.

Continue close outpatient follow-up for lipid response and cardiovascular risk-factor management.
