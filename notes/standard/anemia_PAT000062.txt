Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Jordan Spencer is a 93-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 60 months. He denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, pallor, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or other current anemia-related symptoms.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for approximately 31 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 11 years, and chronic infection for 10 years. He continues to smoke. No current anemia-directed medication regimen is documented.

Objective

Height 176 cm; weight 68.8 kg; BMI 22.2 kg/m².

Blood pressure 139/82 mmHg, pulse 90 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory data: hemoglobin 9.5 g/dL, hematocrit 29.0%, RBC 3.41 million/µL, MCV 85 fL, serum iron 20 µg/dL, ferritin 201 ng/mL, TIBC 229 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 8.7%.

Review of systems is negative for fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, and pallor.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, currently without reported symptoms. Hemoglobin and hematocrit remain reduced, with MCV 85 fL. Low serum iron and transferrin saturation with ferritin 201 ng/mL and relatively low TIBC are most consistent with iron-restricted erythropoiesis in the setting of chronic inflammation and longstanding chronic kidney disease. His chronic infection may also contribute. There is no reported evidence of hemodynamic instability or symptomatic cardiopulmonary compromise today. The anemia has not been actively managed.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, with potential contribution to impaired erythropoiesis. No formal stage assigned.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Chronic infection, longstanding.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Obtain or trend a complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to further characterize the anemia and assess marrow response. Recommend serum vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as part of the anemia evaluation.

Review the chronic infection and kidney disease with attention to ongoing inflammatory activity and renal contribution to anemia. Assess for clinically relevant blood loss, including gastrointestinal sources, based on history and examination.

Discuss anemia-directed treatment after reassessment of renal function, inflammatory status, reticulocyte response, and iron availability. Oral or intravenous iron and an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent may be considered when clinically appropriate, particularly if anemia persists in the setting of chronic kidney disease; treatment should be individualized given his advanced age and comorbidities.

Encourage smoking cessation. Continue routine monitoring and reassess promptly for new fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, chest discomfort, or reduced exercise tolerance.
