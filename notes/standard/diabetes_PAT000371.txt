Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective
Richard Lawson is a 71-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 34 years ago. Diabetes is currently not actively managed. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. He does not smoke.

He also has a 28-year history of hypertension. No interval cardiovascular or neurologic symptoms are reported.

Objective
Height 182 cm; weight 89.4 kg; BMI 27.0 kg/m².

Blood pressure 144/89 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Hemoglobin A1c 9.4%. Fasting glucose 270 mg/dL.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently markedly above glycemic goal based on HbA1c of 9.4% and fasting glucose of 270 mg/dL. Despite the absence of hyperglycemic, neuropathic, or infectious symptoms, his prolonged disease duration and current laboratory values indicate inadequate control.

Hypertension with mildly elevated blood pressure today in the setting of longstanding disease.

Plan
Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of re-establishing an active diabetes management plan. Reviewed nutrition measures focused on reducing refined carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, regular physical activity as tolerated, and weight maintenance.

Recommend initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy after reviewing prior treatment history, renal function, contraindications, and patient preferences; metformin would be a reasonable first-line option if appropriate. Recommend home glucose monitoring with review of readings at follow-up.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and a comprehensive diabetic foot examination. Recommend routine dilated retinal examination given the 34-year duration of diabetes. Continue monitoring blood pressure and reinforce dietary sodium reduction and regular physical activity.

Follow up promptly to establish treatment and review glucose readings; repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval to assess response.
