Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for anemia.

Subjective
Adam Ross is a 90-year-old man, 164 cm and 67.5 kg (BMI 25.1 kg/m²), seen for review of significant anemia. He denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or noticeable pallor. Anemia has not been under active management. He has a 55-year history of rheumatoid arthritis. He currently smokes.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 138/78 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.2 g/dL
Hematocrit 23.9%
RBC 2.72 million/µL
MCV 88 fL
Serum iron 32 µg/dL
Ferritin 344 ng/mL
TIBC 210 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 15.2%

Assessment
Significant normocytic anemia, with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and low TIBC. The pattern is most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of longstanding rheumatoid arthritis. He is currently hemodynamically stable and denies cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms, but the hemoglobin level warrants prompt evaluation and active management.

Plan
Arrange prompt repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to confirm severity and assess marrow response.

Evaluate for contributing factors, including renal function, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers. Assess clinically for occult blood loss or other sources of anemia.

Review rheumatoid arthritis activity and coordinate management with the clinician treating his chronic inflammatory disease. Consider hematology consultation if anemia persists, worsens, or the etiology remains unclear. Iron replacement or an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent should be considered only after further evaluation and review of renal function, inflammatory status, and bleeding risk.

Seek emergency care for new shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, palpitations, marked weakness, or evidence of bleeding. Encourage smoking cessation.
