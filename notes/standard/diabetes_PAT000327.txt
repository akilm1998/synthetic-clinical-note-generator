Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Julia Stone is a 61-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 7 years ago. Recent laboratory results show HbA1c 9.6% and fasting glucose 234 mg/dL, consistent with inadequate glycemic control. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She does not smoke. Comorbid conditions include obesity for approximately 7 years, dyslipidemia for 27 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 27 years. No diabetes medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 126/81 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 175 cm; weight 100.4 kg; BMI 32.8 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.6%. Fasting glucose 234 mg/dL.

No acute distress or diabetes-related symptoms reported. No examination findings indicating neuropathy, skin breakdown, or other diabetes complications are provided.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c 9.6% and fasting glucose 234 mg/dL. Despite the absence of symptomatic hyperglycemia, current glycemic levels warrant treatment intensification and close follow-up.

Obesity, BMI 32.8 kg/m².

Dyslipidemia.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose control is above goal and increases the long-term risk of microvascular and cardiovascular complications. Reinforced reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, increased intake of vegetables and fiber, and gradual progression toward regular physical activity as tolerated. Weight reduction was encouraged.

Recommend initiation or optimization of guideline-directed glucose-lowering medication therapy. Metformin would be a reasonable first-line option if renal and hepatic considerations permit; other agents, including therapies with cardiovascular or weight-related benefit, can be considered based on treatment goals, contraindications, cost, and patient preference. No medication regimen was assumed or documented today.

Recommend monitoring fasting and postprandial glucose as directed and reviewing home glucose readings at the next visit. Obtain renal function with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before or during treatment planning. Recommend fasting lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment and management. Continue attention to the associated dyslipidemia and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot examination and routine dilated retinal evaluation; patient should report any new vision changes, foot wounds, numbness, or persistent hyperglycemic symptoms promptly.

Follow up in approximately 4–6 weeks to review glucose readings and treatment response, with repeat HbA1c in approximately 3 months.
