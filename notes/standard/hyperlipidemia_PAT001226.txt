Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Aiden Hawkins is a 64-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 22 years and not currently under active lipid-lowering management. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or claudication, and dizziness. He reports fatty skin deposits consistent with xanthomas and yellowish eyelid deposits consistent with xanthelasma. He does not smoke.

He also has a 6-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. No interval cardiovascular symptoms are reported.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 121/77 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 161 cm, weight 61.4 kg, BMI 23.7 kg/m².

Recent lipid panel: total cholesterol 307 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 196 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 44 mg/dL, triglycerides 335 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, longstanding for approximately 22 years and currently untreated/not actively managed. Presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma supports a prolonged significant lipid burden. He is presently without chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness. His type 2 diabetes mellitus further increases his long-term cardiovascular risk. Blood pressure and BMI are currently within a favorable range, and he is a nonsmoker.

Plan

Discussed the significantly elevated cholesterol and triglyceride levels and the importance of active treatment to reduce cardiovascular risk. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after shared decision-making, with medication selection and dosing reviewed at the visit. Review for possible secondary contributors to dyslipidemia, including glycemic control, thyroid disease, renal disease, liver disease, and dietary factors.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy eating pattern with reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess dietary sugars; increased intake of vegetables, fiber, and unsaturated fats; and regular aerobic physical activity as tolerated. Continue avoidance of tobacco.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation to assess response and adherence. Consider additional risk assessment with ApoB and lipoprotein(a), and consideration of coronary artery calcium scoring if treatment decisions remain uncertain after discussion. Given the LDL level near 190 mg/dL and physical findings of xanthomas and xanthelasma, consider evaluation for a possible inherited lipid disorder and assess for premature cardiovascular disease in first-degree relatives.

Continue routine management of type 2 diabetes mellitus and reinforce that improved glycemic control may also help reduce triglyceride levels. Follow up after repeat lipid testing to reassess response and adjust therapy. Advise prompt evaluation for new chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or other cardiovascular symptoms.
