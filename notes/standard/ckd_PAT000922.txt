Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Eliza Chandler is an 81-year-old woman, nonsmoker, presenting for routine follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 19 years. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies itching, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, leg swelling, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No interval symptoms suggestive of acute fluid overload are reported.

Her known renal-related comorbidity includes secondary hyperparathyroidism, present for approximately 3 years. No current medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Height 162 cm; weight 56.2 kg; BMI 21.4 kg/m².

Blood pressure 143/88 mmHg, pulse 69/min, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 4.36 mg/dL, BUN 72 mg/dL, estimated GFR 11 mL/min/1.73 m².

No peripheral edema or respiratory symptoms reported today. No additional examination findings provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of approximately 19 years’ duration with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. She is clinically stable by history without nausea, pruritus, cramping, oliguria symptoms, edema, or dyspnea, but reports fatigue that may be related to advanced renal dysfunction. Current management appears incomplete and requires prompt reassessment.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism associated with chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 3 years.

Blood pressure is mildly elevated at today’s visit.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current creatinine, BUN, and estimated GFR results with the patient. Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management, assessment of complications, and discussion of future renal replacement therapy planning as clinically appropriate.

Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Consider renal ultrasound if not recently completed to assess renal structure and exclude an obstructive process.

Review and reconcile all medications at the next visit, with attention to renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications, particularly NSAIDs. Encourage maintenance of hydration consistent with renal and cardiovascular status and avoidance of excess dietary sodium.

Monitor blood pressure and renal-related symptoms, including worsening fatigue, nausea, itching, muscle cramps, reduced urination, swelling, or shortness of breath. Seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, inability to maintain oral intake, marked reduction in urination, confusion, or rapidly progressive swelling.

Follow up after nephrology assessment and repeat laboratory evaluation, or sooner for any clinical deterioration.
