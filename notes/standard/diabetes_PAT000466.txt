Chief Complaint

Annual review of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Grant Porter is a 76-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 17-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, currently inadequately controlled based on recent laboratory values. He reports numbness in the hands or feet but denies tingling. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections.

His comorbidity is longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, present for approximately 50 years. He does not smoke. Current weight is 75.7 kg at a height of 174 cm, with a BMI of 25.0 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 126/76 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.7%. Fasting glucose 209 mg/dL.

He is clinically hemodynamically stable. No additional examination findings are documented for this visit.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with significant hyperglycemia. Hemoglobin A1c of 9.7% and fasting glucose of 209 mg/dL indicate poor glycemic control despite the absence of typical osmotic or constitutional symptoms. The reported distal numbness raises concern for possible diabetic peripheral sensory involvement, although a complication has not been established in the available information.

Longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

BMI 25.0 kg/m², at the upper end of the healthy range.

Plan

Discussed the clinical significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of active diabetes management. Review and initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy should be addressed promptly through shared decision-making, taking into account his age, overall health, hypoglycemia risk, renal function, nutritional status, and treatment preferences.

Recommend individualized nutrition counseling emphasizing consistent carbohydrate intake, avoidance of sugar-sweetened foods and beverages, and a predominantly whole-food dietary pattern. Encourage regular, appropriately supervised physical activity as tolerated, with attention to balance and fall prevention.

Recommend home glucose monitoring if clinically indicated by the treatment plan, with education regarding symptoms of hypoglycemia and marked hyperglycemia. Reassess glycemic control after treatment changes.

Annual diabetes surveillance should include a comprehensive foot examination with assessment of protective sensation and pulses, evaluation of the reported numbness, and counseling regarding daily foot care and prompt reporting of skin breakdown. Recommend a dilated retinal examination and screening for diabetic kidney disease with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine, and estimated glomerular filtration rate. A fasting lipid profile is also appropriate for cardiovascular risk assessment.

Continue clinical monitoring of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and review liver-related risk factors during follow-up. Preventive care should include review of immunizations, fall risk, functional status, oral health, vision and hearing, and age-appropriate cancer screening based on his preferences and prior screening history.

Follow up after completion of recommended diabetes evaluation and treatment planning, with earlier review for polyuria, polydipsia, vomiting, confusion, persistent marked hyperglycemia, new foot wounds, or progressive sensory symptoms.
