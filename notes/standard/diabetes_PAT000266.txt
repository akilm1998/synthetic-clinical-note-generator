Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Evan Bryant is a 76-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 37 years ago. Diabetes control remains suboptimal, with current HbA1c 9.6% and fasting glucose 212 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has a 30-year history of hypertension and a 33-year history of obesity. He does not smoke. Current diabetes medications and home glucose monitoring regimen were not provided for review.

Objective

Height 182 cm; weight 102.0 kg; BMI 30.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 139/90 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.6%; fasting glucose 212 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled. HbA1c and fasting glucose are significantly above individualized outpatient targets despite absence of reported hyperglycemic, neuropathic, infectious, or constitutional symptoms. At age 76, treatment goals should be individualized based on functional status, comorbidity burden, hypoglycemia risk, and patient preferences.

Obesity, BMI 30.8 kg/m², contributing to insulin resistance.

Hypertension, longstanding, with blood pressure today 139/90 mmHg.

Plan

Diabetes control and treatment strategy reviewed. Recommend prompt medication reconciliation and initiation or intensification of glucose-lowering therapy based on the regimen currently being used, renal function, comorbidities, hypoglycemia risk, cost, and patient preference. Metformin may be considered if renal function and tolerability permit; additional agents or insulin may be required given the degree and duration of hyperglycemia. Avoid sulfonylurea-associated hypoglycemia when safer alternatives are appropriate for this older adult.

Encourage structured home glucose monitoring, particularly if insulin or a medication with hypoglycemia risk is prescribed. Review recognition and treatment of hypoglycemia. Counsel regarding consistent carbohydrate intake, portion control, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, and gradual activity appropriate to his functional capacity and safety.

Recommend assessment for diabetes complications as clinically appropriate, including comprehensive foot examination, retinal screening, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine/eGFR, and fasting lipid profile. These results should guide renal protection, cardiovascular risk reduction, and medication selection.

Continue blood pressure surveillance and address hypertension in the context of overall cardiovascular and renal risk. Reinforce weight reduction through nutrition and physical activity; even modest weight loss may improve glycemic control.

Follow up after medication reconciliation and completion of recommended diabetes monitoring to reassess glucose trends, treatment tolerance, and glycemic response.
