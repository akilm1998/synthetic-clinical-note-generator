Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Allison Hamilton is a 70-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 40 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include obesity of approximately 35 years’ duration, hypertension for 17 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 7 years. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Height 162 cm. Weight 90.0 kg. BMI 34.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 147/89 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent lipid profile: total cholesterol 380 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 263 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 50 mg/dL, and triglycerides 336 mg/dL.

Review of provided symptoms is negative for chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. No additional physical examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Severe, longstanding hyperlipidemia with marked elevation of LDL cholesterol and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia. The current lipid profile confers substantial long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of age 70, hypertension, obesity, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. She is presently asymptomatic for angina or peripheral arterial disease. The degree and duration of LDL elevation warrant active pharmacologic management rather than lifestyle therapy alone.

Hypertension with today’s blood pressure of 147/89 mmHg.

Obesity, BMI 34.3 kg/m².

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated LDL and triglyceride levels and the importance of cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after shared decision-making, with selection and dosing based on treatment preferences, medication reconciliation, hepatic history, and tolerance. Her non-alcoholic fatty liver disease is not, by itself, a contraindication to statin therapy.

Recommend a heart-healthy, Mediterranean-style eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, fish, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated fat, trans fat, refined carbohydrates, and added sugars. Encourage gradual weight reduction and regular aerobic activity as tolerated, with avoidance of tobacco exposure.

Recommend evaluation for potentially reversible contributors to dyslipidemia, including thyroid dysfunction, glycemic abnormalities, renal disease, and medication-related causes, as clinically appropriate. Consider measurement of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a) for additional risk characterization. A fasting lipid panel should be repeated after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response and adherence.

If LDL cholesterol remains substantially elevated despite maximally tolerated statin therapy, recommend consideration of add-on ezetimibe and, depending on subsequent response and overall risk assessment, referral for additional lipid-lowering therapy. Triglyceride management should focus initially on statin therapy, weight reduction, dietary carbohydrate/alcohol review, and assessment for secondary causes.

Recommend ongoing blood pressure monitoring and coordinated management of hypertension, obesity, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease to reduce overall cardiovascular risk. Follow up after initiation or adjustment of lipid-lowering therapy to review tolerance, adherence, and repeat lipid results.
