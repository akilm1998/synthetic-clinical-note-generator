Chief Complaint

Annual review of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Ivy Henderson is a 77-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 13-year history of chronic kidney disease and has not been receiving active disease management. She denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath.

Relevant comorbidities include secondary hyperparathyroidism, present for 21 years, and coronary artery disease, present for 20 years. She does not smoke. Height is 163 cm, weight 77.1 kg, and BMI 29.0 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 147/85 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 3.12 mg/dL, BUN 68 mg/dL, estimated GFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m².

No edema or other kidney-related physical symptoms are reported today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of 13 years’ duration with substantially reduced renal filtration and elevated nitrogenous waste markers. She is currently clinically asymptomatic without reported evidence of fluid overload or uremic symptoms. The absence of symptoms does not mitigate the significant risk of progressive renal dysfunction. Current blood pressure is above the usual renal-protective target and warrants confirmation and active management.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism associated with chronic kidney disease.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Overweight by BMI.

Preventive health review appropriate for age and comorbidity burden.

Plan

Recommend prompt re-engagement with structured chronic kidney disease care, including nephrology consultation for assessment of progression, complication management, renal-protective strategies, and education regarding future renal replacement options if clinically indicated. Medication reconciliation should be completed before treatment decisions are made, with review for nephrotoxic agents and appropriate renal dosing.

Repeat renal function and serum electrolytes, and obtain urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to better characterize kidney disease and risk. Recommend CBC to assess for kidney-associated anemia and intact parathyroid hormone assessment in view of longstanding secondary hyperparathyroidism. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously characterized or if clinically indicated.

Confirm blood pressure control with repeat office or home measurements. Dietary counseling should emphasize sodium reduction, avoidance of nonprescription nephrotoxins such as NSAIDs, individualized protein and potassium guidance, and maintenance of appropriate hydration without excessive fluid intake.

Continue cardiovascular risk reduction through blood pressure management, weight management, regular activity as tolerated, and review of lipid and antiplatelet therapy indications based on the established coronary artery disease history. No medication regimen is documented today; treatment should be determined after reconciliation and review of renal function.

Review age-appropriate immunizations, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, and shingles vaccination as appropriate. Address fall risk, nutrition, physical activity, mood, bone health, and individualized cancer screening at the preventive visit. Discuss advance care planning in the context of advanced chronic illness and cardiovascular comorbidity.

Follow up after recommended laboratory evaluation and specialist assessment, or sooner for new edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, progressive weakness, confusion, or significant change in urine output.
