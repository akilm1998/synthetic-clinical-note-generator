Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Eliza Chandler is a 72-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 5 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid results are markedly elevated. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, and dizziness. She reports xanthomas. No xanthelasma is reported.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 42 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 1 year. She currently smokes. Current weight is 87.2 kg at a height of 179 cm, with BMI 27.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 135/75 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results: Total cholesterol 398 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 280 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 43 mg/dL, triglycerides 373 mg/dL.

Xanthomas are reported. No chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, dizziness, or xanthelasma reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and triglycerides, currently untreated. The degree of LDL elevation and presence of xanthomas confer substantially increased long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of advanced age, long-standing type 2 diabetes, and active tobacco use. No current symptoms suggestive of angina or peripheral arterial claudication.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of prompt initiation of lipid-lowering therapy. Recommend starting a high-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, unless contraindicated, with treatment selection and dosing reviewed with the patient. Given the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, addition of ezetimibe should be considered if the response is inadequate or if LDL remains substantially elevated despite maximally tolerated statin therapy.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation to assess response, along with appropriate safety monitoring and evaluation for secondary contributors to the severe lipid elevation. Consider measurement of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a), and assessment of overall atherosclerotic risk; coronary artery calcium imaging may be considered if it would assist shared decision-making.

Counseled regarding reduction of saturated and trans fats, limitation of refined carbohydrates and excess alcohol, increased dietary fiber, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encouraged weight maintenance or modest weight reduction.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered support with cessation planning. Reviewed symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including new chest discomfort, shortness of breath, or exertional leg pain.

Follow up after initiation of therapy and repeat lipid testing to reassess tolerance, adherence, and degree of lipid improvement.
