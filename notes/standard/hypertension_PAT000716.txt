Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for blood pressure management.

Subjective

Julian Holland is an 84-year-old male presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 18 years and currently without an active treatment regimen documented. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and nosebleeds. He also has a 6-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. He reports no tobacco use. Current weight is 104.2 kg at a height of 187 cm, with BMI 29.8 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 160/96 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 139 mmol/L, potassium 4.5 mmol/L.

Patient is clinically stable by history and vital signs, without reported symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive end-organ involvement.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure of 160/96 mmHg. Hypertension has been present for approximately 18 years and is not currently controlled with a documented management regimen. He is asymptomatic today, with no reported chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, visual changes, or epistaxis to suggest an acute hypertensive emergency.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 6 years ago.

Overweight, with BMI 29.8 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given his age, longstanding hypertension, and coexisting diabetes. Recommend confirming the blood pressure pattern with properly performed home readings, including readings at different times of day, and bringing a written log to follow-up.

Recommend initiation of an individualized antihypertensive treatment plan after review of home readings, overall cardiovascular risk, renal status, orthostatic symptoms, and medication tolerance. Because of his age, blood pressure should be lowered carefully with monitoring for dizziness, falls, or orthostatic hypotension.

Counseled on reducing dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, pursuing gradual weight reduction, and limiting alcohol if applicable. Continue avoidance of tobacco.

Recommend routine evaluation for hypertension-related complications, including renal assessment, urinalysis, lipid profile, and electrocardiogram, as clinically appropriate. Serum sodium and potassium are currently within expected ranges.

Follow up in the near term for blood pressure review and treatment planning, sooner for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, visual changes, or markedly higher blood pressure readings.
