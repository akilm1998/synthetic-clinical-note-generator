Chief Complaint

Annual review and evaluation of markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Sophie Bryant is a 52-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease assessment. She has a 7-year history of hyperlipidemia that has not been under active treatment. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 396 mg/dL, LDL-C 274 mg/dL, HDL-C 44 mg/dL, and triglycerides 390 mg/dL. She reports fatty skin deposits consistent with xanthomas. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness.

Past medical history is significant for coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. She currently smokes. No current lipid-lowering medications are provided. She is 174 cm tall and weighs 81.7 kg.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 123/80 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

BMI 27.0 kg/m².

Available laboratory data:
Total cholesterol 396 mg/dL
LDL-C 274 mg/dL
HDL-C 44 mg/dL
Triglycerides 390 mg/dL

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported today. Xanthomas are reported by history.

Assessment

Severe untreated hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and triglycerides, longstanding for approximately 7 years, with reported xanthomas. The degree of LDL elevation raises concern for a significant underlying dyslipidemic disorder and places her at very high cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of known coronary artery disease and active tobacco use.

Coronary artery disease, present for approximately 2 years, without reported angina or claudication today.

Tobacco use, an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

Overweight, BMI 27.0 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the markedly abnormal lipid profile and the need for prompt, sustained lipid-lowering therapy given her coronary artery disease and LDL-C of 274 mg/dL. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy unless contraindicated, with shared decision-making regarding treatment and potential adverse effects. If LDL-C remains substantially above goal despite adherence and maximally tolerated statin therapy, recommend addition of ezetimibe and consideration of specialist-directed therapy, including a PCSK9 inhibitor.

Repeat a fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation and adjustment to assess response. Recommend evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia, including thyroid, glycemic, hepatic, and renal abnormalities. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk characterization. Given established coronary artery disease and markedly elevated LDL-C, consideration of coronary artery disease risk optimization and cardiology involvement is appropriate.

Counseled extensively on smoking cessation. Reviewed heart-healthy dietary measures emphasizing reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol, with increased soluble fiber and plant-based foods. Encourage gradual regular aerobic activity as tolerated and weight reduction toward a healthy range.

Continue monitoring for exertional chest discomfort, dyspnea, claudication, or other ischemic symptoms; seek urgent care for persistent chest pain or acute cardiopulmonary symptoms.

Address routine preventive care at follow-up, including age-appropriate cancer screening, immunizations, and cardiovascular risk-factor review. Follow up after repeat lipid testing to assess response and finalize long-term management.
