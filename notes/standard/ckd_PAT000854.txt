Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Hailey Johnston is a 73-year-old woman, 150 cm and 61.6 kg (BMI 27.4 kg/m²), with a 32-year history of chronic kidney disease, longstanding hypertension, and coronary artery disease diagnosed 19 years ago. She presents for outpatient medication review. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. She does not smoke.

No active CKD medication regimen is documented for review today; therefore, medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance cannot be confirmed. A complete medication reconciliation, including prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal products, is needed before making treatment changes.

Objective

Vital Signs: BP 155/92 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Weight 61.6 kg; BMI 27.4 kg/m².

Laboratory Results: Creatinine 4.41 mg/dL, eGFR 11 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 64 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated creatinine/BUN. She has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, edema, dyspnea, or urinary change. Longstanding hypertension and coronary artery disease are important comorbidities. Blood pressure is above goal today. CKD management is not currently established, and medication effectiveness or adherence cannot be assessed without a verified medication list.

Plan

Complete medication reconciliation promptly, including doses, recent changes, adherence, adverse effects, and use of NSAIDs or other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Review renal dosing and safety of every medication before continuation or initiation; avoid NSAIDs and non-prescribed supplements unless specifically cleared.

Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation given creatinine 4.41 mg/dL and eGFR 11 mL/min/1.73 m². Obtain updated renal monitoring, including serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone, as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound as part of CKD evaluation.

Monitor blood pressure at home and bring readings to follow-up. Antihypertensive therapy should be reassessed after the medication list is verified, with attention to renal function and coronary artery disease.

Counseled to seek urgent care for new or worsening shortness of breath, marked swelling, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or significant decrease in urine output.

Follow up promptly after medication reconciliation and renal evaluation.
