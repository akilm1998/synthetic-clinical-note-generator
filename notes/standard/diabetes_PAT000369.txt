Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Victoria Richardson is a 55-year-old woman with an 18-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, obesity, dyslipidemia, and anxiety. She presents for review of diabetes treatment effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance. Current diabetes regimen and adherence require clarification before treatment effectiveness or medication tolerance can be assessed. She reports increased numbness in the hands or feet, without tingling. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or frequent infections. She does not smoke.

Objective

Height 169 cm, weight 114.2 kg, BMI 40.0 kg/m². Blood pressure 120/75 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin A1c 9.4% and fasting glucose 224 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with suboptimal glycemic control based on elevated hemoglobin A1c and fasting glucose. She has chronic distal numbness concerning for possible diabetic neuropathic symptoms, although no formal complication has been established. She denies current hyperglycemic or infectious symptoms.

Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance cannot be fully evaluated until her current diabetes medications, dosing, timing, and use pattern are clarified. Her severe obesity and dyslipidemia are important contributors to overall cardiometabolic risk. Anxiety has been present for 17 years.

Plan

Reconcile all current prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use, including any diabetes medications, and review dosing, missed doses, access, adverse effects, and barriers to adherence. Discuss initiation or intensification of evidence-based glucose-lowering therapy based on the confirmed medication history, renal function, comorbidities, preferences, and tolerance.

Counseled regarding reducing concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, regular meals, and gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated. Weight reduction was encouraged in the context of longstanding obesity.

Recommend home glucose monitoring if appropriate for the eventual treatment regimen, with review of fasting and postprandial readings at follow-up. Repeat hemoglobin A1c after an appropriate interval to assess response to therapy. Recommend evaluation of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes eye and foot care. At the next visit, perform a focused foot examination with assessment of sensation and peripheral pulses, particularly given reported numbness.

Continue monitoring for polyuria, polydipsia, visual changes, unexplained weight loss, skin or foot wounds, and worsening numbness. Follow up after medication reconciliation and treatment planning, sooner for significant hyperglycemic symptoms or any foot lesion.
