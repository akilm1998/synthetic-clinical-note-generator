Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective
Aria Patterson is a 65-year-old woman seen for follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 15 years and currently untreated. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness. She reports fatty deposits involving the skin and eyelids, consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma. She continues to smoke. Relevant comorbidity includes coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. No current lipid-lowering medications are provided.

Objective
Height 157 cm, weight 67.5 kg, BMI 27.4 kg/m². Blood pressure 134/81 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 356 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 267 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 44 mg/dL, triglycerides 223 mg/dL.

Assessment
Severe longstanding hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and moderately elevated triglycerides. The presence of coronary artery disease and cutaneous lipid deposits places her at high cardiovascular risk. Lipid control is currently inadequate and requires active management. Tobacco use and overweight status further increase cardiovascular risk. She has no reported angina, claudication, or dizziness today.

Plan
Discussed the significantly elevated cholesterol levels and the importance of lipid-lowering therapy, particularly given her coronary artery disease. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy unless contraindicated, with consideration of additional therapy such as ezetimibe if LDL cholesterol remains above goal. Medication risks, benefits, adherence, and potential intolerance should be reviewed before prescribing.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and measurement of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) for further cardiovascular risk assessment. Given known coronary artery disease, management should prioritize aggressive LDL reduction; coronary artery calcium scanning is not necessary to establish risk.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy diet low in saturated and trans fats, increased physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and smoking cessation. Continue cardiovascular risk-factor monitoring and follow up after lipid management is initiated to review tolerance and laboratory response.
