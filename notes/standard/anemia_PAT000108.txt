Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Caleb Freeman is a 92-year-old man with a 15-year history of anemia in the setting of longstanding malignancy, reported duration 38 years. He is not currently receiving documented anemia-directed treatment. He denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor has been noted.

Medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be assessed because no active anemia medication regimen is provided. He is a current smoker. Height is 187 cm, weight 90.6 kg, BMI 25.9 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 127/77 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respirations 20/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

General: Pallor noted.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 9.4 g/dL
Hematocrit 28.3%
RBC 3.18 million/µL
MCV 89 fL
Serum iron 25 µg/dL
Ferritin 254 ng/mL
TIBC 206 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 12.1%

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation associated with longstanding malignancy. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are reduced, with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated/adequate ferritin, and low TIBC, supporting impaired iron utilization rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. He is currently clinically stable without cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms, although pallor is present.

No anemia-directed medication is currently documented; therefore, treatment effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance are not applicable at this visit.

Plan

Discussed laboratory pattern and likely contribution of chronic disease/inflammation. Recommend reassessment with CBC, reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers. Continue evaluation for ongoing blood loss or other contributors as clinically appropriate.

Review management options after reassessment, including oral or intravenous iron if indicated and consideration of an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent only when appropriate to the underlying disease and overall goals of care. Avoid empiric iron therapy without further assessment given the ferritin level and chronic disease pattern.

Encouraged smoking cessation. Monitor for new fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, chest discomfort, or declining exercise tolerance. Follow up after repeat laboratory evaluation or sooner for worsening symptoms.
