Chief Complaint

Annual review with assessment of longstanding hyperlipidemia and chronic medical conditions.

Subjective

Tristan Burke is a 44-year-old male presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 13-year history of hyperlipidemia that is currently untreated. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness. Xanthelasma is present. No xanthomas are reported.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, chronic kidney disease of approximately 1 year’s duration, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease present for 18 years. He continues to smoke. Current weight is 80.7 kg at 179 cm, with BMI 25.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 132/80 mmHg, pulse 84 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory data:
Total cholesterol 398 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 279 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 39 mg/dL
Triglycerides 398 mg/dL

Xanthelasma reported. No other examination findings provided.

Assessment

1. Severe mixed hyperlipidemia, longstanding and untreated. Total cholesterol 398 mg/dL, LDL 279 mg/dL, triglycerides 398 mg/dL, and HDL 39 mg/dL represent markedly abnormal atherogenic lipid burden. The presence of xanthelasma and established coronary artery disease further increases concern for ongoing atherosclerotic risk.

2. Coronary artery disease, approximately 1 year since diagnosis, without current chest discomfort, dizziness, or intermittent claudication. Given the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, aggressive secondary prevention is indicated.

3. Chronic kidney disease, approximately 1 year in duration. Renal function may influence medication selection and dosing; no formal stage is assigned based on the information available.

4. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding for approximately 18 years. This is relevant to cardiovascular risk and should be considered when monitoring lipid-lowering therapy. Fatty liver disease alone is not a contraindication to statin therapy.

5. Tobacco use. Continued smoking substantially increases cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of coronary artery disease and severe dyslipidemia.

6. Preventive health review. BMI is 25.2 kg/m² and blood pressure is currently 132/80 mmHg.

Plan

Discussed the seriousness of the lipid profile and the need for prompt initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy for secondary cardiovascular prevention. Recommend initiation of a high-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after review of contraindications, potential adverse effects, renal considerations, and patient preference. Given the LDL level and established coronary artery disease, addition of ezetimibe should be considered if adequate LDL reduction is not achieved or if statin therapy is not tolerated. Referral to a lipid or cardiovascular specialist is appropriate for assistance with achieving an intensive LDL-lowering target.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid testing after treatment initiation and titration, with consideration of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk characterization. Recommend assessment of hepatic and renal function before and during therapy, using results to guide treatment safely. Persistent triglyceride elevation should be reassessed after LDL-directed therapy, dietary modification, and evaluation for secondary contributors; triglyceride-lowering therapy may be considered if elevation persists.

Counseled regarding a Mediterranean-style diet with reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and alcohol; increased intake of vegetables, whole grains, legumes, and soluble fiber; and regular aerobic and resistance exercise as tolerated. Weight maintenance is appropriate at the current BMI.

Strongly advised complete smoking cessation. Offered structured cessation support and pharmacologic options as clinically appropriate.

Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management in coordination with the clinician managing his coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease. Seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening chest discomfort, shortness of breath, syncope, or exertional leg symptoms.

Continue routine preventive care, including age-appropriate cancer screening, immunizations, blood pressure surveillance, and periodic assessment for diabetes and other modifiable cardiovascular risk factors. Follow up after initiation or adjustment of lipid therapy to review tolerance and response.
