Chief Complaint

Annual review and chronic disease follow-up.

Subjective

Diana Black is a 38-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 10-year history of essential hypertension that has not been actively managed. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. No acute concerns reported today.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for approximately 1 year and hyperlipidemia for approximately 6 years. She does not smoke. Height is 166 cm, weight 57.6 kg, and BMI 20.9 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 165/97 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 147 mmol/L, potassium 4.3 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and no current management documented. She is asymptomatic today without features concerning for hypertensive emergency. Given the longstanding history and coexisting chronic kidney disease, blood pressure control requires prompt attention and close follow-up.

Chronic kidney disease, approximately 1 year in duration, requiring consideration when selecting antihypertensive therapy and monitoring renal function and electrolytes.

Hyperlipidemia, approximately 6 years in duration.

Preventive health review completed in the context of annual examination. She is a nonsmoker with a healthy BMI.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current blood pressure reading and the increased cardiovascular and renal risks associated with longstanding untreated hypertension. Recommend confirming blood pressure with repeat office measurements and home blood pressure monitoring, with a written log for review.

Arrange prompt outpatient follow-up to establish an antihypertensive treatment plan. Medication selection should account for chronic kidney disease, and renal function and electrolyte monitoring should be obtained as clinically appropriate after treatment is initiated or adjusted. The current potassium is 4.3 mmol/L; sodium is mildly elevated at 147 mmol/L.

Counseled on a heart-healthy, lower-sodium eating pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Continue tobacco avoidance.

Recommend review of lipid management and overall cardiovascular risk at follow-up. Preventive care should include verification and updating of immunizations and age-appropriate cervical, breast, and colorectal cancer screening according to screening history and current guidelines.

Seek urgent medical attention for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, acute neurologic symptoms, significant visual change, or markedly worsening blood pressure readings.

Follow up soon for blood pressure reassessment and chronic kidney disease review; continue routine annual preventive care.
