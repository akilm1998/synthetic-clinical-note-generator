Chief Complaint

Medication review for longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Hayden Johnston is a 63-year-old man with a 15-year history of essential hypertension and coronary artery disease of approximately 11 years’ duration, presenting for routine medication review. Current blood pressure is well controlled at 130/82 mmHg. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, or epistaxis. He reports palpitations. Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance were reviewed in the context of the current controlled blood pressure; no treatment-limiting concern is identified from the information available today.

He continues to smoke. Weight is 73.2 kg at a height of 185 cm, with BMI 21.4 kg/m².

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 130/82 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Laboratory Data: Serum sodium 140 mmol/L and potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding, currently controlled. Blood pressure is at goal on the present treatment approach, with normal supplied serum sodium and potassium values. No symptoms suggestive of hypertensive urgency or uncontrolled hypertension are reported.

2. Palpitations. Intermittent palpitations are reported without associated chest pain, dyspnea, dizziness, or fatigue. Given his history of coronary artery disease, this warrants clinical surveillance and rhythm assessment if persistent or recurrent.

3. Coronary artery disease, longstanding. No current anginal or heart-failure symptoms reported.

4. Tobacco use. Ongoing smoking increases cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of hypertension and coronary artery disease.

Plan

Continue the current antihypertensive regimen without intensification given the controlled office blood pressure and stable electrolytes. Reinforce adherence to prescribed therapy and review for dizziness, orthostatic symptoms, edema, cough, or other medication-related intolerance at subsequent visits.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with documentation of readings for medication review. Continue lifestyle measures supportive of blood pressure control, including limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, and avoiding excess alcohol.

Recommend smoking cessation counseling and cessation support because of the combined cardiovascular risk from hypertension, coronary artery disease, and tobacco use.

For palpitations, recommend an ECG, with further evaluation guided by symptom frequency and findings. Seek urgent care for palpitations accompanied by chest pain, shortness of breath, syncope, or persistent severe symptoms.

Continue routine cardiovascular risk monitoring, including consideration of lipid profile and urinalysis as clinically appropriate. Follow up for blood pressure and medication review, sooner for worsening palpitations or new cardiovascular symptoms.
