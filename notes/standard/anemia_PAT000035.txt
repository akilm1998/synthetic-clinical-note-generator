Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Morgan Oliver is a 75-year-old woman seen for follow-up of longstanding anemia, present for approximately 29 years, in the setting of rheumatoid arthritis diagnosed 35 years ago. She reports headache but denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor. There is no reported interval change in cardiopulmonary symptoms.

Her anemia has not been actively managed with a documented anemia-directed treatment plan. Smoking status is positive. Current weight is 66.1 kg and height is 157 cm, with BMI 26.8 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 129/80 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory data: Hemoglobin 8.5 g/dL, hematocrit 26.1%, RBC count 2.81 million/µL, MCV 93 fL. Serum iron 21 µg/dL, ferritin 257 ng/mL, TIBC 234 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 9.0%.

Cardiovascular examination is without tachycardia based on recorded pulse. Respiratory status is stable with normal oxygen saturation. Pallor is not reported.

Assessment

Chronic anemia with normocytic indices and significant reduction in hemoglobin and hematocrit. The low serum iron and transferrin saturation with relatively elevated ferritin and low TIBC are compatible with iron-restricted erythropoiesis in the setting of chronic inflammation, particularly given her longstanding rheumatoid arthritis. The degree of anemia remains clinically important despite limited symptoms. Headache may be associated with the anemia, although she denies other constitutional or cardiopulmonary manifestations.

Longstanding rheumatoid arthritis, a likely contributor to chronic inflammatory anemia. Current inflammatory disease activity is not otherwise characterized in the available information.

Tobacco use. Smoking may adversely affect overall cardiopulmonary health and should be addressed as part of routine care.

Plan

Review and address the anemia as an active medical problem rather than continuing observation alone. Repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear is appropriate to assess marrow response and confirm persistence of the current pattern. Recommend evaluation of vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers to assess for additional or contributing causes.

The iron profile suggests that low circulating iron may reflect inflammation rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Further assessment should guide whether oral or intravenous iron is appropriate; empiric treatment should be based on the overall clinical evaluation and response to testing. If anemia persists or worsens, consideration of hematology evaluation is appropriate.

Optimize management of the underlying rheumatoid arthritis in coordination with the clinician managing that condition, as ongoing inflammation may be contributing to impaired erythropoiesis.

Monitor for worsening fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, chest discomfort, syncope, or bleeding. Seek urgent evaluation for new or progressive cardiopulmonary symptoms or symptomatic deterioration.

Discuss smoking cessation and avoidance of continued tobacco exposure. Continue routine follow-up with reassessment of symptoms and hemoglobin trend after the recommended evaluation.
