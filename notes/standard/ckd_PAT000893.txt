Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease and hypertension.

Subjective

Charlie Brown is a 44-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, identified approximately 1 year ago. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. No uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported today.

He has a 12-year history of hypertension, an 11-year history of hyperlipidemia, and anemia of chronic disease for approximately 4 years. He currently smokes. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Height 181 cm; weight 73.1 kg; BMI 22.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 154/92 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Serum creatinine 3.66 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m². BUN 92 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and substantial azotemia. Although he is currently without reported uremic symptoms, the creatinine, eGFR, and BUN indicate significant chronic renal impairment. Disease control appears suboptimal, particularly in the setting of persistent hypertension and no documented active management plan. There is no reported edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, or change in urination to suggest clinically evident fluid overload or symptomatic uremia at this visit.

Hypertension, longstanding for 12 years, currently above goal at 154/92 mmHg and likely contributing to ongoing renal injury.

Anemia of chronic disease, present for approximately 4 years, in the setting of significant chronic kidney disease. No current hemoglobin value is available in the supplied laboratory data.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding for approximately 11 years, increasing overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation given the markedly reduced eGFR and BUN of 92 mg/dL. Monitor closely for nausea, vomiting, confusion, progressive fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, worsening edema, shortness of breath, or significant changes in urine output; urgent evaluation is warranted if these occur.

Obtain a serum electrolyte panel, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone level, and renal ultrasound as part of evaluation and complication assessment. Repeat renal function testing as clinically indicated.

Address blood pressure control promptly with review and initiation or adjustment of an appropriate antihypertensive regimen, taking renal function into account. Confirm all current prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications and avoid nephrotoxic agents, particularly nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, unless specifically directed.

Evaluate and manage anemia in the context of chronic kidney disease after reviewing the complete blood count. Assess for CKD-related mineral and bone disorder based on calcium/phosphate-related testing and intact parathyroid hormone results.

Continue cardiovascular risk reduction with management of hyperlipidemia, smoking-cessation counseling, dietary sodium reduction, and avoidance of excess dietary protein or unregulated supplements. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended after the above evaluation.
