Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of anemia.

Subjective

Tristan Burke is a 71-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 25-year history of inflammatory bowel disease and has had anemia identified for approximately 1 year. He currently reports no fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, or subjective pallor.

He does not smoke. No anemia-directed medication or treatment regimen is provided for review today.

Assessment

Anemia of chronic disease/inflammation, likely associated with longstanding inflammatory bowel disease. Laboratory pattern demonstrates a moderate normocytic anemia with low serum iron and TIBC and elevated ferritin, which is more consistent with inflammation-related iron restriction than uncomplicated iron deficiency. Transferrin saturation is reduced. He is currently clinically asymptomatic, but the hemoglobin and hematocrit remain below normal and warrant further evaluation and active follow-up.

Longstanding inflammatory bowel disease, duration 25 years; current inflammatory activity is not documented in the information available for this visit.

Annual preventive health review.

Plan

Repeat CBC with reticulocyte count to assess persistence and marrow response. Obtain peripheral smear, vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate. Continue to monitor iron indices; interpretation should account for chronic inflammation and elevated ferritin.

Review inflammatory bowel disease control and coordinate ongoing gastrointestinal care. Assess for active intestinal inflammation or blood loss and ensure age-appropriate colorectal cancer surveillance is current through the appropriate treating clinician.

Discuss treatment options after clarification of the anemia’s persistence, inflammatory activity, renal function, nutritional status, and gastrointestinal disease status. Oral or intravenous iron should be considered only if additional evaluation supports iron-restricted erythropoiesis or iron deficiency. Consider specialist hematology input if anemia worsens, fails to improve, becomes symptomatic, or the cause remains uncertain. Erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy is not indicated for routine use without an appropriate underlying indication.

Advise the patient to seek prompt medical attention for new shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, syncope, significant dizziness, overt gastrointestinal bleeding, or rapidly worsening exercise tolerance.

Review routine preventive care, including immunizations, physical activity, balanced nutrition, fall-risk reduction, and age-appropriate cancer and cardiovascular risk screening. Arrange outpatient follow-up after repeat blood work to review trends and determine next steps.
