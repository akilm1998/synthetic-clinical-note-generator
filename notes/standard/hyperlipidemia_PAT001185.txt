Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective
Sophie Bryant is a 69-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 17 years and currently without an active treatment regimen documented. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, and dizziness. She has xanthomas and xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 23 years, hypertension for 11 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 37 years. Recent lipid results remain markedly elevated: total cholesterol 353 mg/dL, LDL-C 240 mg/dL, HDL-C 45 mg/dL, and triglycerides 341 mg/dL.

Objective
Height 158 cm; weight 69.4 kg; BMI 27.8 kg/m². Blood pressure 146/87 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Xanthomas and xanthelasma are present. No chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness reported. No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment
Hyperlipidemia with severely elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, longstanding and not currently adequately controlled. The degree of LDL elevation, presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma, and established coronary artery disease place her at very high cardiovascular risk. HDL is 45 mg/dL.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, without reported anginal symptoms today.

Hypertension with above-goal office blood pressure of 146/87 mmHg.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan
Discussed the markedly abnormal lipid profile and the importance of active lipid-lowering treatment in the setting of coronary artery disease and diabetes. Recommend initiation or re-initiation of a high-intensity statin, if no contraindication, with medication selection and dosing reviewed after confirming current medication history, prior tolerance, and potential drug interactions. If LDL cholesterol remains substantially elevated despite maximally tolerated statin therapy, recommend consideration of add-on ezetimibe and, if needed, referral for assessment of additional lipid-lowering therapy.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, whole grains, legumes, fish, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess dietary cholesterol. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated and continued avoidance of tobacco.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment is established to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and measurement of additional cardiovascular risk markers, including lipoprotein(a) or apolipoprotein B, as clinically appropriate. Given known coronary artery disease, management should target substantial LDL reduction and be coordinated with ongoing cardiovascular care.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and diabetes control closely; reinforce adherence to prescribed therapies once the complete medication list is confirmed. Follow up after treatment initiation and repeat lipid testing, or sooner for new chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dyspnea, or other concerning cardiovascular symptoms.
