Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated blood glucose.

Subjective

Alice Freeman is a 73-year-old woman with a 40-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, currently poorly controlled and not receiving documented active management. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling. No acute hyperglycemic symptoms are reported.

Medical history is also significant for chronic kidney disease for 12 years, obstructive sleep apnea for 36 years, and depression for 22 years. She currently smokes. No diabetes medications are provided in the available history.

Objective

Height 176 cm; weight 75.0 kg; BMI 24.2 kg/m².
Blood pressure 134/81 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.
Hemoglobin A1c 10.0%.
Fasting glucose 246 mg/dL.
No abnormal physical examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, markedly uncontrolled based on fasting glucose of 246 mg/dL and HbA1c of 10.0%. Despite the degree and duration of hyperglycemia, she is currently asymptomatic and hemodynamically stable, with no reported infectious or neuropathic symptoms.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding; renal function must be considered when selecting and dosing antihyperglycemic therapy.

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding.

Depression, longstanding.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange prompt diabetes treatment initiation or intensification and medication reconciliation. Given HbA1c of 10.0%, discuss combination pharmacotherapy and consideration of insulin if clinically indicated; metformin should only be used if renal function permits. Review home glucose monitoring, hypoglycemia precautions, sick-day management, nutrition, and physical activity appropriate to her overall health.

Obtain renal function assessment including serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before finalizing therapy, along with a fasting lipid profile. Recommend comprehensive diabetes complication surveillance, including dilated retinal evaluation and formal foot examination with sensory and vascular assessment.

Counsel regarding smoking cessation and adherence to treatment. Continue management of chronic kidney disease, obstructive sleep apnea, and depression in coordination with the appropriate treating clinicians.

Seek emergency care for vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, severe weakness, inability to maintain oral intake, rapidly worsening thirst or urination, or other symptoms concerning for acute metabolic decompensation. Close outpatient reassessment is recommended after treatment is initiated or adjusted.
