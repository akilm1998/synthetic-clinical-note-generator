Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Pamela Donovan is a 96-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 7-year history of chronic kidney disease, currently without an established management plan documented. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath.

Relevant comorbidities include gout for 19 years, secondary hyperparathyroidism for 19 years, and hyperlipidemia for 45 years. She currently smokes. No medication list or allergies were provided for review.

Objective

Height 164 cm; weight 64.0 kg; BMI 23.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 153/87 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Serum creatinine 4.27 mg/dL. BUN 86 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m².

No peripheral edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, urinary frequency change, or asterixis is reported. No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated creatinine and BUN. The patient has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, fluid overload symptoms, or respiratory compromise. Given the degree of renal impairment and the absence of an established management plan, she requires prompt comprehensive renal evaluation and close monitoring for complications.

Elevated blood pressure at today’s visit, relevant to renal and cardiovascular risk. Current antihypertensive therapy is not documented.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, longstanding, in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

History of gout, longstanding. Reduced renal function may affect urate handling and medication safety.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Active tobacco use.

Advanced age with need for individualized preventive care, medication safety review, functional assessment, and goals-of-care discussion.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease, review of renal replacement therapy considerations appropriate to her age and preferences, and assessment of prognosis and goals of care.

Repeat kidney function and serum electrolyte testing, with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone recommended to assess renal complications. Consider renal ultrasound if clinically indicated after specialist assessment.

Perform complete medication reconciliation before initiating or adjusting therapy. Avoid nephrotoxic medications when possible and review all prescriptions, over-the-counter agents, and supplements for renal dosing. Do not start or continue medications for gout or mineral-bone disease without renal-specific review.

Recheck blood pressure with appropriate technique and review home readings if available. Develop an individualized blood pressure treatment plan after medication reconciliation, taking into account renal function, fall risk, orthostasis, and overall goals of care.

Monitor for warning symptoms including worsening shortness of breath, new swelling, markedly reduced urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or progressive weakness; seek urgent evaluation if these occur.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of tobacco exposure.

Review nutrition and hydration with renal guidance, avoiding excessive sodium and over-the-counter NSAIDs. Maintain adequate but not excessive fluid intake unless otherwise directed by nephrology.

Review preventive care appropriate for age and comorbidity, including immunization status, fall-risk assessment, functional status, hearing and vision, mood, advance care planning, and alignment of treatment intensity with the patient’s preferences. Follow up promptly after renal evaluation and laboratory reassessment.
