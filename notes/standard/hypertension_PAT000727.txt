Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Jason Coleman is a 74-year-old man seen for outpatient follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 28 years. His blood pressure is currently not under active treatment or management. He reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. He continues to smoke. He has chronic kidney disease of approximately 11 years’ duration. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Height 183 cm; weight 71.0 kg; BMI 21.2 kg/m². Blood pressure 168/92 mmHg. Pulse 76 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 36.8°C. Oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent serum electrolytes: sodium 142 mmol/L and potassium 4.1 mmol/L.

Assessment

Longstanding essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure and associated intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. Blood pressure control is currently inadequate. He does not report symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency at this visit, including chest pain, dyspnea, focal visual complaints, or severe headache.

Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 11 years, increasing the importance of sustained blood pressure control and renal-protective management.

Active cigarette smoking.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current blood pressure and the increased cardiovascular and renal risks associated with prolonged uncontrolled hypertension. Recommend confirming the reading with repeat office measurement and home blood pressure monitoring, with a written log for review.

Recommend prompt initiation or re-establishment of an individualized antihypertensive treatment plan, taking chronic kidney disease and electrolyte status into account. Review renal function and electrolytes before and after treatment changes; current sodium and potassium are within acceptable limits. Recommend urinalysis and additional kidney function assessment as clinically appropriate.

Recommend ECG, lipid profile, and cardiovascular risk assessment as part of evaluation of longstanding hypertension. Counsel regarding reduction of dietary sodium, regular physical activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Strongly encouraged smoking cessation and offered support with cessation planning.

Advised immediate emergency evaluation for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe or sudden headache, new neurologic symptoms, acute visual changes, or persistently escalating blood pressure. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment adjustment.
