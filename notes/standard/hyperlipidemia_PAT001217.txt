Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up of markedly elevated cholesterol and triglycerides.

Subjective

Bella Dean is a 74-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of hyperlipidemia present for approximately 6 years and currently not under active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 389 mg/dL, LDL-C 277 mg/dL, HDL-C 48 mg/dL, and triglycerides 322 mg/dL. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

Relevant history includes coronary artery disease for approximately 3 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 9 years. No current lipid-lowering medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Blood pressure 122/77 mmHg. Pulse 73 bpm. Respiratory rate 16/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Height 175 cm, weight 83.6 kg, BMI 27.3 kg/m². No additional examination findings provided.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe elevation of LDL-C and concurrent hypertriglyceridemia, currently untreated. The LDL-C of 277 mg/dL is particularly concerning in the setting of known coronary artery disease and represents a high atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk profile. Triglycerides are also elevated at 322 mg/dL. She is presently without anginal, claudication, or neurologic symptoms.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, chronic. This is not an automatic contraindication to statin therapy, but liver-directed monitoring should be incorporated into treatment planning.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated LDL-C and triglycerides, particularly given her coronary artery disease. Recommend prompt initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy through her treating clinician, generally beginning with maximally tolerated high-intensity statin therapy for secondary prevention, with addition of ezetimibe or other therapy if LDL-C remains above goal or statin intolerance occurs. Medication selection and dosing should be confirmed after review of her complete medication history and liver status.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk characterization and evaluation for a possible inherited contribution to the degree of LDL elevation. Review for secondary contributors, including thyroid disease, glycemic abnormalities, renal disease, dietary factors, and medication effects, using clinically appropriate testing.

Counseled regarding reduction of saturated and trans fats, avoidance of excess refined carbohydrates and alcohol, increased intake of soluble fiber, weight management, and regular physical activity as tolerated. BMI is 27.3 kg/m².

Because of her known coronary artery disease, she was advised to seek emergency care for new or persistent chest discomfort, dyspnea, diaphoresis, syncope, or other acute ischemic symptoms. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended to confirm treatment initiation and review lipid response.
