Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and review of markedly abnormal renal function.

Subjective

Valeria Ellis is a 73-year-old woman with a 23-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and approximately 7 years of chronic kidney disease who presents for urgent outpatient follow-up. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. She currently smokes. No medication list is provided.

Objective

Height 168 cm, weight 58.4 kg, BMI 20.7 kg/m². Blood pressure is 147/91 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, and oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results show creatinine 5.13 mg/dL, BUN 94 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 9 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. The degree of renal impairment, in the setting of longstanding diabetes and suboptimally controlled blood pressure, requires urgent reassessment and nephrology involvement. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction, although she currently reports no nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, fluid overload symptoms, dyspnea, or urinary change. There is no reported clinical evidence of acute volume overload at this visit.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, with 23 years’ duration.

Elevated blood pressure in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Given creatinine 5.13 mg/dL, BUN 94 mg/dL, and eGFR 9 mL/min/1.73 m², recommend same-day medical evaluation to confirm stability and assess for complications requiring urgent treatment. She should proceed to the emergency department promptly for worsening fatigue, nausea or vomiting, confusion, new weakness, chest pain, shortness of breath, swelling, markedly reduced urination, or other acute change.

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management and discussion of renal replacement planning if clinically indicated. Recommend repeat renal function and serum electrolyte assessment, complete blood count, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and evaluation for CKD-related complications, including mineral-bone abnormalities. Renal imaging may be considered if clinically appropriate.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications at urgent follow-up; avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a clinician. Blood pressure and diabetes management should be reassessed with renal dosing of any medications. Counsel regarding smoking cessation. Close follow-up is required after urgent evaluation.
