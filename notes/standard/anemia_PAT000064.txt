Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Colin Graham is a 91-year-old man seen for outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 37 years. He reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, palpitations, shortness of breath, reduced exercise tolerance, or pallor. He does not smoke.

Relevant chronic conditions include heart failure for 16 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 36 years, and malignancy for 5 years. The anemia has not been actively managed recently. No anemia-directed medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Height 162 cm. Weight 55.6 kg. BMI 21.2 kg/m².

Blood pressure 132/82 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 23/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin 9.8 g/dL, hematocrit 29.4%, RBC count 3.42 million/µL, and MCV 86 fL. Serum iron is low at 25 µg/dL, ferritin 226 ng/mL, TIBC 229 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 10.9%.

Assessment

Anemia of chronic disease/inflammation, longstanding and currently asymptomatic. The anemia is normocytic with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated-range ferritin, and low TIBC, a pattern consistent with impaired iron availability in the setting of chronic disease rather than isolated iron depletion. His chronic heart failure, diabetes, and malignancy may all contribute. Hemoglobin remains reduced at 9.8 g/dL, without reported cardiopulmonary symptoms or tachycardia today.

Plan

Review the anemia and laboratory pattern with the patient and continue clinical monitoring.

Obtain a repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to assess trend and marrow response. Consider vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as part of the evaluation for contributing factors. Assess for any evidence of blood loss or change in symptoms at follow-up.

Given his age and significant chronic comorbidities, coordinate anemia management with the clinicians managing his heart failure, diabetes, and malignancy. Consider iron replacement or other anemia-directed therapy only after reassessing the cause, iron availability, renal function, inflammatory burden, and overall goals of care.

He should seek earlier evaluation for new shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, worsening weakness, bleeding, or reduced exercise tolerance. Routine outpatient follow-up after repeat evaluation.
