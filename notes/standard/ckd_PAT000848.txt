Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Aiden Hawkins is a 54-year-old man, 161 cm tall and weighing 73.1 kg, with BMI 28.2 kg/m², presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. There are no reported symptoms suggesting current uremic prodrome or clinically apparent fluid overload.

Relevant comorbidities include anemia of chronic disease for approximately 4 years, hyperlipidemia for 19 years, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 8 years. He currently smokes. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: blood pressure 143/82 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory results reviewed: serum creatinine 4.03 mg/dL, estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 70 mg/dL.

He is overweight by BMI. No edema or respiratory distress is reported, and oxygenation is normal. No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with significantly reduced renal function, present for approximately 1 year. Current creatinine, eGFR, and BUN are markedly abnormal, although he denies fatigue, nausea, pruritus, cramps, sleep disturbance, urinary changes, edema, or dyspnea. The condition does not appear to be under active management and requires prompt comprehensive evaluation and kidney-focused follow-up.

Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, longstanding and associated with chronic kidney disease.

Hyperlipidemia.

Blood pressure is mildly elevated at today’s visit.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the seriousness of the abnormal renal function and the importance of timely nephrology evaluation. Recommend arranging nephrology consultation promptly for assessment of advanced renal impairment, review of complications, and discussion of ongoing kidney-protective and renal replacement planning as clinically indicated.

Recommend obtaining updated serum electrolytes, repeat renal function testing, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Renal ultrasound is also appropriate to evaluate kidney structure and exclude an obstructive process. These should be coordinated promptly rather than deferred.

Review and reconcile all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications at the next contact, with avoidance of NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a clinician. Dietary counseling should emphasize appropriate sodium intake and individualized kidney-friendly nutrition, including management of protein, potassium, phosphorus, and fluid intake based on updated laboratory results and specialist guidance.

Monitor blood pressure outside the office if possible and bring readings to follow-up. Smoking cessation was strongly encouraged. He was advised to seek urgent care for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.

Follow up promptly after the recommended renal evaluation and laboratory testing to review results and establish an active chronic kidney disease management plan.
