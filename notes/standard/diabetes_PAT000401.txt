Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

George Cunningham is a 58-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. Current laboratory results show poor glycemic control with HbA1c 10.0% and fasting glucose 257 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has longstanding obesity, obstructive sleep apnea for approximately 9 years, and anxiety for approximately 8 years. He currently smokes. No diabetes medications or other medication regimen are provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 124/78 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Height 161 cm; weight 83.7 kg; BMI 32.3 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 10.0%. Fasting glucose 257 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently markedly above goal based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. Although he denies symptoms of hyperglycemia, the degree of elevation warrants prompt treatment intensification and close follow-up.

Obesity, BMI 32.3 kg/m².

Tobacco use.

Obstructive sleep apnea and anxiety, chronic.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values indicate inadequate diabetes control despite the absence of reported symptoms. Reviewed the importance of consistent nutrition, portion control, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages and refined carbohydrates, regular physical activity as tolerated, and weight reduction.

Recommend prompt initiation or adjustment of diabetes-directed pharmacologic therapy after medication reconciliation and shared decision-making; metformin would generally be considered if no contraindication, with escalation of therapy based on response and renal function. Patient should begin home glucose monitoring if treatment is initiated and bring readings to follow-up.

Recommend obtaining renal function with serum creatinine/eGFR, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and a fasting lipid profile as part of diabetes risk assessment. Recommend routine dilated retinal examination and comprehensive diabetic foot examination. Continue monitoring for neuropathic symptoms, visual changes, wounds, polyuria, polydipsia, or unexplained weight loss.

Smoking cessation counseling provided; encouraged reduction and cessation and discussed available support options. Reinforced adherence to management of obstructive sleep apnea and attention to anxiety as part of overall health.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review medication plan, glucose readings, and response to treatment.
