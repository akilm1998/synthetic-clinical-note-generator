Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Chloe Cox is a 70-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of longstanding essential hypertension, diagnosed approximately 19 years ago. Hypertension is currently not under active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. She does not smoke.

She has a 39-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. No interval cardiovascular or neurologic symptoms are reported.

Objective

Height 172 cm; weight 67.5 kg; BMI 22.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 155/97 mmHg. Pulse 77 bpm. Respiratory rate 18/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 147 mmol/L, potassium 4.7 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal at 155/97 mmHg, without symptoms or findings provided to suggest acute hypertensive emergency. Given the duration of hypertension and coexisting type 2 diabetes mellitus, sustained blood pressure elevation warrants active treatment and assessment for cardiovascular and renal risk.

Mild hypernatremia at 147 mmol/L with normal potassium. This should be interpreted in clinical context and monitored, particularly before initiating or adjusting antihypertensive therapy.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the importance of re-establishing active blood pressure management. Recommend confirmation of persistent elevation with properly performed repeat office measurements and home blood pressure monitoring, including a written log for review.

Recommend initiation of an individualized antihypertensive treatment plan after reviewing home readings, renal function, diabetes management, medication history, and contraindications. Potential first-line options include an ACE inhibitor or ARB, calcium-channel blocker, or thiazide-type diuretic; medication selection should account for her diabetes and electrolyte status. No current antihypertensive regimen is documented.

Counseled on reducing dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, limiting alcohol if applicable, and continuing a heart-healthy diet. Her normal BMI and nonsmoking status are favorable risk factors.

Recommend cardiovascular risk assessment with lipid profile, urinalysis, and ECG, along with appropriate renal function testing before and during antihypertensive treatment. Recheck serum electrolytes after treatment is initiated or adjusted.

Reviewed symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, acute visual change, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review blood pressure readings and finalize therapy.
