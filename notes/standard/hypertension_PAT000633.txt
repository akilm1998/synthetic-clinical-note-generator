Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Eric Butler is a 53-year-old man with a 6-year history of essential hypertension and chronic kidney disease for approximately 4 years, presenting for routine outpatient follow-up. He is not currently receiving documented antihypertensive treatment. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. He reports no current symptoms suggestive of hypertensive urgency or emergency. He does not smoke.

Objective

Height is 169 cm and weight is 80.8 kg, with BMI 28.3 kg/m². Blood pressure is 151/87 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, and oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Recent serum electrolytes show sodium 145 mmol/L and potassium 5.2 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension remains above goal on today’s reading, with a current blood pressure of 151/87 mmHg. He is asymptomatic, without reported features of acute end-organ involvement, but has an approximately 6-year history of inadequately addressed hypertension.

Chronic kidney disease is a significant comorbidity and increases his cardiovascular and renal risk. Mild hyperkalemia is present with potassium of 5.2 mmol/L; this should be confirmed and considered when selecting antihypertensive therapy. Overweight status with BMI 28.3 kg/m² may also contribute to cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management, dietary sodium restriction, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and avoidance of excessive alcohol intake. He was advised to monitor blood pressure outside the office when possible and bring readings to follow-up.

Given persistent elevation and the history of chronic kidney disease, initiation of antihypertensive therapy should be addressed promptly after review of renal function and repeat potassium. Because potassium is mildly elevated, medications that may further increase potassium should be selected cautiously, with appropriate laboratory monitoring if used. Repeat serum electrolytes and assessment of renal function are recommended before or shortly after treatment initiation. Urinalysis, lipid profile, and an ECG are also appropriate for cardiovascular and renal risk assessment.

Reviewed symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including severe or rapidly worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or acute visual changes. Continue close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment adjustment.
