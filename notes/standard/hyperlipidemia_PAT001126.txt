Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Jacob Long is a 44-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia, identified approximately 2 years ago and not currently managed with medication or a documented lifestyle program. Recent lipid results remain markedly elevated, with total cholesterol 312 mg/dL, LDL-C 221 mg/dL, HDL-C 37 mg/dL, and triglycerides 270 mg/dL. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. He is an active smoker. His relevant comorbidity is non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, present for approximately 13 years.

Objective

Blood pressure 122/77 mmHg, pulse 72/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%. Height 171 cm, weight 68.7 kg, BMI 23.5 kg/m².

Laboratory results: total cholesterol 312 mg/dL, LDL-C 221 mg/dL, HDL-C 37 mg/dL, triglycerides 270 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe elevation of LDL cholesterol and associated hypertriglyceridemia. The current lipid profile is significantly above goal and represents an important long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk despite normal blood pressure and normal BMI. He has no reported symptoms suggestive of current ischemic or peripheral arterial disease and no reported xanthomas or xanthelasma. Treatment has not yet been established.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding. This should be considered when reviewing liver-related laboratory testing, but is not by itself a reason to defer evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy.

Active tobacco use, which further increases cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid values and the importance of initiating intensive lipid-lowering therapy. Recommend starting a high-intensity statin after review of medication history, contraindications, and baseline liver-related testing. If LDL reduction remains inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe or other therapy can be considered based on response and overall risk.

Recommend a fasting lipid panel and liver enzyme assessment to establish a treatment baseline and repeat lipid testing after therapy is initiated to assess adherence and response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to the marked dyslipidemia and measurement of lipoprotein(a) or apolipoprotein B when clinically appropriate. A family history of premature cardiovascular disease should be reviewed at the next visit.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy eating pattern with reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol, along with regular aerobic physical activity. Encouraged smoking cessation and offered discussion of cessation pharmacotherapy and behavioral support.

Continue monitoring the longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease in conjunction with lipid management. Return for follow-up after initiation of therapy and repeat laboratory testing, or sooner for chest discomfort, exertional dyspnea, or other new cardiovascular symptoms.
