Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective
Amelia Foster is a 61-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 19 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid profile shows total cholesterol 288 mg/dL, LDL-C 190 mg/dL, HDL-C 42 mg/dL, and triglycerides 279 mg/dL. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, and dizziness. She reports xanthelasma; no xanthomas are reported.

Relevant comorbidities include non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of approximately 22 years’ duration and chronic kidney disease of approximately 18 years’ duration. She currently smokes. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective
Vital signs: Blood pressure 146/79 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 150 cm, weight 59.2 kg, BMI 26.3 kg/m².

Lipid panel: Total cholesterol 288 mg/dL; LDL-C 190 mg/dL; HDL-C 42 mg/dL; triglycerides 279 mg/dL.

Xanthelasma is reported. No chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness reported.

Assessment
Longstanding untreated hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and elevated triglycerides, associated with xanthelasma. The LDL-C of 190 mg/dL represents substantially increased atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, further amplified by active tobacco use, chronic kidney disease, overweight status, and borderline elevated blood pressure. She has no current symptoms suggestive of angina or peripheral arterial disease.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding; degree of renal impairment is not specified.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan
Discussed the significance of the lipid profile and the importance of initiating pharmacologic therapy. Recommend starting a high-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, following review of the current medication list, renal function, hepatic profile, contraindications, and shared decision-making. Given the LDL-C level, treatment is indicated independent of calculated short-term risk.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess dietary sugars; increase soluble fiber and plant-based foods. Encourage regular aerobic activity as tolerated and continued weight control.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered assistance with a structured cessation plan.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after initiation or adjustment of therapy to assess response and adherence. Consider addition of ezetimibe if LDL-C remains substantially above goal despite maximally tolerated statin therapy. Triglyceride-directed therapy can be reassessed after LDL-C management and lifestyle intervention.

Because of the history of chronic kidney disease and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, recommend confirmation of current renal and hepatic function before and during lipid-lowering treatment, with medication selection and dosing adjusted accordingly.

Blood pressure is elevated at today’s visit; recommend repeat measurements and continued primary care assessment without assigning a hypertension diagnosis based on this single reading.

Follow up after initiation of therapy and repeat lipid assessment to review tolerance, response, and further risk-reduction strategy.
