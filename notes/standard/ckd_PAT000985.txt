Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Cameron West is a 96-year-old male, 189 cm tall and 77.5 kg (BMI 21.7 kg/m²), presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 22 years ago. Current management has not been established. He reports fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath.

Relevant medical history includes hypertension for approximately 50 years, hyperlipidemia for 36 years, and coronary artery disease for 51 years. He currently smokes. Medication list, allergies, prior renal imaging, and prior nephrology or dialysis history were not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 156/98 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL. BUN 89 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m².

No peripheral edema or respiratory symptoms are reported. No additional examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of 22 years’ duration with markedly impaired renal function, creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 89 mg/dL, and eGFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m². The patient has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. Given the degree of renal impairment, he requires prompt comprehensive renal evaluation and assessment for complications and renal replacement therapy, regardless of the limited current symptom burden.

Hypertension, longstanding, with elevated office blood pressure of 156/98 mmHg.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Recommend urgent nephrology evaluation to review the chronicity and trajectory of renal dysfunction, assess for uremic complications, and discuss renal replacement options, including whether dialysis planning is appropriate. Given his age and coronary artery disease, treatment decisions should incorporate overall goals of care, functional status, and patient preferences.

Obtain a comprehensive renal evaluation, including repeat serum creatinine, BUN, electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, complete blood count, intact parathyroid hormone, urinalysis, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. Recommend renal ultrasound if not previously performed to assess renal structure and exclude obstruction.

Review all current prescription medications, over-the-counter products, and supplements at the earliest opportunity. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a treating clinician. Medication dosing should be adjusted for renal function, and blood pressure management should be reassessed after the medication list is confirmed.

Monitor for anemia, mineral and bone complications, metabolic abnormalities, volume overload, and progressive uremic symptoms. Encourage appropriate dietary counseling for advanced kidney disease rather than making empiric dietary or fluid restrictions before laboratory and clinical review.

Seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, marked weakness, persistent vomiting, inability to maintain oral intake, rapidly worsening swelling, or a substantial decrease in urine output.

Arrange close follow-up after nephrology assessment and repeat laboratory evaluation.
