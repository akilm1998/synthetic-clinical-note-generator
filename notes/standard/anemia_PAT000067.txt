Chief Complaint

Initial outpatient consultation for evaluation of chronic anemia.

History of Present Illness

Joshua Ward is a 62-year-old man, 163 cm tall and weighing 73.1 kg, with BMI 27.5 kg/m², presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of anemia present for approximately 5 years. He has not been receiving active management for this condition. He denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor. There is no reported symptomatic change today.

Laboratory results demonstrate hemoglobin 8.7 g/dL, hematocrit 25.8%, and RBC count 2.84 million/µL. The MCV is 91 fL, indicating a normocytic pattern. Serum iron is low at 33 µg/dL, with ferritin elevated at 376 ng/mL, TIBC low at 193 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 17.1%. Overall, the iron profile is more consistent with impaired iron availability in the setting of chronic inflammation or chronic disease than with uncomplicated iron deficiency.

His medical history is significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 10 years and heart failure for 13 years. He currently smokes. No medication list, allergy history, prior procedures, or other medical history is provided.

Laboratory data: Hemoglobin 8.7 g/dL; hematocrit 25.8%; RBC 2.84 million/µL; MCV 91 fL; serum iron 33 µg/dL; ferritin 376 ng/mL; TIBC 193 µg/dL; transferrin saturation 17.1%.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, currently without reported anemia-related symptoms. The degree of anemia is clinically significant. The pattern of low hemoglobin and hematocrit with normal MCV, low serum iron and TIBC, and elevated ferritin is most consistent with anemia of chronic disease or chronic inflammation, with reduced circulating iron availability. His longstanding heart failure and type 2 diabetes may contribute to chronic disease-associated anemia, and renal function should be assessed because chronic kidney disease is also a potential contributor. There is no current evidence from the provided history of symptomatic hypoxia or hemodynamic instability, although the respiratory rate is mildly elevated.

Heart failure, present for 13 years, clinically without reported dyspnea or palpitations today. Anemia may increase cardiac workload and should be addressed in the context of his cardiac disease.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for 10 years. Glycemic control and renal status are relevant to evaluation of the anemia.

Tobacco use. Continued smoking may adversely affect cardiovascular and overall health.

Overweight by BMI at 27.5 kg/m².

Plan

Obtain or review a complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to further characterize the anemia and assess marrow response. Recommend renal function testing, vitamin B12 and folate levels, and inflammatory markers. Review the trend of hemoglobin over time and assess for any evidence of blood loss based on a complete history and examination.

Given the elevated ferritin and low TIBC, do not assume that routine oral iron alone will correct the anemia. Iron replacement, including consideration of intravenous iron, should be guided by the broader evaluation, renal function, inflammatory status, and clinical context. If chronic kidney disease is identified and anemia remains persistent, consideration of an erythropoietin-stimulating agent may be appropriate under specialist or coordinated medical management. No medication is initiated today based on the information provided.

Continue coordinated management of heart failure and diabetes, with particular attention to renal function and volume status. Smoking cessation should be strongly encouraged.

He should seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, fainting, sustained palpitations, marked weakness, or evidence of bleeding. Arrange outpatient follow-up after the recommended evaluation to review the anemia trend and determine targeted treatment.
