Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease and hypertension.

Subjective

Zachary Barnes is a 36-year-old man with an approximately 8-year history of chronic kidney disease, hypertension for 3 years, anemia of chronic disease for 1 year, and coronary artery disease for 1 year. He presents for routine outpatient follow-up. Renal disease has not been under active management.

He reports ongoing fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. Smoking status is documented as current smoker. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Height 174 cm, weight 67.5 kg, BMI 22.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 155/95 mmHg, pulse 84/min, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory results: serum creatinine 3.93 mg/dL, estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 69 mg/dL.

Objective examination findings are otherwise not documented.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. The patient has persistent fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, edema, urinary change, or dyspnea to suggest prominent symptomatic fluid overload or uremic manifestations at this visit. Given the duration of disease and current renal indices, he requires prompt re-engagement in kidney-focused care and close monitoring for complications.

Hypertension, currently above goal at 155/95 mmHg, with chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease increasing cardiovascular risk. Current antihypertensive regimen is not provided.

Anemia of chronic disease, documented for approximately 1 year, potentially contributing to fatigue. No current hemoglobin value is available for review.

Coronary artery disease, documented for approximately 1 year.

Current tobacco use, an additional renal and cardiovascular risk factor.

Plan

Recommend expedited nephrology consultation for comprehensive evaluation and management of substantially impaired kidney function, including discussion of renal replacement planning if clinically indicated. Recommend repeat renal function assessment and serum electrolytes, along with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Renal ultrasound may be considered to assess renal structure and exclude an obstructive process.

Address blood pressure promptly with medication reconciliation and initiation or adjustment of an appropriate antihypertensive regimen by the treating clinician, with attention to renal and coronary comorbidities. Encourage home blood pressure monitoring and avoidance of NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications.

Review dietary counseling appropriate for chronic kidney disease, including sodium restriction and individualized protein, potassium, phosphorus, and fluid guidance after laboratory review. Assess for anemia-related symptoms and manage based on CBC and iron evaluation when available.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation given the effects on cardiovascular and kidney disease progression.

Advised urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, edema, markedly decreased urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended after laboratory reassessment and nephrology evaluation.
