Chief Complaint

Annual review and chronic disease follow-up.

Subjective

Angela Holland is a 74-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. Essential hypertension has been present for approximately 2 years and is not currently under active management. Blood pressure today is markedly elevated at 178/101 mmHg. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis.

Past medical history is significant for chronic kidney disease for 3 years, hyperlipidemia for 5 years, and coronary artery disease for 13 years. She currently smokes. Weight is 65.1 kg at 157 cm, with BMI 26.4 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 178/101 mmHg; pulse 79 bpm; respiratory rate 18/min; temperature 36.8°C; oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory data: Sodium 139 mmol/L; potassium 4.4 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, currently markedly elevated and not under active management. She is asymptomatic without features suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency, but the degree of elevation is clinically significant and requires prompt confirmation and treatment planning, particularly in the setting of chronic kidney disease and established coronary artery disease.

Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 3 years. Electrolytes are currently within the provided reference ranges; renal function cannot be assessed from the available results.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, present for approximately 13 years. No current anginal or heart failure symptoms reported.

Hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 5 years.

Active tobacco use.

Overweight by BMI, with BMI 26.4 kg/m².

Preventive health maintenance reviewed as part of annual evaluation.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement after a period of seated rest and confirm with home or ambulatory readings if feasible. Arrange prompt outpatient follow-up to initiate an individualized antihypertensive regimen, with consideration of renal function, potassium level, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease when selecting therapy. Do not delay evaluation if blood pressure remains severely elevated.

Seek emergency care immediately for new chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, focal neurologic symptoms, confusion, vision loss, or other acute concerning symptoms.

Obtain renal function assessment and urinalysis as clinically appropriate for hypertension and chronic kidney disease monitoring. Consider ECG and review of lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk assessment; no results are available today.

Counseled regarding reduced sodium intake, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight maintenance, moderation of alcohol if applicable, and smoking cessation. Smoking cessation support and pharmacologic options should be offered at follow-up.

Continue coordinated cardiovascular and kidney disease risk-factor management. Review preventive care, including age-appropriate cancer screening, immunizations, fall-risk assessment, and osteoporosis screening, based on prior records and patient preference.

Follow up promptly for blood pressure management and chronic disease review.
