Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and fatigue.

Subjective
Alexandra Boyd is a 97-year-old woman with a 43-year history of chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 19 years, and anemia of chronic disease for 13 years. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. She does not smoke. Current medication use is not documented.

Objective
Height 162 cm, weight 65.6 kg, BMI 25.0 kg/m². Blood pressure 149/88 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respirations 20/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory results: serum creatinine 3.34 mg/dL, estimated GFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 94 mg/dL.

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with marked reduction in renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. Fatigue may be related to chronic kidney disease and known anemia of chronic disease. No current symptoms of fluid overload or overt uremic syndrome reported. Elevated blood pressure noted today. Longstanding diabetes is an important renal risk factor.

Plan
Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation given the degree of renal impairment and BUN elevation, with discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate.

Obtain or review a serum electrolytes panel, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously completed.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications for renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents. Encourage adequate but not excessive hydration and monitoring of blood pressure and urine output.

Seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, confusion, persistent nausea or vomiting, chest pain, markedly decreased urination, or inability to maintain oral intake. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after laboratory review and nephrology assessment.
