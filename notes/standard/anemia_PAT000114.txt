Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Logan Stewart is a 58-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 10 years. He reports no current fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or other cardiopulmonary symptoms. He does not report noticing pallor. Smoking status: never smoker.

Relevant comorbidities include rheumatoid arthritis for 17 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 23 years. No current anemia-directed treatment is documented.

Objective

Height 186 cm; weight 80.3 kg; BMI 23.2 kg/m².

Blood pressure 123/75 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL
Hematocrit 24.9%
RBC count 3.11 million/µL
MCV 80 fL
Serum iron 30 µg/dL
Ferritin 417 ng/mL
TIBC 266 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 11.3%

The CBC demonstrates significant anemia with a low-normal MCV. Iron studies show low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC, a pattern most consistent with iron-restricted erythropoiesis in the setting of chronic inflammation. He is hemodynamically stable by today’s vital signs and denies symptomatic cardiopulmonary compromise.

Assessment

Chronic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation associated with longstanding rheumatoid arthritis. The anemia is significant, with hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL, and has been present for approximately 10 years. The low transferrin saturation warrants confirmation that absolute iron deficiency is not contributing, although the elevated ferritin and low TIBC favor inflammatory iron sequestration rather than uncomplicated iron deficiency. Other potentially reversible contributors, including renal dysfunction, vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, and reticulocyte response abnormalities, have not been characterized in the available data.

Rheumatoid arthritis, longstanding, with potential contribution to chronic inflammatory anemia.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Plan

Obtain repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to further characterize the anemia and marrow response. Recommend renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers. Repeat iron studies may be considered with clinical correlation, particularly if inflammatory activity changes.

Assess rheumatoid arthritis inflammatory burden and pursue optimization of the underlying inflammatory disease in coordination with the clinician managing his rheumatoid arthritis. Review for any evidence of chronic blood loss or other cause of iron deficiency if clinically indicated.

Given the hemoglobin of 8.6 g/dL and prolonged lack of anemia-directed management, recommend timely reassessment after the above evaluation and consideration of hematology consultation. Iron replacement should be guided by confirmation of absolute iron deficiency and the overall clinical context; empiric treatment should not be assumed from the current results. If renal dysfunction is identified, management should account for possible anemia related to chronic kidney disease, including consideration of an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent only when clinically appropriate.

He should seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, palpitations, significant dizziness, or marked decline in exercise tolerance.
