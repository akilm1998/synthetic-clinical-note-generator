Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood glucose and hemoglobin A1c.

Subjective

John Jacob is a 70-year-old man with an approximately 11-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus who presents for focused follow-up of poor glycemic control. Recent laboratory results show HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 210 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. Diabetes management has not been established. He continues to smoke. Relevant comorbidities include longstanding obesity and depression.

Objective

Height is 160 cm, weight 87.0 kg, BMI 34.0 kg/m². Blood pressure is 131/76 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 37.0°C, and oxygen saturation 94%. HbA1c is 9.3%; fasting glucose is 210 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently significantly above glycemic goal based on HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 210 mg/dL. He is presently without hyperglycemic, neuropathic, infectious, or constitutional symptoms, but the degree of elevation warrants prompt treatment planning and monitoring.

Obesity, BMI 34.0 kg/m², contributing to cardiometabolic risk.

Depression, longstanding. No acute psychiatric concerns are reported in this encounter.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values indicate inadequate diabetes control despite the absence of symptoms. Recommend prompt initiation or intensification of a diabetes treatment plan, with medication selection individualized after review of renal function, contraindications, tolerability, cost, and patient preference. Obtain serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before finalizing therapy; obtain a fasting lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment. Encourage reduction of refined carbohydrates, portion control, gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated, and weight reduction.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with documentation of fasting and occasional postprandial readings, and review of results promptly for treatment adjustment. Counsel regarding symptoms of marked hyperglycemia and the need for urgent evaluation if vomiting, dehydration, confusion, severe weakness, or inability to maintain oral intake develops.

Smoking cessation was advised. Continue to address depression and its effect on adherence and lifestyle change. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for review of glucose readings, completion of recommended baseline diabetes evaluation, and treatment response.
