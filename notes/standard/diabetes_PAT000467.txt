Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for diabetes management and elevated blood glucose.

Subjective

Monica Shaw is a 51-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 11 years ago and currently not actively managed. Recent HbA1c is 9.3% and fasting glucose is 200 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has comorbid obesity, obstructive sleep apnea for approximately 12 years, and anxiety for approximately 4 years. Current weight is 82.5 kg at 152 cm. She reports active cigarette smoking. No medication regimen was provided.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 122/76 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 152 cm, weight 82.5 kg, BMI 35.7 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.3%; fasting glucose 200 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently above goal based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. She is asymptomatic today without reported polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, or evidence by history of wound or recurrent infection.

Obesity with BMI 35.7 kg/m².

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding.

Anxiety.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values indicate inadequate glycemic control and reviewed risks of untreated hyperglycemia, including kidney, eye, nerve, and cardiovascular complications.

Recommend prompt initiation of a structured diabetes treatment plan, including medication therapy and individualized nutrition and physical activity counseling. Medication selection should be reviewed with the patient after assessing renal function, contraindications, preferences, and ability to monitor glucose.

Recommend home glucose monitoring as clinically appropriate, with review of fasting and post-meal readings at follow-up.

Recommend renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes-related eye and foot evaluations. These are recommended assessments; no results are available today.

Counseled regarding portion control, reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated, and weight reduction. Encouraged smoking cessation and offered discussion of cessation support.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, marked weakness, inability to maintain hydration, or rapidly worsening hyperglycemic symptoms.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review glucose readings, complete recommended assessments, and establish an ongoing diabetes management regimen.
