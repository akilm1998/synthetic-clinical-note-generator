Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Cody Warren is a 49-year-old man, 162 cm tall and 58.3 kg (BMI 22.2 kg/m²), seen for outpatient medication review and management of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 3 years ago. Current renal indices are markedly abnormal, and the condition has not been actively managed. He reports fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, lower-extremity swelling, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. He does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include secondary hyperparathyroidism for approximately 1 year and hyperlipidemia for 16 years. No current medication regimen is documented for review; therefore, medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance cannot be assessed from the available treatment history. A complete medication reconciliation is needed, including prescription medications, over-the-counter products, and supplements, with particular attention to nephrotoxic agents and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 135/81 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Weight 58.3 kg; BMI 22.2 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 5.28 mg/dL, estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 79 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function and elevated BUN and creatinine. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction. He currently denies symptoms suggesting overt fluid overload or significant uremic manifestations, including nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, altered urination, edema, or dyspnea. Blood pressure is acceptable today. Given the degree of renal impairment and lack of active management, this requires prompt escalation of care and nephrology involvement.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, present for approximately 1 year, likely related to chronic kidney disease. Current treatment effectiveness and biochemical control cannot be determined from the information available today.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding for approximately 16 years. Medication use, adherence, tolerance, and current treatment response require reconciliation and review.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of severe chronic kidney disease and discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically indicated. The patient should seek emergency care for worsening shortness of breath, new swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, severe weakness, or other acute deterioration.

Obtain or arrange updated renal evaluation, including serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate. Review for anemia, electrolyte or acid-base disturbances, and progression of secondary hyperparathyroidism using current laboratory data.

Complete medication reconciliation promptly. Assess adherence, effectiveness, and tolerance of any renal, blood pressure, lipid, or mineral-bone disorder therapies before making medication changes. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a treating clinician. Medication dosing should be reviewed for the markedly reduced renal function.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and symptoms. Reinforce avoidance of unsupervised supplements and the importance of keeping nephrology follow-up. Reassess after medication reconciliation and renal evaluation, or sooner for any worsening symptoms.
