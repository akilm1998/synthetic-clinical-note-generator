Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated cholesterol and triglycerides.

Subjective

Eliana Matthews is a 47-year-old woman with a 6-year history of untreated hyperlipidemia, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 4 years, chronic kidney disease for 3 years, and longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. She currently smokes. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. No acute cardiovascular symptoms are reported today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 137/86 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 150 cm; weight 58.3 kg; BMI 25.9 kg/m².

Lipid profile: Total cholesterol 324 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 210 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 48 mg/dL, triglycerides 330 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia, unspecified, longstanding and currently not treated. Lipid values demonstrate severe LDL elevation with concomitant hypertriglyceridemia, substantially increasing her long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and active tobacco use. She has no reported symptoms suggestive of active ischemia or peripheral arterial disease. There are no reported cutaneous lipid deposits.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease are important comorbidities when selecting and monitoring lipid-lowering therapy.

Tobacco use, an additional modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated LDL and triglycerides and the need for prompt initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy after review of contraindications, renal function, hepatic status, and potential drug interactions. A high-intensity statin would generally be appropriate for this degree of LDL elevation and her diabetes and chronic kidney disease; adjunctive therapy may be considered if adequate LDL reduction is not achieved or if statin intolerance occurs.

Recommend confirmation with a fasting lipid panel and evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia, with monitoring tailored to her diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and fatty liver disease. Consider additional cardiovascular risk stratification, including lipoprotein(a), apolipoprotein B, or coronary artery calcium assessment, if treatment decisions remain uncertain.

Counseled regarding reduction of saturated and trans fats and refined carbohydrates, increased dietary fiber, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight maintenance, and complete smoking cessation. She was advised to seek emergency care for new chest pain, dyspnea, focal neurologic symptoms, or acute limb ischemic symptoms. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended to review treatment initiation and response.
