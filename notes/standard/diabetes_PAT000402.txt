Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Bob Hill is a 71-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 31 years ago. He is not currently reporting increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. Diabetes management has not been active recently. No smoking history; he reports no tobacco use.

Relevant comorbidities include longstanding obesity, present for approximately 33 years, and dyslipidemia for approximately 5 years. Current weight is 140 kg. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 124/77 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 188 cm, weight 140.0 kg, BMI 39.6 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.9%. Fasting glucose 220 mg/dL.

No acute hyperglycemic symptoms are reported. No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, longstanding and currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c of 9.9% and fasting glucose of 220 mg/dL. Despite the degree of hyperglycemia, he denies polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, wounds, or recurrent infections. There is no reported evidence of an acute hyperglycemic illness.

Severe obesity with BMI 39.6 kg/m², a significant contributor to insulin resistance and diabetes control.

Dyslipidemia, longstanding approximately 5 years; current lipid values were not provided.

Plan

Discussed the markedly elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of active diabetes treatment, dietary modification, weight reduction, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Recommend establishing or re-establishing a structured glucose-lowering medication plan, with selection based on renal function, contraindications, affordability, and patient preference. Medication reconciliation and adherence review should be completed at the next medication-focused visit.

Recommend home glucose monitoring as clinically appropriate and review of readings at follow-up. Counsel regarding recognition of hyperglycemia and symptoms requiring prompt evaluation, including persistent vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, inability to maintain hydration, or rapidly worsening urinary frequency and thirst.

Recommend assessment of renal function with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, as well as an updated fasting lipid profile for cardiovascular risk management. Recommend routine diabetic retinal evaluation and comprehensive foot assessment, including sensory and vascular examination, given the long duration of diabetes, although he currently denies neuropathic symptoms or foot wounds.

Encouraged a calorie-controlled, carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, gradual increase in physical activity, and sustained weight-loss efforts. Continue smoking avoidance.

Follow up promptly to review medication options and glucose data, with repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval.
