Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Nicholas Powell is a 67-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 25 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid results are markedly abnormal: total cholesterol 328 mg/dL, LDL-C 223 mg/dL, HDL-C 39 mg/dL, and triglycerides 332 mg/dL. He has known coronary artery disease for 3 years and chronic kidney disease for 17 years. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness. Xanthelasma is present. He does not smoke.

Objective

Height 169 cm, weight 82.8 kg, BMI 29.0 kg/m². Blood pressure 132/85 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%. Xanthelasma noted. No other examination findings provided.

Assessment

Severe mixed dyslipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and triglycerides, longstanding and untreated. His coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease place him at substantially increased cardiovascular risk. Low HDL-C and BMI in the overweight range are additional adverse risk features. No current symptoms suggestive of acute coronary or peripheral ischemia.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid levels and the need for prompt initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy, with a high-intensity statin generally indicated in the setting of coronary artery disease, after review of contraindications and renal considerations. If LDL-C remains above goal or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe and/or other specialist-directed therapy should be considered.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment, along with assessment for secondary contributors and medication safety monitoring using clinically appropriate renal and hepatic testing. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for additional risk assessment. Reinforced a heart-healthy diet low in saturated and trans fats, weight reduction, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Continue monitoring for chest discomfort, dyspnea, dizziness, or exertional leg pain; seek urgent care for new or persistent chest symptoms. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended to review treatment initiation and lipid response.
