Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Hailey Johnston is a 70-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 31 years and currently without active treatment documented. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include hypertension for 14 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 33 years. No interval cardiovascular symptoms are reported.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 148/88 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 180 cm, weight 94.3 kg, BMI 29.1 kg/m².

Recent lipid results: total cholesterol 342 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 230 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 36 mg/dL, triglycerides 382 mg/dL.

No xanthomas or xanthelasma are reported. No additional examination findings documented.

Assessment

Longstanding severe mixed dyslipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, elevated triglycerides, and low HDL cholesterol. The degree and duration of lipid elevation, together with age, hypertension, and longstanding diabetes, confer substantially increased atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk despite the absence of current chest discomfort or claudication. Hyperlipidemia is not currently being actively managed.

Hypertension with blood pressure above goal at today’s visit.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

BMI 29.1 kg/m², consistent with overweight status.

Plan

Discussed the significantly abnormal lipid profile and the importance of active cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after review of medication history, contraindications, potential adverse effects, and shared decision-making. If LDL cholesterol remains substantially elevated despite maximally tolerated statin therapy, consider addition of ezetimibe and further lipid specialist-directed therapy as appropriate.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fiber, whole grains, lean proteins, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and alcohol intake. Encourage gradual regular physical activity as tolerated and continued weight management.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and additional risk assessment, including ApoB and lipoprotein(a), based on shared decision-making and overall cardiovascular risk.

Review blood pressure and diabetes management at follow-up and coordinate comprehensive risk-factor control. Counsel regarding prompt evaluation for new chest discomfort, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain.

Follow up after medication and laboratory review to reassess lipid response and treatment tolerance.
