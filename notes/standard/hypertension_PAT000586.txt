Chief Complaint

Annual review and evaluation of elevated blood pressure with intermittent dizziness.

Subjective

Anna Cook is a 58-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has an approximately 8-year history of hypertension that has not been under active management. She reports dizziness but denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis.

She has a history of chronic kidney disease for approximately 18 years. No antihypertensive medications are listed. She does not smoke. Height is 161 cm and weight is 57.3 kg, with BMI 22.1 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure is 158/98 mmHg. Pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, and oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Serum sodium 142 mmol/L and potassium 4.0 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension

Blood pressure is significantly elevated at today’s visit in the setting of an approximately 8-year history of hypertension without active treatment. Dizziness is present, but she has no chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic visual symptoms, severe headache, or epistaxis to suggest an acute hypertensive emergency. Electrolytes are currently within expected range, with potassium 4.0 mmol/L and sodium 142 mmol/L.

2. Chronic kidney disease

Longstanding chronic kidney disease, reported duration 18 years. Kidney function and degree of renal impairment are not characterized in the available information. Hypertension requires active management with attention to renal protection and monitoring of kidney function and electrolytes.

3. Preventive health maintenance

Annual preventive review completed with attention to cardiovascular risk reduction. She is a nonsmoker and has a healthy BMI of 22.1 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the importance of prompt outpatient treatment of persistent hypertension, particularly given the history of chronic kidney disease. Review prior renal function and urine findings if available and obtain kidney function testing, urinalysis, and urine albumin assessment as clinically appropriate before or alongside medication initiation. Consider an antihypertensive regimen appropriate for chronic kidney disease, with selection guided by renal function, albuminuria, blood pressure response, and tolerability. Repeat serum electrolytes and renal function after treatment is started or adjusted.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, measured after several minutes of rest and using an appropriately sized cuff. Advise a lower-sodium diet, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and continued smoking avoidance. Discussed rising slowly from sitting or lying positions and reporting persistent or worsening dizziness.

An ECG and lipid profile are appropriate for cardiovascular risk assessment. Continue routine monitoring for hypertension-related renal, cardiac, and vascular complications.

Review age-appropriate preventive care, including immunizations and recommended breast, colorectal, cervical, and bone health screening based on prior results and current guideline eligibility.

Arrange close follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning, sooner for worsening dizziness or new symptoms. Seek urgent medical attention for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, or acute visual change.
