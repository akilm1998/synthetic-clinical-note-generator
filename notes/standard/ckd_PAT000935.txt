Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Morgan Oliver is a 98-year-old woman, 154 cm tall and weighing 53.8 kg (BMI 22.7 kg/m²), presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease reported for approximately 61 years. Disease has not been actively managed. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, changes in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath. She continues to smoke. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

She also has a 31-year history of hyperlipidemia.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 144/89 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 62 mg/dL, estimated GFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m².

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. The patient has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, edema, urinary changes, or dyspnea. Given the creatinine of 6.0 mg/dL, BUN of 62 mg/dL, and eGFR of 5 mL/min/1.73 m², this requires prompt medical reassessment and nephrology involvement. Current disease management appears inadequate. Blood pressure is above goal at 144/89 mmHg. Longstanding smoking and hyperlipidemia add cardiovascular risk.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the seriousness of the current kidney function results with the patient. Recommend urgent nephrology evaluation to review renal function, symptom burden, goals of care, and options for management. Recommend prompt repeat assessment with serum electrolytes, renal function testing, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone, as clinically appropriate. Renal ultrasound may also be considered if not previously performed.

Medication reconciliation and review of renal dosing, blood pressure management, and avoidance of nephrotoxic medications are needed; no current medications were provided today. Encourage avoidance of nonprescription NSAIDs unless specifically directed by a clinician.

Reviewed emergency precautions, including immediate evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, marked weakness, persistent nausea or vomiting, new swelling, substantially decreased urination, or other acute deterioration.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and cardiovascular risk reduction. Continue outpatient follow-up after urgent renal assessment, or sooner for any change in symptoms.
