Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Lydia Ford is a 53-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 11 years ago. Diabetes has not been actively managed recently. Recent laboratory results show markedly elevated HbA1c of 10.0% and fasting glucose of 255 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She reports ongoing tobacco use. History is also notable for depression of approximately 30 years’ duration. No current diabetes medications were provided for review.

Objective

Height 160 cm. Weight 59.1 kg. BMI 23.1 kg/m².

Blood pressure 123/82 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Recent HbA1c 10.0%. Fasting glucose 255 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c of 10.0% and fasting glucose of 255 mg/dL. She is presently asymptomatic despite significant hyperglycemia. No neuropathic or infectious symptoms reported. BMI is within the normal range.

Tobacco use.

History of longstanding depression.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values indicate inadequate diabetes control and carry increased long-term risk for microvascular and cardiovascular complications, even in the absence of symptoms. Reviewed nutrition, portion control, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages and refined carbohydrates, and the importance of regular physical activity as tolerated.

Recommend prompt initiation or re-establishment of pharmacologic diabetes treatment through shared decision-making, with medication selection based on renal function, cardiovascular risk, tolerability, cost, and patient preference. Obtain baseline renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before or at the time of treatment planning, along with a fasting lipid profile. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response.

Recommend routine comprehensive foot examination, retinal screening, and ongoing monitoring for symptoms of hyperglycemia, neuropathy, skin breakdown, or infection. Reviewed symptoms that should prompt earlier evaluation, including persistent vomiting, marked weakness, confusion, inability to maintain hydration, or rapidly worsening urinary frequency and thirst.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and offered assistance with cessation planning.

Depression history acknowledged; assess current mood, treatment history, and safety at follow-up and coordinate additional support as clinically indicated.

Follow up promptly to establish a definitive diabetes treatment plan and monitor response.
