Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for management of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Ashton Murray is a 66-year-old man presenting as a new patient for evaluation and management of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 13 years ago. Current diabetes management has not been established. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Medical history is also notable for dyslipidemia for approximately 4 years, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 27 years, and anxiety for approximately 40 years. He currently smokes. No medication regimen or allergies were provided.

Objective

Vital Signs: BP 122/75 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Height 174 cm; weight 86.0 kg; BMI 28.4 kg/m².

Laboratory Results:
Hemoglobin A1c 10.0%.
Fasting glucose 240 mg/dL.

Patient is overweight by BMI. No acute symptoms reported. No diabetic neuropathic or infectious symptoms reported.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications. Glycemic control is markedly above goal based on HbA1c of 10.0% and fasting glucose of 240 mg/dL. Despite the absence of reported hyperglycemic symptoms, he is at increased risk for microvascular and macrovascular complications given the duration of diabetes and currently inadequate management.

Dyslipidemia.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Anxiety.

Current tobacco use.

Overweight.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose, along with the risks of untreated hyperglycemia, including kidney, eye, nerve, and cardiovascular complications.

Recommend initiation of active diabetes treatment after review of renal and hepatic function. Metformin would generally be considered if appropriate; with an HbA1c of 10.0%, additional glucose-lowering therapy, including consideration of insulin, may be needed depending on clinical assessment and response. Medication risks, benefits, and adherence will be reviewed before prescribing.

Counseled on reducing refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, regular physical activity as tolerated, and gradual weight reduction. Encourage home glucose monitoring once a treatment plan is established.

Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes preventive assessments, including a comprehensive foot examination and dilated retinal examination.

Address cardiovascular risk reduction, including management of dyslipidemia and tobacco cessation counseling. Discussed cessation resources and the importance of avoiding tobacco.

Follow up promptly to establish the medication regimen and review diabetes monitoring and preventive care.
