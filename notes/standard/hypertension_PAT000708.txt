Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Logan Stewart is a 64-year-old man with a 25-year history of essential hypertension, currently requiring reassessment of blood pressure control and treatment effectiveness. He reports dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. He does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 26 years, hyperlipidemia for 18 years, and coronary artery disease for 5 years. Medication adherence and treatment tolerance were reviewed in the context of his current blood pressure; the antihypertensive regimen should be confirmed directly before making medication changes. No medication-related concern is documented today.

Objective

Blood pressure is 165/87 mmHg with pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, and oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Height is 170 cm, weight 63.9 kg, BMI 22.1 kg/m².

Serum sodium is 142 mmol/L and potassium is 4.0 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent elevation at today’s visit in the setting of longstanding disease and reported dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. There are no reported symptoms to suggest an acute hypertensive emergency. Given coexisting diabetes mellitus and coronary artery disease, sustained blood pressure elevation warrants prompt treatment review and close monitoring.

Serum electrolytes are currently within expected limits. Renal function and urinary albumin status are not available for medication selection or risk assessment in the information provided.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Plan

Confirm the complete current medication regimen, dosing schedule, adherence, and any adverse effects with the patient and dispensing pharmacy. Review home blood pressure technique and obtain twice-daily home readings for the next 1–2 weeks, including heart rate and documentation of dizziness or palpitations.

Reassess and optimize antihypertensive therapy promptly rather than attributing the current reading to an isolated measurement. Medication selection should account for diabetes and coronary artery disease; an ACE inhibitor or ARB, calcium-channel blocker, or thiazide-type diuretic may be considered as clinically appropriate after medication reconciliation and review of renal function, urinary albumin status, and contraindications. Do not make duplicate or abrupt changes until the active regimen is verified.

Recommend ECG evaluation for palpitations and coronary artery disease history. Recommend lipid profile and urinalysis, with renal function assessment as clinically appropriate before or during antihypertensive adjustment.

Counseled regarding sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and continued tobacco avoidance. Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management.

Seek urgent evaluation for chest pain, new or worsening shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or persistent markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after home readings and medication reconciliation to assess effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance.
