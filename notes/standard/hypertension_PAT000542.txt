Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Chase Fuller is a 73-year-old male seen for follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 14 years ago. He reports intermittent palpitations and fatigue. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. Hypertension has not been actively managed recently. He continues to smoke. Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for approximately 21 years, chronic kidney disease for 4 years, and hyperlipidemia for 34 years.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 167/99 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Height 167 cm, weight 73.3 kg, BMI 26.3 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 3.6 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and currently inadequate control. He is asymptomatic for acute hypertensive complications today, with no chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, or visual changes. Longstanding hypertension is occurring in the setting of chronic kidney disease and established coronary artery disease.

Palpitations and fatigue, without associated chest pain, dyspnea, or syncope reported.

Chronic kidney disease.

Coronary artery disease.

Hyperlipidemia.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure treatment and close follow-up given his age, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease. Recommend confirming readings with home blood pressure monitoring, recording results, and bringing the log to follow-up. Recommend initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy after medication reconciliation, with selection mindful of renal disease and coronary artery disease.

Recommend limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, moderating alcohol intake if applicable, and pursuing smoking cessation. Reviewed avoidance of over-the-counter agents that may raise blood pressure.

Recommend electrocardiogram for evaluation of palpitations and fatigue. Recommend ongoing monitoring of renal function and electrolytes and updated lipid assessment as clinically appropriate. Urinalysis may be obtained as part of hypertension and kidney disease evaluation.

Reviewed urgent precautions, including immediate evaluation for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, vision changes, or markedly higher blood pressure readings with symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning.
