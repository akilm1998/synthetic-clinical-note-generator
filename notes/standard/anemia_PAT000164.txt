Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of longstanding anemia.

Subjective

Charlotte Bailey is a 65-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 17 years. She reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or other anemia-related symptoms. No pallor has been noted by the patient.

Her medical history is notable for heart failure of approximately 18 years’ duration and chronic infection of approximately 21 years’ duration, both of which may contribute to chronic inflammation and anemia. The anemia has not been actively managed. She reports current tobacco use. Height is 174 cm and weight is 88.4 kg, with BMI 29.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 123/80 mmHg, pulse 84 bpm, respiratory rate 24/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin 8.2 g/dL, hematocrit 24.0%, RBC 3.0 million/µL, MCV 80 fL, serum iron 25 µg/dL, ferritin 372 ng/mL, TIBC 203 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 12.3%.

Patient denies symptoms of anemia, and oxygen saturation and pulse are stable at today’s visit. Respiratory rate is mildly elevated.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of longstanding chronic infection and heart failure. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are significantly reduced. The MCV is at the lower end of normal, with low serum iron, low TIBC, elevated ferritin, and low transferrin saturation, a pattern most consistent with impaired iron availability related to chronic disease. A concurrent iron-restricted component should be considered. She is currently clinically stable without reported fatigue, dyspnea, dizziness, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance, although the degree of anemia warrants active evaluation and management.

Tobacco use.

Overweight, BMI 29.2 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the laboratory findings and the relationship between chronic inflammation, heart failure, chronic infection, and reduced red blood cell production. Recommend prompt reassessment with repeat CBC, reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the anemia and assess for reversible contributors. Review for occult blood loss and other sources of iron deficiency should also be considered clinically.

Recommend coordination with the clinicians managing her chronic infection and heart failure. Consider hematology referral given the longstanding anemia and hemoglobin of 8.2 g/dL. Iron replacement, including oral or intravenous therapy, and erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy should be considered only after review of the underlying cause, renal status, inflammatory burden, and overall cardiovascular context.

No anemia-directed treatment is currently documented; treatment options and risks should be reviewed at the next management visit. Advise urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, persistent tachycardia, marked weakness, palpitations, or evidence of bleeding. Counsel regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of tobacco exposure.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after repeat evaluation rather than routine long-interval monitoring.
