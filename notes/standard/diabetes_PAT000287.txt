Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Lily Peterson is a 37-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 11 years ago. Current glycemic control is inadequate, with HbA1c 9.7% and fasting glucose 241 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet.

She has a history of hypertension for approximately 5 years, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 9 years, and obesity for approximately 1 year. She is a current smoker. No diabetes medications or other active treatment regimen are provided in the current record.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 132/82 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 164 cm, weight 106.5 kg, BMI 39.6 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.7%. Fasting glucose 241 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications. Glycemic control is significantly above goal based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. She currently denies symptoms of hyperglycemia, neuropathy, or infection, but the degree of biochemical hyperglycemia warrants active treatment and close follow-up. There is no supplied evidence of renal, retinal, neurologic, or other diabetic complication.

Obesity with BMI 39.6 kg/m², contributing to insulin resistance and increasing cardiometabolic risk.

Hypertension, longstanding, with blood pressure 132/82 mmHg today.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding, relevant to overall metabolic risk and medication selection.

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values indicate inadequately controlled diabetes despite the absence of typical symptoms. Recommend establishing or intensifying a structured diabetes treatment plan, including clinician-guided initiation of pharmacologic therapy such as metformin if appropriate after review of renal function and contraindications. Given the degree of hyperglycemia, medication therapy should not be deferred in favor of lifestyle measures alone.

Recommend nutrition counseling focused on portion control, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, increased fiber, and a sustainable calorie deficit. Encourage gradual increase in regular physical activity as tolerated, with a goal of at least 150 minutes weekly. Weight reduction would be expected to improve glycemic control, blood pressure, and fatty liver risk.

Recommend home glucose monitoring if treatment is initiated, with instructions individualized to the selected regimen. Review for symptoms of marked hyperglycemia, dehydration, or acute illness and seek prompt care if these develop.

Obtain or review renal function, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and liver-related monitoring as clinically appropriate. Recommend routine diabetic retinal evaluation and comprehensive foot examination, including assessment of sensation and pulses, as part of complication screening.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and address overall cardiovascular risk. Strongly counsel smoking cessation and offer evidence-based cessation support.

Follow up after initiation or adjustment of therapy to review glucose readings and tolerance, with repeat HbA1c in approximately 3 months to assess response.
