Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Avery Ward is a 65-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 21 years ago. She reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No interval symptoms suggestive of fluid overload or uremic illness are reported.

Her chronic medical history is also notable for hypertension for 19 years, gout for 5 years, and hyperlipidemia for 6 years. She does not smoke. Height is 165 cm and weight is 79.8 kg, with BMI 29.3 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 153/97 mmHg, pulse 70/min, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory data:
Serum creatinine 5.11 mg/dL
BUN 81 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m²

No edema, respiratory symptoms, or other CKD-related symptoms are reported today. Additional physical examination findings were not provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly impaired renal function on current laboratory testing. Creatinine is 5.11 mg/dL, BUN 81 mg/dL, and eGFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m². Despite the severity of the laboratory abnormalities, she currently denies uremic or fluid-overload symptoms. This requires prompt reassessment and active renal management.

Hypertension, longstanding, with blood pressure above goal at today’s visit.

Gout, longstanding for approximately 5 years.

Hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 6 years.

Overweight, BMI 29.3 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly reduced kidney function and the need for prompt nephrology evaluation to guide ongoing management and renal replacement planning. Recommend contacting nephrology urgently; emergency evaluation is warranted for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, severe weakness, progressive swelling, or markedly decreased urination.

Obtain repeat renal function assessment with serum electrolytes, creatinine, BUN, and eGFR, along with urinalysis and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. Recommend CBC to assess for anemia and intact parathyroid hormone testing for CKD-related mineral and bone complications. Renal ultrasound is also appropriate to evaluate renal structure and possible obstruction.

Review blood pressure management promptly, with attention to kidney-safe treatment and avoidance of nephrotoxic medications, including nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. Medication reconciliation is needed before selecting or adjusting therapy; no current medication regimen was provided.

Continue monitoring for edema, dyspnea, changes in urine output, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, and worsening fatigue. Address gout and hyperlipidemia in the context of advanced kidney dysfunction and coordinate management with the renal specialist.

Follow up promptly after repeat testing and specialist review, or sooner for any concerning symptoms.
