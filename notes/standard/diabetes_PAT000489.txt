Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated diabetes laboratory values.

Subjective

Adrian Wells is a 44-year-old man with a 9-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, currently without active diabetes management. Recent laboratory testing shows HbA1c 9.6% and fasting glucose 244 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 4 years’ duration and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of approximately 14 years’ duration. He does not smoke. Current weight is 70.8 kg at 181 cm, with BMI 21.6 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure 134/79 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

HbA1c 9.6%. Fasting glucose 244 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, poorly controlled based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. Although he is currently asymptomatic, the degree of hyperglycemia warrants prompt treatment and monitoring. His normal BMI and coexisting chronic kidney disease and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease should be considered when selecting therapy.

Chronic kidney disease, approximately 4 years’ duration. Renal function parameters are not provided today; medication selection and dosing should be guided by current kidney function.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, approximately 14 years’ duration. This is relevant to metabolic risk assessment and medication planning.

Plan

Arrange prompt outpatient diabetes management, including initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy after review of current renal and hepatic function. Confirm medication history and adherence at the next contact; no current diabetes medications are documented.

Obtain renal function testing with serum creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate, along with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, before finalizing therapy. Recommend fasting lipid profile and routine diabetes complication assessment, including retinal evaluation and comprehensive foot examination.

Begin home glucose monitoring with fasting and occasional postprandial checks if supplies are available, and maintain a record for review. Reinforce reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, regular physical activity as tolerated, and consistent meal planning.

Seek urgent care for vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, marked weakness, inability to maintain oral intake, or rapidly worsening hyperglycemic symptoms. Follow up promptly, preferably within 1–2 weeks, for treatment initiation and review of glucose readings and kidney function.
