Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension and cardiovascular risk management.

Subjective

Grace Powell is a 38-year-old woman with a 9-year history of essential hypertension, currently without an established management regimen. She presents for routine outpatient follow-up and disease-control review. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis.

Her medical history is also significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 7 years, hyperlipidemia for 6 years, and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She reports current tobacco use. No interval hypertensive or ischemic symptoms are reported.

Objective

Height is 163 cm and weight is 68.8 kg, with BMI 25.9 kg/m². Blood pressure is 152/92 mmHg. Pulse is 79 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, and oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Serum electrolytes show sodium 140 mmol/L and potassium 4.2 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension of 9 years’ duration with an elevated office blood pressure of 152/92 mmHg and inadequate current disease management. She is presently asymptomatic, without reported features suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency. Given the coexisting coronary artery disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, and active smoking, her overall cardiovascular risk is substantially increased and blood pressure control requires prompt attention.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 7 years.

Hyperlipidemia, duration 6 years.

Coronary artery disease, duration 1 year.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of consistent blood pressure management and cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend confirmation of out-of-office blood pressure control with a validated home monitor, including a written log of seated readings, while avoiding delays in establishing treatment. In view of persistent elevation and her coronary artery disease and diabetes, initiate or re-establish clinician-directed antihypertensive therapy after review of the complete medication history and renal function. The current sodium and potassium values are within expected ranges.

Counseled regarding a reduced-sodium dietary pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight control, and smoking cessation. Reinforced the importance of adherence to diabetes and lipid management and coordination of these measures with her cardiovascular risk-reduction plan.

Recommend ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of reassessment of cardiovascular and hypertensive end-organ risk. She should seek urgent medical attention for new chest pain, severe headache, acute shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review home blood pressure readings, complete medication reconciliation, and adjust therapy for sustained control.
