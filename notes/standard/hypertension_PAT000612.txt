Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Hayden Johnston is a 35-year-old male presenting for routine follow-up of essential hypertension, present for approximately 7 years. Blood pressure has not been under active management. He reports intermittent dizziness and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or nosebleeds. He does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 5 years and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. No current medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Height 165 cm; weight 66.2 kg; BMI 24.3 kg/m². Blood pressure 148/93 mmHg. Pulse 81 bpm. Respiratory rate 15/min. Temperature 36.8°C. Oxygen saturation 99%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 144 mmol/L, potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent elevation at today’s visit and associated intermittent dizziness and fatigue. Given the 7-year history and comorbid diabetes and coronary artery disease, blood pressure control warrants prompt outpatient management. No symptoms reported today to suggest hypertensive emergency.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 5 years’ duration.

Coronary artery disease, approximately 1 year’s duration.

Plan

Confirm blood pressure pattern with properly performed home readings, including morning and evening measurements, and bring the log to follow-up. Review sodium intake, physical activity, alcohol use, weight maintenance, and other modifiable cardiovascular risk factors.

Discuss initiation of antihypertensive therapy, with medication selection tailored to his diabetes, coronary artery disease, renal status, and overall cardiovascular risk. Review potential adverse effects and the importance of consistent follow-up before starting treatment.

Recommend baseline cardiovascular and hypertension evaluation with ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis. Continue monitoring serum electrolytes as clinically indicated, particularly after initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy.

Continue routine management of diabetes and coronary artery disease. Seek urgent medical attention for severe or worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated home blood pressure readings.

Return for blood pressure management follow-up after home readings are obtained, or sooner for worsening symptoms.
