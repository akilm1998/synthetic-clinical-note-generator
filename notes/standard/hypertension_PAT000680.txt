Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Natalie Ross is a 67-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 28-year history of essential hypertension that is currently not under active management. Blood pressure today is markedly elevated at 173/104 mmHg. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. She reports intermittent palpitations and fatigue.

Her medical history is also significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 32 years, chronic kidney disease for 14 years, and hyperlipidemia for 6 years. She does not smoke. Weight is stable at 55.4 kg with BMI 21.9 kg/m². No medication list or allergies were provided.

Objective

Height 159 cm; weight 55.4 kg; BMI 21.9 kg/m².
Blood pressure 173/104 mmHg.
Pulse 72 bpm.
Respiratory rate 15/min.
Temperature 37.0°C.
Oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Patient reports no acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms. Serum electrolytes: sodium 136 mmol/L, potassium 4.5 mmol/L.

Assessment

Longstanding essential hypertension, currently markedly uncontrolled in the office at 173/104 mmHg. She is asymptomatic for headache, visual change, chest pain, dyspnea, or other reported symptoms suggestive of acute end-organ involvement, although the degree of elevation requires prompt reassessment and treatment planning. Chronic kidney disease and longstanding diabetes substantially increase cardiovascular and renal risk.

Intermittent palpitations and fatigue. These symptoms warrant assessment in the context of severe hypertension and her cardiovascular risk profile.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, requiring ongoing risk-factor and renal surveillance.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Electrolytes are currently within the provided reference ranges, with sodium 136 mmol/L and potassium 4.5 mmol/L.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding, contributing to elevated cardiovascular risk.

Preventive health maintenance reviewed as part of annual assessment.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure during this visit after seated rest using appropriate cuff size and confirm with measurements in both arms if feasible. Given the reading of 173/104 mmHg and absence of reported acute symptoms, arrange prompt same-day clinical reassessment to establish treatment, review prior blood pressure history, and assess for end-organ effects. She should seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, confusion, acute visual changes, or persistent worsening palpitations.

Review and initiate an individualized antihypertensive regimen promptly, taking chronic kidney disease and diabetes into account. Medication selection should be based on renal function, urine albumin assessment, prior treatment history, contraindications, and repeat blood pressure measurements. Do not delay follow-up given the current level of elevation.

Recommend ECG for evaluation of palpitations and cardiovascular effects of longstanding hypertension. Recommend urinalysis with assessment for albuminuria, renal function testing, and repeat serum electrolytes as clinically appropriate for chronic kidney disease, diabetes, and antihypertensive planning. Obtain or review a lipid profile for cardiovascular risk management.

Counsel regarding limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, avoiding excess alcohol, and continuing tobacco avoidance. Her BMI is in the normal range; encourage maintenance of healthy weight and a balanced diet appropriate for diabetes and kidney disease.

Continue comprehensive diabetes and chronic kidney disease follow-up, including review of glycemic control, renal protection, and cardiovascular risk reduction. Review age-appropriate cancer screening, immunizations, and diabetic eye and foot care at preventive visits.

Arrange close follow-up after blood pressure reassessment and treatment initiation, with home blood pressure monitoring if available.
