Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for untreated hyperlipidemia.

History of Present Illness

Ava Mitchell is a 49-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 6 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 340 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 225 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 38 mg/dL, and triglycerides 383 mg/dL. She reports xanthelasma. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain suggestive of claudication, and dizziness.

Her history is also notable for coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 2 years ago and chronic kidney disease present for approximately 4 years. She currently smokes. No medication list or allergy history was provided for this visit.

Past Medical History

Hyperlipidemia for approximately 6 years, currently untreated.

Coronary artery disease for approximately 2 years.

Chronic kidney disease for approximately 4 years.

Social History

Current tobacco use. No additional tobacco exposure details were provided.

Vital Signs and Measurements

Blood pressure 127/87 mmHg. Pulse 75 bpm. Respiratory rate 15/min. Temperature 37.0°C. Oxygen saturation 98% on room air. Height 161 cm. Weight 64.5 kg. BMI 24.9 kg/m².

Objective

Patient is clinically stable by available vital signs. Xanthelasma is reported. No chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness is reported. No additional physical examination findings are documented.

Laboratory Data

Total cholesterol: 340 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Severe hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and hypertriglyceridemia

Six-year history of untreated dyslipidemia with total cholesterol 340, LDL 225, triglycerides 383, and low HDL of 38 mg/dL. The presence of xanthelasma supports clinically significant lipid excess. Given the degree of LDL elevation and coexisting coronary artery disease, she is at very high cardiovascular risk and requires prompt risk-factor management. The LDL level also warrants assessment for secondary contributors and possible inherited lipid disorder, although no family history is available in the current record.

2. Coronary artery disease

Established coronary artery disease increases the importance of aggressive LDL reduction and tobacco cessation. She currently denies chest discomfort or other reported ischemic symptoms.

3. Chronic kidney disease

Chronic kidney disease may increase cardiovascular risk and has implications for medication selection and dosing. Renal function should be reviewed before finalizing lipid-lowering therapy.

4. Current tobacco use

Ongoing smoking is a major modifiable cardiovascular risk factor in the setting of coronary artery disease and severe dyslipidemia.

Plan

Recommend prompt initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after medication reconciliation and review of renal and hepatic function. Given the LDL level and known coronary artery disease, statin therapy alone may not be sufficient; addition of ezetimibe should be considered if LDL reduction remains inadequate or if the initial response is insufficient. If substantial elevation persists despite maximally tolerated therapy, referral for consideration of additional nonstatin therapy, including a PCSK9 inhibitor, would be appropriate.

Obtain a repeat fasting lipid panel to confirm the current pattern and establish a treatment baseline. Recommend evaluation for secondary causes of severe dyslipidemia with clinically appropriate renal, hepatic, thyroid, and glucose testing. Consider apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a) measurements for further cardiovascular risk characterization. Medication reconciliation is needed before treatment is finalized, including review of prior lipid-lowering therapy, intolerance, and adherence.

Triglycerides are elevated but below the range generally associated with immediate pancreatitis risk. Initial management should focus on LDL reduction, smoking cessation, limiting saturated and trans fats, reducing refined carbohydrates and alcohol if applicable, and maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated. Her BMI is 24.9 kg/m²; weight reduction is not the primary issue, but a heart-healthy dietary pattern is strongly advised.

Counseled regarding complete smoking cessation. Offer evidence-based cessation support and pharmacotherapy after reviewing preferences and contraindications.

Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management in coordination with the clinician managing her coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease. Seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening chest discomfort, shortness of breath, syncope, or exertional leg symptoms.

Follow-up

Return after medication reconciliation and repeat lipid assessment to review results, confirm tolerance of therapy, and adjust treatment intensity.
