Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Isabella Parker is a 77-year-old woman, nonsmoker, presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 3 years ago. Her blood pressure has not been actively managed. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. No acute concerns reported today.

Relevant history includes type 2 diabetes mellitus for 27 years. Current weight is 76.7 kg at a height of 164 cm, with BMI 28.5 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 151/92 mmHg; pulse 74 bpm; respiratory rate 15/min; temperature 36.7°C; oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 164 cm, weight 76.7 kg, BMI 28.5 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 136 mmol/L, potassium 3.7 mmol/L.

Patient is clinically stable and without reported symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive end-organ involvement.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, currently above the desired range and not under active treatment. Patient is asymptomatic today, with no reported features of hypertensive emergency.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, 27 years’ duration.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Discussed the importance of consistent blood pressure management, particularly in the setting of longstanding diabetes and increased cardiovascular and renal risk. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, including measurements taken at different times of day, and review of technique and cuff size.

Reviewed lifestyle measures including reducing dietary sodium, limiting alcohol if applicable, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, and gradual weight reduction. Continue avoidance of tobacco.

Discussed initiation of antihypertensive pharmacotherapy; patient should be engaged in shared decision-making regarding agent selection and monitoring. Renal function and urine testing should be obtained as clinically appropriate before or during treatment planning, given hypertension and diabetes. Consider baseline ECG and lipid assessment as part of cardiovascular risk evaluation.

Serum sodium and potassium are currently within acceptable limits.

Advised urgent evaluation for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, acute visual change, or markedly elevated home blood pressure readings.

Follow up in the near term for blood pressure reassessment and review of home readings, with earlier follow-up if symptoms develop.
