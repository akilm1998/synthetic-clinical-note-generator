Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Allison Hamilton is a 65-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 27 years. Diabetes is currently not actively managed. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No smoking history. 

She has longstanding obesity, present for approximately 19 years.

Objective

Height 168 cm; weight 107.0 kg; BMI 37.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 126/77 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Hemoglobin A1c 9.6%. Fasting glucose 253 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently significantly above glycemic goal based on HbA1c of 9.6% and fasting glucose of 253 mg/dL. She is currently asymptomatic despite marked hyperglycemia.

Severe obesity, longstanding, contributing to insulin resistance and difficulty with glycemic control.

Plan

Discussed the importance of re-establishing active diabetes management and the risks of persistent hyperglycemia, including cardiovascular, renal, ocular, neurologic, and infectious complications.

Recommend initiation of an evidence-based antihyperglycemic medication regimen after review of renal function and contraindications; metformin would be a reasonable initial option if appropriate. Given the degree of hyperglycemia, escalation beyond lifestyle measures alone may be necessary.

Counseled regarding reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, increased dietary fiber, and gradual progression toward regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage weight reduction.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with review of readings at follow-up.

Obtain renal assessment including serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, as well as a fasting lipid profile for comprehensive diabetes risk assessment. Recommend routine diabetic retinal examination and comprehensive foot evaluation.

Follow up promptly after initiation of management to reassess glucose control and tolerance of therapy; repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval.
