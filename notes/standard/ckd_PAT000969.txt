Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Marcus Cross is a 39-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 13-year history of chronic kidney disease and a 3-year history of hypertension. His kidney disease has not been under active management. He reports fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, changes in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath. He does not smoke.

No current medication regimen, allergies, family history, or prior specialist care are documented for this visit.

Objective

Height 175 cm, weight 77.5 kg, BMI 25.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 146/94 mmHg, pulse 75/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Recent laboratory results: creatinine 3.32 mg/dL, estimated GFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 71 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of 13 years’ duration with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN and creatinine. The patient is currently symptomatic with fatigue but has no reported nausea, pruritus, cramps, fluid retention, urinary change, or dyspnea. This requires prompt renal evaluation and structured ongoing management.

Hypertension, above goal today at 146/94 mmHg, in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

BMI 25.3 kg/m². Nonsmoker.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology referral for evaluation of advanced chronic kidney disease, review of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate, and establishment of longitudinal kidney care.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, repeat renal function testing, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Recommend renal ultrasound to assess renal structure and exclude an obstructive process.

Perform careful medication reconciliation at follow-up and review blood pressure treatment options that are appropriate for renal function. Encourage home blood pressure monitoring and a low-sodium diet. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications unless specifically approved by a clinician. Review dosing of all prescribed and over-the-counter medications with renal function in mind.

Counsel regarding symptoms requiring urgent assessment, including worsening shortness of breath, new swelling, marked reduction in urine output, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or rapidly worsening weakness.

Continue routine preventive care, including review of immunization status, cardiovascular risk factors, nutrition, physical activity, and age-appropriate health screening at subsequent visits.

Follow up promptly after laboratory testing and nephrology referral, with earlier review for any worsening symptoms.
