Chief Complaint
Initial consultation for management of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

History of Present Illness
Anthony Cooper is a 76-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 23 years ago. Current glycemic control is poor, with HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 230 mg/dL. He reports numbness and tingling in the hands or feet. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, and frequent infections.

He is not currently described as having an active diabetes management regimen. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 13 years’ duration and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of approximately 32 years’ duration. He currently smokes. No medication list is provided.

Past Medical History
Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 23 years.
Chronic kidney disease, approximately 13 years.
Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, approximately 32 years.

Social History
Current tobacco use. Further social history was not provided.

Review of Systems
Constitutional: Denies fatigue and unintentional weight loss.
Endocrine: Denies increased thirst, frequent urination, and increased hunger.
Eyes: Denies blurred vision.
Neurologic: Reports numbness and tingling involving the hands or feet.
Skin/Infectious: Denies slow-healing wounds and frequent infections.

Objective
Blood pressure 151/77 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 186 cm. Weight 94.4 kg. BMI 27.3 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.9%. Fasting glucose 230 mg/dL.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus with markedly elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose, indicating inadequate glycemic control. He has a longstanding 23-year history of diabetes and is not currently described as receiving active management. Numbness and tingling raise concern for diabetes-associated peripheral sensory symptoms and warrant focused neurologic and foot assessment, although no formal diabetic complication is established in the information available.

Chronic kidney disease and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease are important comorbidities when selecting and dosing diabetes therapy. Elevated office blood pressure is also noted and should be reassessed.

Plan
Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of consistent diabetes treatment, nutrition, physical activity as tolerated, and home glucose monitoring. Review and initiate an individualized glucose-lowering regimen, taking renal function and liver disease into account; obtain current serum creatinine and estimated GFR before selecting or adjusting therapy.

Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and fasting lipid profile as part of diabetes risk assessment. Arrange a comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including monofilament sensation and peripheral vascular assessment, given his numbness and tingling. Recommend a dilated retinal examination and review of routine diabetes preventive care.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and reduction of cardiovascular risk. Recheck blood pressure at follow-up and address persistent elevation in the context of his diabetes and chronic kidney disease. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended to review glucose readings, laboratory evaluation, treatment tolerance, and glycemic response.
