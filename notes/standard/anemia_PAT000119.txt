Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of anemia.

Subjective

Naomi Myers is a 77-year-old woman, 154 cm tall and weighing 69.5 kg, with BMI 29.3 kg/m², presenting for outpatient review of anemia management. She reports no fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, dyspnea, palpitations, or visible pallor. No anemia-directed medication regimen is reported today; therefore, medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance cannot be meaningfully assessed. She has not reported adverse effects from anemia treatment.

Her medical history is notable for inflammatory bowel disease for approximately 43 years, malignancy for approximately 31 years, and rheumatoid arthritis for approximately 9 years, all of which may contribute to chronic inflammation and impaired iron utilization. She currently smokes.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 136/79 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respirations 20/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL
Hematocrit 25.4%
RBC 3.02 million/µL
MCV 84 fL
Serum iron 20 µg/dL
Ferritin 298 ng/mL
TIBC 206 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 9.7%

Assessment

1. Anemia consistent with chronic disease/inflammation. Persistent anemia with hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL and hematocrit 25.4%. The normocytic MCV of 84 fL, low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and low TIBC are most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation with functional iron restriction. Her longstanding inflammatory bowel disease, rheumatoid arthritis, and malignancy are relevant contributors. She is currently clinically stable by vital signs and denies cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms.

2. Medication review. No active iron or erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy is reported. There is no current regimen for which effectiveness, adherence, or tolerance can be evaluated.

3. Tobacco use. Current smoking noted and may adversely affect overall health and chronic disease management.

Plan

Discussed laboratory pattern and likely relationship to chronic inflammatory conditions. No medication changes made today because no active anemia-directed regimen is reported.

Recommend review of treatment options with consideration of the underlying inflammatory conditions and overall goals of care. Oral or intravenous iron should be considered only after individualized assessment of likely benefit and gastrointestinal tolerance; the elevated ferritin does not exclude functional iron deficiency, given the transferrin saturation of 9.7%. An erythropoiesis-stimulating agent may be considered only if clinically appropriate after evaluation of contributing conditions and risks.

Recommend additional evaluation with repeat CBC, reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers. Continue assessment for ongoing blood loss or active inflammation in the context of her inflammatory bowel disease and malignancy.

Reviewed symptoms that should prompt urgent evaluation, including new shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, syncope, marked weakness, or rapid clinical decline. Encourage smoking cessation. Follow up after medication and laboratory review to reassess anemia and determine an appropriate treatment plan.
