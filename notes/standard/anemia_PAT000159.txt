Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Diana Black is a 54-year-old woman presenting for outpatient review of longstanding anemia. Anemia has been present for approximately 22 years in the setting of inflammatory bowel disease for 18 years. She reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or other anemia-related symptoms. No pallor is reported. She does not smoke.

Medication review was directed toward anemia treatment. No active iron or erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy is identified in the available treatment history; therefore, effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be assessed for an established anemia regimen. There are no reported medication-related symptoms.

Objective

Height 172 cm, weight 83.1 kg, BMI 28.1 kg/m².

Blood pressure 127/77 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Hemoglobin 9.2 g/dL, hematocrit 27.4%, RBC 3.19 million/µL, MCV 86 fL. Serum iron 28 µg/dL, ferritin 173 ng/mL, TIBC 233 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 12%.

Assessment

Chronic, currently asymptomatic normocytic anemia. The low serum iron and transferrin saturation with ferritin 173 ng/mL and relatively low TIBC are most consistent with impaired iron availability in the setting of chronic inflammation, particularly given the long history of inflammatory bowel disease. The degree of anemia remains clinically significant despite the absence of cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms.

Iron deficiency may coexist with inflammatory anemia, and the available results do not by themselves establish the relative contribution of each process. No evidence of hemodynamic instability is present based on the provided vital signs. BMI is 28.1 kg/m². Nonsmoker.

Plan

Review the anemia treatment plan and confirm whether oral or intravenous iron has been used previously, including response, adherence, gastrointestinal tolerance, and any adverse effects. Avoid assuming benefit from iron therapy without objective reassessment.

Recommend repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the chronic anemia. Consider reassessment of inflammatory bowel disease activity and ongoing intestinal blood loss or impaired absorption in coordination with the clinician managing her bowel disease.

If iron replacement is indicated after clinical review, route and dose should be selected based on inflammatory bowel disease activity, absorption, prior tolerance, and response to therapy. Intravenous iron may be considered if oral therapy is ineffective or poorly tolerated. Erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy is not indicated solely from the information available and would require an appropriate underlying indication and specialist-directed monitoring.

Monitor for new fatigue, exertional dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, or overt gastrointestinal bleeding; seek urgent evaluation if severe or rapidly progressive symptoms occur. Follow up after repeat evaluation of the anemia and treatment history.
