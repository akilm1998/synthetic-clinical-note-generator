Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of hypertension.

Subjective

Sydney Fuller is a 37-year-old woman with a 12-year history of essential hypertension presenting for medication review. Hypertension has not been actively managed, and no current antihypertensive regimen is identified for effectiveness, adherence, or tolerance review.

She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, or epistaxis. She reports fatigue. No smoking history. Comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 1 year, hyperlipidemia for 4 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 1 year.

Objective

Height 174 cm; weight 73.3 kg; BMI 24.2 kg/m².

Blood pressure 152/87 mmHg
Pulse 73 bpm
Respiratory rate 17/min
Temperature 36.9°C
Oxygen saturation 97%

Serum sodium 147 mmol/L
Serum potassium 4.2 mmol/L

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with above-goal office blood pressure and no active antihypertensive treatment identified. Long duration of disease and coexisting coronary artery disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus increase cardiovascular risk. Current potassium is normal; sodium is mildly elevated.

Fatigue, nonspecific.

Comorbid hyperlipidemia and coronary artery disease.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management, adherence, and long-term cardiovascular risk reduction. Begin home blood pressure monitoring with twice-daily readings and bring the log to follow-up.

Recommend initiation of guideline-directed antihypertensive therapy after medication reconciliation and confirmation of renal function and other baseline safety parameters. An ACE inhibitor or ARB would be reasonable to consider given concomitant diabetes and coronary artery disease, provided there are no contraindications. Review treatment response and tolerance after initiation; specifically monitor for dizziness, cough or angioedema if an ACE inhibitor is selected, and changes in renal function or potassium.

Counseled on limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity, moderating alcohol intake, and continuing weight maintenance. Reinforced adherence to cardiovascular risk-reduction measures.

Recommend ECG, lipid profile, urinalysis, and assessment of renal function as part of cardiovascular and hypertension evaluation. Continue management of hyperlipidemia, diabetes, and coronary artery disease with attention to overall risk-factor control.

Seek urgent evaluation for chest pain, acute shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up promptly after home blood pressure data and baseline evaluation are available to establish and titrate therapy.
