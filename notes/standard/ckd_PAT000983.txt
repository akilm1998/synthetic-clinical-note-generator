Chief Complaint:
Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and markedly reduced renal function.

Subjective:
Matthew Reed is a 92-year-old man, 186 cm tall and 89.6 kg (BMI 25.9 kg/m²), with a reported 48-year history of chronic kidney disease and longstanding hypertension for approximately 25 years. He reports fatigue. He denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. He currently smokes. Medication regimen was not provided.

Objective:
Blood pressure 164/87 mmHg, pulse 73/min, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Serum creatinine 4.12 mg/dL. BUN 94 mg/dL. eGFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m². No edema or other examination findings documented.

Assessment:
Advanced chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced eGFR and azotemia, associated with fatigue. He currently has no reported nausea, pruritus, volume-overload symptoms, or respiratory symptoms. Longstanding hypertension is above goal and may be contributing to ongoing renal injury. At age 92, prompt assessment of renal trajectory, complications, goals of care, and appropriateness of kidney replacement planning is warranted.

Plan:
Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management and discussion of prognosis and kidney replacement options in the context of age and overall goals of care.

Obtain repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound to assess kidney structure and exclude obstruction.

Review and optimize blood pressure management; medication reconciliation is required before initiating or adjusting therapy. Avoid NSAIDs, iodinated contrast when alternatives exist, and other nephrotoxic exposures. Review all medications for renal dosing.

Counseled regarding urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, edema, markedly decreased urine output, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness. Encourage smoking cessation. Close follow-up after laboratory reassessment and nephrology consultation.
