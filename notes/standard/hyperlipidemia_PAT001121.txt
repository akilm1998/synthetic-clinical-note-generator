Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective
Jasmine Spencer is an 86-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 48 years and currently without active management. Recent lipid results demonstrate marked elevation of total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with concomitant hypertriglyceridemia. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include obesity, present for approximately 29 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, present for approximately 12 years.

Objective
Vital signs: Blood pressure 131/80 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 175 cm; weight 96.8 kg; BMI 31.6 kg/m².

Lipid panel:
Total cholesterol 386 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 279 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 42 mg/dL
Triglycerides 327 mg/dL

No cardiovascular or cutaneous symptoms are reported today.

Assessment
Longstanding untreated hyperlipidemia with a markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides. The lipid pattern is consistent with severe mixed dyslipidemia and confers substantial atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk despite the absence of current anginal, claudication, or neurologic symptoms. Her obesity and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease are relevant metabolic comorbidities. Blood pressure is currently 131/80 mmHg.

Plan
Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and triglycerides and the importance of active risk-reduction management.

Recommend initiation of guideline-directed lipid-lowering therapy after review of contraindications, medication history, and patient preferences. A moderate- to high-intensity statin would generally be considered, with addition of non-statin therapy if adequate LDL reduction is not achieved or if statin therapy is not tolerated.

Recommend a heart-healthy dietary pattern emphasizing reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess calories, along with gradual increases in physical activity as tolerated and weight reduction.

Recommend confirmation with a fasting lipid panel and assessment for potentially contributory secondary factors, including thyroid, renal, hepatic, and glycemic abnormalities, before or at the time of treatment initiation. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk characterization.

Monitor for new chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, focal neurologic symptoms, or other manifestations of vascular disease and seek prompt evaluation if these occur.

Follow up after laboratory reassessment and treatment discussion to review response and tolerance.
