Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Maria Armstrong is a 71-year-old woman with an 18-year history of anemia, chronic kidney disease for 15 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 11 years, and inflammatory bowel disease for 47 years. She is a current smoker. She reports headache and appears pale. She denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. No current anemia-directed treatment is documented.

Objective

Height 167 cm, weight 58.0 kg, BMI 20.8 kg/m². Blood pressure 137/83 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Laboratory results: hemoglobin 9.3 g/dL, hematocrit 28.0%, RBC count 3.11 million/µL, MCV 90 fL, serum iron 41 µg/dL, ferritin 327 ng/mL, TIBC 239 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 17.2%.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia with reduced hemoglobin, hematocrit, and RBC count. The low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and low TIBC are most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation, with likely contributions from chronic kidney disease and inflammatory bowel disease. Her long duration of disease and pallor are noted. She is currently hemodynamically stable without cardiopulmonary symptoms or tachycardia. Headache may be associated with anemia but is not accompanied by other reported neurologic or systemic symptoms.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, relevant contributor to impaired erythropoiesis.

Inflammatory bowel disease, longstanding, with potential contribution to chronic inflammation and iron-restricted erythropoiesis.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the laboratory pattern and likely multifactorial nature of the anemia. Recommend initiation of an organized anemia evaluation and management plan, including repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers. Assess for ongoing inflammatory bowel disease activity and other sources of chronic inflammation as clinically indicated.

Given the chronic kidney disease and hemoglobin of 9.3 g/dL, recommend coordination with the clinician managing her renal disease regarding anemia management and consideration of iron replacement or erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy based on renal function, overall clinical status, and repeat testing. Iron therapy should be individualized because ferritin is elevated despite low transferrin saturation.

Continue monitoring for worsening fatigue, weakness, dyspnea, palpitations, dizziness, chest discomfort, or reduced exercise tolerance. Counsel regarding smoking cessation. Arrange outpatient reassessment after the recommended evaluation to review disease control and treatment options.
