Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Gavin Franklin is a 74-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes, diagnosed approximately 10 years ago. Diabetes is currently not being actively managed with an established treatment plan. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has longstanding obesity, present for approximately 27 years. He currently smokes. No diabetes-related complications are reported today.

Objective

Height 188 cm. Weight 126.9 kg. BMI 35.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 131/78 mmHg. Pulse 82 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 36.6°C. Oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin A1c 10.0% and fasting glucose 253 mg/dL, consistent with significantly elevated blood glucose and inadequate glycemic control.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications. Glycemic control is substantially above goal based on hemoglobin A1c of 10.0% and fasting glucose of 253 mg/dL, despite the absence of current hyperglycemic symptoms. His 10-year duration of diabetes and obesity increase his risk for future microvascular and cardiovascular complications.

Obesity, BMI 35.9 kg/m², longstanding.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the laboratory results and the importance of promptly addressing the markedly elevated glucose levels, even in the absence of symptoms. Reviewed nutrition, reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, and gradual increase in regular physical activity as tolerated.

Recommend initiation of a structured diabetes treatment plan, including consideration of pharmacologic therapy after review of renal function, contraindications, cost, and patient preference. Recommend obtaining serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio for baseline diabetes complication and medication assessment. A fasting lipid profile and routine diabetes-related eye and foot evaluations should also be addressed.

Reviewed symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and advised urgent evaluation for vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, inability to maintain hydration, or rapidly worsening weakness. Encouraged home glucose monitoring if available and keeping a log for review.

Counseled regarding weight reduction and smoking cessation. Follow up should be arranged soon to review treatment options and glucose readings, with repeat glycemic assessment after an appropriate interval.
