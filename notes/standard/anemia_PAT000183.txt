Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for chronic anemia and recent headache.

Subjective

Matthew Reed is a 46-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of anemia present for approximately 5 years and not currently under active management. He reports headache but denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor. He has type 2 diabetes mellitus for 15 years. He currently smokes. No medication use was provided.

Objective

Height 169 cm; weight 77.4 kg; BMI 27.1 kg/m². Blood pressure 126/81 mmHg, pulse 88 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent laboratory results: hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL, hematocrit 26.3%, RBC 3.06 million/µL, MCV 86 fL, serum iron 29 µg/dL, ferritin 337 ng/mL, TIBC 218 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 13.3%.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic disease based on significant normocytic anemia with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, reduced TIBC, and elevated ferritin. The degree of anemia warrants prompt evaluation despite limited symptoms. Diabetes is a relevant chronic comorbidity; renal and other inflammatory causes should be assessed. Headache may be related to anemia but requires reassessment if persistent or worsening.

Plan

Arrange prompt outpatient evaluation with repeat CBC and reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers. Review for occult blood loss, chronic infection, autoimmune or inflammatory disease, and other contributing causes; consider evaluation for mixed iron deficiency if clinically indicated. Do not start iron therapy solely on the current results without clarifying the etiology, given the elevated ferritin and low TIBC. Consider hematology referral if anemia persists, worsens, or the initial evaluation is unrevealing.

Advise urgent evaluation for worsening headache, new neurologic symptoms, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, syncope, or marked weakness. Encourage smoking cessation and continued primary care management of longstanding diabetes.
