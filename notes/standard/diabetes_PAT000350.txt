Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Brandon Cox is a 38-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 5 years ago. Diabetes has not been actively managed. Recent glycemic markers remain elevated, with HbA1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 241 mg/dL.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. He reports numbness and tingling in the hands and feet. No medication regimen is provided. He continues to smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include obstructive sleep apnea for 3 years, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 2 years, and anxiety for 17 years.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 120/78 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 179 cm, weight 95.5 kg, BMI 29.8 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.2%. Fasting glucose 241 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with poor glycemic control based on HbA1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 241 mg/dL. Although he denies typical hyperglycemic symptoms, he reports numbness and tingling of the hands and feet, concerning for possible early peripheral neuropathic involvement and warranting further evaluation.

Overweight, with BMI 29.8 kg/m².

Tobacco use.

Obstructive sleep apnea, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and anxiety by history.

Plan

Discussed the significance of current hyperglycemia and the increased risk of microvascular and cardiovascular complications. Recommend establishing an active diabetes treatment plan, including consideration of pharmacologic therapy and referral or follow-up with a clinician experienced in diabetes management. Medication options should account for weight, renal function, liver disease, cardiovascular risk, cost, and patient preference.

Counseled regarding reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, increased dietary fiber, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage gradual weight reduction.

Recommend home glucose monitoring if clinically appropriate, with review of fasting and postprandial readings at follow-up. Recommend repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response.

Recommend evaluation of renal status with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive foot examination, and diabetic retinal screening. Review for progression of numbness or tingling, foot injury, skin breakdown, or weakness at subsequent visits.

Smoking cessation counseling provided; encouraged cessation and offered support with a structured quit plan.

Continue management of obstructive sleep apnea, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and anxiety in coordination with the primary care team. Follow up promptly to initiate and monitor diabetes treatment, with earlier reassessment for worsening symptoms, marked hyperglycemia, vomiting, dehydration, or confusion.
