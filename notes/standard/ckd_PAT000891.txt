Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Jacob Long is a 59-year-old man, 186 cm tall and 89.3 kg (BMI 25.8 kg/m²), presenting for outpatient medication review in the setting of an 8-year history of chronic kidney disease. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No current CKD-directed medication regimen is documented for review; therefore, medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be assessed today. He continues to smoke.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 135/83 mmHg, pulse 81/min, respirations 16/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 3.05 mg/dL
BUN 66 mg/dL
eGFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m²

Anemia of chronic disease, documented for approximately 2 years. No additional examination findings provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with significant reduction in renal filtration and elevated nitrogenous waste markers. The patient is currently asymptomatic from a uremic and fluid-overload standpoint, with stable-appearing vital signs and no reported edema or dyspnea. His blood pressure is acceptable at today’s visit. Given the degree of renal impairment and the absence of an established documented treatment regimen, medication reconciliation and coordinated renal management are important.

Anemia of chronic disease associated with chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 2 years.

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Plan

Complete medication reconciliation, including prescription medications, over-the-counter agents, and supplements. Establish whether any renal-protective, antihypertensive, diuretic, phosphate-binding, calcium-based, or anemia-directed therapies are currently being taken before making medication changes. Assess adherence, effectiveness, and tolerance once the active regimen is confirmed.

Recommend nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease and planning of ongoing renal care.

Recommend monitoring serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone, along with renal ultrasound if clinically indicated, to further characterize complications and guide treatment.

Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications; review all medication dosing for renal adjustment. Maintain appropriate hydration while avoiding excessive sodium intake.

Continue blood pressure monitoring and reinforce a kidney-conscious diet with sodium restriction. Counsel regarding smoking cessation, as ongoing tobacco use increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

Follow up promptly for decreased urine output, new swelling, shortness of breath, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, or worsening generalized symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after medication reconciliation and repeat renal monitoring.
