Chief Complaint

Initial evaluation of longstanding high blood pressure.

Subjective

Charlie Brown is an 83-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient consultation regarding essential hypertension, reported to have been present for approximately 48 years and currently without active management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. He does not smoke.

Relevant medical history includes coronary artery disease for approximately 22 years, chronic kidney disease for approximately 24 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 52 years. Current medication use, allergies, and home blood pressure readings are not provided.

Objective

Height 162 cm; weight 73.5 kg; BMI 28.0 kg/m².

Blood pressure 164/104 mmHg. Pulse 81 bpm. Respiratory rate 18/min. Temperature 37.0°C. Oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 147 mmol/L, potassium 3.7 mmol/L.

He is clinically asymptomatic at today’s visit, with no reported symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive end-organ injury. No additional examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure in an 83-year-old man with a 48-year history of hypertension that has not been actively managed. The current reading of 164/104 mmHg is significantly elevated. He has no reported symptoms of hypertensive emergency, including chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, visual disturbance, or epistaxis.

Management is clinically important given his advanced age and substantial cardiovascular and renal comorbidity, including coronary artery disease, chronic kidney disease, and longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus. Sodium is mildly elevated at 147 mmol/L; potassium is within the provided laboratory range at 3.7 mmol/L. BMI is 28.0 kg/m².

Plan

Confirm the blood pressure with repeat seated measurements using an appropriately sized cuff and initiate a structured home blood pressure log, including time of day and relation to meals or activity.

Given the degree and duration of blood pressure elevation and the presence of coronary artery disease, chronic kidney disease, and diabetes, recommend prompt clinician-directed initiation and titration of antihypertensive therapy, with selection based on renal function, cardiovascular history, orthostatic tolerance, and medication reconciliation. Avoid overly rapid reduction in blood pressure in view of his age and longstanding hypertension.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine protein, as well as urinalysis, lipid profile, and electrocardiogram as part of cardiovascular and hypertension evaluation. Review prior kidney function and cardiovascular records when available before finalizing therapy.

Counsel on sodium restriction, avoidance of excess alcohol, weight management, and regular activity appropriate to functional capacity and cardiovascular status. Reinforce adherence once a treatment regimen is established.

He should seek immediate emergency care for new chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, focal weakness, confusion, acute visual change, or other symptoms concerning for hypertensive emergency.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment adjustment.
