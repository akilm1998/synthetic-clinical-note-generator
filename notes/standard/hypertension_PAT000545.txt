Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of hypertension.

Subjective

Monica Shaw is a 38-year-old woman with a 14-year history of essential hypertension presenting for outpatient medication review. Hypertension is currently not under active treatment management. No antihypertensive medication regimen is identified in the current treatment information, so medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be assessed today.

She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. She continues to smoke. Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 7 years and hyperlipidemia for 2 years.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 151/88 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Height 178 cm, weight 65.0 kg, BMI 20.5 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: Sodium 135 mmol/L, potassium 4.0 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal at today’s reading. She is asymptomatic without features suggesting hypertensive emergency. Lack of an identified active antihypertensive regimen represents an opportunity to address cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and ongoing tobacco use. Electrolytes are acceptable for outpatient management, with sodium at the low end of the reference range and potassium normal.

2. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 7 years. This increases her overall cardiovascular and renal risk and supports timely optimization of blood pressure management.

3. Hyperlipidemia, duration 2 years, contributing to elevated cardiovascular risk.

4. Tobacco use. Continued smoking increases cardiovascular risk and may adversely affect blood pressure control.

Plan

Discussed the importance of consistent blood pressure management and the risks of leaving longstanding hypertension untreated. Recommend confirming the blood pressure pattern with a properly fitted home cuff, recording readings at consistent times, and bringing the log to follow-up. Avoid excess dietary sodium, maintain regular physical activity as tolerated, and limit alcohol intake.

Recommend initiation of an appropriate antihypertensive regimen after review of her full medication and renal history, with selection guided by her diabetes and cardiovascular risk profile. Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance should be reassessed after treatment is established; reinforce daily adherence and review for dizziness, edema, cough, or other adverse effects based on the selected agent.

Recommend routine monitoring of serum electrolytes and renal function after starting or adjusting antihypertensive therapy, as clinically appropriate. Consider ECG, urinalysis, and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk assessment if not recently completed.

Address diabetes and hyperlipidemia as part of comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction. Strongly encouraged smoking cessation and offered cessation counseling and pharmacologic support options.

Seek urgent evaluation for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Follow up in the near term for blood pressure review and treatment planning.
