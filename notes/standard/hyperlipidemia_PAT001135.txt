Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for elevated cholesterol and triglycerides.

History of Present Illness

Autumn West is a 75-year-old woman presenting for evaluation of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 5 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid testing shows total cholesterol 324 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 207 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 47 mg/dL, and triglycerides 352 mg/dL. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma.

Her history is also significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus of approximately 28 years’ duration. Smoking status is negative. No medication regimen, medication allergies, family history, or prior cardiovascular testing was provided for this visit.

Review of Systems

Cardiovascular: Denies chest discomfort and intermittent claudication.

Neurologic: Denies dizziness.

Skin: Denies fatty skin deposits or yellow eyelid deposits.

Other systems reviewed as relevant to today’s lipid consultation.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 133/78 mmHg, pulse 75 beats/min, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 160 cm; weight 65.0 kg; BMI 25.4 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: Total cholesterol 324 mg/dL. LDL cholesterol 207 mg/dL. HDL cholesterol 47 mg/dL. Triglycerides 352 mg/dL.

Physical examination findings relevant to xanthomas, xanthelasma, corneal arcus, tendon xanthomas, and carotid bruits were not provided.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with marked elevation of LDL cholesterol and concurrent hypertriglyceridemia. The LDL of 207 mg/dL and triglycerides of 352 mg/dL represent substantially elevated atherogenic lipid levels. She is currently asymptomatic from a cardiovascular standpoint, without chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness. Her age and long-standing type 2 diabetes mellitus substantially increase her overall atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk. She is a nonsmoker with BMI in the overweight range.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 28 years. Diabetes is an important cardiovascular risk modifier in the setting of severe lipid elevation. Glycemic control data and current diabetes treatment were not available for review today.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the increased cardiovascular risk associated with longstanding diabetes and markedly elevated LDL cholesterol.

Recommend initiation of guideline-directed lipid-lowering therapy after reviewing medication history, contraindications, allergies, prior statin exposure, and patient preferences. A high-intensity statin would generally be appropriate if tolerated, with consideration of additional therapy such as ezetimibe if LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated.

Recommend dietary counseling focused on reducing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess sugars; increasing fiber-rich foods; and emphasizing vegetables, legumes, whole grains, and unsaturated fats. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated and continued avoidance of tobacco.

Obtain a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and review of diabetes control. Given the LDL level, clarify family history of premature cardiovascular disease and markedly elevated cholesterol when available. Additional risk assessment, including lipoprotein(a), apolipoprotein B, or coronary artery calcium imaging, may be considered if it would assist shared decision-making regarding long-term preventive therapy.

Follow up after medication and laboratory review to finalize the lipid-lowering plan and monitor response and tolerability.
