Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Sean Palmer is a 47-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 13 years ago. His CKD has not been under active management. He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. No interval uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported.

Relevant comorbidities include anemia of chronic disease for approximately 4 years and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. He currently smokes. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 143/89 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Height 176 cm, weight 92.3 kg, BMI 29.8 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 2.96 mg/dL, estimated GFR 20 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 63 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal filtration and elevated nitrogenous waste markers. The patient is currently clinically asymptomatic without reported edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, or urinary changes; however, the degree of renal impairment warrants structured renal follow-up and evaluation for complications. Blood pressure is above goal in the setting of CKD and coronary artery disease.

Anemia of chronic disease, present for approximately 4 years; current severity cannot be assessed from the available data.

Coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago.

Overweight by BMI.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the creatinine, eGFR, and BUN results and the need for ongoing kidney-focused care. Recommend nephrology referral for comprehensive CKD management and counseling regarding renal-protective therapy and future planning if kidney function declines.

Recommend repeat renal function assessment with serum electrolytes, creatinine, eGFR, and BUN, along with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to quantify proteinuria. Recommend CBC to reassess anemia and intact parathyroid hormone testing to evaluate for CKD-related mineral and bone disorder. Renal ultrasound is recommended to assess renal structure and exclude obstruction.

Blood pressure should be monitored closely, with treatment selection coordinated with renal and cardiovascular considerations. Avoid nephrotoxic medications, particularly NSAIDs, and review all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplemental agents for renal dosing. Counsel regarding a kidney-conscious, lower-sodium diet and avoidance of excessive protein intake while maintaining appropriate nutrition.

Strongly advised smoking cessation given CKD and coronary artery disease. Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management and coordinate care for coronary artery disease. Seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, edema, markedly reduced urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, or chest pain. Follow up after completion of recommended renal evaluation and laboratory monitoring.
