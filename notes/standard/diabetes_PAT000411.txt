Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Diana Black is a 98-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 67 years ago. Diabetes control is currently suboptimal. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No smoking history is reported.

Her medical history is also notable for chronic kidney disease of 31 years’ duration and hypertension of 29 years’ duration. Current medication use and home glucose monitoring regimen are not provided.

Objective

Height is 157 cm, weight 62.6 kg, and BMI 25.4 kg/m². Blood pressure is 154/89 mmHg, pulse 71 beats/min, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, and oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin A1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 216 mg/dL, consistent with persistent hyperglycemia. No diabetic neuropathic, infectious, constitutional, or visual symptoms are reported today. No additional examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, with inadequate glycemic control based on hemoglobin A1c of 9.2% and fasting glucose of 216 mg/dL. Despite the absence of symptomatic hyperglycemia, these values indicate clinically significant chronic glucose elevation. Given her advanced age and longstanding chronic kidney disease, treatment decisions should prioritize avoidance of hypoglycemia, renal safety, functional status, and individualized glycemic goals.

Hypertension with an office blood pressure of 154/89 mmHg, above usual treatment targets, in the setting of longstanding chronic kidney disease.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding; current renal function is not provided and should be considered when selecting or adjusting diabetes therapy.

Plan

Review the current medication list, adherence, dietary pattern, home glucose measurements, and any history of hypoglycemia with the patient and caregivers, if applicable. Discuss individualized glycemic targets appropriate for her age, comorbidity burden, renal status, and overall treatment goals.

Recommend prompt medication reconciliation and consideration of initiating or intensifying glucose-lowering therapy, with selection and dosing guided by current serum creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate. Avoid overly aggressive glucose lowering and emphasize hypoglycemia prevention. Reinforce a consistent, kidney-conscious meal pattern and adequate hydration, with physical activity limited to what is safe for her mobility and cardiovascular status.

Recommend repeat hemoglobin A1c at an clinically appropriate interval after treatment changes, along with fasting or home glucose monitoring as appropriate. Renal assessment should include serum creatinine, estimated glomerular filtration rate, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio if not recently assessed. A fasting lipid profile is also appropriate for cardiovascular risk assessment.

Reassess blood pressure and review antihypertensive adherence and tolerability. Coordinate diabetes and hypertension management with attention to renal protection and avoidance of orthostatic symptoms.

Recommend routine diabetic foot evaluation, including skin integrity and sensation, and age-appropriate retinal assessment. Counsel regarding prompt evaluation of new visual changes, wounds, infection, excessive thirst or urination, confusion, or symptoms of hypoglycemia.

Follow up after medication and renal-status review to reassess glycemic and blood pressure control.
