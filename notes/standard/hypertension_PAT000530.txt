Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Joseph Bailey is a 71-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension, present for approximately 21 years. He is not currently receiving documented antihypertensive management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. He reports no tobacco use. His medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus of approximately 37 years’ duration.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 155/96 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 162 cm, weight 60.9 kg, BMI 23.2 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 146 mmol/L, potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

Patient is asymptomatic at today’s visit. No additional examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure at 155/96 mmHg in an asymptomatic 71-year-old man with a 21-year history of hypertension and no documented active antihypertensive regimen. The current reading indicates inadequate blood pressure control and warrants prompt outpatient management. Long-standing type 2 diabetes mellitus increases his cardiovascular and renal risk.

Mildly elevated serum sodium at 146 mmol/L. Potassium is within an acceptable range for consideration of standard antihypertensive therapies, with ongoing monitoring required.

Plan

Discussed the importance of initiating an active blood pressure management plan rather than continued observation. Recommend confirmation of blood pressure pattern with properly performed home readings, including a written log of morning and evening measurements, and review of technique.

Recommend dietary sodium reduction, avoidance of excess alcohol, regular physical activity as tolerated, and continued maintenance of healthy body weight. He should continue avoiding tobacco.

Recommend evaluation for hypertension-related cardiovascular and renal risk, including an electrocardiogram, lipid profile, and urinalysis. Serum electrolytes should be rechecked after initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy, with particular attention to potassium and sodium.

Given the degree and duration of blood pressure elevation and coexisting long-standing diabetes, recommend discussing initiation of guideline-directed antihypertensive therapy at this visit, with selection based on the broader renal and cardiovascular assessment and subsequent titration to response.

Seek urgent medical attention for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, or acute visual changes. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended after home blood pressure measurements and initial management are completed.
