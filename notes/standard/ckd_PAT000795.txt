Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Kennedy Walters is a 43-year-old woman, 154 cm tall and weighing 61.0 kg, with a 13-year history of chronic kidney disease and anemia of chronic disease for approximately 3 years. She presents for routine outpatient follow-up and reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. She does not smoke.

There is no active renal-directed management documented at this visit. She is not reporting symptoms suggestive of acute fluid overload or a symptomatic uremic syndrome.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 137/92 mmHg, pulse 80/min, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

BMI: 25.7 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Serum creatinine 3.24 mg/dL, BUN 85 mg/dL, estimated GFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m².

No additional examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced kidney function. Current creatinine, BUN, and estimated GFR indicate advanced chronic renal impairment in the setting of a 13-year disease history. Although she is currently asymptomatic and has no reported edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, or urinary change, the degree of renal dysfunction warrants prompt reassessment and re-establishment of specialty care. Blood pressure is mildly elevated, particularly the diastolic pressure.

Anemia of chronic disease, present for approximately 3 years. No current hemoglobin or hematocrit is available for assessment today.

Overweight by BMI, 25.7 kg/m².

Plan

Arrange expedited nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease, including discussion of renal replacement planning if clinically indicated.

Repeat renal function testing and obtain a serum electrolyte panel to assess current metabolic status. Obtain urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio for renal risk stratification, complete blood count to reassess anemia, and intact parathyroid hormone as part of chronic kidney disease mineral-bone evaluation. Renal ultrasound is recommended to assess renal structure and exclude an obstructive or structural contributor.

Monitor blood pressure closely and review renal-protective blood pressure management at follow-up. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents; medication reconciliation is needed before initiating or adjusting therapy.

Continue surveillance for fluid overload and uremic symptoms. She was advised to seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, progressive edema, persistent vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, or a significant decrease in urine output.

Follow up promptly after repeat testing and nephrology assessment.
