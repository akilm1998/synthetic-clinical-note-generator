Chief Complaint

Annual review with focus on lipid management and chronic disease risk reduction.

Subjective

Frank Montgomery is a 42-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 3-year history of hyperlipidemia that is not currently being actively managed. He denies chest discomfort, exertional calf pain or other intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. He does not smoke.

He has known type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 10 years and obesity for approximately 5 years. Current weight is 116.6 kg at a height of 174 cm. No lipid-lowering or other chronic medications are provided in the current record.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 122/82 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

BMI is 38.5 kg/m².

Available lipid profile: total cholesterol 309 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 206 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 35 mg/dL, triglycerides 338 mg/dL.

No reported cutaneous lipid deposits or cardiovascular prodromal symptoms.

Assessment

Marked hyperlipidemia with LDL cholesterol 206 mg/dL, elevated triglycerides, low HDL cholesterol, and total cholesterol 309 mg/dL. The pattern represents significant mixed dyslipidemia and confers increased long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus and obesity. There are no current symptoms suggesting angina, peripheral arterial disease, or other symptomatic vascular disease.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 10 years’ duration. Current glycemic control cannot be assessed from the information available today.

Obesity with BMI 38.5 kg/m², present for approximately 5 years.

Blood pressure is currently within an acceptable range. He is a nonsmoker.

Plan

Discussed the importance of initiating active lipid management. Given the LDL cholesterol above 190 mg/dL and coexisting diabetes, recommend prompt initiation of high-intensity statin therapy unless contraindicated, with medication selection and dosing reviewed after confirming current medication history, allergy history, and patient preference. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe and specialist lipid management should be considered.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, legumes, whole grains, fish, and unsaturated fats, with reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, sugary beverages, and excess alcohol. Encourage gradual progression toward at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity physical activity weekly as tolerated, together with resistance exercise and a structured weight-reduction strategy.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation and adherence assessment. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and measurement of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a) to refine risk assessment. Coronary artery calcium imaging may be considered if treatment decisions remain uncertain, although the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and diabetes already support pharmacologic risk reduction.

Continue comprehensive diabetes care, including review of glycemic monitoring and diabetes-related renal, retinal, foot, and cardiovascular risk surveillance at the appropriate preventive visits. Reinforce routine immunizations, age-appropriate cancer screening, dental care, sleep and activity counseling, and avoidance of tobacco exposure.

Advised to seek urgent evaluation for new or persistent chest discomfort, shortness of breath, syncope, or exertional leg pain. Follow up after medication and laboratory review to assess adherence, tolerance, lipid response, and overall cardiometabolic risk reduction.
