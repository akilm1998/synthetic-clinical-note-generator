Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Christian Cook is a 33-year-old man presenting for urgent follow-up of hypertension present for approximately 8 years and currently without active management. Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 1 year, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 3 years, and chronic kidney disease for 1 year. He does not smoke. No current antihypertensive medications were provided.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 153/96 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.
Height 179 cm, weight 78.2 kg, BMI 24.4 kg/m².
Serum electrolytes: sodium 137 mmol/L, potassium 4.5 mmol/L.
Patient is asymptomatic from a hypertensive standpoint at today’s visit. No additional examination findings provided.

Assessment
Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently untreated, with blood pressure elevated at 153/96 mmHg. No symptoms suggesting an acute hypertensive emergency today. His coronary artery disease, type 2 diabetes, and chronic kidney disease increase the importance of prompt blood pressure control and careful medication selection. Electrolytes are currently within expected ranges.

Plan
Discussed the need to initiate and maintain a structured hypertension management plan rather than continued observation. Confirm blood pressure with repeat measurements using appropriate technique and obtain home readings if available. Review and start an antihypertensive regimen promptly, accounting for his coronary artery disease, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease; medication selection should be guided by current renal function and the broader clinical record.

Recommend dietary sodium restriction, regular physical activity as tolerated, moderation of alcohol if applicable, and continuation of smoking avoidance. Recommend assessment of renal function, urinalysis, lipid profile, and ECG as clinically appropriate for cardiovascular risk and end-organ assessment. Continue coordinated management of diabetes, coronary artery disease, and chronic kidney disease.

Seek emergency care immediately for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, visual changes, or recurrent markedly elevated blood pressure. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment adjustment.
