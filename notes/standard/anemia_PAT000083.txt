Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for anemia.

Subjective

Connor Walters is a 51-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of anemia previously identified approximately 4 years ago. He reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or visible pallor. The anemia has not been actively managed to date.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 20 years and a chronic infection for approximately 9 years. He currently smokes. No medication use, allergies, or other treatment history are provided.

Objective

Height 172 cm, weight 75.4 kg, BMI 25.5 kg/m². Blood pressure 124/81 mmHg, pulse 87 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results: hemoglobin 9.0 g/dL, hematocrit 27.2%, RBC count 3.36 million/µL, MCV 81 fL, serum iron 36 µg/dL, ferritin 465 ng/mL, TIBC 246 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 14.6%.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with chronic inflammation. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are reduced, with a low RBC count and low-normal MCV. The low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC are most consistent with iron-restricted erythropoiesis in the setting of chronic disease/inflammation rather than uncomplicated iron deficiency. His longstanding chronic infection is a likely contributing factor. His 20-year history of type 2 diabetes also warrants review for associated chronic complications, including possible renal contribution, although renal function results are not available today.

He is currently clinically stable without reported cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms. No evidence of acute symptomatic decompensation based on the history and vital signs provided.

Tobacco use. Current smoking may adversely affect overall health and chronic disease control.

Plan

Review the anemia with the patient and discuss that the current pattern is compatible with anemia associated with chronic inflammation. Recommend evaluation of the underlying chronic infection and other sources of ongoing inflammation, with coordination of care as clinically appropriate.

Recommend repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers. Additional iron assessment should be interpreted in the context of the elevated ferritin and chronic inflammatory state. Consider evaluation for occult blood loss if clinically indicated by history or subsequent findings.

Do not begin empiric iron therapy solely on the current ferritin level; determine whether iron supplementation is appropriate after review of the complete clinical picture and repeat testing. If anemia persists or worsens despite management of the underlying condition, consider hematology consultation and discussion of iron replacement or other disease-directed therapy.

Continue routine management of type 2 diabetes and address smoking cessation at follow-up. Advise prompt evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, marked weakness, or bleeding. Arrange outpatient reassessment after the recommended evaluation to review trends and update management.
