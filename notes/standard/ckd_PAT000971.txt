Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Anna Cook is a 57-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 21 years. She reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, lower-extremity swelling, change in urination, or shortness of breath. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of acute fluid overload or symptomatic uremia.

Her chronic kidney disease has not been actively managed recently. Relevant comorbidities include hypertension for approximately 14 years and secondary hyperparathyroidism for approximately 11 years. She currently smokes. No medication regimen is documented for review today.

Objective

Height 160 cm. Weight 63.5 kg. BMI 24.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 156/99 mmHg, pulse 77/min, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Recent laboratory results show serum creatinine 3.11 mg/dL, estimated GFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 65 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function based on creatinine 3.11 mg/dL, estimated GFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 65 mg/dL. The condition has been present for 21 years and is currently without reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms. Despite the absence of symptoms, the degree of renal impairment and longstanding lack of active management require prompt reassessment and coordinated care.

Hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal at 156/99 mmHg. Poor blood pressure control may contribute to further renal decline and requires active treatment planning.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, present for approximately 11 years, in the setting of advanced chronic kidney disease.

Tobacco use. Continued smoking increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive chronic kidney disease management, assessment of complications, renal replacement therapy education, and discussion of future planning if renal function remains at this level. Obtain updated renal monitoring, including serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone, as clinically appropriate. Renal ultrasound may be considered to assess chronic renal structure if not previously evaluated.

Address hypertension promptly with confirmation of blood pressure readings and initiation or adjustment of an appropriate antihypertensive regimen after medication reconciliation and review of renal function and electrolytes. Emphasize sodium restriction, avoidance of NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents, and avoidance of over-the-counter supplements unless reviewed medically.

Continue clinical monitoring for nausea, pruritus, progressive fatigue, muscle cramps, changes in urination, edema, or shortness of breath. Seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening dyspnea, marked swelling, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or significant reduction in urine output.

Discuss smoking cessation and offer evidence-based cessation support. Follow up closely after laboratory reassessment and nephrology review.
