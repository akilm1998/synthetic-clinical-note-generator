Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of anemia.

Subjective

Blake Hunter is an 87-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 4 years. He reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor is noted. No interval concerns are reported in the available history.

He has a 16-year history of rheumatoid arthritis, a chronic inflammatory condition relevant to the etiology of his anemia. He continues to smoke. Current weight is 77.9 kg and height is 184 cm, with BMI 23.0 kg/m². No anemia-directed medications are documented.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 138/82 mmHg, pulse 85 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

General: Pallor present. No reported constitutional or cardiopulmonary symptoms.

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin 9.6 g/dL, hematocrit 29.0%, RBC count 3.26 million/µL, MCV 89 fL. Serum iron 46 µg/dL, ferritin 338 ng/mL, TIBC 235 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 19.6%.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of longstanding rheumatoid arthritis. The anemia is normocytic, with reduced serum iron and TIBC and an elevated ferritin, a pattern more supportive of inflammatory iron sequestration than isolated absolute iron deficiency. Hemoglobin remains reduced at 9.6 g/dL, although he is currently clinically stable without fatigue, dyspnea, dizziness, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor is present.

Rheumatoid arthritis, longstanding, with 16 years’ duration and likely contributing chronic inflammatory burden.

Plan

Obtain interval complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to reassess severity and marrow response. Recommend evaluation of vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers to assess for additional contributors to the anemia. Continue clinical monitoring for worsening fatigue, weakness, dyspnea, dizziness, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or other evidence of symptomatic anemia.

Review rheumatoid arthritis disease activity and coordinate management of the underlying inflammatory condition, as improved inflammatory control may improve the anemia. Iron supplementation should not be initiated empirically without further assessment, given the elevated ferritin and inflammatory iron profile; consider iron replacement strategy, including intravenous iron or other anemia-directed therapy, based on repeat studies and overall clinical context. Consider hematology evaluation if anemia worsens, becomes symptomatic, or remains unexplained despite the recommended evaluation.

Smoking cessation should be encouraged as part of overall health maintenance. Follow up after repeat laboratory assessment or sooner for new or worsening cardiopulmonary symptoms.
