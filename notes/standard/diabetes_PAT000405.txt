Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Blake Hunter is an 80-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 33 years ago. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet. No acute concerns are reported today.

Diabetes control is currently poor, with recent hemoglobin A1c of 9.7% and fasting glucose of 260 mg/dL. Current diabetes medications and adherence were not provided and should be reconciled. He does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include obesity, dyslipidemia of approximately 27 years’ duration, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of approximately 16 years’ duration.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 134/82 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 187 cm, weight 114.7 kg, BMI 32.8 kg/m².

Patient is clinically stable by vital signs. No diabetes-related symptoms are reported. Recent laboratory results: hemoglobin A1c 9.7%; fasting glucose 260 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently substantially above glycemic goal based on hemoglobin A1c 9.7% and fasting glucose 260 mg/dL. Despite the absence of hyperglycemic, neuropathic, infectious, or constitutional symptoms, laboratory control is inadequate and requires active management.

Obesity, BMI 32.8 kg/m².

Longstanding dyslipidemia.

Longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Plan

Perform medication reconciliation, including confirmation of any current diabetes therapy, dosing, adherence, tolerance, and access. Discuss the need for prompt treatment intensification in view of the markedly elevated A1c and fasting glucose. Selection of therapy should account for age, renal function, hepatic history, hypoglycemia risk, comorbidities, and patient preferences; avoid initiating or adjusting medication without reviewing the current regimen and relevant laboratory data.

Reinforce consistent carbohydrate-controlled nutrition, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, and gradual activity appropriate to functional status and safety. Encourage weight reduction as tolerated.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, along with a fasting lipid profile, to support diabetes risk stratification and medication planning. Continue monitoring for symptoms of hyperglycemia, hypoglycemia, neuropathy, visual change, skin breakdown, or infection.

Review preventive diabetes care at follow-up, including foot examination and retinal evaluation recommendations. Reassess glycemic control after treatment changes, with repeat hemoglobin A1c at an appropriate interval. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended given the current degree of hyperglycemia.
