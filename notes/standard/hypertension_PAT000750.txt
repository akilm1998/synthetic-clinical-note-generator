Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated blood pressure.

Subjective
Valeria Ellis is an 86-year-old woman with a 48-year history of hypertension, currently without active blood pressure management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Known comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 26 years, chronic kidney disease for 23 years, and hyperlipidemia for 27 years. She does not smoke. Height 155 cm, weight 58.4 kg, BMI 24.3 kg/m².

Objective
Blood pressure 172/100 mmHg
Pulse 81 bpm
Respiratory rate 18/min
Temperature 36.7°C
Oxygen saturation 97%

Serum electrolytes: sodium 143 mmol/L, potassium 4.7 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported.

Assessment
Markedly elevated blood pressure in the setting of longstanding essential hypertension without current management. She is presently asymptomatic, with no reported features suggestive of acute end-organ injury; however, her advanced age, coronary artery disease, and chronic kidney disease increase cardiovascular and renal risk.

Coronary artery disease.
Chronic kidney disease.
Hyperlipidemia.

Plan
Repeat blood pressure after a period of seated rest and confirm with appropriate cuff and technique. Given the degree of elevation and significant comorbid coronary and renal disease, arrange prompt same-day clinician evaluation to initiate or reinstitute a carefully titrated antihypertensive regimen and review all current medications and contraindications. Avoid rapid, unsupervised blood pressure reduction.

Recommend assessment of renal function and repeat serum electrolytes as clinically appropriate before and after antihypertensive therapy. Consider ECG, urinalysis, and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular and hypertensive risk evaluation.

Proceed to the emergency department immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic deficit, confusion, severe headache, visual changes, or persistent substantially elevated readings. Close outpatient follow-up is needed after treatment initiation or adjustment.
