Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Christina Pierce is a 34-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 11 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. Coronary artery disease is documented with a duration of approximately 1 year. She does not smoke. Current weight is 77.5 kg at 179 cm, with BMI 24.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 125/78 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 363 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 277 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 38 mg/dL, triglycerides 239 mg/dL.

Assessment

Severe hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia. The LDL level of 277 mg/dL is substantially above the threshold warranting pharmacologic therapy, particularly in the setting of documented coronary artery disease. HDL is below the optimal range. She is currently asymptomatic from a cardiovascular standpoint, with normal blood pressure, BMI in the normal range, and no tobacco use. Long duration of untreated disease raises concern for a significant primary lipid disorder; secondary contributors should also be evaluated.

Plan

Discussed the substantially increased atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk associated with the current lipid profile and the importance of sustained LDL reduction. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, unless contraindicated, with treatment selection after discussion of risks, benefits, reproductive considerations, and patient preference.

Recommend a fasting lipid panel after initiation or adjustment of therapy to assess response and adherence. If LDL reduction is inadequate, recommend intensification of therapy and consideration of adjunctive ezetimibe; referral for advanced lipid management and consideration of PCSK9-directed therapy may be appropriate given the markedly elevated LDL and documented coronary artery disease.

Recommend evaluation for secondary causes of dyslipidemia and consideration of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for risk characterization. Reinforced a heart-healthy dietary pattern emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, increased soluble fiber, and avoidance of excess refined carbohydrates; continue regular aerobic and resistance exercise. Continue tobacco avoidance.

Follow up after treatment initiation and repeat lipid assessment to review response, tolerability, and need for additional LDL-lowering therapy.
