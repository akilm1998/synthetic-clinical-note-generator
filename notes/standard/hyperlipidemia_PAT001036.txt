Chief Complaint

Annual review of longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Scarlett Hughes is a 75-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 23-year history of hyperlipidemia that is not currently under active management. Recent lipid results show markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with hypertriglyceridemia. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain consistent with claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

Comorbidity includes type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 41 years. No current lipid-lowering or other medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Height 167 cm; weight 67.8 kg; BMI 24.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 129/78 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 346 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 226 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 44 mg/dL, triglycerides 379 mg/dL.

Assessment

Longstanding severe mixed-pattern dyslipidemia, currently untreated or without documented active management. LDL cholesterol is markedly elevated at 226 mg/dL, with concomitant hypertriglyceridemia. In the setting of advanced age and long-standing type 2 diabetes, this represents a substantially increased atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk despite the absence of current anginal or claudication symptoms. The degree and duration of LDL elevation also warrant consideration of a primary lipid disorder after assessment for secondary contributors.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus of 41 years’ duration; glycemic control cannot be assessed from the information available today.

Preventive health review completed in the context of annual care.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active lipid management and cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, barring contraindications or intolerance, with medication reconciliation and review of prior lipid-lowering treatment history before prescribing. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, consider addition of nonstatin therapy such as ezetimibe and/or referral for further lipid management.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, with consideration of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a) measurement. Evaluate for potentially reversible secondary contributors to dyslipidemia as clinically appropriate. Reinforce a Mediterranean-style eating pattern, reduction of saturated and trans fats and refined carbohydrates, regular physical activity appropriate for functional status, and maintenance of current healthy BMI. Continue tobacco avoidance.

Review diabetes care at the annual visit, including ongoing glycemic monitoring and routine screening for diabetes-related vascular, renal, retinal, and neurologic complications as appropriate. Continue blood pressure surveillance.

Recommend review and updating of age-appropriate immunizations, cancer screening, osteoporosis prevention, fall-risk assessment, and advance care planning according to preventive care history and patient preferences.

Arrange follow-up after lipid reassessment and sooner for new chest discomfort, exertional leg symptoms, or other concerning cardiovascular symptoms.
