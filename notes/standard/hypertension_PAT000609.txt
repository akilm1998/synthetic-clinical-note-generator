Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective
Michael Brooks is a 50-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. Blood pressure control has not been actively managed. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. He is a nonsmoker.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 20 years, hyperlipidemia for 16 years, and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. No current medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective
Vital signs: Blood pressure 150/92 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 163 cm; weight 75.7 kg; BMI 28.5 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 136 mmol/L, potassium 4.0 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported. No additional examination findings documented.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and limited active management. The patient is currently asymptomatic, without reported features suggesting hypertensive emergency. His cardiovascular risk is increased by longstanding type 2 diabetes, hyperlipidemia, established coronary artery disease, and overweight BMI.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago.

Plan
Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given the current reading and coexisting diabetes and coronary artery disease. Recommend confirming the blood pressure pattern with home readings, including measurements taken at consistent times with appropriate technique, and bringing the log to follow-up.

Recommend initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy after medication reconciliation and review of renal function and urine albumin status. Potential therapy should be selected with consideration of his diabetes and coronary artery disease. Review adherence, access to medications, and tolerability before establishing a regimen.

Counseled on reducing dietary sodium, limiting alcohol if applicable, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, pursuing gradual weight reduction, and following a heart-healthy dietary pattern.

Recommend review of overall cardiovascular risk management, including current treatment and control of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and coronary artery disease. Obtain or review an ECG, lipid profile, urinalysis, and renal function testing as clinically appropriate; no additional results are available today.

Advised prompt evaluation for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic changes, or markedly elevated home blood pressure readings. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to reassess blood pressure and finalize the treatment plan.
