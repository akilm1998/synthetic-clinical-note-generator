Chief Complaint

Medication review and blood pressure follow-up.

Subjective

Maya Russell is a 49-year-old woman presenting for outpatient review of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 12 years. Blood pressure management has not been established, and she is not currently on a documented antihypertensive regimen. There is therefore no current medication effectiveness or tolerance to assess. Adherence counseling reviewed; no active regimen is available for her to follow.

She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. She does not smoke. History is also notable for chronic kidney disease for approximately 7 years.

Objective

Height 156 cm. Weight 70.1 kg. BMI 28.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 161/100 mmHg. Pulse 71 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 99%.

Serum sodium 141 mmol/L. Serum potassium 3.9 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, inadequately controlled and currently untreated. Blood pressure is significantly elevated today without symptoms suggestive of acute end-organ injury. Chronic kidney disease increases the importance of consistent blood pressure control. Mild excess weight may also contribute.

Chronic kidney disease, approximately 7-year duration.

Plan

Discussed the importance of initiating a consistent antihypertensive treatment plan and reviewed that lifestyle measures alone are unlikely to be sufficient at the current blood pressure. Selection of therapy should account for chronic kidney disease and should be guided by renal function and urine protein assessment. Review renal function and urinary protein testing before or at initiation of therapy, with repeat electrolytes and renal function after treatment changes.

Counseled on reducing dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, gradual weight reduction, and limiting alcohol if applicable. Advised home blood pressure monitoring with a written log for review.

Reviewed medication adherence strategies, including taking medication at the same time daily and not stopping therapy without discussing it with the office. No medication adverse effects are present to address because she is not currently taking a documented antihypertensive.

Follow up promptly for blood pressure reassessment and treatment initiation or adjustment. Seek emergency care for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, significant vision changes, or recurrent nosebleed with markedly elevated blood pressure.
