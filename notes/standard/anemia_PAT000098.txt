Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Patricia Greene is a 94-year-old woman with approximately 20 years of anemia in the setting of chronic kidney disease for 43 years, heart failure for 24 years, and rheumatoid arthritis for 4 years. She is not currently receiving documented anemia-directed management. She denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, headache, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor has been noted. No medication regimen is provided for effectiveness, adherence, or tolerance review today.

She continues to smoke. Height is 177 cm and weight is 84.3 kg, with BMI 26.9 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 137/82 mmHg, pulse 84 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

General: Pallor noted. No reported constitutional or cardiopulmonary symptoms.

Laboratory data: Hemoglobin 9.1 g/dL, hematocrit 27.6%, RBC 3.1 million/µL, MCV 89 fL. Serum iron 43 µg/dL, ferritin 435 ng/mL, TIBC 206 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 20.9%.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation. The low hemoglobin and hematocrit with normal MCV, low serum iron and TIBC, and elevated ferritin support an inflammatory/functional iron-restricted pattern rather than straightforward iron depletion. Her longstanding chronic kidney disease, rheumatoid arthritis, and heart failure are likely contributing factors. She is currently clinically stable without reported fatigue, dyspnea, dizziness, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance, although pallor persists.

No anemia-directed medication is documented, so treatment effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance cannot be assessed at this encounter. There is no indication of acute symptomatic decompensation based on the information available.

Plan

Complete medication reconciliation at the next visit, including confirmation of any iron supplementation, erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy, or other treatments, with specific review of adherence, perceived benefit, and adverse effects.

Recommend repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to assess persistence and marrow response. Recommend renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate to further characterize the chronic normocytic anemia and exclude reversible contributors.

Given the elevated ferritin and chronic inflammatory comorbidities, avoid assuming benefit from empiric oral iron alone. Consider iron replacement strategy only after review of the complete clinical picture and iron indices. If anemia remains persistent in the setting of chronic kidney disease, discuss renal-directed anemia management, including whether an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent or intravenous iron is appropriate, with attention to heart failure and overall goals of care.

Continue monitoring for worsening fatigue, weakness, dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, or declining exercise tolerance. Smoking cessation should be encouraged, particularly given her heart failure and chronic kidney disease. Follow up after medication reconciliation and recommended laboratory reassessment, or sooner for new or worsening symptoms.
