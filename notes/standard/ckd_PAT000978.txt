Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly reduced kidney function.

Subjective

Simon Bishop is a 98-year-old man with approximately 60 years of chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 9 years, and anemia of chronic disease for 43 years. Chronic kidney disease is currently not under active management. He reports fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. He continues to smoke.

Objective

Height 163 cm; weight 57.9 kg; BMI 21.8 kg/m². Blood pressure 146/86 mmHg, pulse 84/min, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Serum creatinine 5.78 mg/dL, BUN 61 mg/dL, estimated GFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with severely reduced renal function and significant azotemia. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction and known anemia of chronic disease. No reported symptoms of overt volume overload or uremic gastrointestinal or neurologic manifestations at this visit. Diabetes and suboptimally controlled blood pressure are relevant comorbid risk factors. Given his age, prolonged disease duration, and lack of active management, prompt specialist assessment is warranted.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management and discussion of renal replacement therapy suitability and goals of care.

Obtain recommended CKD evaluation, including serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound, if consistent with the patient’s goals of care.

Review blood pressure and diabetes management with attention to renal safety; avoid nephrotoxic medications and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. No medication regimen is documented today, so treatment should be reconciled before initiation or adjustment.

Counsel regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of dehydration. Seek urgent emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, edema, markedly decreased urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.
