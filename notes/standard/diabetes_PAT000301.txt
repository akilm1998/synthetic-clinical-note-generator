Chief Complaint

Annual review of type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic medical conditions.

Subjective

Christian Cook is an 83-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 24-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. Current laboratory results show HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 206 mg/dL, indicating suboptimal glycemic control. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections.

He reports numbness and tingling in the hands and feet. No additional details regarding progression or functional limitation are provided. He does not smoke. His other chronic conditions include non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 41 years and depression for approximately 25 years. Current diabetes medications and adherence were not provided for review.

Objective

Blood pressure 134/81 mmHg. Pulse 75 bpm. Respiratory rate 18/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 95%. Height 175 cm. Weight 73.2 kg. BMI 23.9 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.3%. Fasting glucose 206 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complication, with hyperglycemia and inadequate current control. HbA1c is elevated at 9.3% after 24 years of disease. He is not reporting classic osmotic or constitutional symptoms, but his distal numbness and tingling raise concern for possible diabetes-associated peripheral sensory symptoms and warrant further assessment.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding, approximately 41 years. No current liver laboratory results or treatment details provided.

Depression, longstanding, approximately 25 years. Current symptom control and treatment regimen require review.

Preventive health and geriatric care review. He is an 83-year-old nonsmoker with normal BMI and stable vital signs today.

Plan

Review current diabetes treatment, home glucose readings if available, adherence, nutrition, and activity level. Given the HbA1c of 9.3%, discuss initiation or intensification of glucose-lowering therapy with consideration of renal function, hepatic history, hypoglycemia risk, comorbidities, and overall treatment goals appropriate for his age. Reinforce regular meals, reduction of concentrated carbohydrates, hydration, and safe physical activity as tolerated.

Obtain or review a fasting lipid profile, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and repeat HbA1c at an appropriate interval to assess response. Consider additional diabetes evaluation, including formal foot examination and assessment of distal sensation and pulses. Recommend routine dilated retinal examination and ongoing foot care, including daily inspection and prompt attention to skin breaks or nonhealing areas.

Discuss symptoms of hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia and when to seek urgent care. Encourage continued smoking avoidance.

Review the status and current management of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, including medication and alcohol history when available, and coordinate monitoring with the diabetes plan.

Assess current depressive symptoms, functional status, sleep, appetite, safety, and treatment response. Continue age-appropriate preventive care, including immunization review, fall-risk assessment, cognitive and functional screening, and advance care planning as appropriate. Follow up after diabetes treatment and laboratory review, with earlier evaluation for worsening numbness, new foot wounds, visual changes, marked thirst or urination, or other concerning symptoms.
