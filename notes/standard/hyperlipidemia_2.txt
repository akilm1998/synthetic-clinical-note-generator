Chief Complaint

Annual review and chronic disease follow-up.

Subjective

Dylan Rivera is an 86-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 52-year history of hyperlipidemia and a 30-year history of coronary artery disease. He denies current chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. He reports no tobacco use.

He also has obesity of approximately 14 years’ duration. Current weight is 102.0 kg at a height of 182 cm, with BMI 30.8 kg/m². No additional medication or treatment information is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 136/78 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 182 cm; weight 102.0 kg; BMI 30.8 kg/m².

Lipid results: total cholesterol 181 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 106 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 48 mg/dL, triglycerides 133 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding. Current total cholesterol and triglycerides are controlled to a reasonable degree, with LDL cholesterol 106 mg/dL and HDL cholesterol 48 mg/dL. Given his established coronary artery disease, his cardiovascular risk remains high and further LDL reduction may be clinically appropriate depending on his current treatment, tolerance, goals of care, and overall health priorities. No reported symptoms suggestive of active ischemia or peripheral vascular disease today.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, without reported chest discomfort, dizziness, or intermittent claudication at this visit.

Obesity, BMI 30.8 kg/m², longstanding.

Preventive health maintenance reviewed in the context of advanced age and chronic cardiovascular disease.

Plan

Review and reconcile all current medications, including any lipid-lowering therapy, adherence, adverse effects, and prior intolerance. If not already receiving appropriate therapy, discuss evidence-based LDL reduction for secondary cardiovascular prevention, with treatment intensity individualized to functional status, comorbidities, life expectancy, and patient preferences. Consider repeat lipid monitoring after any treatment adjustment.

Encourage a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, fish, and unsaturated fats while limiting saturated and trans fats, excess sodium, and highly processed foods. Encourage regular physical activity appropriate to his abilities, with attention to balance and fall prevention, and gradual weight reduction if feasible.

Continue monitoring for new chest discomfort, exertional dyspnea, dizziness, syncope, or leg pain with walking. Seek urgent evaluation for persistent or severe chest pain, shortness of breath, or other acute cardiovascular symptoms.

Continue routine cardiovascular risk-factor follow-up. Review age-appropriate immunizations, fall-risk and functional assessment, medication safety, advance care planning, and preventive screening decisions based on health status and personal preferences. Follow up routinely for annual review or sooner for new symptoms or concerns.
