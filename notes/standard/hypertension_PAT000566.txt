Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic hypertension.

Subjective

Naomi Myers is a 44-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 15-year history of essential hypertension that has not been actively managed. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis.

Medical history is significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 4 years, chronic kidney disease for 3 years, and coronary artery disease for 1 year. Current medication regimen and allergies were not provided. She is a current smoker. BMI is 25.5 kg/m². No acute cardiovascular or neurologic symptoms reported today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 160/100 mmHg; pulse 87 bpm; respiratory rate 16/min; temperature 36.9°C; oxygen saturation 98% on room air. Height 155 cm; weight 61.3 kg; BMI 25.5 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

No abnormal physical examination findings are provided. Patient is currently asymptomatic despite persistently elevated blood pressure.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled, with today’s reading of 160/100 mmHg. No symptoms or findings reported to suggest acute hypertensive emergency. Her diabetes, chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease, and active tobacco use increase overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 4-year history.

Chronic kidney disease, 3-year history.

Coronary artery disease, 1-year history.

Current tobacco use.

Overweight by BMI, 25.5 kg/m².

Plan

Repeat blood pressure after a period of seated rest and confirm with home blood pressure monitoring using an appropriately sized cuff. Arrange close outpatient follow-up rather than deferring management, given the degree and duration of elevation and the presence of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease.

Discuss initiation or re-establishment of an antihypertensive treatment plan, selected with consideration of renal disease, diabetes, coronary artery disease, potassium level, and renal function. Review adherence, access to care, and prior medication tolerance at the next contact. Recheck electrolytes and obtain renal function testing after treatment is initiated or adjusted.

Recommend a low-sodium, heart-healthy eating pattern, avoidance of excess alcohol, regular moderate physical activity as tolerated, weight maintenance, and smoking cessation. Offer evidence-based tobacco cessation counseling and pharmacologic support if appropriate.

Recommend assessment of diabetes control and cardiovascular risk, including hemoglobin A1c, fasting lipid profile, urinalysis with urine albumin assessment, and ECG as clinically appropriate. Continue coordination of chronic kidney disease, diabetes, and coronary artery disease care.

Review age-appropriate preventive care, including immunizations and recommended breast, cervical, and colorectal cancer screening based on prior results and screening history. Counsel regarding prompt evaluation for new chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, severe headache, acute visual change, or markedly higher blood pressure readings.

Follow up promptly for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning, with ongoing monitoring at short intervals until controlled.
