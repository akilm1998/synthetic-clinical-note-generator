Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up of abnormal kidney function.

Subjective

Preston Oliver is a 60-year-old male with an approximately 8-year history of chronic kidney disease, currently without active disease-directed management. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. No smoking history. Relevant comorbidities include hypertension for 9 years and hyperlipidemia for 20 years.

Objective

Height 162 cm, weight 76.9 kg, BMI 29.3 kg/m². Blood pressure 154/93 mmHg, pulse 76/min, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Serum creatinine 3.57 mg/dL. BUN 66 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function and azotemia. Although currently asymptomatic, the creatinine and BUN are markedly elevated, with eGFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m². Etiology is not established in the available information; longstanding hypertension is a significant risk factor. Blood pressure is above goal today. Hyperlipidemia and overweight status are additional cardiovascular and renal risk factors.

Plan

Prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation and management of advanced chronic kidney disease, including discussion of renal-protective therapy and future renal replacement planning as clinically indicated.

Obtain repeat kidney function testing with serum electrolytes, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Recommend renal ultrasound to assess renal structure and exclude obstructive pathology.

Address hypertension promptly with review and initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy by the treating clinician; medication reconciliation is needed before selecting therapy. Counsel regarding avoidance of NSAIDs and other potential nephrotoxins, blood pressure monitoring, sodium moderation, and prompt reporting of reduced urine output, vomiting, new edema, confusion, chest pain, or shortness of breath.

Continue management of cardiovascular risk, including hyperlipidemia, in coordination with primary care. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended after repeat testing and nephrology evaluation.
