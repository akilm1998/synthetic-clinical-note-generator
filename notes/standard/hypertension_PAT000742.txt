Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Hunter Ford is a 73-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of essential hypertension, present for approximately 23 years and currently not actively managed. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and nosebleeds. No interval hypertensive symptoms reported.

Relevant history includes coronary artery disease for 5 years, chronic kidney disease for 31 years, and hyperlipidemia for 33 years. He does not smoke. Current weight is 64.6 kg at 175 cm, with BMI 21.1 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure 157/92 mmHg. Pulse 75 bpm. Respiratory rate 15/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 141 mmol/L, potassium 5.0 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent above-goal office blood pressure, currently without reported symptoms of acute end-organ injury. Longstanding hypertension in the setting of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

Potassium is 5.0 mmol/L, which is relevant when selecting or adjusting antihypertensive therapy, particularly agents that may further increase potassium.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease.

Hyperlipidemia.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given the persistent elevation and history of coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease. Recommend confirming the current blood pressure pattern with home readings using a validated upper-arm cuff, with documentation of readings and timing.

Recommend follow-up with the treating clinician to initiate or adjust antihypertensive therapy, with medication selection individualized to his coronary and renal comorbidities and current potassium level. Avoid empiric escalation of agents that may worsen hyperkalemia without repeat laboratory monitoring.

Recommend repeat serum electrolytes and renal function after treatment is initiated or adjusted, with attention to potassium. Continue monitoring for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or vision changes; seek urgent evaluation if these occur.

Reinforced a low-sodium eating pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and continued smoking avoidance. Continue routine follow-up for hypertension, chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease, and hyperlipidemia.
