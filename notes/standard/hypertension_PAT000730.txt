Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective
John Jacob is a 67-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 10 years ago. Blood pressure remains elevated, and his hypertension is currently not under active management. He reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or nosebleeds. No acute hypertensive symptoms are reported.

Relevant history includes chronic kidney disease of approximately 25 years’ duration. He continues to smoke. Current weight is 70.8 kg at 168 cm, with BMI 25.1 kg/m². No antihypertensive medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 164/95 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respirations 17/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 4.3 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and associated intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. Blood pressure is above goal and requires active outpatient management. He has no symptoms to suggest an acute hypertensive emergency at this visit.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, which increases the importance of consistent blood pressure control and renal-protective management.

Current tobacco use.

Plan
Discussed the importance of initiating and maintaining a structured hypertension treatment plan. Recommend confirming blood pressure with home readings, including seated measurements at consistent times, and bringing the log to follow-up. Review dietary sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and maintenance of current healthy weight.

Recommend initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy with consideration of kidney-protective treatment after review of renal function and the complete medication history. Monitor serum creatinine and potassium after starting or changing therapy; potassium is currently 4.3 mmol/L.

Recommend evaluation with urinalysis, lipid profile, and ECG as part of cardiovascular and kidney risk assessment. Continue monitoring electrolytes and renal function.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, including the benefit of stopping tobacco use for blood pressure and cardiovascular risk reduction.

Advised urgent evaluation for severe or persistent headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, marked visual changes, or other acute worsening. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to reassess blood pressure and response to treatment.
