Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for poorly controlled type 2 diabetes.

Subjective

Joshua Ward is a 49-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 12 years ago. Recent HbA1c is 9.5% and fasting glucose is 208 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No acute diabetic symptoms reported.

He does not smoke. Relevant comorbidities include hypertension for 3 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 8 years. Current diabetes treatment regimen is not documented.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 142/85 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 183 cm, weight 76.7 kg, BMI 22.9 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.5%; fasting glucose 208 mg/dL.

Review of symptoms is negative for hyperglycemic, constitutional, neuropathic, or infectious symptoms as noted above. No additional examination findings documented.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without complications, currently significantly above glycemic goal based on HbA1c 9.5% and fasting glucose 208 mg/dL. Patient is asymptomatic today but requires prompt treatment planning and closer follow-up.

Hypertension with blood pressure above goal at today’s visit.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of prompt diabetes management despite the absence of symptoms. Review and initiate an appropriate glucose-lowering regimen at follow-up, taking into account his comorbid hypertension and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Counseled on reducing refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened foods, maintaining balanced meals, and engaging in regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage home glucose monitoring if a monitoring plan and supplies are established.

Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine/eGFR, fasting lipid profile, and appropriate diabetic eye and foot evaluation as part of complication screening. Continue monitoring liver disease and cardiovascular risk factors.

Review blood pressure readings at the next visit and consider treatment adjustment if persistently elevated.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up to reassess glucose control and finalize the treatment plan. Seek urgent care for vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, marked weakness, inability to keep fluids down, or symptoms of severe hyperglycemia.
