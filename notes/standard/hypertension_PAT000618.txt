Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Amelia Foster is a 93-year-old woman with a 56-year history of essential hypertension and coronary artery disease present for approximately 31 years. She is not currently receiving documented management for her hypertension. She reports intermittent palpitations and fatigue. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. She continues to smoke. No medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Blood pressure is 167/96 mmHg with pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, and oxygen saturation 95% on room air. Height is 172 cm, weight 60.1 kg, and BMI 20.3 kg/m².

Recent serum electrolytes show sodium 140 mmol/L and potassium 5.2 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension remains inadequately controlled, with markedly elevated office blood pressure in the setting of prolonged untreated disease and established coronary artery disease. She is currently without symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency. Palpitations and fatigue are present and warrant clinical reassessment, particularly given her age and coronary history. Potassium is mildly elevated at 5.2 mmol/L and should be considered when selecting antihypertensive therapy.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of establishing active blood pressure management and reducing cardiovascular risk. Antihypertensive therapy should be selected cautiously in view of her age, coronary artery disease, and mildly elevated potassium; medication reconciliation and confirmation of current treatment status are recommended before initiation or adjustment.

Recommend repeat blood pressure assessment with home or ambulatory readings if feasible, along with follow-up serum electrolytes to reassess potassium before or soon after treatment decisions. Recommend ECG evaluation for palpitations and routine cardiovascular risk assessment, including lipid profile and urinalysis, as clinically appropriate.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of excess dietary sodium. She was advised to seek urgent evaluation for chest pain, new or worsening shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or sustained worsening palpitations. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended for blood pressure reassessment and completion of the management plan.
