Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Austin Dean is a 55-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. Blood pressure has not been actively managed. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. No acute cardiovascular or neurologic concerns reported today.

Past medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 9 years and hyperlipidemia for 18 years. He does not smoke. Current medication regimen was not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 147/90 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 188 cm, weight 85.9 kg, BMI 24.3 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 137 mmol/L, potassium 5.0 mmol/L.

Patient is asymptomatic today. No additional examination findings documented.

Assessment

Hypertension with blood pressure above goal at today’s visit. He remains without symptoms concerning for acute end-organ involvement, but the condition has not been actively managed over the past approximately 2 years.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 9 years.

Hyperlipidemia, duration 18 years.

Normal BMI. Nonsmoker.

Potassium is at the upper end of the provided range and should be considered when selecting or adjusting antihypertensive therapy.

Plan

Discussed the importance of consistent blood pressure management given his concurrent diabetes and hyperlipidemia, even in the absence of symptoms.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a validated upper-arm cuff, recording readings for review. Reinforced proper technique, including seated rest before measurement and avoiding measurements immediately after exertion.

Recommend a heart-healthy, lower-sodium eating pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, moderation of alcohol if applicable, and continued avoidance of tobacco.

Review current medication use and adherence at the next visit and determine an appropriate antihypertensive treatment plan. Recheck renal function and electrolytes as clinically appropriate before or after initiating therapy, with attention to the potassium level of 5.0 mmol/L.

Recommend routine cardiovascular risk assessment, including lipid management and diabetes control review. Consider ECG, urinalysis, and updated lipid evaluation as part of hypertension and cardiometabolic risk assessment if not recently completed.

Follow up after a period of home blood pressure monitoring for reassessment and treatment planning. Seek urgent care for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.
