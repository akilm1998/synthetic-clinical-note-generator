Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood glucose.

Subjective

Preston Oliver is an 82-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 43 years. Diabetes has not been actively managed recently. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. He does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include hypertension for 27 years, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 26 years, and obstructive sleep apnea for 8 years. No current medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Height 185 cm; weight 99.6 kg; BMI 29.1 kg/m². Blood pressure 155/85 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Recent laboratory results: HbA1c 9.3%; fasting glucose 212 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently significantly above individualized glycemic goal based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. He is presently asymptomatic without reported hyperglycemic symptoms, constitutional symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, or wound concerns.

Hypertension with elevated office blood pressure today.

Overweight by BMI, with longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and obstructive sleep apnea.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values indicate inadequate diabetes control and warrant prompt treatment review. Recommend medication reconciliation and initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy with consideration of age, renal function, hypoglycemia risk, comorbidities, and personal treatment goals; no medication regimen is documented today.

Advised a consistent carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, and regular physical activity appropriate for his functional status. Recommend home glucose monitoring if treatment is initiated or adjusted, with recording of readings for review.

Recommend evaluation of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes eye and foot assessment as clinically appropriate. Repeat HbA1c after treatment changes to assess response.

Blood pressure remains above goal; recommend repeat measurements and review of current antihypertensive management at follow-up.

Reviewed urgent precautions, including persistent vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, confusion, marked weakness, severe drowsiness, or rapidly worsening symptoms. Close outpatient follow-up recommended to address glycemic and blood pressure control.
