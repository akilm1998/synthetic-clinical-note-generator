Chief Complaint

Annual review of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Violet Murphy is a 43-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 13 years ago. Diabetes has not been actively managed recently. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has a history of chronic kidney disease for approximately 2 years and obesity for approximately 5 years. She currently smokes. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Height 171 cm, weight 113.7 kg, BMI 38.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 130/81 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Recent laboratory results: HbA1c 9.3%; fasting glucose 214 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c of 9.3% and fasting glucose of 214 mg/dL. Although she denies symptomatic hyperglycemia, the degree and duration of elevation increase her risk for microvascular and cardiovascular complications.

Chronic kidney disease, duration approximately 2 years; current renal function and degree of albuminuria are not provided. This is clinically important in selecting and dosing diabetes therapies.

Obesity, BMI 38.9 kg/m².

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of re-establishing consistent diabetes care and the risks associated with prolonged hyperglycemia, including kidney, eye, nerve, and cardiovascular complications. Review current dietary pattern, physical activity, barriers to care, and readiness for treatment at follow-up.

Recommend obtaining renal function testing with serum creatinine and estimated GFR, along with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, to characterize kidney involvement and guide medication selection. Recommend fasting lipid profile and repeat HbA1c at an appropriate interval to assess response to treatment. Consider initiation or intensification of glucose-lowering therapy after medication reconciliation and review of renal function; no current medication regimen was provided.

Counseled regarding a gradual, sustainable weight-loss plan emphasizing reduced intake of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, increased vegetables and fiber, and regular physical activity as tolerated.

Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including skin assessment, pulses, and sensory testing, at a subsequent visit. Recommend a dilated retinal examination because of the long duration of diabetes and current poor glycemic control. Review immunizations and preventive screening according to age and risk factors, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, and hepatitis B vaccination as clinically appropriate.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered discussion of behavioral and pharmacologic support options.

Follow up promptly after laboratory evaluation to review kidney status and establish a documented diabetes treatment plan. Continue routine chronic disease follow-up with reassessment of glycemic control.
