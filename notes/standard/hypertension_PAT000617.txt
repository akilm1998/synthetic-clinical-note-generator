Chief Complaint

Initial evaluation of longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

John Mitchell is a 66-year-old man presenting for an initial outpatient consultation regarding hypertension diagnosed approximately 22 years ago. Hypertension has not been actively managed. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. No acute hypertensive symptoms are reported.

He has a history of chronic kidney disease for approximately 18 years. Current medication use, allergies, and prior antihypertensive treatment history are not documented in the available information. He does not smoke. Height is 186 cm and weight is 74.0 kg; BMI is 21.4 kg/m².

Objective

Vital Signs:
Blood pressure 155/99 mmHg
Pulse 74 bpm
Respiratory rate 16/min
Temperature 36.8°C
Oxygen saturation 99% on room air

Laboratory Data:
Serum sodium 144 mmol/L
Serum potassium 4.7 mmol/L

No additional examination findings are provided.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal at 155/99 mmHg. The patient is asymptomatic without reported features concerning for hypertensive emergency. Given the 22-year duration and lack of active management, evaluation for cardiovascular and renal target-organ involvement is appropriate.

2. Chronic kidney disease, longstanding by history, approximately 18 years. Kidney function data beyond serum electrolytes are not provided. Potassium is currently 4.7 mmol/L and sodium is 144 mmol/L; these values should be considered when selecting and monitoring antihypertensive therapy.

3. Normal BMI and nonsmoking status. Despite a healthy body weight and no tobacco use, blood pressure remains elevated.

Plan

Confirm blood pressure with repeat office measurements using appropriate technique and obtain home blood pressure readings, including a log for review. Assess adherence and any prior antihypertensive exposure at the next visit.

Recommend initiation of an individualized antihypertensive treatment plan, taking the chronic kidney disease history into account. An agent affecting the renin-angiotensin system may be considered if clinically appropriate, with renal function and potassium checked after initiation or dose changes. Avoid empiric treatment without review of current kidney function and prior treatment history.

Recommend renal function testing, including serum creatinine and estimated GFR, and urine testing for protein or albumin as part of chronic kidney disease and hypertension assessment. Obtain urinalysis, lipid profile, and ECG to evaluate cardiovascular risk and possible hypertensive target-organ effects.

Counseled regarding a low-sodium dietary pattern, avoidance of excessive alcohol, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Maintain current healthy weight and continue abstinence from tobacco.

Reviewed symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, acute neurologic symptoms, or marked worsening of blood pressure.

Follow up in approximately 2–4 weeks for blood pressure review, renal assessment, and treatment adjustment, sooner if concerning symptoms develop.
