Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Brian Simmons is an 85-year-old man seen for follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 27 years and currently untreated. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthelasma, and xanthomas. No interval lipid-lowering treatment is reported.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 40 years and hypertension for approximately 55 years. He continues to smoke. Current weight is 88.1 kg at 187 cm, with BMI 25.2 kg/m². The markedly abnormal lipid profile was reviewed with the patient, including the associated cardiovascular risk in the setting of advanced age, diabetes, hypertension, and ongoing tobacco use.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 141/87 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air. BMI 25.2 kg/m².

Recent lipid profile:
Total cholesterol 331 mg/dL.
LDL cholesterol 234 mg/dL.
HDL cholesterol 31 mg/dL.
Triglycerides 331 mg/dL.

No reported clinical symptoms suggestive of active angina or claudication. No xanthomas or xanthelasma reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe elevation of LDL cholesterol and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia and low HDL cholesterol. The degree and longstanding nature of dyslipidemia, together with diabetes, hypertension, and active smoking, place him at very high risk for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease despite the absence of current ischemic symptoms. The condition is presently not receiving lipid-lowering management.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Hypertension, longstanding, with current blood pressure 141/87 mmHg.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the lipid results and cardiovascular risk reduction in detail. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after shared decision-making and review of contraindications, medication interactions, and treatment goals. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe should be considered; referral for specialist lipid management and consideration of additional therapy may be appropriate if severe LDL elevation persists.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, along with appropriate safety and secondary-cause evaluation before or during therapy. Consider measurement of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) for further risk characterization. Coronary artery calcium imaging may be considered only if treatment decisions remain uncertain after discussion of risks and benefits, recognizing the patient’s age and substantial baseline risk.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy diet with reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol, with increased fiber and regular physical activity as tolerated. Strongly advised smoking cessation; cessation support and pharmacologic options should be offered.

Continue close management of diabetes and hypertension with his treating clinicians. Follow up after lipid reassessment to review treatment tolerance, adherence, and response. Instructed to seek urgent evaluation for new chest discomfort, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain.
