Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for poorly controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Christian Cook is a 53-year-old man with a 2-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic kidney disease of approximately 3 years’ duration. He presents for focused follow-up after markedly elevated diabetic laboratory values. He is not currently documented as following an active diabetes treatment regimen.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the extremities. He does not smoke.

Objective

Height 168 cm; weight 77.9 kg; BMI 27.6 kg/m².

Blood pressure 126/82 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

HbA1c 9.2%. Fasting glucose 203 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently substantially above glycemic goal. HbA1c of 9.2% and fasting glucose of 203 mg/dL indicate persistent hyperglycemia despite absence of acute hyperglycemic symptoms. He is overweight and has not had active diabetes management documented.

2. Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 3 years. Renal function must be considered when selecting and dosing glucose-lowering therapy.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the risks of ongoing hyperglycemia. Recommend prompt initiation or re-establishment of a structured diabetes treatment plan, with medication selection and dosing guided by current renal function. Review adherence, diet, physical activity, and home glucose monitoring at the next contact.

Recommend checking serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to guide treatment and renal risk assessment. Continue routine monitoring of HbA1c, with reassessment after treatment is established.

Counseled on reducing concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, regular physical activity as tolerated, and weight reduction. Advised to seek urgent care for vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, confusion, severe weakness, or markedly worsening glucose readings.

Recommend routine diabetic foot and retinal evaluations and continued kidney-protective risk-factor management. Close outpatient follow-up is needed given the degree of hyperglycemia and coexisting chronic kidney disease.
