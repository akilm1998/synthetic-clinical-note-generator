Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Christopher Perry is a 66-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 31 years. Blood pressure control has not been actively managed. He reports dizziness but denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms reported today.

Past medical history is significant for coronary artery disease for 13 years, hyperlipidemia for 25 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 23 years. He currently smokes. Height is 160 cm and weight is 60.9 kg, with BMI 23.8 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 165/96 mmHg, pulse 87 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Recent serum electrolytes: sodium 137 mmol/L and potassium 5.1 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled, with office blood pressure of 165/96 mmHg and associated dizziness. There are no reported symptoms suggesting an acute hypertensive emergency. The mildly elevated potassium is clinically relevant when selecting antihypertensive therapy and warrants confirmation and assessment of renal function before initiating or intensifying agents that may further increase potassium.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding. He denies chest pain or shortness of breath today.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Active tobacco use, increasing overall cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the significance of persistent elevated blood pressure and the increased cardiovascular risk in the setting of coronary artery disease, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and tobacco use. Recommend confirming blood pressure with repeat office measurement and home monitoring, with a written log for review. He should seek urgent evaluation for severe or rapidly worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, or marked visual change.

Antihypertensive treatment should be initiated or re-established promptly, with medication selection guided by repeat potassium and assessment of renal function given potassium of 5.1 mmol/L. Review adherence, prior medication tolerance, and contributing factors at the next medication reconciliation. Counsel on reducing dietary sodium, avoiding excess alcohol, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, and smoking cessation.

Recommend repeat serum electrolytes with renal function testing, urinalysis, lipid profile, and electrocardiogram as part of cardiovascular risk and hypertension assessment. Continue follow-up of diabetes, coronary artery disease, and hyperlipidemia in coordination with the overall primary care plan. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended after blood pressure monitoring and laboratory reassessment.
