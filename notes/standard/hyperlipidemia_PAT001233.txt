Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Cameron West is an 82-year-old man seen for interval follow-up of hyperlipidemia, identified approximately 2 years ago. He is a nonsmoker. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain consistent with claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. No interval symptoms suggestive of acute cardiovascular ischemia are reported.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 28 years ago, hypertension for 15 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 37 years. Current lipid-lowering medications are not documented, and lipid management has not been established. Dietary and exercise habits were not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 142/88 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 183 cm, weight 79.0 kg, BMI 23.6 kg/m².

Lipid panel: Total cholesterol 372 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 279 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 32 mg/dL, triglycerides 305 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with marked elevation of total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol, accompanied by hypertriglyceridemia and low HDL cholesterol. The degree of LDL elevation is clinically significant, particularly in the setting of established coronary artery disease, longstanding diabetes, and hypertension. He is currently without reported chest discomfort, claudication, dizziness, or cutaneous lipid deposits. Overall cardiovascular risk is very high despite a normal BMI and absent tobacco use.

Blood pressure is elevated above usual treatment targets for a patient with coronary artery disease and diabetes; this should be addressed in the context of his longitudinal hypertension management.

Plan

Discussed the substantial cardiovascular implications of the current lipid profile and the importance of active lipid-lowering therapy for secondary prevention. Review and reconciliation of all current medications is recommended, with initiation or optimization of maximally tolerated statin therapy if not contraindicated. If LDL cholesterol remains substantially elevated despite adherence, addition of ezetimibe and consideration of other evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy may be appropriate.

Recommend a heart-healthy dietary pattern emphasizing reduction of saturated and trans fats and refined carbohydrates, increased soluble fiber, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated and appropriate for his coronary artery disease and overall functional status.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid testing after therapy is initiated or adjusted to assess response. Consider evaluation for contributing secondary causes of severe dyslipidemia and measurement of additional atherosclerotic risk markers when clinically useful. Management should be individualized given his age and comorbid coronary artery disease, diabetes, and hypertension.

Continue monitoring for new chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, or other ischemic symptoms; seek urgent evaluation for persistent or severe chest symptoms. Reassess lipid management and blood pressure control at the next routine follow-up.
