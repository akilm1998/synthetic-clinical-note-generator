Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up of markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Kayla Pierce is an 85-year-old woman with a reported 27-year history of hyperlipidemia, currently without documented lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 286 mg/dL, LDL-C 191 mg/dL, HDL-C 43 mg/dL, and triglycerides 261 mg/dL. She reports fatty skin deposits consistent with xanthomas. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, or xanthelasma.

She currently smokes. Relevant comorbidities include longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease reported for 56 years and chronic kidney disease reported for 38 years. No current medications were provided.

Objective

Blood pressure 141/85 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96%. Height 153 cm, weight 63.2 kg, BMI 27.0 kg/m². No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms reported. Xanthomas reported; no xanthelasma reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with substantially elevated LDL-C and elevated triglycerides, untreated to date. The degree and duration of elevation, along with active tobacco use and comorbid chronic kidney disease, increase cardiovascular risk. No current symptoms suggestive of angina or intermittent claudication. Xanthomas may be associated with longstanding lipid elevation.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and chronic kidney disease, longstanding by history; these should be considered when selecting and monitoring therapy.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the markedly abnormal lipid profile and cardiovascular risk reduction, including a heart-healthy diet low in saturated and trans fats, increased activity as tolerated, weight maintenance, and smoking cessation.

Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of lipid-lowering therapy, with consideration of a statin after review of renal and hepatic status, medication interactions, treatment goals, and patient preferences. If LDL reduction is inadequate or therapy is not tolerated, additional lipid-lowering options may be considered.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or lifestyle intervention to assess response. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) testing if results would affect risk assessment or management. Further cardiovascular risk evaluation, including consideration of coronary artery calcium imaging, should be individualized given her age and overall goals of care.

Monitor for new chest discomfort, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain and seek urgent evaluation if these occur. Follow up promptly to review treatment decisions and lipid management.
