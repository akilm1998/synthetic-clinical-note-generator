Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective
Sarah Griffin is a 37-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. She reports no increased thirst, polyuria, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. Diabetes is currently not actively managed. No diabetes medication regimen is documented.

She has a 4-year history of dyslipidemia. She does not smoke.

Objective
Height 165 cm; weight 74.6 kg; BMI 27.4 kg/m².
Blood pressure 121/80 mmHg, pulse 68 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.
Hemoglobin A1c 9.3%.
Fasting glucose 201 mg/dL.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 201 mg/dL. She is asymptomatic despite significant hyperglycemia. No neuropathic or infectious symptoms are reported. BMI is in the overweight range.

Dyslipidemia, present for approximately 4 years.

Plan
Discussed that current glycemic indices are substantially above the usual treatment target and require active management.

Recommend initiation of structured diabetes therapy, including consideration of metformin as first-line pharmacologic treatment if renal function is appropriate. Given the degree of hyperglycemia, consider escalation to combination therapy if adequate improvement is not achieved or if clinically indicated.

Counseled regarding reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, weight reduction, and regular aerobic and resistance exercise as tolerated. Encourage home glucose monitoring with review of fasting and postprandial values at follow-up.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before and during therapy, along with a fasting lipid profile for cardiometabolic risk assessment. Recommend routine comprehensive foot examination and dilated retinal examination for diabetes-related complications.

Follow up after initiation of active treatment and review of home glucose data and updated laboratory evaluation.
