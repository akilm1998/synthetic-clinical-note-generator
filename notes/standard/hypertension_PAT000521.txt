Chief Complaint

Annual review of elevated blood pressure and chronic cardiovascular risk.

Subjective

Diana Prince is a 51-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review of hypertension diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. Hypertension has not been actively managed. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 10 years, coronary artery disease for 5 years, and chronic kidney disease for 1 year. She currently smokes. No medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Height 174 cm; weight 63.9 kg; BMI 21.1 kg/m².

Blood pressure 153/99 mmHg. Pulse 75 bpm. Respiratory rate 15/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 97%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 141 mmol/L, potassium 3.9 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure, currently untreated or otherwise not actively managed. She is asymptomatic today, without reported features suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Her diabetes, chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease, and active tobacco use place her at high cardiovascular and renal risk despite a normal BMI.

Electrolytes are currently within expected ranges. The available results do not establish renal function or cardiovascular risk control.

Plan

Confirm the blood pressure with repeat standardized office measurements and recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log. Review adherence and prior treatment history at the next visit; no current antihypertensive regimen is documented.

Given hypertension in the setting of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease, discuss prompt initiation of guideline-directed antihypertensive therapy, with selection informed by renal function, urine albumin status, cardiovascular history, contraindications, and repeat blood pressure measurements. Obtain renal function and urine albumin assessment before or soon after treatment initiation, with electrolyte monitoring after any renin-angiotensin system therapy or diuretic is started.

Recommend ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular and hypertensive risk assessment. Continue coordinated management of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, avoidance of excess dietary sodium, regular physical activity as tolerated, and maintenance of a healthy weight. Review alcohol intake if applicable. Address age-appropriate preventive care, including immunizations and recommended breast, cervical, colorectal, and other routine screening based on prior results and screening history.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent assessment, including severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, visual disturbance, or markedly elevated home blood pressure readings. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure review and treatment planning.
