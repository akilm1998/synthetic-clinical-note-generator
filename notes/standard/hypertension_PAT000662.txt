Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Adam Ross is a 73-year-old man seen for outpatient follow-up of hypertension, present for approximately 15 years and currently without active pharmacologic management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. No interval hypertensive symptoms are reported.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus of 34 years’ duration and hyperlipidemia of 42 years’ duration. He does not smoke. Current weight is 89.4 kg at a height of 175 cm, with BMI 29.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 150/93 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 146 mmol/L, potassium 4.5 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension

Blood pressure remains above goal at today’s visit in the setting of longstanding hypertension without active management. He is currently asymptomatic, with no reported features suggestive of acute end-organ involvement. Potassium is normal at 4.5 mmol/L; sodium is mildly elevated at 146 mmol/L. His age, overweight status, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia increase overall cardiovascular risk.

2. Type 2 diabetes mellitus

Longstanding disease, present for 34 years, and an important consideration in blood pressure risk stratification and therapeutic selection.

3. Hyperlipidemia

Longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for 42 years, contributing to cardiovascular risk.

4. Overweight

BMI 29.2 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the significance of persistent blood pressure elevation and the cardiovascular and renal risks associated with prolonged uncontrolled hypertension, particularly in the setting of diabetes and hyperlipidemia. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a validated upper-arm cuff, recording readings at consistent times and bringing the log to follow-up.

Recommend initiation of an antihypertensive treatment plan after review of baseline renal function and urine assessment, with selection tailored to his diabetes and overall cardiovascular risk. Serum electrolytes and renal function should be monitored after therapy is initiated or adjusted. An ECG and lipid profile are appropriate components of cardiovascular risk assessment; urinalysis is also recommended in view of longstanding hypertension and diabetes.

Counseled regarding reduced dietary sodium intake, moderation of alcohol if applicable, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and adherence to a structured dietary pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and lean proteins. Continue avoidance of tobacco.

He was advised to seek urgent evaluation for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Continue follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment adjustment.
