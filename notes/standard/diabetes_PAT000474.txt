Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for long-standing type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Spencer Lawson is an 89-year-old male presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of type 2 diabetes mellitus, reported duration approximately 59 years. Diabetes has not been actively managed recently. Recent laboratory results show HbA1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 216 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimal glycemic control.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No diabetic complications are reported in the available history.

Relevant comorbidities include hypertension for approximately 16 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 17 years. He currently smokes. Quantity and duration of tobacco use were not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 146/86 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 184 cm. Weight 96.2 kg. BMI 28.4 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.2%. Fasting glucose 216 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, with current laboratory evidence of poor glycemic control. The patient is asymptomatic despite elevated fasting glucose and HbA1c. He denies symptoms suggestive of hyperglycemia, neuropathy, recurrent infection, or impaired wound healing. No diabetes medication regimen was provided.

2. Hypertension, longstanding, with blood pressure 146/86 mmHg today. Current antihypertensive treatment is not documented.

3. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding. No current liver-related symptoms or treatment details were provided.

4. Overweight status with BMI 28.4 kg/m².

5. Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the increased risk of microvascular and macrovascular complications with persistent hyperglycemia. Given his advanced age and long duration of diabetes, treatment goals should be individualized with attention to functional status, hypoglycemia risk, renal function, nutritional status, and overall treatment burden.

Review and reconcile all current medications at the next opportunity before selecting therapy. Initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering medication should be considered based on renal and hepatic function, oral intake, hypoglycemia risk, and patient preferences. Avoid overly aggressive glucose lowering without appropriate monitoring.

Recommend home glucose monitoring as clinically appropriate, with instruction to report symptomatic or recurrent low glucose readings. Encourage a consistent carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, and regular physical activity within his capabilities.

Recommend evaluation of renal status with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and assessment of cardiovascular risk with a fasting lipid profile. A comprehensive diabetic foot examination and retinal evaluation should be arranged as part of routine diabetes care. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and review the current hypertension regimen when medication information is available. Reinforce tobacco cessation counseling and offer cessation support.

Follow up promptly after medication and risk-factor review to establish an individualized diabetes management plan; otherwise reassess glycemic control within approximately 3 months.
