Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Edward Holland is a 63-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 15 years. His lipid disorder has not been actively managed with a documented medication regimen. Recent laboratory testing shows markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with elevated triglycerides. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. He does not smoke.

He has longstanding obesity, reported for approximately 31 years. Current weight is 98.5 kg at a height of 176 cm, with BMI 31.8 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 127/81 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Lipid panel: Total cholesterol 324 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 221 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 45 mg/dL, triglycerides 289 mg/dL.

No symptomatic cardiovascular or cutaneous manifestations of hyperlipidemia are reported today.

Assessment

Longstanding hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol at 221 mg/dL and elevated triglycerides at 289 mg/dL. He is currently without chest discomfort, claudication, dizziness, or reported xanthomatous findings, but the degree and duration of lipid elevation place him at increased risk for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. Treatment is not currently established.

Obesity, BMI 31.8 kg/m², longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the importance of active treatment. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after shared decision-making, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, with medication selection and dosing to be finalized at the visit. Reviewed potential adverse effects and advised him to report unexplained muscle pain, weakness, or dark urine.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, lean proteins, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated and trans fats, processed foods, and excess refined carbohydrates. Encouraged gradual regular aerobic activity as tolerated and weight reduction. Continued tobacco avoidance reinforced.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid testing after treatment initiation to assess response and adherence. Consider evaluation for contributing secondary causes and additional cardiovascular risk assessment as clinically appropriate. Follow up after treatment is started to review tolerance, lipid response, and ongoing risk reduction.
