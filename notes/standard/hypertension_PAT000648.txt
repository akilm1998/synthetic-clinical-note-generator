Assessment

Essential hypertension with an elevated office blood pressure of 156/97 mmHg in a 69-year-old man with an approximately 11-year history and no documented active treatment. He is currently asymptomatic, without features suggesting hypertensive emergency. The duration of hypertension and current degree of elevation warrant prompt confirmation and treatment planning, with assessment for cardiovascular and renal end-organ effects.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding for approximately 16 years, with current lipid values and treatment status not provided. This increases overall cardiovascular risk in the setting of hypertension and ongoing tobacco use.

Current tobacco use, an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

Normal BMI. Serum sodium and potassium are within the provided reference context and do not suggest an electrolyte abnormality.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement after appropriate rest and obtain home blood pressure readings, if feasible, to confirm the persistent pattern and guide treatment. Given the longstanding hypertension and current elevation, pharmacologic therapy should be initiated or discussed promptly rather than relying on lifestyle measures alone. Reasonable first-line options include an ACE inhibitor, ARB, calcium-channel blocker, or thiazide-type diuretic, selected after review of renal function, urine studies, comorbidities, and patient preference. No medication is documented as prescribed today.

Recommend evaluation for hypertension-related target-organ disease with serum renal function testing, repeat electrolytes as clinically indicated, urinalysis, ECG, and assessment of cardiovascular risk including a lipid profile. Review prior lipid results and determine whether lipid-lowering therapy is indicated.

Counseled regarding reduction of dietary sodium, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excessive alcohol intake, and smoking cessation. Discussed the importance of adherence to follow-up and blood pressure monitoring.

He should seek urgent medical attention for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, acute visual changes, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review home readings, complete cardiovascular risk assessment, and finalize antihypertensive treatment.
