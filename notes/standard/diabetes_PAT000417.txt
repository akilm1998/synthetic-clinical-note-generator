Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Jason Coleman is a 50-year-old male presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 15 years ago. Current laboratory results show HbA1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 237 mg/dL, consistent with inadequate glycemic control. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has a history of anxiety for approximately 10 years. He currently smokes. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 122/75 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 178 cm, weight 89.7 kg, BMI 28.3 kg/m².

Laboratory data: HbA1c 9.2%. Fasting glucose 237 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications. Glycemic control is above goal based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. Despite the absence of hyperglycemic, neuropathic, or infectious symptoms, the degree and duration of elevation increase his risk for microvascular and cardiovascular complications.

Overweight, BMI 28.3 kg/m².

Tobacco use.

Anxiety, longstanding, approximately 10 years.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of active diabetes management. Reviewed nutrition strategies emphasizing reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, increased dietary fiber, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Weight reduction was encouraged.

Recommend initiating evidence-based pharmacologic therapy for diabetes, with medication selection guided by renal function, cardiovascular risk, preferences, and contraindications. Medication risks, benefits, and adherence should be reviewed before prescribing. Encourage home glucose monitoring if pharmacologic therapy is started and review readings at follow-up.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes eye and foot evaluations to screen for complications. Reinforced daily foot care and prompt evaluation of skin breakdown or nonhealing wounds.

Smoking cessation counseling provided; encouraged setting a quit date and discussing cessation aids if desired.

Encouraged ongoing attention to anxiety symptoms and consideration of behavioral health support if anxiety is interfering with sleep, functioning, or diabetes self-management.

Follow up after treatment initiation and review of glucose data and repeat HbA1c to assess response.
