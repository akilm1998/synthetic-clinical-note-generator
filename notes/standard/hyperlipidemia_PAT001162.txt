Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Pamela Donovan is a 43-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 14 years and not currently under an established treatment plan. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 340 mg/dL, LDL-C 246 mg/dL, HDL-C 36 mg/dL, and triglycerides 289 mg/dL. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma.

Relevant cardiovascular risk factors include active tobacco use, BMI 28.3 kg/m², hypertension for 11 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 10 years. No lipid-lowering medications are provided in the current history.

Objective

Height 171 cm; weight 82.8 kg; BMI 28.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 125/92 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 340 mg/dL, LDL-C 246 mg/dL, HDL-C 36 mg/dL, triglycerides 289 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and elevated triglycerides, untreated or without a documented active management plan. The lipid profile confers substantial long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of diabetes, hypertension, tobacco use, low HDL, and overweight status. She has no current symptoms suggesting acute coronary or peripheral ischemia.

Hypertension with elevated diastolic pressure today.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the need for prompt initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy. Recommend high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after review of contraindications, potential adverse effects, pregnancy considerations, and patient preference. If LDL-C remains substantially elevated despite adherence and tolerated therapy, consider addition of ezetimibe and further risk assessment.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, whole grains, legumes, lean proteins, and unsaturated fats while limiting saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol. Encourage regular aerobic activity as tolerated and continued weight reduction.

Strongly advised smoking cessation; offer behavioral support and pharmacologic cessation options at follow-up.

Coordinate ongoing management of diabetes and hypertension, as these conditions materially increase cardiovascular risk. Recommend repeat fasting lipid testing after treatment initiation or adjustment and consideration of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement to further characterize risk. Consider coronary artery calcium assessment if treatment decisions remain uncertain after shared discussion.

Seek urgent evaluation for new chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain. Close outpatient follow-up recommended to establish and monitor therapy.
