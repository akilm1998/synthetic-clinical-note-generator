Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for long-standing hypertension.

Subjective

Anna Cook is an 87-year-old woman, 151 cm tall and weighing 57.5 kg (BMI 25.2 kg/m²), seen for outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension present for approximately 27 years. Her hypertension has not been actively managed. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. She does not smoke.

Her medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus of approximately 48 years’ duration and hyperlipidemia of approximately 45 years’ duration. No current antihypertensive regimen is provided.

Objective

Blood pressure is 152/85 mmHg with pulse 70 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.9°C, and oxygen saturation 95%. Serum sodium is 143 mmol/L and potassium is 5.2 mmol/L. No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms are reported today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with blood pressure above goal at today’s visit in the setting of long-standing disease that has not been actively managed. She is asymptomatic, without reported features of hypertensive emergency. The potassium is mildly elevated at 5.2 mmol/L and should be considered when selecting antihypertensive therapy, particularly agents that may further increase serum potassium. Her advanced age and coexisting long-standing diabetes and hyperlipidemia warrant gradual, individualized blood pressure management with attention to orthostatic symptoms and overall tolerability.

Plan

Discussed the importance of re-establishing active hypertension management and obtaining additional blood pressure measurements outside the office, if feasible, to assess persistence and variability of elevation. Recommend review of dietary sodium intake, avoidance of excess alcohol, and regular activity appropriate to her functional status.

Recommend repeat serum electrolytes with assessment of renal function before or soon after initiation of antihypertensive therapy, given the potassium of 5.2 mmol/L. Antihypertensive treatment should be selected and titrated cautiously with monitoring for orthostatic symptoms, renal function, and potassium changes. Continue follow-up for blood pressure control and ongoing management of diabetes and hyperlipidemia. She should seek urgent evaluation for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure accompanied by symptoms.
