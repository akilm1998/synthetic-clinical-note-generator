Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Arthur Nichols is a 50-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 7 years ago. His blood pressure has not been under active management. He reports intermittent palpitations and fatigue but denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. He does not smoke.

His medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 15 years and chronic kidney disease for 9 years. No current antihypertensive regimen is documented.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 165/90 mmHg; pulse 74 bpm; respiratory rate 15/min; temperature 36.8°C; oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 179 cm; weight 85.2 kg; BMI 26.6 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: Sodium 140 mmol/L; potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, inadequately controlled. Blood pressure is markedly elevated at today’s visit in the setting of a 7-year history of hypertension that is not currently being actively managed. He has no reported symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency. His comorbid diabetes and chronic kidney disease substantially increase cardiovascular and renal risk and favor prompt establishment of a structured blood pressure treatment plan. Electrolytes are currently acceptable, with potassium at the upper end of the usual reference range.

Palpitations and fatigue. These symptoms are nonspecific but clinically relevant in the setting of uncontrolled hypertension. There is no associated chest pain, dyspnea, syncope, or resting tachycardia reported.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, duration 15 years. This increases overall cardiovascular risk and is an important consideration in blood pressure and renal-protective management.

Chronic kidney disease, duration 9 years. Renal function and albuminuria should be incorporated into antihypertensive selection and monitoring.

Overweight by BMI, 26.6 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the importance of prompt treatment of persistently elevated blood pressure given the patient’s diabetes and chronic kidney disease. Confirm blood pressure with repeat office measurements and home monitoring, including a written log of resting readings. Initiation of an evidence-based antihypertensive regimen should be arranged promptly, with selection guided by renal function, urine albumin status, potassium level, and overall cardiovascular risk. Medication adherence and tolerance should be reviewed at subsequent visits.

Recommend dietary sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight optimization, and limitation of alcohol if applicable. Continue smoking avoidance.

Obtain or review renal function testing and urinalysis with urine albumin assessment before and after therapy initiation as clinically appropriate. An ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis are also appropriate components of cardiovascular risk and hypertension evaluation. Monitor serum electrolytes after starting or titrating therapy, particularly given chronic kidney disease and potassium of 4.8 mmol/L.

Evaluate palpitations with an ECG; seek urgent evaluation for chest pain, shortness of breath, syncope, new neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or persistently extreme blood pressure elevation.

Follow up promptly for blood pressure reassessment and treatment initiation or titration.
