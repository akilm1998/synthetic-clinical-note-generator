Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for hypertension.

Subjective

Avery Ward is a 76-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of hypertension, reportedly present for approximately 6 years and currently without active management. She reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, and epistaxis. No smoking history. Current weight is 76.0 kg and height is 165 cm, with BMI 27.9 kg/m².

Relevant medical history includes chronic kidney disease for approximately 19 years, hyperlipidemia for 5 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 4 years. No current antihypertensive medications are documented. Available serum electrolytes show sodium 143 mmol/L and potassium 3.7 mmol/L.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 183/94 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

General: Outpatient presentation with no reported acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms.

Cardiovascular: Pulse regular at 72 bpm by vital-sign measurement. No additional examination findings provided.

Respiratory: Oxygen saturation 98%; no reported shortness of breath.

Laboratory data: Sodium 143 mmol/L. Potassium 3.7 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, inadequately controlled. Blood pressure is markedly elevated at 183/94 mmHg in the setting of approximately 6 years of hypertension without active management. Dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue may be related but are nonspecific. She currently denies symptoms strongly suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency, including chest pain, dyspnea, severe headache, visual disturbance, or epistaxis; nevertheless, the degree of elevation and her comorbid chronic kidney disease and diabetes confer increased cardiovascular and renal risk.

2. Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, approximately 19 years. Renal function parameters beyond the supplied electrolytes are not available for review. This is an important consideration in antihypertensive selection and monitoring.

3. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 4 years.

4. Hyperlipidemia, approximately 5 years.

5. Intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure during the visit using appropriate cuff size and standardized technique, with confirmation by repeat measurements. Given the markedly elevated reading, arrange prompt clinical reassessment and initiation of an individualized antihypertensive regimen rather than continued observation without treatment. Medication selection should account for her chronic kidney disease and diabetes; an ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker may be considered if appropriate after review of renal function and potassium, with alternatives such as a calcium-channel blocker or thiazide-type diuretic considered according to renal function, volume status, and overall treatment response.

Obtain recommended baseline evaluation, including renal function, repeat electrolytes after treatment initiation or adjustment, urinalysis with assessment for albuminuria, ECG, and lipid profile. Review diabetes and lipid management as part of global cardiovascular risk reduction.

Advise home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, avoiding excess dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, moderating alcohol intake if applicable, and pursuing gradual weight reduction. She does not smoke.

Because of the current blood pressure level and associated dizziness/palpitations, she should seek urgent or emergency evaluation for chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic deficit, confusion, severe or sudden headache, visual loss, syncope, or persistent worsening symptoms. Follow up promptly after repeat blood pressure assessment and completion of the initial evaluation.
