Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Valeria Ellis is a 79-year-old woman, 178 cm tall and 73.2 kg (BMI 23.1 kg/m²), presenting for outpatient medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 11 years ago. She has a 24-year history of hypertension and currently smokes. She denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, lower-extremity swelling, changes in urination, or shortness of breath.

No active medication regimen is recorded for review today; therefore, medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be confirmed. She has no reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms at this visit.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 166/91 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: Serum creatinine 4.8 mg/dL, estimated GFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 62 mg/dL.

Patient is alert and clinically stable by vital signs, without reported respiratory distress or peripheral edema. No additional examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated creatinine and BUN. Despite the absence of reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms, the degree of renal impairment warrants prompt renal evaluation and close monitoring. Longstanding hypertension is currently above goal and is a significant risk factor for ongoing renal disease progression.

Medication regimen is not established in the available history, so adherence, efficacy, adverse effects, and need for renal dose adjustment require direct reconciliation. No medication should be initiated or continued without confirming the actual home medication list and allergy history.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Perform a complete medication reconciliation, including prescription medications, over-the-counter agents, supplements, and NSAID use. Assess adherence, blood pressure response, and adverse effects once the active regimen is confirmed. Avoid nephrotoxic medications and ensure all medications are renally dosed.

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for evaluation of advanced chronic kidney disease and discussion of renal-protective management and future planning. Advise urgent evaluation for new nausea or vomiting, worsening fatigue, confusion, marked weakness, reduced urine output, swelling, chest pain, or shortness of breath.

Repeat renal and metabolic assessment with serum electrolytes, creatinine/eGFR, BUN, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound to evaluate kidney structure.

Address blood pressure after medication reconciliation, with home blood pressure monitoring if feasible. Reinforce sodium moderation, avoidance of NSAIDs, smoking cessation, and maintenance of appropriate hydration without excessive fluid intake. Follow up promptly after medication reconciliation and repeat assessment.
