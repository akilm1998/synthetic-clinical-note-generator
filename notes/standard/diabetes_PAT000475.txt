Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for elevated blood glucose.

Subjective
Jacob Long is an 83-year-old man seen for follow-up of recently identified type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 1 year and currently untreated. Recent HbA1c is 9.9% and fasting glucose is 253 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, unintentional weight loss, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling. No acute hyperglycemic symptoms reported.

Past medical history includes dyslipidemia for 4 years, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 51 years, and obstructive sleep apnea for 26 years. He currently smokes. Height 164 cm, weight 76.7 kg, BMI 28.5 kg/m².

Objective
Vital signs: BP 138/75 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respirations 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Alert older man without reported acute distress. No additional examination findings provided.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with marked hyperglycemia. HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 253 mg/dL indicate inadequate glycemic control despite absence of hyperglycemic symptoms. Overweight status and ongoing tobacco use increase overall cardiometabolic risk.

Dyslipidemia.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Obstructive sleep apnea.

Plan
Discussed laboratory results and the importance of prompt diabetes treatment. Recommend initiation of glucose-lowering pharmacotherapy after review of renal function and medication appropriateness for age and comorbidities; metformin may be considered if renal function permits. Obtain serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio as part of diabetes evaluation. Recommend home glucose monitoring with documentation of fasting and symptomatic readings.

Counseled on reducing concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, regular physical activity as tolerated, and maintenance of hydration. Reviewed symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, severe weakness, inability to maintain oral intake, or persistent markedly elevated glucose.

Recommend comprehensive foot examination and dilated retinal examination, along with ongoing cardiovascular risk assessment. Strongly advised smoking cessation. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review glucose readings and treatment response.
