Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for management of longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Maxwell Knight is a 33-year-old man, 186 cm tall and weighing 75.4 kg, presenting as a new patient for outpatient evaluation of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 12 years ago. Diabetes has not been actively managed. Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin A1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 188 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimally controlled diabetes.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, frequent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet.

Relevant comorbidities include non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and depression, each present for approximately 8 years. He currently smokes. No diabetes medications or other current medications were provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 125/81 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 186 cm. Weight 75.4 kg. BMI 21.8 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.3%. Fasting glucose 188 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings or laboratory results are available from today’s information.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications. Diabetes has been present for approximately 12 years and is currently inadequately controlled, with A1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 188 mg/dL. Although he reports no current hyperglycemic, neuropathic, or infectious symptoms, the duration and degree of hyperglycemia place him at risk for microvascular and cardiovascular complications. His BMI is normal, so management should not focus solely on weight loss.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding. This is clinically relevant when selecting and monitoring diabetes therapy and reinforces the importance of limiting alcohol if applicable, avoiding excess dietary sugar, and maintaining regular physical activity.

Depression, longstanding. Mood symptoms and current treatment status should be reviewed because depression may affect adherence and diabetes self-management.

Tobacco use. Active smoking increases cardiovascular and other health risks in the setting of diabetes.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated A1c and fasting glucose and the need to establish consistent diabetes care. Recommend prompt review of treatment options, including initiation of evidence-based glucose-lowering medication such as metformin if no contraindication is identified, with medication selection guided by renal function, liver history, preferences, cost, and treatment goals. Medication should be started and titrated through shared decision-making after completing the initial medication and safety review.

Recommend a balanced carbohydrate-controlled eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, high-fiber foods, lean protein, and minimally processed foods. Encourage regular aerobic activity with resistance training as tolerated. Because BMI is 21.8 kg/m², avoid unnecessary caloric restriction or further weight loss.

Recommend home glucose monitoring during treatment initiation and adjustment, with education regarding symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia. Review sick-day precautions and provide diabetes self-management education.

Obtain baseline renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, along with a fasting lipid profile, to assess diabetes-related risk and guide preventive care. Recommend comprehensive dilated eye examination and complete diabetic foot examination, including sensation and pulses, given the 12-year duration of diabetes. These are recommendations and have not been completed today.

Address smoking cessation at follow-up and offer counseling and pharmacologic support as appropriate. Review depressive symptoms, safety, functional impact, and any current behavioral health treatment; coordinate additional support if indicated.

Follow up soon for review of treatment options, medication reconciliation, diabetes education, and results of recommended baseline evaluation. Reassess glycemic control after an appropriate interval following treatment initiation or adjustment.
