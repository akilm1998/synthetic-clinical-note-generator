Chief Complaint

New patient evaluation of longstanding elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Jason Coleman is a 57-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient consultation regarding hyperlipidemia present for approximately 18 years and currently untreated. He reports xanthomas. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain consistent with claudication, and dizziness. No xanthelasma is reported.

Relevant medical history includes type 2 diabetes mellitus for 17 years, coronary artery disease for 8 years, and obesity for 8 years. He currently smokes. Current medication use, allergies, prior lipid-lowering treatment, dietary history, and family history were not provided for this initial visit and should be clarified as part of medication and cardiovascular risk reconciliation.

Objective

Height 177 cm; weight 99.9 kg; BMI 31.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 125/81 mmHg. Pulse 83 bpm. Respiratory rate 16/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 97%.

Available lipid panel:
Total cholesterol 353 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 276 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 30 mg/dL
Triglycerides 237 mg/dL

Reported xanthomas. No xanthelasma reported. No current chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness reported.

Assessment

Severe hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, low HDL cholesterol, and elevated triglycerides. The degree and duration of LDL elevation, together with reported xanthomas and established coronary artery disease, indicate very high atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk. A primary or familial lipid disorder should be considered, although this cannot be confirmed from the information currently available.

Coronary artery disease, reported duration 8 years, without current chest discomfort or claudication.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, reported duration 17 years.

Obesity with BMI 31.9 kg/m².

Current tobacco use, contributing substantially to cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the need for prompt, sustained lipid-lowering therapy given the LDL cholesterol of 276 mg/dL and history of coronary artery disease. After medication reconciliation and review of prior tolerance, recommend initiation or reinitiation of a high-intensity statin unless contraindicated, with consideration of additional lipid-lowering therapy if LDL remains substantially above goal. Treatment adherence and prior response should be reviewed carefully.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after therapy is established to assess response. Consider assessment for secondary contributors to severe dyslipidemia and additional risk stratification, including ApoB and lipoprotein(a), as clinically appropriate. Given the reported xanthomas and longstanding severe LDL elevation, obtain a detailed family history of premature cardiovascular disease and markedly elevated cholesterol and consider referral to a lipid specialist if control is difficult or a hereditary disorder is suspected.

Counseled on reducing saturated and trans fats, increasing fiber-rich foods, limiting refined carbohydrates and excess calories, and pursuing gradual weight reduction. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated, taking coronary disease into account.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered structured cessation support and pharmacologic options after review of his history and preferences.

Continue routine diabetes and coronary artery disease management with attention to comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction. He should seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening chest discomfort, shortness of breath, syncope, or exertional leg symptoms.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after medication reconciliation and initiation of the lipid management plan, with review of tolerance, adherence, and repeat lipid results.
