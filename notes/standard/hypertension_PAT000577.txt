Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Alexandra Boyd is an 88-year-old woman seen for follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 33 years. Blood pressure control has been suboptimal, and she is not currently documented as having an active management regimen. She reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis.

Medical history is significant for chronic kidney disease for 48 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 34 years, and coronary artery disease for 39 years. She continues to smoke. Current weight is 67.8 kg at a height of 159 cm, with BMI 26.8 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure 169/94 mmHg. Pulse 80 bpm. Respiratory rate 14/min. Temperature 36.6°C. Oxygen saturation 96%.

Serum sodium 136 mmol/L and potassium 4.0 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and associated intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. No reported chest pain, dyspnea, visual disturbance, severe headache, or epistaxis to suggest an acute hypertensive emergency today. Given her advanced age and long-standing hypertension, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease, careful confirmation and gradual improvement of blood pressure control are important, with attention to orthostatic symptoms and renal function.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Available sodium and potassium are within the provided reference context; renal function values are not available in today’s information and should guide antihypertensive selection and monitoring.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding. Palpitations warrant clinical reassessment, particularly in the setting of coronary disease.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Review and reconcile all current prescription and over-the-counter medications, adherence, sodium intake, and home blood pressure readings. She should begin or resume a clinician-directed antihypertensive regimen appropriate for her chronic kidney disease, diabetes, coronary artery disease, age, and fall risk; treatment should be titrated gradually with monitoring for dizziness or orthostatic hypotension.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with documented readings and assessment of sitting and standing blood pressure when feasible. Reassess promptly after treatment initiation or adjustment rather than allowing prolonged uncontrolled pressure.

Obtain renal function and repeat electrolytes as clinically appropriate before and after antihypertensive therapy changes. Consider urinalysis and cardiovascular risk assessment, including lipid profile, as part of ongoing management. An ECG is appropriate to evaluate palpitations and for baseline assessment given her coronary artery disease.

Counseled regarding sodium restriction, avoidance of excess alcohol if applicable, physical activity appropriate to her functional status, weight maintenance, and smoking cessation. She should seek urgent evaluation for new chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, severe headache, acute visual change, or markedly higher blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up in the near term for blood pressure reassessment and review of symptoms, home readings, medication tolerance, and laboratory monitoring.
