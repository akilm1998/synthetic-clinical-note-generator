Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Emily Cooper is a 53-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 5 years ago. Her kidney disease is currently not under active management. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, or shortness of breath.

Relevant comorbidities include hypertension for approximately 3 years, gout for 10 years, and secondary hyperparathyroidism diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She currently smokes. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

Objective

Height 154 cm, weight 57.6 kg, BMI 24.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 149/101 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Serum creatinine 3.02 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m². BUN 79 mg/dL.

No edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, urinary frequency change, or other fluid-overload or prominent uremic symptoms reported.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine, associated with fatigue. The disease has been present for approximately 5 years and is not currently under active management. Although she denies symptoms suggesting acute fluid overload or significant uremic syndrome, the degree of renal impairment warrants prompt specialist involvement and close monitoring.

Hypertension, currently above goal at 149/101 mmHg, in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, present for approximately 1 year.

History of gout for 10 years.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation and management of advanced chronic kidney disease, including discussion of renal-protective care and future planning as clinically appropriate.

Obtain repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Consider renal ultrasound as part of evaluation if not previously completed.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplemental medications at the next visit and avoid nephrotoxic agents, particularly NSAIDs, unless specifically directed by a clinician. Encourage adequate but not excessive fluid intake and avoidance of high-sodium foods.

Address elevated blood pressure promptly with medication reconciliation and individualized antihypertensive management. Continue monitoring home blood pressures if available.

Counsel regarding smoking cessation, which is important for cardiovascular and renal risk reduction.

Discuss urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.

Arrange close primary care follow-up after laboratory testing and nephrology evaluation.
