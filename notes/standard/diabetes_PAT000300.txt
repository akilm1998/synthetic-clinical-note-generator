Chief Complaint

Medication review for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Ian Lawson is a 70-year-old man with a 33-year history of type 2 diabetes presenting for outpatient treatment review. Current glycemic control is poor, with hemoglobin A1c 9.1% and fasting glucose 221 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or frequent infections. He reports numbness and tingling in the hands and feet.

Medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance were reviewed. A specific current diabetes medication regimen is not provided for this visit. He does not smoke. History is also notable for depression for approximately 9 years.

Objective

Blood pressure 133/76 mmHg, pulse 68 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 181 cm, weight 90.7 kg, BMI 27.7 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.1%; fasting glucose 221 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complication, with significantly elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose indicating inadequate glycemic control. He is not reporting typical hyperglycemic symptoms, but does report bilateral distal numbness and tingling, which warrants further clinical assessment.

Depression, longstanding, approximately 9 years.

Plan

Reconcile all current medications, including any diabetes therapies, and clarify adherence, dosing, missed doses, and prior treatment intolerance before making medication changes.

Glycemic therapy should be initiated or intensified promptly based on the reconciled medication history and renal function. Discussed the importance of consistent medication use, nutrition focused on reducing concentrated carbohydrates, and regular physical activity as tolerated.

Obtain renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to guide medication selection and assess diabetic kidney risk. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response.

Assess the reported numbness and tingling at follow-up with a focused neurologic and foot examination; reinforce daily foot inspection and prompt reporting of wounds or skin changes.

Continue monitoring mood symptoms and review current depression treatment and adherence as part of medication reconciliation.

Follow up soon for review of the complete medication list, treatment tolerance, and glucose control.
