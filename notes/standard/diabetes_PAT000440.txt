Chief Complaint

Medication review for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Alan Wheeler is a 75-year-old man seen for outpatient review of diabetes management. He has a 7-year history of type 2 diabetes and is not currently described as actively managed. Recent HbA1c is 9.6% and fasting glucose is 214 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimal glycemic control.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, and recurrent infections. He reports numbness in the hands or feet without associated tingling. Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance were reviewed; a specific current diabetes medication regimen is not provided, so treatment decisions should be based on confirmation of what he is actually taking and how consistently.

Relevant history includes obesity for approximately 15 years and chronic kidney disease for approximately 28 years. He does not smoke.

Objective

Vital Signs: BP 141/80 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respirations 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 171 cm. Weight 102.3 kg. BMI 35.0 kg/m².

Laboratory Results: HbA1c 9.6%. Fasting glucose 214 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without complications, currently poorly controlled. HbA1c and fasting glucose are above goal. He is largely asymptomatic despite significant hyperglycemia. Reported distal numbness may represent early peripheral neuropathic symptoms, although no objective neurologic examination findings are documented today.

2. Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Renal function and albuminuria are important considerations when selecting or adjusting diabetes therapy. No formal stage is assigned based on the information available.

3. Obesity, BMI 35.0 kg/m², longstanding. This likely contributes to insulin resistance and difficulty achieving glycemic control.

4. Borderline elevated blood pressure at today’s visit. This is relevant to overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Confirm the complete medication list, including any diabetes agents, dosing, missed doses, affordability or access issues, and adverse effects before making changes. Reinforce consistent medication use if treatment is prescribed and tolerated. Given the HbA1c of 9.6%, treatment intensification is indicated after medication reconciliation and review of renal function.

Recommend serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to guide medication selection and renal risk assessment. Recommend fasting lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk management.

Discuss reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, and gradual activity appropriate for his age and functional status. Encourage weight reduction where feasible.

Review daily foot care, avoidance of walking barefoot, and prompt evaluation of wounds or infections. Perform a focused foot and sensory examination at follow-up, with ophthalmologic evaluation recommended for diabetic retinal screening. Counsel regarding symptoms of marked hyperglycemia and the need to seek care for vomiting, dehydration, confusion, or rapidly worsening symptoms.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and renal risk factors. Follow up after medication reconciliation and updated renal assessment to establish an individualized diabetes regimen and reassess glycemic control.
