Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Arthur Nichols is a 31-year-old man presenting for follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 5 years ago. He is not currently receiving active treatment for blood pressure. He reports no headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. History is notable for coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. He does not smoke.

Objective

Blood pressure: 156/88 mmHg
Pulse: 79 bpm
Respiratory rate: 18/min
Temperature: 36.9°C
Oxygen saturation: 99%
Height: 170 cm
Weight: 71.1 kg
BMI: 24.6 kg/m²

Serum electrolytes:
Sodium 136 mmol/L
Potassium 4.7 mmol/L

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and currently without active treatment. Patient is asymptomatic today. Electrolytes are within expected physiologic range.

Coronary artery disease, reported duration approximately 1 year, without current anginal or heart failure symptoms.

Plan

Discussed the importance of consistent blood pressure management, particularly in the setting of coronary artery disease. Recommend confirming control with appropriately measured home blood pressure readings and bringing a log to follow-up.

Recommend initiation of antihypertensive therapy after reviewing the coronary artery disease history, prior treatment history, and shared treatment preferences. Reinforced a low-sodium diet, regular physical activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol.

Recommend baseline cardiovascular risk assessment, including ECG and lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of hypertension evaluation. Continue monitoring renal function and electrolytes when pharmacologic therapy is initiated or adjusted.

Reviewed urgent precautions for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning.
