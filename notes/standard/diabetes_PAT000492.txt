Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Caroline Rivera is a 52-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 14 years ago. Current glycemic control is poor based on recent hemoglobin A1c of 9.8% and fasting glucose of 236 mg/dL. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Her comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 3 years’ duration, dyslipidemia for 10 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 2 years. She currently smokes. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 143/82 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 179 cm; weight 83.6 kg; BMI 26.1 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Hemoglobin A1c 9.8%. Fasting glucose 236 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings provided.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently markedly above glycemic targets after approximately 14 years of disease. Although she denies classic hyperglycemic symptoms and neuropathic or infectious symptoms, the elevated A1c and fasting glucose indicate significant chronic hyperglycemia. Chronic kidney disease is an important comorbidity that will influence medication selection and glycemic management.

Dyslipidemia.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Elevated blood pressure reading in the setting of diabetes and chronic kidney disease.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated A1c and fasting glucose and the increased long-term risk of microvascular and macrovascular complications. Recommend prompt initiation or re-establishment of an individualized pharmacologic diabetes treatment plan rather than lifestyle measures alone. Metformin may be considered if renal function permits; medication selection and dosing should be guided by current creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate, with consideration of agents that provide renal and cardiovascular benefit when clinically appropriate.

Recommend obtaining updated serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess renal risk and guide therapy. Continue periodic hemoglobin A1c monitoring to assess response. Encourage home glucose monitoring as clinically appropriate.

Counseled regarding reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, increased dietary fiber, regular aerobic and resistance activity as tolerated, and maintenance of a healthy weight. Current BMI is 26.1 kg/m².

Recommend fasting lipid assessment and review of cardiovascular risk management, given longstanding diabetes and dyslipidemia. Blood pressure should be reassessed at follow-up and managed in the context of diabetes and chronic kidney disease.

Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot examination and dilated retinal examination, with attention to neuropathy, skin integrity, pulses, and retinal complications. She currently reports no foot sensory symptoms, wounds, or visual symptoms.

Smoking cessation was strongly advised; offer structured cessation support and pharmacotherapy if acceptable.

Follow up after updated renal and metabolic assessment to finalize diabetes treatment and review glycemic response.
