Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Shannon Douglas is a 54-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 13 years ago. She is not currently receiving documented active diabetes management. Recent laboratory testing shows HbA1c 9.7% and fasting glucose 236 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimal glycemic control.

She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. She does not smoke. Her medical history is also notable for depression, present for approximately 34 years.

Objective

Height 174 cm; weight 72.4 kg; BMI 23.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 120/79 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.7%; fasting glucose 236 mg/dL.

No diabetes-related physical examination abnormalities are documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, poorly controlled based on HbA1c of 9.7% and fasting glucose of 236 mg/dL. She currently reports no osmotic, constitutional, infectious, visual, or peripheral neuropathic symptoms. Despite a normal BMI, glycemic control is significantly above goal and warrants prompt treatment and closer follow-up.

Depression, longstanding, approximately 34 years. No current mood symptoms or treatment details provided today.

Plan

Discussed the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the increased risk of microvascular and macrovascular complications with persistent hyperglycemia. Recommend initiation of evidence-based diabetes therapy, with metformin considered if no contraindication after review of renal function and other relevant factors. Medication selection and dosing should be finalized after medication reconciliation and shared decision-making.

Recommend nutrition counseling focused on consistent carbohydrate intake, limiting sugar-sweetened foods and beverages, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage home glucose monitoring if medication therapy is initiated, with review of readings at follow-up.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes preventive care, including a comprehensive foot examination and retinal evaluation. These should be coordinated as part of ongoing diabetes management.

Reinforced return precautions for marked hyperglycemia, vomiting, dehydration, confusion, or new visual or neurologic symptoms. Follow up after treatment initiation and review of glucose data, preferably within 4–6 weeks, with repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval to assess response.

Continue to monitor longstanding depression and address mood symptoms or treatment needs at subsequent visits.
