Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 12 years, hyperlipidemia for 29 years, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 5 years. He does not smoke.

Height 165 cm, weight 77.9 kg, BMI 28.6 kg/m².
Blood pressure 129/86 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99%.
Serum creatinine 3.57 mg/dL, estimated GFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 64 mg/dL.

Assessment:
Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function in the setting of longstanding diabetes and associated secondary hyperparathyroidism. Current renal indices warrant prompt specialist evaluation despite the absence of uremic or volume-overload symptoms. Blood pressure is currently acceptable. No clinical symptoms suggesting acute fluid overload are reported.

Plan:
Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management and renal replacement therapy education/planning as clinically appropriate.
Obtain/review serum electrolytes, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as part of disease characterization and complication assessment.
Perform medication and supplement reconciliation at follow-up; avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potential nephrotoxins unless specifically directed.
Continue attention to blood pressure and diabetes control, with renal-appropriate treatment decisions coordinated with nephrology.
Seek emergency care for new shortness of breath, progressive swelling, markedly reduced urine output, persistent vomiting, confusion, or chest pain.
