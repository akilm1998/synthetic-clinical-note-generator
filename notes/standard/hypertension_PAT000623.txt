Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Hunter Ford is a 92-year-old male with a 30-year history of hypertension, currently not under active management, presenting for focused follow-up after an elevated office blood pressure reading. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. No acute hypertensive symptoms reported. Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 24 years and chronic kidney disease for 11 years. He currently smokes. Medication use and allergies were not provided.

Objective

Blood pressure 170/93 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%. Height 190 cm, weight 101.8 kg, BMI 28.2 kg/m². Serum sodium 140 mmol/L and potassium 4.3 mmol/L. No acute distress or abnormal physical examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Longstanding essential hypertension with significantly elevated blood pressure and no reported symptoms suggestive of acute end-organ involvement. Hypertension has not been under active management. Risk is increased by advanced age, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, overweight status, and ongoing tobacco use. Current electrolyte values are within expected ranges.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure after a period of seated rest and confirm with appropriate technique; encourage home blood pressure monitoring if feasible. Arrange prompt outpatient reassessment to establish a safe antihypertensive treatment plan, taking age, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease into account. Review current medications, adherence, dietary sodium intake, and tobacco use at the next visit. Recommend limiting sodium, avoiding excess alcohol, and smoking cessation.

Given the markedly elevated reading, seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, vision changes, confusion, or recurrent nosebleed. Follow up promptly for blood pressure reassessment and ongoing management.
