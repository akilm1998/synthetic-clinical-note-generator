Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly reduced kidney function and fatigue.

Subjective

Scarlett Hughes is a 77-year-old woman, 163 cm and 78.1 kg (BMI 29.4 kg/m²), returning for urgent outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 6 years. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. No acute cardiopulmonary or uremic symptoms are reported today.

Relevant history includes type 2 diabetes mellitus for 40 years, anemia of chronic disease for 7 years, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 31 years. She currently smokes. No medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 141/87 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respirations 20/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Weight 78.1 kg; height 163 cm; BMI 29.4 kg/m².

Laboratory results: serum creatinine 5.83 mg/dL, estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 64 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly impaired renal function, currently not under active documented management. The degree of renal dysfunction places her at significant risk for electrolyte abnormalities, volume overload, uremic complications, and progression requiring renal replacement planning. She has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, oliguria-related symptoms, or dyspnea. Diabetes of 40 years’ duration and hypertension contribute important risk factors. Blood pressure is mildly elevated today.

Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding, with fatigue possibly related; no current blood count is available for assessment.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, longstanding, in the setting of advanced chronic kidney disease.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Recommend urgent nephrology evaluation, ideally promptly, for comprehensive assessment of advanced chronic kidney disease and discussion of renal replacement planning and conservative management options as appropriate.

Obtain prompt serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, CBC, intact parathyroid hormone, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate. Review prior renal function and diabetes and blood pressure control records if available.

Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications; all medications should be reviewed for renal dosing. Avoid unmonitored over-the-counter supplements and dehydration. Dietary counseling should address sodium and kidney-appropriate protein, potassium, and phosphorus intake after laboratory review.

Monitor blood pressure, daily weight, urine output, edema, dyspnea, nausea, confusion, pruritus, and worsening fatigue. Proceed to the emergency department for shortness of breath, new swelling, markedly reduced urine output, persistent vomiting, chest pain, confusion, or acute worsening.

Continue close follow-up after nephrology and laboratory evaluation.
