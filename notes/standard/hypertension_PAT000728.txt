Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Ashton Murray is a 37-year-old man with a 13-year history of hypertension and chronic kidney disease for approximately 1 year, presenting for routine outpatient follow-up. He is not currently under active treatment for his blood pressure. He reports intermittent palpitations and fatigue. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. No acute hypertensive symptoms reported.

He does not smoke. Weight is stable at 64.2 kg with BMI 22.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 147/98 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 170 cm; weight 64.2 kg; BMI 22.2 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 139 mmol/L, potassium 4.9 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal, without reported symptoms or findings suggestive of acute end-organ injury. He has not been receiving active antihypertensive management. Chronic kidney disease increases the importance of consistent blood pressure control. Potassium is at the upper end of the provided reference context and should be considered when selecting therapy.

Palpitations with associated fatigue. Pulse is regular by recorded vital signs at 72 bpm, but intermittent symptoms warrant outpatient evaluation.

Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 1 year. Current electrolyte values are stable as provided, though additional renal assessment is appropriate for treatment planning.

Plan

Discussed the importance of initiating active blood pressure management, home blood pressure monitoring, reduced dietary sodium intake, regular physical activity, and limiting alcohol consumption. He should record seated home readings and bring the log to the next visit.

Recommend confirming renal function and reassessing potassium before selecting and titrating antihypertensive therapy. An ACE inhibitor or ARB may be appropriate in the setting of chronic kidney disease if renal function and potassium permit; monitor electrolytes and renal function after initiation or dose changes. A calcium-channel blocker or thiazide-type agent may be considered based on the complete renal profile and response.

Recommend urinalysis and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular and kidney risk assessment. Recommend a 12-lead ECG for evaluation of palpitations and consideration of further rhythm monitoring if symptoms persist or become more frequent.

Reviewed urgent precautions, including severe or persistent headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Follow up after home blood pressure readings and recommended testing to update treatment.
