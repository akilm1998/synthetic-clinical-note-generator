Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Joshua Ward is an 85-year-old man seen for follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 13 years and not currently under active treatment. He denies chest discomfort, dizziness, or exertional leg pain/claudication. He reports fatty skin deposits consistent with xanthomas; no xanthelasma is reported.

He continues to smoke. Comorbidities include coronary artery disease for approximately 7 years, obesity for 16 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 23 years. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 134/76 mmHg, pulse 85 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Height 176 cm, weight 99.1 kg, BMI 32.0 kg/m².

Recent lipid panel: total cholesterol 282 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 194 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 39 mg/dL, triglycerides 243 mg/dL.

Xanthomas reported. No other examination findings provided.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with marked elevation of LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, untreated over a prolonged period. The low HDL and ongoing tobacco use further increase cardiovascular risk. Known coronary artery disease makes aggressive risk-factor modification particularly important. No current anginal or claudication symptoms reported.

Obesity, BMI 32.0 kg/m², contributing to cardiometabolic risk and likely worsening dyslipidemia and fatty liver disease.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding. Lipid-lowering therapy is not contraindicated solely on the basis of this diagnosis, but liver status should be considered when initiating treatment.

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Plan

Discussed the substantially elevated LDL cholesterol of 194 mg/dL and the importance of lipid lowering in the setting of established coronary artery disease. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of a statin, with consideration of a moderate- to high-intensity regimen appropriate for his age, comorbidities, functional status, and tolerance. Review for prior statin intolerance, potential drug interactions, and patient preferences before prescribing.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider baseline hepatic and metabolic safety testing as clinically appropriate; no additional laboratory results are available today. If LDL remains above goal despite maximally tolerated statin therapy, addition of ezetimibe may be considered.

Counseled on a heart-healthy diet emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, avoidance of excess refined carbohydrates, weight reduction, and regular activity as tolerated. Given triglycerides of 243 mg/dL, focus initially on dietary modification, weight management, and LDL-directed therapy.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered cessation support. Reviewed the need to seek urgent care for new or worsening chest discomfort, dyspnea, syncope, or exertional leg symptoms.

Continue management of coronary artery disease, obesity, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease with coordinated cardiovascular risk reduction. Follow up after lipid reassessment to review response and finalize long-term therapy.
