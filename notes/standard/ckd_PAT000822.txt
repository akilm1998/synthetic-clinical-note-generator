Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for chronic kidney disease.

History of Present Illness

Robert Collins is a 70-year-old man, 190 cm tall and weighing 93.5 kg, with a BMI of 25.9 kg/m², presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of chronic kidney disease known for approximately 10 years. Current laboratory data show serum creatinine 4.31 mg/dL, estimated GFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 62 mg/dL. He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, shortness of breath, or changes in urination frequency. There are no reported current symptoms suggestive of volume overload or uremic prodrome.

His history is also notable for coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 16 years ago, secondary hyperparathyroidism for 22 years, and hyperlipidemia for 6 years. Smoking status is documented as never smoker. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.

Cardiovascular: Denies peripheral edema.

Gastrointestinal: Denies nausea.

Genitourinary: Denies changes in urination frequency.

Skin: Denies pruritus.

Musculoskeletal: Denies muscle cramps.

Neurologic/Sleep: Denies sleep disturbance.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 134/88 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Anthropometrics: Height 190 cm, weight 93.5 kg, BMI 25.9 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Serum creatinine 4.31 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m². BUN 62 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function on current laboratory testing, present for approximately 10 years. Although Mr. Collins is presently asymptomatic and has no reported edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, or urinary frequency changes, the creatinine, BUN, and estimated GFR indicate advanced renal impairment requiring prompt specialist assessment and close monitoring. His blood pressure is reasonably controlled at today’s visit. Relevant associated conditions include longstanding secondary hyperparathyroidism, coronary artery disease, and hyperlipidemia.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for comprehensive evaluation, ongoing renal care, assessment of complications, and discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate. Recommend serum electrolyte panel, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, CBC, intact parathyroid hormone, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio as part of the renal evaluation. Renal ultrasound may also be considered to assess kidney structure and chronicity.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications at follow-up, with particular attention to renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents such as NSAIDs unless specifically directed by a treating clinician. Continue attention to blood pressure and cardiovascular risk reduction. Dietary counseling should address renal-appropriate sodium, protein, potassium, and phosphorus intake based on forthcoming laboratory results and nephrology guidance.

He was advised to seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, chest pain, or substantial change in urination. Follow up in primary care after nephrology evaluation or sooner if symptoms develop.
