Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Scott Douglas is a 31-year-old man with a 7-year history of chronic kidney disease presenting for routine outpatient follow-up. He is not currently under active disease management. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. He has a 1-year history of gout. He does not smoke. Weight is 56.5 kg at 161 cm, with BMI 21.8 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 127/85 mmHg, pulse 73/min, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Recent laboratory results show serum creatinine 4.17 mg/dL, BUN 76 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m². No edema or other fluid-overload symptoms are reported.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated nitrogenous waste products. Despite the significant laboratory abnormalities, he currently denies uremic or fluid-overload symptoms. Blood pressure is acceptable at today’s visit. Given the longstanding disease, current renal function, and lack of active management, he requires prompt nephrology involvement and structured renal monitoring.

Gout, present for approximately 1 year, requiring treatment planning that accounts for substantially impaired kidney function.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation and management of advanced chronic kidney disease, including discussion of renal replacement planning if clinically indicated. Obtain repeat renal function testing and a serum electrolytes panel, along with urinalysis and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to further characterize kidney disease. Recommend CBC and intact parathyroid hormone testing as part of chronic kidney disease surveillance, and renal ultrasound if not previously performed.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications with a clinician before use. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Gout treatment should be coordinated with nephrology or an appropriately experienced clinician, with renal dosing of any medication.

Counseled regarding moderation of dietary sodium and avoidance of dehydration. Monitor blood pressure and urine output. Seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, persistent nausea or vomiting, marked reduction in urination, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.

Follow up promptly after nephrology evaluation and repeat laboratory testing.
