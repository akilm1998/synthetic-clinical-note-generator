Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Simon Bishop is an 87-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia present for approximately 27 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. He denies chest discomfort, exertional calf pain or other intermittent claudication symptoms, and dizziness. He has noticed fatty deposits involving the skin and eyelids, consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma.

His medical history is also significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 35 years and obesity for approximately 20 years. He does not smoke. Current weight is 96.4 kg at a height of 172 cm, with BMI 32.6 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 129/80 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Laboratory results reviewed: total cholesterol 299 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 216 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 35 mg/dL, and triglycerides 239 mg/dL.

Reported examination findings include xanthomas and xanthelasma. No chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, elevated triglycerides, and low HDL cholesterol, consistent with a mixed dyslipidemic pattern. The degree and longstanding nature of LDL elevation, together with xanthomas and xanthelasma, indicate substantial cumulative atherosclerotic risk, particularly in the setting of advanced age and longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus. He is currently without reported ischemic or claudication symptoms.

Obesity, BMI 32.6 kg/m², contributing to overall cardiometabolic risk.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus of 35 years’ duration, an important cardiovascular risk factor.

Plan

Discussed the significant elevation in LDL cholesterol and the associated cardiovascular risk. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of lipid-lowering therapy, taking into account his age, overall health, functional status, preferences, and goals of care. A moderate- to high-intensity statin would ordinarily be considered if clinically appropriate and acceptable to the patient; nonstatin therapy may be considered if LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated.

Recommend repeating a fasting lipid panel to confirm the current pattern and assess response after any treatment is initiated. Consider evaluation for contributing secondary factors and measurement of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a) if results would help refine risk assessment or management. Medication reconciliation and review of potential contraindications or tolerability concerns should precede treatment.

Counseled on reducing saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates and excess dietary calories, emphasizing vegetables, whole grains, legumes, and lean protein, and pursuing physical activity appropriate to his mobility and safety. Weight reduction would be beneficial. Continue routine diabetes and cardiovascular risk-factor management. Follow up after discussion of treatment preferences and review of repeat lipid measurements.
