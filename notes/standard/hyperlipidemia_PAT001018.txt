Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of longstanding elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Rebecca Burke is a 65-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has an approximately 18-year history of hyperlipidemia that is currently not under active management. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She continues to smoke. No medication regimen is provided for review.

She has a history of hypertension for 11 years. No acute cardiovascular or neurologic symptoms are reported today.

Objective

Height 154 cm; weight 48.9 kg; BMI 20.6 kg/m². Blood pressure 132/91 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Available lipid results: total cholesterol 362 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 256 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 50 mg/dL, and triglycerides 280 mg/dL.

Assessment

Longstanding hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol and moderately elevated triglycerides. The degree and duration of elevation confer significant cardiovascular risk despite the absence of reported chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness. No xanthomas or xanthelasma are reported.

Hypertension by history, with diastolic blood pressure elevated at today’s visit.

Active tobacco use, an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

Plan

Discussed the lipid results and the importance of treatment to reduce long-term cardiovascular risk. Recommend initiation of guideline-directed lipid-lowering therapy, typically a high-intensity statin unless contraindicated or not tolerated. Review medication history, allergies, prior statin exposure, and patient preferences before prescribing. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, consider addition of non-statin therapy such as ezetimibe and assess need for specialist input.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and additional risk stratification with ApoB and lipoprotein(a) testing when clinically appropriate. Coronary artery calcium scanning may be considered if treatment decisions remain uncertain, though the markedly elevated LDL level warrants active management regardless.

Counseled on a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, fish, and unsaturated fats while limiting saturated and trans fats, processed foods, and excess refined carbohydrates. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated and maintenance of current healthy body weight.

Strongly advised smoking cessation. Discussed behavioral support and evidence-based cessation medications; patient should be offered a cessation plan at follow-up.

Continue monitoring blood pressure, with attention to the persistently elevated diastolic reading. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring and review of readings at follow-up; treatment should be reconciled with her current regimen once available.

Reviewed preventive care appropriate for age, including routine cancer screening, immunizations, tobacco-related health counseling, and periodic assessment of cardiovascular risk. Return for lipid and blood pressure follow-up after treatment planning, or sooner for chest discomfort, new exertional leg pain, dizziness, or other concerning symptoms.
