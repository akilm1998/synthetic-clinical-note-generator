Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Tristan Burke is a 78-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 24-year history of hypertension that is currently not under active management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, and nosebleeds. He reports fatigue. No acute hypertensive symptoms are reported today.

Past medical history is significant for hyperlipidemia for 17 years and chronic kidney disease for 16 years. He currently smokes. Height is 171 cm, weight 67.0 kg, BMI 22.9 kg/m². No current antihypertensive regimen is provided.

Objective

Blood pressure 177/100 mmHg. Pulse 77 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 96%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 5.0 mmol/L.

Assessment

Longstanding essential hypertension with markedly elevated blood pressure today, without reported symptoms suggestive of acute end-organ injury. Hypertension has not been actively managed and requires prompt reassessment and treatment planning.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Potassium is at the upper end of the reported range, which is clinically relevant when selecting or adjusting antihypertensive therapy.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Fatigue, nonspecific.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure promptly using appropriate technique, including confirmation with repeat readings and consideration of home blood pressure monitoring. Given the degree of elevation and lack of current management, arrange timely outpatient follow-up to initiate an evidence-based antihypertensive regimen and establish a specific blood pressure target appropriate for his age and kidney disease.

Review renal function and repeat serum electrolytes before and after starting or adjusting therapy. Use caution with agents that may increase potassium, and avoid empiric medication changes without accounting for his chronic kidney disease and potassium of 5.0 mmol/L.

Recommend ECG, urinalysis, and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular and hypertensive assessment. Review kidney disease monitoring and consider renal-focused follow-up if not already established.

Counseled regarding reduced sodium intake, regular activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and maintenance of a healthy weight. Strongly advised smoking cessation; offer cessation counseling and pharmacologic support at follow-up.

Discussed emergency precautions: seek immediate medical attention for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, visual changes, confusion, or other acute deterioration.

Continue comprehensive preventive care appropriate for age, including review of immunizations, cancer screening status, fall risk, mood, cognition, and advance care planning at follow-up. Schedule close follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment initiation.
