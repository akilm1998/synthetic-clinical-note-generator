Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Nicholas Powell is a 94-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 13 years. He reports no current fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, or noticeable pallor. He does not smoke. His relevant comorbidity is a chronic infection of approximately 1 year’s duration. No anemia-directed management has been established to date.

Objective

Height 187 cm, weight 81.8 kg, BMI 23.4 kg/m².

Blood pressure 132/78 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Recent laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.4 g/dL
Hematocrit 24.9%
RBC count 3.0 million/µL
MCV 83 fL
Serum iron 32 µg/dL
Ferritin 441 ng/mL
TIBC 207 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 15.5%

Assessment

Chronic anemia, currently asymptomatic by history. The anemia is normocytic with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and reduced TIBC, a pattern most consistent with anemia associated with chronic inflammation. His chronic infection may be contributing. Hemoglobin is significantly reduced despite the absence of reported cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms. No evidence of acute hemodynamic instability based on today’s vital signs.

Plan

Obtain updated CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to assess persistence and marrow response. Recommend renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers as part of the anemia evaluation. Continue evaluation and management of the underlying chronic infection, as ongoing inflammation may be contributing to impaired iron utilization.

Review the need for anemia-directed therapy after the above evaluation. Iron supplementation should not be initiated solely on the basis of the low serum iron given the elevated ferritin and low TIBC; consider oral or intravenous iron only if further assessment supports functional or absolute iron deficiency. Consider hematology consultation if the anemia persists, worsens, or remains unexplained.

Discussed monitoring for new fatigue, weakness, dizziness, exertional dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, syncope, bleeding, or other acute changes. Seek urgent evaluation for significant shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, or active bleeding. Follow up after repeat laboratory assessment or sooner if symptoms develop.
