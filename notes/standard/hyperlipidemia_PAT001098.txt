Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Joseph Bailey is a 45-year-old male presenting for outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 2 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 15 years, chronic kidney disease for 4 years, and obesity for approximately 3 years. He currently smokes. No medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 132/86 mmHg, pulse 88 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 176 cm; weight 117.4 kg; BMI 37.9 kg/m².

Recent lipid panel:
Total cholesterol 348 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 255 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 38 mg/dL
Triglycerides 274 mg/dL

No lipid-related physical findings are provided. Patient is currently asymptomatic from a cardiovascular standpoint.

Assessment

1. Hyperlipidemia, unspecified

Markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with elevated triglycerides and low HDL cholesterol. The LDL level is substantially elevated and, in the setting of long-standing diabetes, chronic kidney disease, obesity, and active tobacco use, represents a significant cardiovascular risk. Hyperlipidemia has been without active management for approximately 2 years. No current symptoms suggestive of angina or intermittent claudication.

2. Type 2 diabetes mellitus

Long-standing disease of 15 years’ duration. Diabetes increases overall atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk and is an important consideration in lipid-lowering treatment.

3. Chronic kidney disease

Present for 4 years. Kidney disease further increases cardiovascular risk and should be considered when selecting and monitoring therapy.

4. Obesity

BMI 37.9 kg/m², with obesity present for approximately 3 years.

5. Tobacco use

Currently smoking, contributing substantially to cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the severity of the lipid abnormalities and the importance of active treatment. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy unless contraindicated, with medication selection and dosing reviewed in the context of diabetes and chronic kidney disease. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, consider addition of non-statin therapy such as ezetimibe.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and measurement of additional cardiovascular risk markers, including apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a), if clinically useful.

Counseled on a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, whole grains, legumes, lean proteins, and unsaturated fats while minimizing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and processed foods. Encouraged gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated and weight reduction.

Strongly advised smoking cessation; reviewed that stopping tobacco is a major modifiable component of cardiovascular risk reduction.

Continue coordination of diabetes and chronic kidney disease care, with treatment decisions and monitoring tailored to renal function. Follow up after repeat lipid testing to review response, tolerance, and need for additional therapy.
