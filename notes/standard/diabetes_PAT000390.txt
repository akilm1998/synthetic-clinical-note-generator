Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Richard Lawson is a 46-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 6 years ago. He is not currently engaged in active diabetes management. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has a history of obesity for 6 years, dyslipidemia for 3 years, and obstructive sleep apnea for 10 years. He does not smoke.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 121/75 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 182 cm. Weight 114.3 kg. BMI 34.5 kg/m².

Recent laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.2%. Fasting glucose 192 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently poorly controlled based on elevated hemoglobin A1c and fasting glucose. He is asymptomatic today, without reported hyperglycemic symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, or evidence of slow-healing wounds or recurrent infection.

Obesity with BMI 34.5 kg/m².

Dyslipidemia, present for approximately 3 years.

Obstructive sleep apnea, present for approximately 10 years.

Plan

Discussed that current glycemic values are above goal and that active treatment is needed to reduce the risk of future microvascular and cardiovascular complications. Reviewed nutrition, portion control, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, and gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated.

Recommended initiation or re-engagement with appropriate pharmacologic diabetes management; medication options and risks should be reviewed based on renal function, cardiovascular risk, cost, and patient preference. Encourage home glucose monitoring if a monitoring plan is established.

Recommend assessment of diabetes-related risk, including urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine/eGFR, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive foot examination, and retinal screening. Continue monitoring for polyuria, polydipsia, visual changes, neuropathy, skin breakdown, or other symptoms of hyperglycemia.

Address weight reduction as part of diabetes management. Continue routine management of dyslipidemia and obstructive sleep apnea. Follow up after treatment is initiated or adjusted to reassess home glucose readings, adherence, tolerance, and glycemic control.
