Subjective

Ashton Murray is a 79-year-old male presenting for routine follow-up of anemia diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. He reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or other reported symptoms related to anemia. He has no reported pallor. His anemia has not been actively managed to date. Relevant chronic conditions include inflammatory bowel disease for approximately 54 months and malignancy for approximately 36 months. He continues to smoke.

Objective

Height 169 cm, weight 71.4 kg, BMI 25.0 kg/m². Blood pressure 135/81 mmHg, pulse 92 bpm, respiratory rate 23/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin 9.5 g/dL, hematocrit 28.7%, RBC count 3.54 million/µL, and MCV 81 fL. Serum iron is 50 µg/dL, ferritin 381 ng/mL, TIBC 257 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 19.5%. No pallor is reported. Cardiovascular and respiratory findings are not suggestive of acute decompensation; oxygenation is preserved.

Assessment

Anemia associated with chronic disease/inflammation, present for approximately 4 years and currently without reported symptoms. The laboratory pattern of reduced hemoglobin and hematocrit with a low-normal MCV, low serum iron, elevated ferritin, reduced TIBC, and transferrin saturation of 19.5% is most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation, in the setting of inflammatory bowel disease and malignancy. He is hemodynamically stable, although his pulse and respiratory rate are mildly elevated. No symptoms currently suggest acute blood loss or cardiopulmonary compromise.

Plan

Discussed the laboratory findings and the likely contribution of chronic inflammatory disease and malignancy. Recommend reassessment with a complete blood count, reticulocyte count, peripheral blood smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the anemia and assess for additional reversible contributors.

Recommend coordination with the clinicians managing his inflammatory bowel disease and malignancy to optimize control of the underlying inflammatory burden. Review for occult gastrointestinal blood loss should be considered in the context of his inflammatory bowel disease and overall clinical history.

Iron supplementation should not be initiated empirically based solely on the current ferritin level; iron status and the clinical context should be reviewed before considering oral or intravenous iron. If anemia persists or worsens despite management of underlying disease, consideration may be given to hematology evaluation and anemia-directed therapy, including an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent when clinically appropriate.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, which may improve overall cardiopulmonary health and treatment tolerance. Advised to seek urgent care for new shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, sustained palpitations, marked weakness, or evidence of bleeding. Continue routine outpatient follow-up after repeat evaluation.
