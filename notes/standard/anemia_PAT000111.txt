Chief Complaint

Initial evaluation of anemia.

Subjective

Lily Peterson is an 80-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient consultation regarding anemia identified approximately 1 year ago. She denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor is present. She does not smoke.

Relevant chronic conditions include chronic kidney disease for 19 years, inflammatory bowel disease for 11 years, and heart failure for 2 years. No current anemia-directed therapy is documented.

Objective

Height 155 cm; weight 67.3 kg; BMI 28.0 kg/m².

Blood pressure 141/79 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respiratory rate 24/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Physical examination is notable for pallor. Cardiopulmonary examination is otherwise not described as abnormal.

Laboratory results: hemoglobin 9.2 g/dL, hematocrit 27.5%, RBC count 2.93 million/µL, MCV 94 fL, serum iron 30 µg/dL, ferritin 409 ng/mL, TIBC 252 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 11.9%.

Assessment

Anemia with a normocytic pattern and reduced RBC count. The combination of low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC is most consistent with iron-restricted erythropoiesis in the setting of chronic inflammation, with chronic kidney disease likely contributing through impaired erythropoietin production. Her longstanding inflammatory bowel disease and chronic kidney disease are important contributors; heart failure may further complicate management. The current hemoglobin is clinically significant, although she is hemodynamically stable and denies cardiopulmonary symptoms. Pallor is present.

A component of absolute iron deficiency cannot be excluded, particularly in the setting of inflammatory bowel disease, despite the elevated ferritin.

Plan

Obtain a reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to further characterize marrow response and red cell morphology. Check vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers as part of the anemia evaluation.

Review inflammatory bowel disease activity and assess for gastrointestinal blood loss or impaired iron absorption as clinically indicated. Coordinate management with the clinicians overseeing her kidney disease and inflammatory bowel disease.

Given transferrin saturation of 11.9%, assess candidacy for iron replacement after reviewing renal function, inflammatory disease activity, and gastrointestinal tolerance. If anemia persists after reversible contributors are addressed, consideration may be given to specialist-directed erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy in the context of chronic kidney disease; no such therapy is currently documented.

Monitor CBC and iron indices during evaluation. She should seek urgent care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, sustained palpitations, or marked weakness.
