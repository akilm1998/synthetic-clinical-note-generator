Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly abnormal cholesterol levels.

Subjective

Colin Graham is a 37-year-old male seen for follow-up of hyperlipidemia present for approximately 7 years and currently untreated. Recent lipid results show significant elevation. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. He smokes tobacco. Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 2 years and chronic kidney disease for 1 year. No current lipid-lowering medication is documented.

Objective

Height 164 cm; weight 61.3 kg; BMI 22.8 kg/m².
Blood pressure 138/86 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Total cholesterol 362 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 262 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 31 mg/dL
Triglycerides 343 mg/dL

Assessment

Severe untreated hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, low HDL cholesterol, and elevated triglycerides. His cardiovascular risk is increased by active tobacco use, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, and borderline elevated blood pressure. He currently reports no ischemic or claudication symptoms.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the need for prompt risk-factor reduction. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after shared decision-making, with medication selection and dosing reviewed today. Consider addition of ezetimibe if LDL remains substantially elevated despite adherence and tolerated statin therapy.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation to assess response, along with evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement given the degree of LDL elevation and young age. Review whether coronary artery calcium imaging would meaningfully assist risk discussion if treatment decisions remain uncertain.

Counseled on smoking cessation, Mediterranean-style diet, avoidance of trans fats and excess saturated fats, regular physical activity as tolerated, and continued diabetes and kidney disease management. Advised urgent evaluation for new chest discomfort, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain. Close outpatient follow-up recommended to review treatment tolerance and lipid response.
