Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, present for approximately 25 years. Current creatinine is 3.79 mg/dL, BUN 76 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m². She is presently without reported fatigue, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, urinary frequency change, or dyspnea. The prolonged duration and degree of renal impairment warrant structured nephrology assessment and clarification of goals of care.

2. Secondary hyperparathyroidism associated with chronic kidney disease, documented duration 36.

3. Anemia of chronic disease, documented duration 32. No current hemoglobin value is available for review.

4. Gout, documented duration 56.

5. Elevated blood pressure at today’s visit, 146/86 mmHg. No antihypertensive regimen is provided.

Plan

Obtain updated renal evaluation including serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, CBC, intact parathyroid hormone, urinalysis, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. These studies will help assess complications of advanced chronic kidney disease and guide management of anemia and secondary hyperparathyroidism.

Recommend renal ultrasound to assess kidney structure and exclude obstructive or other structural contributors.

Arrange nephrology consultation for comprehensive management, medication review, renal-protective strategies, and discussion of conservative kidney care versus renal replacement planning in the context of her age, long-standing disease, current kidney function, comorbidities, and absence of active uremic symptoms.

Perform complete medication reconciliation before initiating or adjusting therapy. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Review all over-the-counter medications and supplements for renal dosing and safety.

Monitor blood pressure and reassess the need for treatment based on serial readings, overall health status, and renal goals of care.

Continue clinical monitoring for nausea, worsening fatigue, confusion, pruritus, muscle cramps, reduced urine output, edema, or shortness of breath. Seek urgent evaluation for acute symptom development or significant decline in urine output.
