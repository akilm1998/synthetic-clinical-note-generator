Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Sydney Fuller is a 41-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 13 years and currently not under active treatment. Recent lipid profile shows markedly elevated cholesterol levels. She denies chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness. She reports fatty skin deposits and fatty deposits around the eyelids consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma.

She has a history of coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She currently smokes. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

Objective

Height 153 cm; weight 52.9 kg; BMI 22.6 kg/m².

Blood pressure 118/77 mmHg, pulse 81/min, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Lipid profile:
Total cholesterol 349 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 261 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 42 mg/dL
Triglycerides 230 mg/dL

Assessment

1. Hyperlipidemia, unspecified

Severe, longstanding dyslipidemia with LDL cholesterol 261 mg/dL, elevated triglycerides, and suboptimal HDL cholesterol. The presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma, together with the degree and duration of LDL elevation, raises concern for a significant underlying lipid disorder. Given her established coronary artery disease, she is at particularly high cardiovascular risk despite a normal BMI and currently normal blood pressure. Hyperlipidemia is not presently being actively managed.

2. Coronary artery disease

History of coronary artery disease for approximately 1 year. She denies current chest discomfort, dizziness, or exertional leg symptoms. The markedly elevated LDL requires aggressive risk reduction and secondary prevention.

3. Tobacco use

Current smoking is an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor and substantially increases her risk in the setting of coronary artery disease and severe hyperlipidemia.

Plan

Recommend initiation of high-intensity lipid-lowering therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after discussion of risks, benefits, contraindications, pregnancy considerations, and patient preference. Given the markedly elevated LDL level and coronary artery disease, treatment should not be delayed. If LDL remains substantially above goal despite maximally tolerated statin therapy, addition of ezetimibe and consideration of further lipid-lowering therapy would be appropriate.

Obtain a fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation to assess response. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement, evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia, and assessment for an inherited lipid disorder given the longstanding severe LDL elevation and xanthomatous findings. Consider cardiology or lipid specialist input for treatment intensification in the setting of coronary artery disease and markedly elevated LDL.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy diet low in saturated and trans fats, increased soluble fiber, avoidance of tobacco exposure, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Strongly advised smoking cessation; offer structured cessation support and pharmacologic assistance as appropriate.

Reviewed symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including new or worsening chest discomfort, shortness of breath, syncope, or exertional symptoms. Continue close outpatient follow-up to reassess lipid response, adherence, and cardiovascular risk reduction.
