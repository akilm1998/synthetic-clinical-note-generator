Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Sophie Bryant is an 85-year-old woman with a 15-year history of chronic kidney disease, hypertension for 31 years, gout for 35 years, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 21 years. She presents for outpatient medication review. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. She currently smokes.

Medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance were reviewed in the context of her renal disease. No specific medication regimen or recent medication changes are documented for assessment today; CKD-directed therapy and antihypertensive treatment require confirmation before determining effectiveness or making adjustments. Particular attention is needed to renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic or over-the-counter medications.

Objective

Height 179 cm; weight 71.1 kg; BMI 22.2 kg/m².

Blood pressure 175/94 mmHg, pulse 80/min, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Laboratory results: serum creatinine 3.08 mg/dL, eGFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 90 mg/dL.

No peripheral edema, shortness of breath, nausea, pruritus, or urinary frequency change reported. No additional examination findings documented.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function and marked azotemia, symptomatic primarily with fatigue. The condition has been present for approximately 15 years and is currently not adequately managed. The degree of renal impairment warrants prompt nephrology involvement and structured medication reconciliation, although no formal disease stage is assigned here.

Severely elevated blood pressure in the setting of chronic kidney disease. Current antihypertensive regimen, adherence, and tolerance require clarification.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism associated with chronic kidney disease.

History of gout and long-standing hypertension, both relevant to ongoing renal risk. Active tobacco use.

Plan

Complete medication reconciliation, including prescription medications, over-the-counter agents, supplements, adherence, adverse effects, and renal dosing. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a clinician. Any renally cleared medications should be reviewed for dose appropriateness.

Prompt nephrology referral is recommended for CKD management, evaluation of persistent azotemia, assessment of secondary hyperparathyroidism, and discussion of renal replacement planning if clinically appropriate.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as part of CKD reassessment. Renal ultrasound may be considered if not previously performed or if clinically indicated.

Reassess blood pressure promptly and confirm adherence to the current antihypertensive regimen before adjustment. Home blood pressure monitoring is encouraged if feasible. Seek urgent care for chest pain, new or worsening shortness of breath, confusion, persistent vomiting, marked weakness, or substantially reduced urine output.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of non-prescribed medications that may worsen kidney function. Follow up promptly after medication reconciliation and repeat renal assessment.
