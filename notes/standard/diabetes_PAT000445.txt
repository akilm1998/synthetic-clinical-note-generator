Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Grant Porter is a 41-year-old man with an 8-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and 7-year history of dyslipidemia, presenting for routine follow-up. Diabetes has not been actively managed. Recent laboratory testing shows HbA1c 9.6% and fasting glucose 239 mg/dL, consistent with substantially elevated glycemia.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No diabetic complications are reported.

He currently smokes. Medication use and adherence were not provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 122/75 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 177 cm, weight 73.9 kg, BMI 23.6 kg/m².

Laboratory results:
HbA1c: 9.6%
Fasting glucose: 239 mg/dL

No abnormal physical examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c 9.6% and fasting glucose 239 mg/dL. Despite marked hyperglycemia, he is presently asymptomatic and has no reported neuropathic, infectious, visual, or constitutional symptoms.

Dyslipidemia, longstanding, requiring ongoing cardiovascular risk assessment.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the risks of sustained hyperglycemia, including microvascular and cardiovascular complications.

Recommend prompt initiation or re-initiation of evidence-based antihyperglycemic therapy following complete medication reconciliation and shared decision-making. Metformin would be a reasonable first-line option if renal function and other clinical considerations permit; additional therapy may be needed given the degree of hyperglycemia.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with fasting and occasional postprandial measurements, along with documentation of readings for review.

Counseled on reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, balanced nutrition, regular physical activity, and maintenance of current healthy body weight. Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered cessation support.

Recommend assessment of renal status with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive foot examination, and dilated retinal examination as part of diabetes complication surveillance. Review dyslipidemia management and overall cardiovascular risk at follow-up.

Return for diabetes management reassessment in approximately 4–6 weeks, with repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval. Advise earlier evaluation for symptomatic hyperglycemia, vomiting, dehydration, confusion, or other acute concerns.
