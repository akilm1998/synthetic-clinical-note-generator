Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Hannah Simmons is a 34-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 15-year history of chronic kidney disease, currently without active disease management documented. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath. No tobacco use. She has a history of secondary hyperparathyroidism for approximately 1 year. Current medication list and allergies are not provided.

Objective

Height 152 cm, weight 65.4 kg, BMI 28.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 128/84 mmHg, pulse 68/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory results: serum creatinine 3.14 mg/dL, estimated GFR 15 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 72 mg/dL.

No edema, respiratory symptoms, or other abnormal physical examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of 15 years’ duration with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN and creatinine. The patient is clinically stable by reported symptoms and vital signs, without current evidence of volume-overload symptoms, but fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction. Longstanding renal disease and the associated secondary hyperparathyroidism require coordinated management. Given the degree of renal impairment and lack of documented active management, timely nephrology evaluation is important to clarify etiology, optimize conservative care, assess complications, and discuss renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism associated with chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 1 year.

Overweight by BMI of 28.3 kg/m².

Preventive health maintenance reviewed in the context of chronic kidney disease.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive management of chronic kidney disease, including review of renal trajectory, dietary and medication considerations, complication monitoring, and education regarding future renal replacement options if indicated. Advise avoidance of NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter products unless specifically approved by a clinician. Medication reconciliation is needed before initiating or adjusting therapy.

Recommend repeat renal function assessment with serum electrolytes, creatinine/eGFR, BUN, urinalysis, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. Recommend CBC to evaluate fatigue and screen for anemia of chronic disease, along with assessment of mineral and bone parameters including calcium, phosphorus, and intact parathyroid hormone. Renal ultrasound may be considered if not previously completed or if the etiology and structural status of the chronic kidney disease have not been established.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and renal-related symptoms, including reduced urine output, new edema, worsening shortness of breath, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, or progressive weakness. Seek urgent medical attention for severe shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, or markedly decreased urine output.

Discussed kidney-protective lifestyle measures, including moderation of dietary sodium, avoidance of excess processed foods, weight management, and maintaining appropriate hydration without forcing fluids. Nutrition counseling with renal dietary expertise is recommended.

Review and update age-appropriate immunizations and preventive screening at follow-up, with attention to recommendations relevant to chronic kidney disease. Encourage ongoing avoidance of tobacco. Follow up after nephrology assessment and updated laboratory evaluation, or sooner for worsening symptoms.
