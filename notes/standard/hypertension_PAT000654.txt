Chief Complaint
Medication review for hypertension.

Subjective
Patrick Harper is a 52-year-old man, height 162 cm, weight 69.8 kg, BMI 26.6 kg/m², presenting for review of hypertension management. Hypertension has been present for approximately 4 years and has not been actively managed. He is not reporting an active antihypertensive regimen; therefore, medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be assessed for current therapy.

He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. He does not smoke. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for 4 years, hyperlipidemia for 17 years, and coronary artery disease for 3 years.

Objective
Vital signs: Blood pressure 155/91 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 140 mmol/L, potassium 3.7 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure, currently without an active treatment regimen. He is asymptomatic today, with no reported features suggesting acute hypertensive end-organ injury. Given coexisting chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease, untreated hypertension represents an important modifiable cardiovascular and renal risk.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Chronic kidney disease, duration 4 years.

Coronary artery disease, duration 3 years.

Plan
Discussed the importance of initiating and maintaining consistent blood pressure therapy. Review and reconcile all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use before selecting a regimen. An ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker would be reasonable to consider in the setting of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease, provided renal function and potassium are acceptable; current potassium is 3.7 mmol/L. A calcium-channel blocker or thiazide-type diuretic may also be considered based on the complete clinical profile and subsequent blood pressure response.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a validated upper-arm cuff and documentation of readings for medication titration. Reinforce sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight control, and moderation of alcohol intake.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urinary protein/albumin as clinically appropriate before and after initiation or adjustment of renin-angiotensin system therapy. Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management, including review of lipid-lowering therapy and adherence given established coronary artery disease and longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Follow up promptly for medication initiation and blood pressure reassessment. Seek urgent care for chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.
