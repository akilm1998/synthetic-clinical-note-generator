Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Ryan Hughes is a 98-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia, identified approximately 2 years ago and currently without active lipid-lowering management. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. No interval symptoms suggestive of acute cardiovascular ischemia are reported.

Relevant history includes coronary artery disease for approximately 40 years, chronic kidney disease for 19 years, and obesity for 5 years. He continues to smoke. Current weight is 135.7 kg at a height of 188 cm. Medication use and allergies were not provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 140/80 mmHg, pulse 85 bpm, respirations 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

BMI 38.4 kg/m².

Recent lipid panel:
Total cholesterol 288 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 210 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 30 mg/dL
Triglycerides 239 mg/dL

No xanthomas or xanthelasma are reported. No interval cardiovascular symptoms reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, low HDL cholesterol, and elevated triglycerides. Lipid control is substantially above goal in the setting of longstanding coronary artery disease, chronic kidney disease, obesity, advanced age, and ongoing tobacco use. He is currently not receiving documented lipid-lowering therapy.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, without reported anginal symptoms at this visit.

Obesity, BMI 38.4 kg/m².

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significant cardiovascular risk associated with LDL cholesterol of 210 mg/dL, particularly given his history of coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of lipid-lowering therapy, with a statin such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin considered after medication reconciliation and review of renal function, tolerability, goals of care, and treatment preferences. If LDL reduction remains inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe may be considered.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, and lean proteins while reducing saturated and trans fats, processed foods, and excess dietary calories. Encourage physical activity within his functional and cardiovascular limitations, with gradual progression as tolerated.

Smoking cessation counseling provided; cessation is strongly recommended given established coronary artery disease and markedly abnormal lipid profile.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid testing after treatment is initiated or adjusted to assess response. Consider baseline safety monitoring, including renal and hepatic function testing, before selecting and dosing therapy. Continue monitoring for new chest discomfort, dyspnea, dizziness, or exertional leg pain and seek urgent evaluation for acute or worsening symptoms.

Follow up after medication reconciliation and initiation or adjustment of lipid-lowering therapy to review tolerance and lipid response.
