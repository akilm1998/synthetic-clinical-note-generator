Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Victor Pierce is a 54-year-old man presenting for outpatient medication review. He has a 17-year history of chronic kidney disease and has not been receiving consistent disease-directed management. He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath. No medication-related adverse effects are reported today.

He also has anemia of chronic disease, present for approximately 8 years, and hyperlipidemia for approximately 17 years. He currently smokes. Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance were reviewed as priorities; the current regimen requires formal reconciliation before assessing response to individual agents or making medication changes.

Objective

Height 172 cm, weight 81.1 kg, BMI 27.4 kg/m². Blood pressure 146/82 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 4.51 mg/dL, estimated GFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 82 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated creatinine and BUN after approximately 17 years of disease. He is currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, but the degree of renal impairment warrants prompt active management. Blood pressure remains above goal. Medication effectiveness and adherence cannot be meaningfully assessed until the complete medication list, dosing, and actual use are confirmed.

Anemia of chronic disease, present for 8 years, in the setting of significant chronic kidney disease.

Hyperlipidemia, present for 17 years. Current lipid-lowering treatment and response require medication reconciliation.

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Complete medication reconciliation with all prescription medications, over-the-counter products, and supplements, including dose, frequency, adherence, and any intolerance. Emphasized avoiding nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and checking all new medications for renal dosing and safety. No medication changes are documented today pending confirmation of the active regimen.

Given the creatinine of 4.51 mg/dL, BUN of 82 mg/dL, and eGFR of 10 mL/min/1.73 m², arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management and discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate. Recommend serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as part of ongoing evaluation. Repeat renal function testing should be obtained promptly through the treating clinician.

Monitor blood pressure closely and review the medication regimen for an appropriate kidney-protective and blood-pressure-lowering strategy once adherence and current medications are confirmed. Counsel on avoiding excess dietary sodium, maintaining appropriate hydration, and avoiding nephrotoxic medications.

Address anemia in coordination with kidney disease management after CBC assessment. Review and optimize treatment of hyperlipidemia after confirming current therapy and adherence.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, including the importance of stopping tobacco use to reduce cardiovascular and renal risk.

Seek urgent medical attention for new shortness of breath, worsening swelling, marked reduction in urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.
