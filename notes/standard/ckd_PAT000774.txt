Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Victoria Richardson is a 79-year-old woman, height 151 cm, weight 51.5 kg, BMI 22.6 kg/m², presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 30 years. Her only documented comorbidity is longstanding hypertension, diagnosed approximately 39 years ago. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. She continues to smoke.

There is no reported interval fluid-overload symptomatology or gastrointestinal complaint. Current medication regimen and adherence were not provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 156/95 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Laboratory data: Serum creatinine 3.97 mg/dL, estimated GFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 75 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. The patient has a longstanding approximately 30-year history of kidney disease, with current creatinine 3.97 mg/dL, BUN 75 mg/dL, and eGFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m². She has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, dyspnea, or change in urinary frequency. The disease is not currently under active documented management and requires prompt nephrology-directed evaluation and treatment planning.

Hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal at 156/95 mmHg, increasing risk for further renal and cardiovascular injury.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation given the degree of renal impairment and azotemia. Discuss renal replacement therapy planning and conservative kidney care options with nephrology as clinically appropriate; there is no current documentation of an emergent dialysis indication based on the symptoms provided.

Obtain and review a comprehensive metabolic panel including electrolytes and bicarbonate, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as part of CKD assessment. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously performed or if clinically indicated.

Perform careful medication reconciliation at the next opportunity. Avoid nephrotoxic medications, particularly NSAIDs, and review all prescription and over-the-counter agents for renal dosing. Emphasize avoidance of dehydration and unmonitored herbal preparations or supplements.

Blood pressure requires reassessment and active management, with a goal individualized to tolerance and overall health. Review home blood pressure readings if available and initiate or adjust antihypertensive therapy based on the complete medication history, renal function, potassium level, and volume status.

Counsel regarding a kidney-conscious diet with moderation of sodium and avoidance of excessive protein, with dietitian involvement as appropriate. Monitor for new or worsening nausea, vomiting, confusion, progressive weakness, pruritus, muscle cramps, reduced urine output, edema, chest discomfort, or shortness of breath; seek urgent evaluation if these occur.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, given the contribution of tobacco use to vascular and renal disease progression. Close outpatient follow-up after laboratory review and nephrology assessment.
