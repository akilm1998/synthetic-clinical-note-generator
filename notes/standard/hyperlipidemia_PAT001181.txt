Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Melanie Knight is a 57-year-old woman seen for follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 5 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. She denies chest discomfort, dizziness, or exertional leg pain. She also denies known xanthomas or xanthelasma. Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 12 years and coronary artery disease for 8 years. She continues to smoke. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

Objective

Height 176 cm; weight 76.2 kg; BMI 24.6 kg/m². Blood pressure 125/79 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Recent lipid panel: total cholesterol 320 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 199 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 44 mg/dL, triglycerides 384 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides. Given the history of coronary artery disease and diabetes, current lipid levels represent suboptimal cardiovascular risk reduction. No associated chest discomfort, claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma reported.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the increased cardiovascular risk associated with diabetes, coronary artery disease, and continued smoking.

Review and reconcile all current medications, adherence, prior statin exposure, and any history of intolerance. Recommend initiation or escalation to a maximally tolerated high-intensity statin, if not already prescribed and if no contraindication. If LDL cholesterol remains above goal despite adherence, consider addition of ezetimibe and further cardiovascular risk management as clinically indicated.

Counseled on a heart-healthy diet with reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and alcohol; increase dietary fiber and regular physical activity as tolerated. Reinforced smoking cessation and offered assistance with a cessation plan.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and measurement of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) given the degree of LDL elevation and established coronary artery disease.

Continue routine management of diabetes and coronary artery disease. Return for lipid and medication follow-up after repeat testing, or sooner for new chest discomfort, dyspnea, or exertional symptoms.
