Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Lydia Ford is a 40-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 14-year history of chronic kidney disease that is not currently under active management. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath.

Relevant comorbidities include hyperlipidemia for approximately 6 years and gout for approximately 1 year. She does not smoke. Height is 173 cm and weight is 72.4 kg, with BMI 24.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 134/91 mmHg, pulse 74/min, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 4.75 mg/dL
BUN 88 mg/dL
eGFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m²

No peripheral edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, or reported urinary frequency change. No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function

Longstanding chronic kidney disease with current creatinine 4.75 mg/dL, BUN 88 mg/dL, and eGFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m². Fatigue may be related to advanced renal impairment. She currently denies symptoms suggestive of clinically evident fluid overload or prominent uremic symptoms, including edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, cramps, or sleep disturbance. Blood pressure is mildly elevated, particularly diastolic, at 134/91 mmHg. Given the degree of renal dysfunction and lack of active management, this requires prompt specialist assessment and coordinated renal care.

2. Hyperlipidemia

Six-year history. Cardiovascular risk reduction is particularly important in the setting of chronic kidney disease. Current lipid values and treatment status are not provided.

3. Gout

One-year history. Renal impairment is clinically relevant to gout management and medication selection. No current flare symptoms are reported.

4. Preventive health maintenance

Annual preventive review completed. She is a nonsmoker with a normal BMI. Ongoing age- and sex-appropriate preventive care should be maintained.

Plan

1. Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive assessment of severe chronic kidney disease and discussion of renal replacement planning if clinically indicated. She should seek immediate emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, marked weakness, rapidly worsening swelling, or significant reduction in urine output.

2. Obtain prompt renal evaluation including repeat renal function and serum electrolytes, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate. These studies will help assess complications of chronic kidney disease and guide treatment.

3. Review blood pressure carefully and reinforce a low-sodium diet. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications unless specifically approved by a clinician. Medication dosing should be reviewed for renal adjustment.

4. Continue monitoring for anemia-related symptoms, mineral and bone complications, electrolyte abnormalities, fluid retention, and uremic symptoms. Fatigue should be reassessed in the context of the renal findings.

5. Address hyperlipidemia as part of cardiovascular risk reduction after review of a current lipid profile and overall risk assessment.

6. Review gout history and flare frequency with renal-adjusted treatment considerations; avoid empiric medication changes without assessment of renal function and current therapy.

7. Review routine preventive care, including immunizations and age-appropriate cervical and breast cancer screening, and update based on her prior records and preferences.

8. Close primary care follow-up after renal evaluation, with earlier review for any change in symptoms.
