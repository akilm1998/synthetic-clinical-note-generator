Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure with dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue.

Subjective

Emma Carter is a 53-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 14 years and currently without active management. She reports dizziness, intermittent palpitations, and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. She has known coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She currently smokes. Her weight is 54.3 kg at 155 cm, with BMI 22.6 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure is 160/91 mmHg. Pulse 80 bpm. Respiratory rate 15/min. Temperature 37.0°C. Oxygen saturation 98% on room air. Serum sodium is 146 mmol/L and potassium is 4.9 mmol/L. No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms are reported today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and associated dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. Given the long duration of hypertension, lack of current management, and coexisting coronary artery disease, prompt treatment and cardiovascular risk assessment are indicated. No symptoms currently suggest hypertensive emergency.

Tobacco use, which increases cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement after a period of rest and arrange prompt outpatient initiation of an antihypertensive treatment plan, taking coronary artery disease and current electrolytes into account. Encourage home blood pressure monitoring and review of readings at follow-up.

Recommend ECG, lipid profile, urinalysis, and repeat serum electrolytes as part of cardiovascular and hypertension evaluation. Counsel on reducing dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, limiting alcohol if applicable, and smoking cessation.

Seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, new or severe shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, severe headache, acute visual changes, or markedly worsening palpitations or dizziness. Close follow-up is recommended to reassess blood pressure, symptoms, and treatment response.
