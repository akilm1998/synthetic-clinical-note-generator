Chief Complaint

Annual review of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Victoria Richardson is a 36-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She is not currently engaged in active diabetes management. Recent HbA1c is 9.4% and fasting glucose is 226 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet.

She has a history of chronic kidney disease for approximately 1 year and anxiety for approximately 5 years. She currently smokes. No diabetes-related complications are reported today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 137/86 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 177 cm, weight 85.5 kg, BMI 27.3 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.4%. Fasting glucose 226 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently significantly above glycemic target based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. She is asymptomatic but requires prompt initiation of structured diabetes care.

Chronic kidney disease, reported duration approximately 1 year. Renal function and albuminuria should be reviewed before selecting and dosing glucose-lowering therapy.

Anxiety, chronic.

Tobacco use.

Overweight.

Preventive health needs reviewed in the context of diabetes and tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose, along with the risks of untreated hyperglycemia, including kidney, eye, nerve, and cardiovascular complications. Recommend beginning an individualized diabetes treatment plan promptly, including nutrition counseling, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight-management strategies, and pharmacologic therapy after review of renal function. Metformin would be a reasonable consideration if renal function permits; treatment should be individualized after medication and renal review.

Recommend checking serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, as well as a fasting lipid profile, to assess diabetes-related renal and cardiovascular risk. Repeat HbA1c after approximately 3 months of active treatment.

Recommend annual dilated retinal examination and comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including assessment of skin integrity, sensation, and pulses. Encourage daily foot inspection and prompt evaluation of any wound or infection.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and offered cessation support. Discussed heart-healthy nutrition, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, and gradual increase in physical activity toward routine weekly exercise as tolerated.

Blood pressure is mildly above ideal for a patient with diabetes; continue monitoring and reassess alongside renal risk evaluation.

Reviewed preventive care appropriate for diabetes, including immunization review and age-appropriate cervical cancer screening. Continue follow-up for anxiety and assess its impact on self-care and diabetes management.

Follow up promptly to establish the treatment regimen and review renal and cardiovascular risk testing, with diabetes reassessment in approximately 3 months.
