Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Zachary Barnes is a 78-year-old man, 163 cm tall and 63.0 kg (BMI 23.7 kg/m²), seen for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease documented for approximately 1 year. He reports no current fatigue, itching, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. He does not smoke. His medical history is also notable for secondary hyperparathyroidism of approximately 16 years’ duration and gout for approximately 32 years. There is no medication list available for review today, and no specific kidney-directed treatment is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 143/91 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 94 mg/dL.

He is currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms. No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly impaired renal function, currently clinically asymptomatic by history. Creatinine is 6.0 mg/dL with BUN 94 mg/dL and estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m², requiring prompt specialist assessment and close monitoring. The absence of nausea, pruritus, fatigue, edema, dyspnea, or urinary changes does not lessen the significance of these laboratory abnormalities.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, longstanding.

Gout, longstanding.

Blood pressure is mildly elevated at today’s visit.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for assessment of the advanced reduction in kidney function, review of renal replacement therapy planning when appropriate, and management of associated mineral-bone disease and anemia risk. Recommend prompt repeat renal and metabolic testing, including serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone, as clinically directed. Renal ultrasound may also be considered to assess kidney structure and obstruction.

Patient was advised to seek emergency care immediately for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, severe weakness, or other acute change in condition. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications unless specifically cleared by a clinician. Review all prescribed and over-the-counter medications with a clinician for renal dosing. Recommend renal-appropriate dietary counseling and home blood pressure monitoring. Close outpatient follow-up is needed after urgent renal evaluation.
