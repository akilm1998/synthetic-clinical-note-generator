Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Preston Oliver is a 40-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease follow-up. He has a one-year history of hyperlipidemia, coronary artery disease, and hypertension. Hyperlipidemia has not been under active management. He denies chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, and dizziness. He reports xanthomas and xanthelasma.

He does not smoke. Height is 185 cm, weight 81.5 kg, BMI 23.8 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 136/84 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Lipid panel:
Total cholesterol 349 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 241 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 33 mg/dL
Triglycerides 377 mg/dL

Assessment

1. Hyperlipidemia, unspecified

Markedly abnormal lipid profile with substantial elevation of LDL cholesterol and triglycerides and low HDL cholesterol. The presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma is clinically consistent with prolonged or significant lipid elevation. Given his known coronary artery disease, these results represent a high cardiovascular-risk profile despite his normal BMI and absence of current exertional symptoms.

2. Coronary artery disease

History of coronary artery disease for approximately one year. No current chest discomfort, dizziness, or exertional leg pain reported. Risk-factor reduction and aggressive lipid management are indicated.

3. Hypertension

History of hypertension for approximately one year. Blood pressure today is 136/84 mmHg, mildly above ideal for a patient with coronary artery disease. No associated symptoms reported.

4. Preventive health maintenance

Annual preventive review completed. He is a nonsmoker with a healthy BMI. Ongoing attention to cardiovascular risk reduction is particularly important.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the increased cardiovascular risk associated with LDL cholesterol of 241 mg/dL, triglycerides of 377 mg/dL, and HDL cholesterol of 33 mg/dL. Recommend initiation of guideline-directed high-intensity lipid-lowering therapy, with treatment selection and shared decision-making at this visit. If LDL reduction is inadequate or therapy is not tolerated, addition of a non-statin agent such as ezetimibe should be considered. Medication adherence and adverse effects should be reviewed closely.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, fish, and unsaturated fats, with reduction of saturated fats, trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and alcohol. Continue regular aerobic and resistance exercise as tolerated, provided there are no restrictions from his cardiac care team.

Obtain a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment is established to assess response. Evaluation for secondary contributors to severe mixed dyslipidemia, including thyroid, hepatic, renal, and glucose-related causes, is appropriate. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk assessment. Because of the known coronary artery disease, lipid management should be coordinated with his established cardiovascular care as clinically appropriate.

Continue blood pressure monitoring and reinforce dietary sodium reduction, regular physical activity, and maintenance of healthy weight. Review antihypertensive therapy and blood pressure goals at follow-up; no medication regimen was provided for review today.

Seek urgent medical attention for new or persistent chest pain, shortness of breath, syncope, or exertional symptoms.

Continue routine preventive care, including immunization review and age-appropriate cancer screening. Return for follow-up after repeat lipid testing and earlier for any new cardiovascular symptoms.
