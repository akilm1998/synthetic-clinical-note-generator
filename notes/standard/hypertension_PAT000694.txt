Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for hypertension.

Subjective

Richard Lawson is a 94-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of longstanding hypertension, reported for approximately 31 years and currently not actively managed. He reports intermittent dizziness and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or epistaxis.

Past medical history is notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 49 years and hyperlipidemia for approximately 64 years. No current medications are provided. He does not smoke. Height is 180 cm and weight is 86.5 kg, with BMI 26.7 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 149/92 mmHg; pulse 78 bpm; respiratory rate 15/min; temperature 37.0°C; oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

General: No acute distress documented.

Laboratory data:
Serum sodium 137 mmol/L.
Serum potassium 4.9 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with an elevated office blood pressure of 149/92 mmHg in the setting of longstanding disease that has not been actively managed. The patient is currently without symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Dizziness and fatigue warrant attention to possible blood-pressure variability or orthostatic hypotension, particularly given advanced age, although no orthostatic measurements are available today.

Comorbid longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus and hyperlipidemia increase overall cardiovascular risk. Serum sodium is within the expected range; potassium is upper-normal and should be considered when selecting antihypertensive therapy.

Plan

Confirm the blood-pressure pattern with repeated seated measurements and home blood-pressure monitoring, using a validated cuff and a written log. Obtain orthostatic blood pressures because of reported dizziness.

Review the risks and benefits of initiating antihypertensive therapy, with a cautious, individualized approach given age and risk of falls or symptomatic hypotension. Any treatment should be started conservatively and titrated according to home readings, symptoms, renal function, and electrolyte monitoring. Medication reconciliation is recommended before selecting therapy.

Counsel regarding moderation of dietary sodium, regular activity as tolerated, maintenance of current healthy weight, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Continue smoking avoidance.

Recommend baseline cardiovascular and end-organ assessment, including ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis, along with renal function testing before or during initiation of therapy. Continue coordinated management of diabetes and hyperlipidemia.

Seek urgent medical attention for severe or persistent headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, acute neurologic symptoms, or major visual change.

Follow up after blood-pressure monitoring and medication review for reassessment and treatment planning.
