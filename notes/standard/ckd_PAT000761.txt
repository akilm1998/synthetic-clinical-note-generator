Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Teresa Cunningham is a 35-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 13 years ago. She reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No interval symptoms of fluid overload or uremic illness are reported. She does not smoke.

Comorbidities include gout for approximately 1 year and hyperlipidemia for approximately 1 year. Current medication use is not documented.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 137/86 mmHg, pulse 68/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Height 171 cm, weight 76.9 kg, BMI 26.3 kg/m².

Laboratory data:
Serum creatinine 5.48 mg/dL.
BUN 83 mg/dL.
Estimated GFR 6 mL/min/1.73 m².

No edema, respiratory symptoms, or other abnormal physical findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of approximately 13 years’ duration with markedly impaired renal function, creatinine 5.48 mg/dL, BUN 83 mg/dL, and eGFR 6 mL/min/1.73 m². Despite the severity of the laboratory abnormalities, she currently denies symptoms suggestive of uremia or fluid overload. Blood pressure is acceptable at today’s visit. Disease management appears insufficiently established and requires prompt reassessment and coordinated renal care.

Gout, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, in the setting of markedly reduced kidney function.

Hyperlipidemia, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago.

Overweight by BMI of 26.3 kg/m².

Plan

Recommend urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of markedly reduced kidney function and discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate, even in the absence of current symptoms.

Obtain repeat renal function testing and serum electrolytes, along with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone level to assess complications of chronic kidney disease. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously performed to evaluate renal structure and chronicity.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplemental medications when available. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents; ensure medication dosing is adjusted for renal function. Dietary counseling should include individualized sodium and protein guidance, with attention to potassium and phosphorus once current electrolyte data are available.

Monitor blood pressure and volume status. Continue surveillance for fatigue, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, worsening urine output, edema, or shortness of breath. She should seek emergency care for new or progressive shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, inability to maintain oral intake, or markedly decreased urine output.

Address gout and hyperlipidemia in the context of severe renal impairment; treatment selection should be renal-dose appropriate. Close follow-up is recommended after laboratory reassessment and renal consultation.
