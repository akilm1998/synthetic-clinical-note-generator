Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for evaluation of anemia.

History of Present Illness

Ivy Henderson is a 60-year-old woman referred for evaluation of anemia identified approximately two years ago. She reports no current fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, dyspnea, palpitations, or other cardiopulmonary symptoms. She does not describe pallor. Available laboratory data demonstrate a hemoglobin of 9.3 g/dL, hematocrit 27.9%, and RBC count 3.17 million/µL. The MCV is 88 fL, consistent with a normocytic anemia. Iron studies show serum iron 36 µg/dL, ferritin 300 ng/mL, TIBC 260 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 13.8%.

Her medical history is notable for chronic kidney disease of approximately eight years’ duration and rheumatoid arthritis of approximately two years’ duration, both of which are clinically relevant potential contributors to chronic inflammation and impaired erythropoiesis. The anemia is currently not being actively managed. She does not smoke. No medication use, allergies, bleeding symptoms, or prior anemia-directed treatment are documented in the available history.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue, weakness, or reduced exercise tolerance.

Neurologic: Denies dizziness or headache.

Cardiopulmonary: Denies shortness of breath or palpitations.

Hematologic: No pallor reported.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 143/85 mmHg, pulse 80 beats/minute, respiratory rate 20/minute, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 164 cm, weight 75.0 kg, BMI 27.9 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Hemoglobin 9.3 g/dL; hematocrit 27.9%; RBC 3.17 million/µL; MCV 88 fL; serum iron 36 µg/dL; ferritin 300 ng/mL; TIBC 260 µg/dL; transferrin saturation 13.8%.

Assessment

Normocytic anemia, present for approximately two years, most consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation in the setting of longstanding chronic kidney disease and rheumatoid arthritis. The low serum iron and reduced transferrin saturation with an elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC support inflammatory iron sequestration rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency, although the degree of reduced iron availability warrants further characterization. Chronic kidney disease may also contribute through reduced erythropoietin production. She is currently clinically stable without reported symptoms of tissue hypoxia, tachycardia, dyspnea, or exercise intolerance.

Plan

Obtain a complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to further characterize erythropoietic response and morphology. Recommend assessment of renal function and inflammatory activity, along with serum vitamin B12 and folate levels, to evaluate for additional reversible contributors to the anemia. Repeat iron indices as clinically appropriate and interpret them in the context of her chronic inflammatory conditions.

Management should address the underlying chronic kidney disease and rheumatoid arthritis in coordination with the clinicians managing those conditions. If anemia persists or worsens after evaluation of reversible causes, consideration may be given to iron replacement, including intravenous iron when clinically appropriate, or an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent in the setting of kidney disease after individualized risk-benefit assessment. She should seek prompt medical attention for new dyspnea, chest discomfort, sustained palpitations, syncope, significant dizziness, or marked decline in functional capacity.
