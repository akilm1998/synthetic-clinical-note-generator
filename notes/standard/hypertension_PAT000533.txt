Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure and dizziness.

Subjective
Eliza Chandler is a 44-year-old woman with a 10-year history of hypertension that has not been actively managed. She reports dizziness. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. She has type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic kidney disease, each diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. She does not smoke.

Objective
Height 168 cm; weight 68.9 kg; BMI 24.4 kg/m².
Blood pressure 150/104 mmHg; pulse 73 bpm; respiratory rate 17/min; temperature 36.9°C; oxygen saturation 97% on room air.
Serum sodium 135 mmol/L; potassium 5.0 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with persistently elevated diastolic pressure and associated dizziness. No reported symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency at this visit. Long duration without active management increases cardiovascular and renal risk.
Type 2 diabetes mellitus.
Chronic kidney disease. Potassium is at the upper end of the reported range and should be considered when selecting antihypertensive therapy.
BMI in the normal range. Nonsmoker.

Plan
Arrange prompt outpatient initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy with renal function and potassium considerations; avoid empiric changes without review of current kidney status and medication history.
Repeat blood pressure using proper technique and encourage home blood pressure monitoring with a written log.
Recheck serum electrolytes and obtain renal function testing as clinically appropriate after treatment is initiated or adjusted. Obtain urinalysis, lipid profile, and ECG as part of cardiovascular and renal risk assessment.
Continue coordinated management of hypertension, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease. Counsel on sodium restriction, regular physical activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol.
Seek emergency care immediately for severe or worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, vision loss, confusion, or markedly higher blood pressure readings.
Follow up promptly for blood pressure reassessment and review of laboratory results.
