Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective
Zachary Barnes is a 95-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 36 years. Diabetes has not been actively managed recently. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 40 years’ duration, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of 31 years’ duration, and obesity of 7 years’ duration. He continues to smoke. No current diabetes medication regimen is documented for review today.

Objective
Height 185 cm; weight 111.6 kg; BMI 32.6 kg/m².

Blood pressure 148/82 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Hemoglobin A1c 9.4%. Fasting glucose 230 mg/dL.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on hemoglobin A1c of 9.4% and fasting glucose of 230 mg/dL. Despite significant hyperglycemia, he reports no classic hyperglycemic symptoms and no neuropathic or infectious symptoms. Given his advanced age and longstanding chronic kidney disease, treatment should be individualized with attention to renal function, hypoglycemia avoidance, functional status, and overall goals of care.

Obesity, BMI 32.6 kg/m², contributing to metabolic risk.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Renal function and albuminuria should guide diabetes treatment selection and dosing.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Elevated office blood pressure at 148/82 mmHg; requires continued monitoring and individualized management in the context of advanced age and chronic kidney disease.

Plan
Discussed that current glycemic control is above goal and warrants active management. Perform medication reconciliation and establish a simplified, renal-appropriate diabetes regimen, avoiding overly aggressive treatment and hypoglycemia. Renal function should be reviewed before initiating or adjusting glucose-lowering therapy.

Recommend home glucose monitoring as appropriate, with attention to symptoms of hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia. Repeat hemoglobin A1c after a clinically appropriate treatment interval to assess response.

Recommend assessment of serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio for medication safety and diabetic kidney disease risk stratification. A fasting lipid profile and liver-related laboratory monitoring may also be considered in the context of diabetes, obesity, chronic kidney disease, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Counseled regarding portion control, reduction of concentrated carbohydrates, and activity appropriate to his mobility and safety. Encourage smoking cessation and avoidance of tobacco exposure.

Continue monitoring blood pressure. Treatment decisions should be individualized given age, chronic kidney disease, and risk of adverse effects.

Follow up routinely after medication reconciliation and review of renal and metabolic monitoring, sooner for marked hyperglycemia, confusion, dehydration, vomiting, new vision changes, foot wounds, or other acute concerns.
