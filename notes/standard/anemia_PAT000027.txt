Chief Complaint

Initial outpatient consultation for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Kayla Pierce is a 93-year-old woman presenting for initial evaluation of longstanding anemia. Anemia has been present for approximately 41 years and is currently not under active management. She reports headache but denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor.

She has a history of malignancy diagnosed approximately 12 years ago. Smoking status is documented as current smoker. No medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 139/79 mmHg, pulse 89 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 174 cm; weight 83.3 kg; BMI 27.5 kg/m².

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL
Hematocrit 27.1%
RBC count 2.88 million/µL
MCV 94 fL
Serum iron 45 µg/dL
Ferritin 371 ng/mL
TIBC 241 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 18.7%

Symptom review is negative for fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, and reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor is not reported. Hemodynamically stable at today’s visit.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia with hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL and hematocrit 27.1%. Iron studies demonstrate low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and low TIBC, a pattern most consistent with anemia associated with chronic inflammation or chronic disease rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Her longstanding anemia and history of malignancy are clinically relevant potential contributors. No current cardiopulmonary symptoms are reported, and oxygen saturation and pulse are acceptable today. Headache may occur in the setting of anemia but is nonspecific.

Plan

Recommend further outpatient characterization with repeat CBC, reticulocyte count, peripheral blood smear, vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers. Review prior blood counts if available to confirm the long-term pattern and identify any recent change.

Given the history of malignancy, recommend coordination with the clinician managing that condition to assess whether the malignancy or its treatment may be contributing to the anemia and to clarify overall treatment goals. Consider hematology consultation if the anemia is worsening, unexplained after initial evaluation, or difficult to manage.

Do not start empiric iron solely on the basis of the low serum iron, as the elevated ferritin and low TIBC favor inflammation-associated iron restriction. Iron replacement, including intravenous iron, should be considered only after review of the complete clinical picture and repeat studies. Erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy would require a specific indication and specialist-directed risk-benefit discussion.

Counseled regarding urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest discomfort, sustained palpitations, syncope, severe dizziness, marked weakness, or bleeding. Smoking cessation should be addressed at a subsequent visit. Follow up after recommended laboratory evaluation and review of the malignancy history.
