Chief Complaint

Annual review with evaluation of markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Adriana Wells is a 67-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease assessment. She has a 16-year history of hyperlipidemia that is not currently under active management. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 376 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 266 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 49 mg/dL, and triglycerides 305 mg/dL. She reports fatty skin deposits consistent with xanthomas. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, and dizziness. She does not smoke.

Relevant chronic conditions include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 17 years, chronic kidney disease for 20 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 33 years. Current medication use, allergies, and home glucose data were not provided for review today.

Objective

Height 157 cm. Weight 68.3 kg. BMI 27.7 kg/m².

Blood pressure 138/79 mmHg. Pulse 75 bpm. Respiratory rate 16/min. Temperature 36.8°C. Oxygen saturation 96%.

Patient is clinically stable by vital signs. Xanthomas are reported. No chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness reported. No additional examination findings are documented today.

Laboratory data:
Total cholesterol 376 mg/dL.
LDL cholesterol 266 mg/dL.
HDL cholesterol 49 mg/dL.
Triglycerides 305 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe elevation of LDL cholesterol and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia, longstanding for approximately 16 years and presently untreated or not actively managed. The LDL level of 266 mg/dL confers substantially increased lifetime atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, overweight status, and advanced age. Reported xanthomas further support significant chronic lipid burden. She currently reports no symptoms suggestive of angina, peripheral arterial disease, or cerebrovascular hypoperfusion.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, with current glycemic control not assessed in the information available for this visit.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Renal function and current stage are not provided.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding. Marked dyslipidemia warrants attention to overall metabolic and hepatic risk, although fatty liver disease alone is not a reason to defer appropriate lipid-lowering therapy.

Overweight, BMI 27.7 kg/m².

Preventive health and annual chronic disease review.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and triglycerides and the associated cardiovascular risk. Recommend prompt shared decision-making regarding initiation of high-intensity lipid-lowering therapy, generally with a statin unless contraindicated or not tolerated. Given the degree of LDL elevation, combination therapy with ezetimibe or other additional treatment may be considered if adequate reduction is not achieved with initial therapy.

Recommend medication reconciliation and review for prior statin exposure, intolerance, contraindications, and potential secondary contributors to dyslipidemia. Obtain or review renal and hepatic function and consider evaluation for other secondary causes as clinically indicated. A repeat fasting lipid panel should be obtained after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement may further refine cardiovascular risk assessment.

Counseled on a cardioprotective eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, legumes, whole grains, fiber, and unsaturated fats while limiting saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol. Encourage gradual regular aerobic and resistance activity as tolerated, with attention to renal and diabetes-related limitations, and continued weight management. She remains a nonsmoker.

Continue coordinated management of diabetes and chronic kidney disease, including review of glycemic control, renal-protective strategies, blood pressure, and cardiovascular risk reduction. Avoid assuming treatment adherence until the current medication list is reviewed.

For annual preventive care, review age-appropriate cancer screening, immunization status, diabetic retinal and foot care, renal monitoring, dental care, fall-risk assessment, and advance care planning at the appropriate visits.

Follow up after medication and laboratory review, preferably within 4–12 weeks after any lipid-lowering treatment is initiated or changed, with earlier assessment for new chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, neurologic symptoms, or medication-related adverse effects.
