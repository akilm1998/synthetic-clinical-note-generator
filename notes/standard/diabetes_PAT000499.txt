Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Samuel Wood is a 47-year-old man with a 7-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus presenting for medication review. His diabetes is not currently being actively managed with an established treatment regimen. There is therefore no current medication regimen for which effectiveness, adherence, or treatment tolerance can be assessed. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Recent laboratory testing demonstrates HbA1c of 9.7% and fasting glucose of 238 mg/dL, consistent with substantially above-target glycemic control despite the absence of reported symptoms. He has a history of depression for 16 years. He currently smokes. No medication adverse effects are reported.

Objective

Blood pressure is 126/78 mmHg, pulse 79 beats/min, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, and oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Height is 188 cm and weight 76.7 kg, with BMI 21.7 kg/m². He is afebrile and hemodynamically stable. No diabetic complications are reported in the available history.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, with inadequate glycemic control based on HbA1c 9.7% and fasting glucose 238 mg/dL. The patient is asymptomatic but requires prompt initiation or re-establishment of active pharmacologic and lifestyle management. Given his normal BMI and absence of reported weight loss or catabolic symptoms, the current presentation remains clinically consistent with type 2 diabetes; however, the degree of hyperglycemia warrants close follow-up.

Depression, longstanding, without additional symptoms or treatment details provided.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose, including the risk of future microvascular and cardiovascular complications even in the absence of symptoms. Reviewed the importance of medication adherence, regular glucose monitoring, nutrition, and physical activity.

Recommend initiation of evidence-based glucose-lowering therapy, with metformin as a reasonable first-line option after review of renal function and other contraindications. If glycemic response is inadequate or if marked hyperglycemia persists, additional therapy should be considered promptly. Treatment selection and dose escalation should be individualized according to tolerance, renal function, cost, and patient preference.

Recommend obtaining serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio for diabetes-related renal risk assessment, along with a fasting lipid profile for cardiovascular risk stratification. Recommend routine diabetic retinal evaluation and comprehensive foot examination. Counsel regarding smoking cessation and offer behavioral support.
