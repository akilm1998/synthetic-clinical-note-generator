Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Logan Stewart is an 82-year-old man presenting for follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 22 years ago. Blood pressure control remains poor, and he is not currently under an established treatment regimen. He reports intermittent dizziness and palpitations. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, or nosebleeds.

Medical history is significant for chronic kidney disease for approximately 34 years and coronary artery disease for approximately 9 years. He continues to smoke. Weight is stable at 74.7 kg; BMI is 23.3 kg/m². No medication list is provided for review today.

Objective

Blood pressure 167/97 mmHg. Pulse 81 bpm. Respiratory rate 18/min. Temperature 37.0°C. Oxygen saturation 95% on room air. Height 179 cm. Weight 74.7 kg. BMI 23.3 kg/m².

Recent serum electrolytes: sodium 141 mmol/L, potassium 3.9 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and associated dizziness and palpitations. Given his longstanding hypertension, chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease, advanced age, and ongoing tobacco use, improved blood pressure control is important. No current symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency are reported.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the markedly elevated blood pressure and cardiovascular and renal risks of uncontrolled hypertension. Confirm blood pressure with repeat measurements and encourage a home blood pressure log, including readings at different times of day. Review all current medications and adherence at the next contact and initiate or adjust antihypertensive therapy promptly with consideration of his chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease, age, and current electrolytes.

Recommend a low-sodium diet, regular activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and smoking cessation. Counsel regarding slow position changes and fall precautions while blood pressure management is being addressed.

Recommend ECG evaluation for palpitations and assessment of cardiovascular risk factors, including lipid profile and urinalysis, as clinically appropriate. Monitor renal function and electrolytes with any antihypertensive treatment changes.

Seek immediate emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, confusion, vision loss, or persistently severe blood pressure elevation with symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning.
