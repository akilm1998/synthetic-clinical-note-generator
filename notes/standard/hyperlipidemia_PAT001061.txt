Chief Complaint

Annual review of hyperlipidemia and chronic medical conditions.

Subjective

Jane Watson is a 34-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. Hyperlipidemia has been present for approximately 9 years and has not been actively treated. She denies chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, hypertension diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease present for approximately 9 years. No current medication regimen was provided.

Objective

Height 169 cm. Weight 77.1 kg. BMI 27.0 kg/m².

Blood pressure 133/84 mmHg. Pulse 78 bpm. Respiratory rate 18/min. Temperature 37.0°C. Oxygen saturation 98%.

Lipid profile: total cholesterol 337 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 218 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 49 mg/dL, triglycerides 350 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides. The LDL level of 218 mg/dL represents a substantial atherogenic burden, particularly in the setting of documented coronary artery disease and hypertension. The lipid disorder has been present for approximately 9 years without active management. There are no reported clinical symptoms of peripheral ischemia or lipid deposition. The pattern warrants evaluation for secondary contributors and possible inherited dyslipidemia; family history of premature cardiovascular disease should be specifically reviewed.

Coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She currently denies chest discomfort, dizziness, or exertional leg symptoms. Her lipid profile places her at high cardiovascular risk and requires active secondary-prevention management.

Hypertension, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. Current blood pressure is 133/84 mmHg, mildly above ideal in the context of coronary artery disease.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, present for approximately 9 years, occurring with overweight status and significant dyslipidemia. Lipid-lowering therapy is generally compatible with fatty liver disease, with appropriate clinical monitoring.

Overweight, BMI 27.0 kg/m².

Preventive care: Annual cardiovascular risk-factor review is appropriate. Tobacco avoidance should be continued. Age-appropriate preventive care, immunizations, cervical cancer screening, and reproductive health counseling should be reviewed according to standard recommendations.

Plan

Discuss initiation of intensive lipid-lowering therapy, generally with a high-intensity statin given the LDL cholesterol level and established coronary artery disease. Treatment should be selected after reviewing contraindications, pregnancy potential, medication interactions, and patient preference. If LDL reduction remains inadequate or therapy is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe and consideration of further specialist-directed therapy may be appropriate.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, along with evaluation for secondary causes of dyslipidemia, including thyroid disease, renal disease, diabetes, dietary contributors, and medication-related causes. Consider measurement of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a), particularly given the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and premature coronary artery disease. Obtain a detailed family history and consider evaluation for familial hypercholesterolemia.

Recommend a heart-healthy dietary pattern emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, increased soluble fiber, vegetables, legumes, and whole grains, with limitation of refined carbohydrates and alcohol because of the triglyceride elevation and fatty liver disease. Encourage regular aerobic and resistance exercise as tolerated and gradual weight reduction.

Continue close blood pressure monitoring with a goal individualized to her coronary artery disease. Review home blood pressure readings and current antihypertensive treatment, if any, at follow-up.

Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management in coordination with the clinician managing her coronary artery disease. She should seek urgent medical attention for new or persistent chest pressure, shortness of breath, syncope, or other acute ischemic symptoms.

Follow up after initiation or adjustment of lipid therapy to review tolerance, adherence, blood pressure, and lipid response, with ongoing periodic monitoring thereafter.
