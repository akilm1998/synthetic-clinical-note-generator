Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of hypertension.

Subjective

Ariana Lawson is a 77-year-old woman with a 46-year history of essential hypertension and 17-year history of hyperlipidemia. She presents for review of blood pressure management. No current antihypertensive medication regimen is documented, so medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be fully assessed today.

She reports dizziness. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. She does not smoke. Weight is 57.7 kg at a height of 165 cm, with BMI 21.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 153/90 mmHg, pulse 70 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory data: Sodium 145 mmol/L; potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and associated dizziness. Hypertension has been present for approximately 46 years and is not currently documented as receiving active pharmacologic management. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency, including chest pain, dyspnea, severe headache, visual change, or epistaxis.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding for approximately 17 years. No lipid values or lipid-lowering medication information are provided.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given the current reading of 153/90 mmHg and long duration of hypertension. Reconcile all prescribed, over-the-counter, and previously discontinued medications at the next visit; if she is not taking an antihypertensive, recommend initiation of an individualized regimen after review of renal function, orthostatic symptoms, contraindications, and treatment preferences. If a regimen is started, monitor response and tolerance closely, particularly for worsening dizziness, symptomatic hypotension, electrolyte abnormalities, or renal effects.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with properly sized cuff, including seated readings taken at consistent times, and bring the log to follow-up. Assess for orthostatic blood pressure changes because of her dizziness and age.

Encourage a low-sodium dietary pattern, regular activity as tolerated, and continued avoidance of tobacco. Her BMI is in the healthy range.

Recommend routine hypertension evaluation with renal function testing, urinalysis, ECG, and lipid profile if not recently completed. Continue management of hyperlipidemia based on an updated lipid profile and overall cardiovascular risk assessment.

Seek urgent care for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe or new neurologic symptoms, marked visual changes, or severely elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and medication decision-making.
