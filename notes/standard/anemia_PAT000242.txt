Chief Complaint

Initial evaluation of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Kimberly Franklin is a 72-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of anemia reportedly present for approximately 20 years. She has a history of malignancy of approximately 8 years’ duration. She is not currently receiving documented management for the anemia.

She denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, headache, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor has been noted. Smoking status: current smoker. No medication, allergy, family history, or additional social history information is provided.

Objective

Height 153 cm; weight 50.6 kg; BMI 21.6 kg/m².

Blood pressure 121/76 mmHg, pulse 89 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Patient appears clinically stable. Pallor is present. Cardiovascular examination is otherwise normal. Respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory data:
Hemoglobin 9.0 g/dL
Hematocrit 27.8%
RBC 3.23 million/µL
MCV 86 fL
Serum iron 49 µg/dL
Ferritin 415 ng/mL
TIBC 246 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 19.9%

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/disease in the setting of longstanding anemia and comorbid malignancy. The elevated ferritin with low serum iron and reduced TIBC supports impaired iron availability related to chronic disease rather than straightforward absolute iron deficiency, although mixed etiologies cannot be excluded. She is currently hemodynamically stable and reports no cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms, but pallor and hemoglobin of 9.0 g/dL warrant further evaluation.

Plan

Obtain CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to further characterize the anemia and marrow response. Recommend vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers. Reassess iron indices as clinically indicated and review for evidence of occult blood loss or other contributing conditions.

Given the chronicity, malignancy history, and lack of active anemia management, recommend coordination with her treating oncology team and consideration of hematology consultation for evaluation of persistent anemia and discussion of treatment options. Avoid empiric iron supplementation until the overall cause and iron availability are clarified; iron therapy should be guided by repeat assessment and specialist input.

Review symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, palpitations, syncope, dizziness, or marked weakness. Counsel regarding smoking cessation. Arrange outpatient follow-up after the recommended evaluation to review results and establish an ongoing management plan.
