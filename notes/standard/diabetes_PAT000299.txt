Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective
Nathan Jenkins is a 44-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes, diagnosed approximately 15 years ago. He is not currently engaged in active diabetes management. Recent testing shows HbA1c 9.6% and fasting glucose 239 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has a 13-year history of dyslipidemia. He currently smokes. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective
Height 179 cm; weight 91.6 kg; BMI 28.6 kg/m².

Blood pressure 116/79 mmHg
Pulse 77 bpm
Respiratory rate 16/min
Temperature 36.7°C
Oxygen saturation 97%

Laboratory results:
HbA1c 9.6%
Fasting glucose 239 mg/dL

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, with substantially elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose. Although he is currently asymptomatic, glycemic control is inadequate and requires active management.

Dyslipidemia, longstanding.

Overweight by BMI.

Current tobacco use.

Plan
Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose, including the risk of future microvascular and cardiovascular complications even in the absence of symptoms.

Review prior diabetes treatment history and initiate an appropriate glucose-lowering regimen after shared decision-making; medication selection should account for renal function, contraindications, cost, and treatment preferences. Emphasized that lifestyle measures alone are unlikely to be sufficient at the current level of hyperglycemia.

Counseled regarding reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, increased intake of vegetables and fiber, and gradual regular physical activity as tolerated. Encouraged weight reduction from current BMI of 28.6 kg/m².

Recommend renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetic eye and comprehensive foot examinations. Review dyslipidemia management and overall cardiovascular risk at follow-up.

Smoking cessation counseling provided; encouraged a quit attempt and discussion of cessation supports.

Reviewed symptoms that should prompt earlier evaluation, including marked thirst or urination, vomiting, weakness, confusion, or inability to maintain oral intake.

Arrange close follow-up after treatment initiation and repeat HbA1c in approximately 3 months to assess response.
