Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Ethan Griffin is a 55-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 1 year ago and not currently under active management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. He is a nonsmoker.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for 13 years, hyperlipidemia for 11 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 4 years. Current weight is 72.1 kg at 164 cm, with BMI 26.8 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure 154/92 mmHg. Pulse 76 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 36.8°C. Oxygen saturation 97%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 3.7 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, currently above goal on today’s reading and without reported symptoms or findings suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency. Given the duration of hypertension and comorbid chronic kidney disease and diabetes, cardiovascular and renal risk is increased. Active treatment and close monitoring are indicated.

2. Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, clinically relevant to antihypertensive selection and monitoring. Available sodium and potassium are within acceptable range; renal function values are not provided.

3. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for 4 years, increasing overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

4. Hyperlipidemia, present for 11 years, contributing to cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discuss initiation of a structured antihypertensive treatment plan, with medication selection guided by chronic kidney disease, diabetes, renal function, potassium level, allergies, and medication reconciliation. Confirm home blood pressure readings if available and encourage twice-daily measurements with a written log.

Recommend low-sodium dietary intake, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight control, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Continue smoking avoidance.

Obtain or review renal function, urinalysis, and urine albumin assessment before or soon after treatment initiation, with repeat electrolytes and renal function after any medication change. An ECG and updated lipid assessment are appropriate for cardiovascular risk evaluation.

Educated regarding emergency evaluation for severe or rapidly worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, vision loss, or markedly elevated blood pressure. Arrange close outpatient reassessment to review blood pressure response and establish ongoing management.
