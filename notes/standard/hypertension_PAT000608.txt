Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hypertension.

She reports palpitations and fatigue. No smoking history. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for 33 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 21 years, and hyperlipidemia for 6 years.

Objective
Blood pressure 174/95 mmHg. Pulse 73 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 97%. Height 165 cm, weight 62.9 kg, BMI 23.1 kg/m². Serum sodium 136 mmol/L and potassium 4.3 mmol/L. No additional examination findings documented.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure and currently inadequate control. She has no reported symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive end-organ injury today, including no chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, visual changes, or severe headache. Longstanding hypertension is occurring in the setting of chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes, and hyperlipidemia. Palpitations and fatigue are also reported and warrant evaluation.

Plan
Repeat the blood pressure during the visit if feasible and confirm control with home blood pressure monitoring, using a validated upper-arm cuff and recording readings for review. Discussed the importance of active antihypertensive treatment, with medication selection and dosing individualized for advanced age, chronic kidney disease, diabetes, renal function, and fall risk. Review adherence and medication list at the next encounter; no current antihypertensive regimen is documented today.

Recommend a low-sodium eating pattern, continued appropriate physical activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Recommend obtaining an ECG to evaluate palpitations and assess for hypertension-related cardiac effects. Recommend urinalysis, lipid profile, and monitoring of renal function and electrolytes as part of cardiovascular and kidney risk assessment; current sodium and potassium are within the provided ranges.

Seek urgent evaluation for chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, severe headache, acute visual change, or persistently very high blood pressure. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning.
