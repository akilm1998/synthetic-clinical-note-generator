Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Ellie Wood is a 63-year-old woman seen for outpatient review of type 2 diabetes, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. Diabetes is currently not actively managed with a documented medication regimen. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She is a current smoker. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 9 years’ duration and depression of approximately 30 years’ duration. Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance cannot be assessed against a specific diabetes agent today because no current diabetes medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 142/84 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respirations 14/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 179 cm, weight 71.8 kg, BMI 22.4 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.8%. Fasting glucose 230 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently significantly above glycemic target based on HbA1c 9.8% and fasting glucose 230 mg/dL. Although she is asymptomatic, glycemic control is poor and active treatment is indicated.

Chronic kidney disease, duration approximately 9 years; renal function must be considered before selecting or dosing diabetes medication.

Depression, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed laboratory results and the importance of initiating consistent diabetes management despite the absence of current hyperglycemic symptoms. Review and reconcile all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplemental medications at the next contact. Initiation of pharmacologic therapy should be considered promptly, with medication selection and dosing guided by current renal function and albuminuria status. Obtain serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before or at treatment initiation if not recently available.

Reviewed dietary measures, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, and home glucose monitoring. Reinforced recognition of symptoms of marked hyperglycemia and the need to seek care for vomiting, dehydration, confusion, or inability to maintain oral intake.

Recommend fasting lipid assessment and routine diabetes complication screening, including a comprehensive foot examination and retinal evaluation. Encourage smoking cessation and offer cessation support.

Blood pressure is mildly elevated today; continue monitoring at follow-up.

Close follow-up recommended to review renal studies, finalize diabetes medication therapy, and reassess adherence, tolerance, and glycemic response.
