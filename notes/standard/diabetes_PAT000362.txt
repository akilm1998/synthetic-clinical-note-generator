Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Alexander Fisher is a 48-year-old man returning for outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 5 years ago. Glycemic management has not been established, and he is not documented as taking any diabetes medication. Recent laboratory testing shows HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 218 mg/dL, consistent with persistent hyperglycemia. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, unintentional weight loss, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He also has a 1-year history of hypertension and a 14-year history of depression. He currently smokes. No medication regimen, allergies, or prior diabetes complications are provided.

Objective

Height is 178 cm, weight 67.2 kg, BMI 21.2 kg/m². Blood pressure is 136/82 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, and oxygen saturation 96% on room air. No additional examination findings are documented today.

Laboratory data: HbA1c 9.3%; fasting glucose 218 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently substantially above glycemic goal based on HbA1c of 9.3% and fasting glucose of 218 mg/dL. Although he reports no classic hyperglycemic symptoms and has a normal BMI, the degree of biochemical hyperglycemia warrants prompt treatment intensification and assessment for diabetes-related end-organ involvement.

Hypertension with blood pressure currently 136/82 mmHg.

Depression, longstanding by history.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the risks of untreated hyperglycemia, including microvascular and cardiovascular complications. Reviewed nutrition focused on consistent carbohydrate intake, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages and refined carbohydrates, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Because glycemic control is markedly above goal and diabetes is currently not being actively managed, initiation of pharmacologic glucose-lowering therapy should occur promptly after review of renal function and other baseline safety parameters. Treatment selection and escalation will be individualized based on kidney function, cardiovascular risk, tolerability, cost, and patient preference.

Recommend checking serum creatinine/eGFR, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and fasting lipid profile for diabetes risk assessment and treatment planning. Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot evaluation and a dilated retinal examination. Continue monitoring blood pressure and address cardiovascular risk factors.

Smoking cessation was strongly encouraged, with counseling regarding the adverse effects of tobacco use on cardiovascular and diabetes-related outcomes. Depression should be reassessed at follow-up, including current symptoms and safety concerns, to support adherence and overall diabetes care.

Return for close diabetes follow-up after initiation of an appropriate treatment plan and review of recommended testing, with repeat HbA1c monitoring to assess response.
