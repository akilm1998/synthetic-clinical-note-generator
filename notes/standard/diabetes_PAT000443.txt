Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c of 9.1% and fasting glucose of 222 mg/dL. Despite the degree of hyperglycemia, she does not report polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual blurring, recurrent infections, or impaired wound healing. Paresthesias of the hands or feet may represent diabetic peripheral nerve involvement, although this requires focused examination and further clinical assessment; no formal complication is diagnosed today.

Overweight status with BMI 28.2 kg/m².

Longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Plan

Complete medication reconciliation, including any current or previously used metformin, other oral agents, or insulin, and review adherence, tolerance, cost, and ability to safely self-administer medication. Discuss initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy based on the medication history, renal function, nutritional status, hypoglycemia risk, and individualized goals appropriate for her age and overall health. Avoid assuming a current regimen until this review is completed.

Provide diabetes self-management counseling focused on consistent carbohydrate intake, portion control, limiting sugar-sweetened foods and beverages, regular hydration, and gradual physical activity as tolerated. Given her age and elevated HbA1c, emphasize avoidance of hypoglycemia and development of a practical regimen that she can follow reliably.

Obtain or review renal assessment, including serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, before selecting or dosing medications. A fasting lipid profile is also appropriate for cardiovascular risk assessment. Continue monitoring HbA1c and fasting or home glucose readings to assess response to treatment.

Perform a focused diabetic foot examination at follow-up, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensory testing, particularly in view of her reported tingling. Counsel her to inspect her feet regularly and to report wounds, skin breakdown, or new loss of sensation promptly. Recommend routine eye evaluation for diabetes-related retinal disease.

Continue attention to weight management and liver-healthy nutrition in the setting of longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Review available liver history and medications for hepatic safety when the treatment plan is finalized.

Return for follow-up after medication reconciliation and recommended laboratory assessment, or sooner for marked hyperglycemic symptoms, inability to maintain hydration, confusion, new vision changes, or foot wounds.
