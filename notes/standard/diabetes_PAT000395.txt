Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Lydia Ford is a 47-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 17 years ago. Diabetes control has been suboptimal, with recent HbA1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 204 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections.

She reports tingling in the hands or feet. No numbness is reported. No current diabetes treatment regimen is documented. She continues to smoke. History is also notable for obesity of approximately 1 year and depression for 7 years.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 122/79 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respirations 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 158 cm, weight 80.4 kg, BMI 32.2 kg/m².

Laboratory results:
HbA1c: 9.2%
Fasting glucose: 204 mg/dL

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. She is not reporting osmotic or constitutional symptoms, but does report distal tingling that warrants evaluation for possible diabetes-related neuropathic symptoms.

Obesity, BMI 32.2 kg/m².

Depression, longstanding, approximately 7 years.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed that current glycemic measurements are above goal and that improved diabetes management is needed to reduce the risk of microvascular and cardiovascular complications.

Review diabetes treatment options, including initiation or adjustment of pharmacologic therapy; metformin would be a reasonable first-line option if no contraindication is present. Medication selection should account for renal function, comorbidities, cost, and patient preference.

Counseled on reducing refined carbohydrates and portion sizes, increasing dietary fiber, and working toward regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage gradual weight reduction.

Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, and fasting lipid profile for diabetes risk assessment and treatment planning. Recommend routine dilated retinal examination and comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including assessment of sensation and peripheral pulses.

Monitor the tingling symptoms and review for progression, numbness, weakness, or foot wounds at follow-up.

Provided smoking-cessation counseling and encouraged identification of a cessation plan.

Continue follow-up for depression and assess current symptoms and treatment needs at subsequent visits.

Return for diabetes follow-up after medication and lifestyle planning, with repeat HbA1c at an appropriate interval.
