Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Kayla Pierce is an 88-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 3 years and not currently under active treatment. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

She has a 48-year history of hypertension. No medication list or recent treatment changes were provided.

Objective

Height 162 cm, weight 69.5 kg, BMI 26.5 kg/m².

Blood pressure 149/84 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent lipid panel:
Total cholesterol 311 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 203 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 39 mg/dL
Triglycerides 345 mg/dL

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia, unspecified. Markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with elevated triglycerides and low HDL cholesterol. She currently has no reported chest discomfort, claudication, dizziness, or cutaneous lipid deposits. Hyperlipidemia has not been actively managed, and the degree of elevation warrants discussion of pharmacologic therapy in the context of her advanced age, overall health, preferences, and treatment goals.

Hypertension, longstanding. Blood pressure is elevated today at 149/84 mmHg.

BMI 26.5 kg/m², consistent with overweight range.

Plan

Discussed the lipid results and associated cardiovascular risk. Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, fish, and lean proteins while limiting saturated fats, trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess dietary sugars. Encourage activity appropriate to her mobility and overall health, with gradual regular exercise as tolerated. Continue avoidance of tobacco.

Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of lipid-lowering medication, such as a statin, after reviewing potential benefits, risks, tolerability, pill burden, and her personal goals of care. If treatment is initiated, obtain follow-up lipid testing to assess response and tolerance. A repeat fasting lipid panel may be obtained to confirm the current pattern and guide management. Consider evaluation for contributing secondary factors if clinically indicated.

Recommend continued blood pressure monitoring and review of her current antihypertensive regimen at follow-up. Reinforce dietary sodium moderation and regular physical activity as tolerated.

Follow up in the outpatient setting after treatment discussion and repeat lipid assessment, or sooner for new chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, or other concerning symptoms.
