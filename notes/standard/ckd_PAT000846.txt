Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Daniel Hayes is an 86-year-old man, 171 cm tall and 74.3 kg (BMI 25.4 kg/m²), presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 34 years ago. He reports ongoing fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. He does not smoke.

His medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 40 years, gout for 29 years, and hyperlipidemia for 46 years. No current medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 144/82 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Laboratory Results: Serum creatinine 5.61 mg/dL, estimated glomerular filtration rate 9 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 90 mg/dL.

He is not reporting symptoms suggestive of fluid overload or acute uremic symptoms at this visit. No additional examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced kidney function, longstanding for approximately 34 years. Current creatinine, eGFR, and BUN are significantly abnormal and, together with fatigue, indicate advanced renal impairment requiring prompt specialty assessment. He currently denies nausea, pruritus, cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, reduced urination, or dyspnea.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Gout, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Blood pressure is mildly elevated today.

Plan

Discussed the severity of the current kidney function results with the patient. Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation to assess progression, complications, renal replacement therapy planning, and overall goals of care. He should seek urgent medical attention for worsening shortness of breath, new swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or inability to maintain oral intake.

Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically indicated. Renal ultrasound may be considered to evaluate kidney structure and exclude obstruction. These investigations should be coordinated promptly given the current eGFR and BUN.

Review all medications and avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Ensure all medications are renally dosed. Continue close monitoring and management of blood pressure, diabetes, gout, and hyperlipidemia in the context of severe renal impairment; no specific medication changes are documented today because his current regimen was not provided.

Follow up promptly after nephrology assessment or sooner for any change in symptoms.
