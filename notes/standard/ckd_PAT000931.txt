Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Natalie Ross is a 63-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease follow-up. She has a 5-year history of chronic kidney disease and reports persistent fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, changes in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath.

Her medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 7 years and anemia of chronic disease for 18 years. She does not smoke. No current medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Height 160 cm, weight 76.0 kg, BMI 29.7 kg/m². Blood pressure 141/82 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Recent laboratory results show serum creatinine 3.12 mg/dL, estimated glomerular filtration rate 15 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 69 mg/dL.

No edema, respiratory distress, or other examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function and elevated creatinine and BUN. She currently reports fatigue but no symptoms suggesting overt fluid overload or uremic gastrointestinal symptoms. The degree of renal impairment warrants prompt specialist assessment and close monitoring, particularly in the setting of diabetes and longstanding anemia.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 7-year history, with chronic kidney disease. Current glycemic control data are not available for review.

Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding, with fatigue possibly contributing. No current hemoglobin value is available.

Elevated blood pressure reading in a patient with chronic kidney disease.

Overweight, with BMI 29.7 kg/m².

Preventive health maintenance reviewed as part of annual examination.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology referral for assessment of advanced renal dysfunction, discussion of renal-protective care, monitoring needs, and anticipatory planning should renal replacement therapy become necessary. Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications with attention to renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents such as NSAIDs unless specifically directed.

Repeat renal assessment with serum electrolytes, creatinine/eGFR, BUN, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound to evaluate kidney structure. Results should be reviewed promptly, with closer follow-up rather than routine annual follow-up alone.

Continue careful blood pressure and diabetes management through individualized treatment planning. Encourage home blood pressure monitoring, moderation of dietary sodium, avoidance of excess processed foods, and appropriate hydration unless otherwise directed by nephrology. Weight management and regular low-impact physical activity are encouraged as tolerated.

Further evaluate the longstanding anemia with CBC review and renal-focused management; coordinate treatment with nephrology as indicated.

Discussed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including new or worsening shortness of breath, rapidly increasing swelling, markedly reduced urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.

Ensure age-appropriate preventive care is up to date, including immunizations, cancer screening, diabetic eye and foot care, nutrition counseling, and cardiovascular risk assessment. Follow up after recommended laboratory testing and specialist evaluation, or sooner for any worsening symptoms.
