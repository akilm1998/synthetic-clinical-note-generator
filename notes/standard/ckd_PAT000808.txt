Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Janet McKenzie is a 62-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, reported duration approximately 16 years. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No acute concerns are reported today.

She has a 7-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and a 14-year history of anemia of chronic disease. Chronic kidney disease has not been under consistent active management. She does not smoke.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 140/91 mmHg, pulse 85 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 173 cm, weight 81.7 kg, BMI 27.3 kg/m².

Recent laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 3.02 mg/dL.
Estimated GFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m².
BUN 61 mg/dL.

No peripheral edema, respiratory symptoms, or other examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function and elevated BUN and creatinine. The patient is currently clinically without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, though fatigue may be related to chronic kidney disease and/or her longstanding anemia. Blood pressure is above goal at today’s visit. Longstanding diabetes and hypertension risk are clinically relevant contributors to progression. Disease management requires re-establishment of active outpatient follow-up.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 7 years.

Anemia of chronic disease, duration 14 years.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current renal function and the importance of consistent follow-up to reduce further progression and monitor for complications.

Recommend prompt nephrology referral for evaluation and longitudinal management given creatinine 3.02 mg/dL, eGFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 61 mg/dL. Recommend assessment of urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum electrolytes, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone, as well as renal ultrasound if not recently performed.

Review blood pressure and diabetes control at follow-up, with emphasis on renal-protective management and avoidance of nephrotoxic medications, including nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs unless specifically directed by a clinician. Encourage appropriate hydration without excessive fluid intake and a kidney-conscious, lower-sodium diet pending individualized renal and dietary evaluation.

Monitor fatigue and anemia; reassess with complete blood count and consider further anemia management based on results and renal specialist recommendations.

Advised urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent nausea or vomiting, marked decrease in urination, or new swelling.

Follow up promptly after recommended renal evaluation and laboratory testing, or sooner for any change in symptoms.
