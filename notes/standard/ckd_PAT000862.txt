Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Julian Holland is a 39-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 14-year history of chronic kidney disease and a 7-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. He is currently smoking. He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, changes in urination, and shortness of breath. No uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported today.

Objective

Height 180 cm, weight 83.3 kg, BMI 25.7 kg/m².

Blood pressure 136/84 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results: serum creatinine 5.75 mg/dL, estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 71 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal filtration and significant azotemia. Despite the absence of reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms, the creatinine, BUN, and eGFR represent advanced renal dysfunction requiring prompt specialist assessment and close monitoring. Management is not currently established.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for 7 years, an important potential contributor to chronic kidney disease. Current glycemic control is not documented in the available information.

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Annual preventive health review.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation of advanced chronic kidney disease and discussion of renal replacement planning, including assessment of whether dialysis education or access planning is appropriate. Urgent evaluation should be sought for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, chest pain, or a marked reduction in urine output.

Recommend serum electrolyte panel, repeat renal function testing, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as part of CKD evaluation. Results should be reviewed promptly, with attention to anemia, mineral-bone complications, and electrolyte abnormalities.

Review diabetes management and obtain current glycemic assessment as clinically appropriate. Emphasize individualized diabetic and renal dietary counseling, avoidance of NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications, and avoidance of dehydration. Medication reconciliation is needed before selecting renally adjusted therapies; no current medication regimen is documented here.

Continue close blood pressure monitoring with a renal-protective treatment strategy determined after medication review and specialist evaluation.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and offered evidence-based cessation support. Review immunization status and age-appropriate preventive screening, including cardiovascular risk assessment, during ongoing primary care follow-up.

Follow up promptly after laboratory evaluation and nephrology review, with closer monitoring than a routine annual visit given the degree of renal impairment.
