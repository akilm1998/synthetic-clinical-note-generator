Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Mason Russell is a 44-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 14 years. He reports persistent fatigue. He denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, peripheral edema, changes in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. He does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 12 years, coronary artery disease for 1 year, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 1 year. No current medication regimen is documented.

Objective

Height 171 cm, weight 78.4 kg, BMI 26.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 133/85 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results: serum creatinine 3.65 mg/dL, estimated GFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 73 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with longstanding disease duration and significant reduction in renal filtration, accompanied by elevated creatinine and BUN. The patient has fatigue but no reported symptoms of fluid overload or overt uremic symptoms such as nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, or sleep disturbance. Blood pressure is acceptable at today’s visit. Given the degree of renal impairment and lack of documented active management, he requires prompt comprehensive renal evaluation and coordinated ongoing care.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, with chronic kidney disease as a major complication risk.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism associated with chronic kidney disease.

Coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, requiring consideration in the overall cardiovascular and renal risk reduction plan.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive management of advanced chronic renal impairment, assessment of progression, discussion of renal replacement therapy education when appropriate, and coordination of long-term follow-up.

Obtain or arrange repeat renal function assessment with serum electrolytes, creatinine, BUN, and estimated GFR. Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to quantify proteinuria, complete blood count to assess for renal anemia, and intact parathyroid hormone evaluation for management of secondary hyperparathyroidism. Renal ultrasound is also recommended to assess renal structure and exclude an obstructive process.

Optimize diabetes management with attention to renal function and avoidance of agents that may be unsafe at the current level of kidney function. Review cardiovascular risk reduction and ensure coordination of care for coronary artery disease.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications at the next visit, with avoidance of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potential nephrotoxins. Counsel regarding renal-protective nutrition, avoidance of excess sodium, and monitoring for new edema, reduced urine output, nausea, vomiting, worsening fatigue, confusion, or shortness of breath.

Continue close outpatient follow-up after renal evaluation and updated laboratory testing.
