Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Jason Coleman is a 64-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of anemia present for approximately 19 years. He reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or other cardiopulmonary symptoms. Pallor is noted. He does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include rheumatoid arthritis of approximately 13 years’ duration and malignancy diagnosed approximately 6 years ago. No current anemia-directed treatment is documented.

Objective

Height 169 cm, weight 70.0 kg, BMI 24.5 kg/m². Blood pressure 123/76 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Patient appears clinically stable. Pallor is present. Cardiovascular examination is normal without tachycardia. Respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory results: hemoglobin 9.0 g/dL, hematocrit 27.8%, RBC count 3.27 million/µL, MCV 85 fL, serum iron 35 µg/dL, ferritin 357 ng/mL, TIBC 258 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 13.6%.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic disease. The anemia is normocytic with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and relatively low TIBC, a pattern favoring impaired iron availability in the setting of chronic inflammatory disease and malignancy rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Hemoglobin remains significantly reduced at 9.0 g/dL, although he is currently without fatigue, dyspnea, palpitations, dizziness, or exercise intolerance and has stable vital signs.

Longstanding rheumatoid arthritis and malignancy are important potential contributors. Pallor persists despite minimal subjective symptoms. No evidence of acute cardiopulmonary compromise is present today.

Plan

Obtain or review a repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to confirm stability and assess marrow response. Recommend serum vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as part of the evaluation for potentially reversible contributors and to further characterize the anemia.

Review the status and treatment of the underlying rheumatoid arthritis and malignancy with the appropriate treating clinicians, as control of chronic inflammation and malignancy may improve anemia. Consider hematology consultation given the persistent hemoglobin of 9.0 g/dL, long duration, and comorbid malignancy.

Iron replacement should be considered only after integrating the full clinical evaluation and confirming whether functional iron restriction or true iron deficiency is present; the elevated ferritin argues against empiric treatment based on low serum iron alone. Erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy or intravenous iron would require individualized assessment, particularly in the setting of malignancy and overall treatment goals.

Monitor for development of fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, chest discomfort, or reduced exercise tolerance, with earlier reassessment if these occur.
