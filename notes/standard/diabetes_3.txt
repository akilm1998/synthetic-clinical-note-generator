Chief Complaint

Annual review of longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Kevin Peterson is a 65-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 32 years ago. He reports increased fatigue and unintentional weight loss. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. He reports numbness and tingling involving the hands and/or feet. He does not smoke.

Known comorbidity includes non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, present for approximately 18 years. Current diabetes control has been suboptimal based on the available home and laboratory data. Medication regimen, allergies, and home glucose monitoring practices were not provided and should be reviewed directly with the patient.

Objective

Height 173 cm; weight 77.5 kg; BMI 25.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 133/80 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

HbA1c 8.3%. Fasting glucose 157 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complication, longstanding and currently above individualized glycemic target. HbA1c of 8.3% and fasting glucose of 157 mg/dL indicate inadequate glycemic control. Fatigue and unintentional weight loss may be related to hyperglycemia but warrant clinical reassessment. Numbness and tingling raise concern for possible diabetic peripheral neuropathy, although a complication is not established from the information available.

2. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding, approximately 18 years. This is an important comorbidity in the context of diabetes and requires ongoing metabolic risk reduction and liver-related monitoring.

3. Overweight by BMI, 25.9 kg/m². Weight loss is reported as unintentional and should be clarified, including amount and timing.

4. Preventive health maintenance. Annual review completed with attention to chronic disease risk, diabetes-related screening, cardiovascular risk reduction, immunizations, and age-appropriate cancer screening.

Plan

1. Review current diabetes medications, adherence, tolerability, dietary pattern, physical activity, and home glucose readings before making treatment changes. Given persistent hyperglycemia, discuss intensification of diabetes therapy if not contraindicated, with consideration of comorbid fatty liver disease and renal function when selecting therapy.

2. Counsel regarding a consistent carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, regular physical activity as tolerated, and maintenance of a healthy weight. Because the reported weight loss is unintentional, assess for the degree and duration of loss, appetite changes, gastrointestinal symptoms, and other systemic symptoms.

3. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval and obtain fasting lipid profile, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio for diabetes risk stratification and medication safety. Consider liver enzyme assessment as clinically appropriate for monitoring known non-alcoholic fatty liver disease; no current liver laboratory results are available for review.

4. Perform a comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensory testing, given the reported numbness and tingling. Discuss daily foot inspection and prompt evaluation of skin breakdown or wounds. Recommend routine dilated retinal examination and ongoing dental care.

5. Address cardiovascular risk reduction through continued blood pressure monitoring, lipid assessment, physical activity, weight management, and smoking avoidance. Blood pressure today is 133/80 mmHg, and he reports no tobacco use.

6. Review and update age-appropriate immunizations and preventive screening, including colorectal cancer screening and other routine preventive services based on prior completion and individual risk factors.

7. Follow up after medication and monitoring data are reviewed, with earlier reassessment for progressive weight loss, worsening fatigue, marked hyperglycemic symptoms, visual changes, or new foot wounds.
