Chief Complaint

Annual review of diabetes and chronic medical conditions.

Subjective

Ivy Henderson is a 53-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 22-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. Current laboratory results show HbA1c 9.4% and fasting glucose 227 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimal glycemic control. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, unintentional weight loss, or fatigue. She also denies numbness or tingling of the hands or feet, slow-healing wounds, and frequent infections.

Her medical history includes hypertension for 18 years, chronic kidney disease for 11 years, and obstructive sleep apnea for 15 years. Current medication regimen is not provided. She does not smoke. No medication allergies or other medication history are documented.

Objective

Height 174 cm; weight 68.4 kg; BMI 22.6 kg/m².

Blood pressure 155/95 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

No acute diabetes-related symptoms reported. No diabetic foot symptoms or wound concerns reported.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with poor current glycemic control based on HbA1c 9.4% and fasting glucose 227 mg/dL. Despite significant hyperglycemia, she reports no polyuria, polydipsia, visual symptoms, weight loss, neuropathic symptoms, or recurrent infections.

Hypertension, above goal at today’s reading of 155/95 mmHg.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding; stage not specified. This increases the importance of renal monitoring and careful selection and dosing of diabetes and blood pressure therapies.

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding.

Annual preventive health review.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values are substantially above individualized treatment targets and warrant prompt treatment review. Review current medications, adherence, home glucose monitoring, dietary pattern, and prior treatment tolerance at the next visit. Initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy should be considered based on renal function, comorbidities, hypoglycemia risk, and patient preferences. Reinforced nutrition counseling emphasizing reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, regular physical activity as tolerated, and continued weight maintenance.

Recommend monitoring fasting and postprandial glucose as clinically appropriate and repeating HbA1c after an interval sufficient to assess treatment response.

Recommend renal surveillance with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, particularly given longstanding diabetes and chronic kidney disease. Avoid nephrotoxic medications where possible and ensure any diabetes therapy is appropriate for renal function.

Repeat blood pressure measurement outside the office or at follow-up if feasible. Review current antihypertensive therapy and adherence; treatment intensification should be considered if hypertension remains above goal, with renal status incorporated into medication selection.

Recommend routine comprehensive foot examination, including skin inspection, pulses, and protective sensation, at an appropriate diabetes visit. Recommend dilated retinal examination and ongoing dental care. Review immunizations appropriate for adults with diabetes and chronic kidney disease, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, and hepatitis B vaccination as indicated.

Counseled regarding symptoms requiring earlier assessment, including persistent marked hyperglycemia, vomiting, dehydration, confusion, new visual changes, chest pain, or focal neurologic symptoms.

Follow up promptly for diabetes and blood pressure management after medication and renal laboratory review; continue regular chronic disease follow-up.
