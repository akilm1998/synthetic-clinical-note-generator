Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Andrew Morris is an 86-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 44 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid results remain markedly abnormal. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. No interval hyperlipidemia-related symptoms are reported.

Relevant history includes coronary artery disease of approximately 30 years’ duration and longstanding obesity. He continues to smoke. Weight is 111.5 kg with BMI 35.6 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 130/76 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respirations 20/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 94%.

Height 177 cm; weight 111.5 kg; BMI 35.6 kg/m².

Recent lipid panel:
Total cholesterol 314 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 198 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 38 mg/dL
Triglycerides 388 mg/dL

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled, with marked elevation of total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol, low HDL cholesterol, and elevated triglycerides. His established coronary artery disease, ongoing tobacco use, advanced age, and obesity place him at very high cardiovascular risk despite the absence of current chest discomfort or claudication.

Obesity, BMI 35.6 kg/m².

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid abnormalities and the increased cardiovascular risk in the setting of known coronary artery disease. Recommend initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy, typically a high-intensity statin unless contraindicated or not tolerated; risks, benefits, and patient preferences should be reviewed before prescribing. If LDL cholesterol remains above goal or statin therapy is not tolerated, consider additional therapy such as ezetimibe in follow-up.

Counseled on reducing saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates and excess dietary sugars, increasing fiber-rich foods, and pursuing gradual weight reduction as tolerated. Given triglycerides of 388 mg/dL, advised avoidance of excess alcohol and concentrated sugars.

Strongly encouraged smoking cessation and offered assistance with a cessation plan. Encourage physical activity appropriate to functional status and coronary disease limitations.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after implementation of therapy and lifestyle measures to assess response, with ongoing monitoring for treatment tolerance. Continue routine follow-up for cardiovascular risk-factor management and reassessment of lipid control.
