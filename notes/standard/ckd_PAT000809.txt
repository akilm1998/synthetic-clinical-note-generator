Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Morgan Oliver is a 44-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. She reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, change in urinary frequency, peripheral edema, or shortness of breath. No uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported today. Chronic kidney disease is not currently under active management. She has a history of coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She currently smokes.

Objective

Height 180 cm; weight 75.5 kg; BMI 23.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 139/84 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Serum creatinine 2.75 mg/dL. BUN 66 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 18 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function and azotemia. Although she is presently asymptomatic and has no reported edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, or urine-volume change, the current renal indices warrant prompt re-establishment of active kidney care and close monitoring. Blood pressure is mildly elevated. Coronary artery disease and ongoing tobacco use increase overall cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation and longitudinal management.

Obtain repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound to assess renal structure.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications for renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents, particularly NSAIDs. Medication reconciliation should be completed before initiating or adjusting therapy.

Monitor blood pressure closely and reinforce a kidney-conscious, sodium-restricted diet with individualized nutrition counseling. Encourage smoking cessation given chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease.

Seek urgent medical attention for new shortness of breath, progressive swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, or chest pain. Follow up promptly after renal evaluation and repeat testing.
