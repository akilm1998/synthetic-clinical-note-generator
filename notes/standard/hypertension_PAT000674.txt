Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Naomi Myers is a 55-year-old woman seen for routine follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 20 years ago. Blood pressure has not been actively managed recently. She reports intermittent dizziness but denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. No symptoms suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency are reported.

She has a 1-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic kidney disease. She currently smokes. No antihypertensive medications are documented. Current serum electrolytes show sodium 146 mmol/L and potassium 3.8 mmol/L.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 160/99 mmHg; pulse 71 bpm; respiratory rate 17/min; temperature 36.8°C; oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 169 cm, weight 69.1 kg, BMI 24.2 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 146 mmol/L, potassium 3.8 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal at 160/99 mmHg, with intermittent dizziness but no reported chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, or other features of hypertensive emergency. Blood pressure control is inadequate and requires active outpatient management.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, increasing overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

Chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, relevant to antihypertensive selection and monitoring.

Tobacco use, an additional modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement during the visit if possible and begin home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, including seated readings taken under consistent conditions. Review readings at close follow-up.

Discuss initiation of antihypertensive therapy, with medication selection guided by diabetes, chronic kidney disease, renal function, and potassium level. An ACE inhibitor or ARB may be considered if clinically appropriate, with renal function and serum potassium monitored after initiation or dose adjustment. A calcium-channel blocker or thiazide-type diuretic may also be considered based on the overall regimen and kidney function. No medication is documented as started today.

Recommend a low-sodium, heart-healthy diet, regular physical activity as tolerated, moderation of alcohol if applicable, and smoking cessation counseling. Continue weight maintenance; current BMI is 24.2 kg/m².

Recommend evaluation of renal function and urine protein status, as well as routine cardiovascular risk assessment including lipid profile and ECG. Urinalysis is also appropriate in the setting of hypertension and chronic kidney disease.

Continue coordinated management of diabetes and chronic kidney disease, with attention to blood pressure control and avoidance of nephrotoxic medications when possible.

Seek urgent medical attention for severe or sudden headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, confusion, or markedly higher blood pressure readings with symptoms. Arrange outpatient follow-up within a few weeks to review home readings, treatment response, renal function, and electrolytes.
