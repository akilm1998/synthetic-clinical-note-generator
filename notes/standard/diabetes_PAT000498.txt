Chief Complaint

Annual review of diabetes and chronic medical conditions.

Subjective

Anna Cook is a 68-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 7-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, currently without reported diabetic symptoms or known complications. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet.

Diabetes has not been actively managed. Most recent HbA1c is 9.6% and fasting glucose is 248 mg/dL. She is a nonsmoker. Comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 2 years’ duration and longstanding depression for 34 years. No medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Height 160 cm; weight 66.0 kg; BMI 25.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 140/83 mmHg, pulse 70 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.6%; fasting glucose 248 mg/dL.

No acute distress or symptomatic hyperglycemia is reported. No diabetic foot or neurologic examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, present for 7 years, currently significantly above glycemic target based on HbA1c 9.6% and fasting glucose 248 mg/dL. She is presently asymptomatic, but the degree of hyperglycemia warrants prompt treatment and monitoring. Chronic kidney disease is an important consideration when selecting and dosing diabetes therapy.

Chronic kidney disease, approximately 2 years’ duration; stage is not specified. Renal function and urinary albumin status should guide diabetes and cardiovascular risk management.

Blood pressure is elevated at 140/83 mmHg and should be reassessed in the context of diabetes and chronic kidney disease.

Longstanding depression, present for approximately 34 years; ongoing mood and functional status should be reviewed as part of chronic disease care.

BMI 25.8 kg/m², in the overweight range. No tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of active diabetes management. Review diet, carbohydrate intake, portion control, physical activity, home glucose monitoring, and recognition of symptoms requiring urgent evaluation. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated and individualized nutrition counseling.

Perform medication reconciliation and discuss initiation of pharmacologic glucose-lowering therapy. Obtain renal function before selecting or dosing medication because of chronic kidney disease. Treatment should be individualized to renal function, hypoglycemia risk, cardiovascular risk, cost, and patient preference.

Recommend repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval and periodic fasting or home glucose monitoring. Obtain serum creatinine with estimated GFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess kidney status and guide therapy. Recommend fasting lipid profile and review overall cardiovascular risk.

Arrange a comprehensive diabetic eye examination and perform a complete foot examination, including skin integrity, pulses, and sensation. Counsel regarding daily foot inspection and prompt evaluation of wounds or infection.

Recheck blood pressure at follow-up and review sodium intake, exercise, and home readings if available. Consider antihypertensive management appropriate for diabetes and kidney disease after confirmation of persistent elevation and review of renal results.

Continue routine monitoring of chronic kidney disease and avoid nephrotoxic medications when possible. Review depression symptoms, sleep, functioning, and safety; continue appropriate mental health follow-up based on clinical assessment.

Review preventive care appropriate for age, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, shingles, and tetanus vaccination status, as well as age-appropriate cancer screening and osteoporosis risk assessment. Follow up promptly to establish the diabetes treatment plan and review renal testing; continue regular chronic disease follow-up.
