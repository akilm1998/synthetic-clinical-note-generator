Chief Complaint

Medication review for untreated hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Kevin Peterson is a 49-year-old man presenting for review of lipid management. He has a 19-year history of hyperlipidemia that is currently not being actively treated with a documented lipid-lowering medication regimen. He reports no chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. Medication adherence and tolerance cannot be assessed for lipid therapy because no current lipid-lowering regimen is documented.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 4 years and hypertension for 17 years. He currently smokes. His height is 160 cm and weight is 69.9 kg, with BMI 27.3 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 142/89 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Lipid panel: Total cholesterol 368 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 256 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 38 mg/dL, triglycerides 369 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol, low HDL cholesterol, and elevated triglycerides. The LDL of 256 mg/dL is substantially above goal and, in the setting of long-standing untreated disease, requires prompt pharmacologic management. His overall cardiovascular risk is further increased by diabetes, longstanding hypertension, active tobacco use, and overweight status. He currently denies symptoms suggestive of angina or intermittent claudication.

Hypertension with current blood pressure above usual treatment target.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 4 years, increasing cardiovascular risk.

Tobacco use, an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the need for active treatment. Recommend initiation of a high-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after shared decision-making and medication reconciliation. Emphasized daily adherence and reviewed the importance of reporting new muscle pain, weakness, dark urine, or other concerning adverse effects. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe should be considered.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern with reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess dietary sugars; increased intake of vegetables, fiber, and unsaturated fats; and regular physical activity as tolerated. Weight reduction would also be beneficial.

Because triglycerides are 369 mg/dL, address glycemic control, dietary carbohydrate intake, alcohol exposure, and other secondary contributors before considering additional triglyceride-lowering medication. Recommend repeat fasting lipid testing after initiation and stabilization of therapy to assess response and adherence. Given the markedly elevated LDL, consideration should also be given to evaluation for secondary causes and measurement of ApoB and lipoprotein(a), with assessment for premature cardiovascular disease in first-degree relatives.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered cessation counseling and pharmacologic support options. Continue close management of blood pressure and diabetes with an emphasis on cardiovascular risk reduction. Reassess medication tolerance, adherence, blood pressure, and lipid response at follow-up.
