Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of anemia.

Subjective

Anthony Cooper is a 32-year-old male presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 3-year history of anemia in the setting of rheumatoid arthritis for 2 years, inflammatory bowel disease for 7 years, and chronic kidney disease for 1 year. He reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or other cardiopulmonary symptoms. No pallor has been noted. He does not smoke.

Current height is 185 cm and weight is 96.5 kg, with BMI 28.2 kg/m². No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 129/81 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Recent laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL
Hematocrit 26.5%
RBC count 2.98 million/µL
MCV 89 fL
Serum iron 28 µg/dL
Ferritin 345 ng/mL
TIBC 206 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 13.6%

Assessment

1. Anemia consistent with chronic disease/inflammation

Persistent, clinically asymptomatic anemia for approximately 3 years. Laboratory pattern is normocytic anemia with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and reduced TIBC, which is most consistent with anemia associated with chronic inflammation and chronic disease. Rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, and chronic kidney disease are all relevant contributors. The hemoglobin of 8.8 g/dL and hematocrit of 26.5% warrant active outpatient evaluation despite the absence of reported symptoms. Coexisting iron deficiency should remain under consideration, particularly in the context of inflammatory bowel disease, as ferritin may be increased by inflammation.

2. Rheumatoid arthritis

History of rheumatoid arthritis for 2 years, potentially contributing to ongoing inflammatory anemia. Current disease activity and treatment status require review.

3. Inflammatory bowel disease

History of inflammatory bowel disease for 7 years. This may contribute through chronic inflammation, impaired iron absorption, or gastrointestinal blood loss. Current bowel symptoms and disease activity should be reviewed.

4. Chronic kidney disease

History of chronic kidney disease for 1 year, which may contribute to reduced erythropoietin production and persistent anemia. Renal function and management should be reassessed.

5. Overweight

BMI 28.2 kg/m². Lifestyle counseling is appropriate as part of preventive care and chronic disease risk reduction.

Plan

Repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to confirm persistence and characterize the anemia. Check renal function and inflammatory markers, along with vitamin B12 and folate levels. Reassess iron indices in clinical context; the low transferrin saturation may indicate limited iron availability despite elevated ferritin. Review for gastrointestinal symptoms or evidence of blood loss related to inflammatory bowel disease.

Coordinate ongoing management of rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, and chronic kidney disease with the relevant treating clinicians. Consider hematology and/or nephrology input if anemia persists, worsens, or remains unexplained after initial evaluation. Iron replacement, erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy, or other treatment should be guided by the repeat evaluation, renal status, inflammatory disease activity, and specialist assessment rather than started empirically today.

Seek urgent care for new shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, sustained palpitations, significant dizziness, or evidence of gastrointestinal bleeding.

Preventive care reviewed in the context of the annual visit. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated, gradual weight reduction toward a healthy BMI, balanced nutrition, and continued avoidance of tobacco. Review immunization status and age-appropriate preventive screening at follow-up, with attention to preventive care relevant to chronic inflammatory disease and immunosuppressive treatment if applicable.

Follow-up after repeat laboratory evaluation to review anemia severity and determine next management steps.
