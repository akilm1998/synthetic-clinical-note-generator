Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of hypertension.

Subjective

Lily Peterson is a 75-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of essential hypertension, present for approximately 11 years. Her blood pressure is currently not under active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. She has longstanding chronic kidney disease of approximately 28 years’ duration and type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 10 years ago. She continues to smoke. Current weight is 90.1 kg at a height of 176 cm, with BMI 29.1 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 167/96 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 140 mmol/L, potassium 5.2 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms are reported today. Physical examination findings provided for this visit are otherwise limited.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure, currently untreated or not actively managed. She is asymptomatic without features concerning for hypertensive emergency at this visit, but the degree of elevation warrants timely intervention. Her chronic kidney disease and diabetes increase her cardiovascular and renal risk and make careful selection and monitoring of antihypertensive therapy important. Potassium is mildly elevated at 5.2 mmol/L, which is relevant when considering agents that may further increase potassium.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Overweight by BMI, with ongoing tobacco use, both of which increase overall cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Repeat the blood pressure after a period of rest and arrange home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, including the time of day and associated readings. Review proper measurement technique. Discuss initiation of antihypertensive therapy promptly, with medication selection guided by kidney function, repeat potassium, diabetes status, and overall tolerance. Because potassium is 5.2 mmol/L, repeat serum electrolytes and assess renal function before and shortly after starting or adjusting any agent that can raise potassium.

Recommend dietary sodium reduction, avoidance of potassium-containing salt substitutes unless specifically approved, gradual physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and limitation of alcohol if applicable. Strongly counsel smoking cessation and offer cessation support.

Obtain or review an ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular and kidney risk assessment if not recently completed. Continue coordinated management of chronic kidney disease and diabetes. She should seek urgent medical attention for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, significant visual change, or markedly higher blood pressure readings. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to reassess blood pressure, electrolytes, kidney function, and response to treatment.
