Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Chloe Cox is a 52-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 7 years. She reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, shortness of breath, or change in urination frequency. She does not smoke. Her known comorbidity is anemia of chronic disease, present for approximately 9 years. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Height 153 cm, weight 59.9 kg, BMI 25.6 kg/m². Blood pressure 143/89 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 3.53 mg/dL, estimated GFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 84 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. The patient is currently clinically without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, but the degree of renal impairment warrants prompt reassessment and active renal management. Blood pressure is above goal in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding for approximately 9 years; current severity cannot be assessed from the available information.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease, discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate, and review of reversible contributors. Repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, creatinine, BUN, and estimated GFR. Obtain urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess proteinuria. Recommend CBC to reassess anemia and intact parathyroid hormone testing to evaluate for CKD-related mineral and bone disorder. Consider renal ultrasound as part of structural evaluation.

Review and optimize blood pressure management, with attention to kidney-protective therapy when appropriate. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents, and review all over-the-counter medications and supplements with a clinician before use. Counsel on moderation of dietary sodium and monitoring for new swelling, shortness of breath, reduced urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, worsening fatigue, confusion, or chest pain; urgent evaluation is recommended if these occur.

Continue close outpatient follow-up after repeat testing and nephrology evaluation.
