Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Abigail Reed is a 72-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 20 years. Blood pressure control has not been actively managed recently. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, or nosebleeds. She reports palpitations but no associated cardiopulmonary symptoms. She does not smoke.

Her medical history is also notable for chronic kidney disease of approximately 30 years’ duration and hyperlipidemia for the past 3 years. No current antihypertensive medications were provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 173/97 mmHg, pulse 70 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 173 cm, weight 79.9 kg, BMI 26.7 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 138 mmol/L, potassium 4.0 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure and currently inadequate disease control. She is asymptomatic for acute hypertensive complications today, without chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, visual changes, or severe headache. Given the duration of hypertension and coexisting chronic kidney disease, sustained blood pressure elevation places her at increased risk for cardiovascular and renal complications.

Intermittent palpitations, without associated chest pain, shortness of breath, dizziness, or syncope reported.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management and the risks of persistently elevated readings, including cardiovascular and renal complications. Recommend confirmation with properly performed repeat office measurements and home blood pressure monitoring, with a written log for review.

Given the current reading of 173/97 mmHg and longstanding untreated or inadequately managed hypertension, recommend prompt initiation or reestablishment of an individualized antihypertensive treatment plan, taking chronic kidney disease and electrolyte status into account. Medication selection and dosing should be reviewed at follow-up with attention to renal function and potassium.

Recommend evaluation with renal function testing and urinalysis, as well as review of cardiovascular risk factors including lipid profile. An ECG is appropriate in view of the reported palpitations and longstanding hypertension.

Counseled on reducing dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, moderating alcohol intake if applicable, and continuing smoking avoidance. Encouraged gradual weight management; current BMI is 26.7 kg/m².

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, or acute visual changes. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to reassess blood pressure, palpitations, renal status, and response to treatment.
