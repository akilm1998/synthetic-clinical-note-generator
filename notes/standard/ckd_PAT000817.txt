Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No tobacco use. He has type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 3 years ago. Medication regimen and adherence were not provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal impairment. Although he currently denies nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, edema, dyspnea, urinary changes, or sleep disturbance, the degree of renal dysfunction warrants prompt specialist assessment and evaluation for complications and renal replacement planning.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 3 years, is a relevant potential contributor to chronic kidney disease.

Elevated blood pressure at today’s visit, 150/91 mmHg, requiring reassessment and management in the context of chronic kidney disease.

Overweight by BMI, 27.8 kg/m².

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for assessment of advanced chronic kidney disease, discussion of renal replacement therapy options, and determination of whether urgent dialysis is indicated based on symptoms, volume status, metabolic abnormalities, and overall clinical course.

Obtain or update serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, CBC, intact parathyroid hormone, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate to evaluate complications and characterize kidney disease.

Review diabetes control and establish an individualized glycemic management plan that accounts for severely reduced renal function.

Repeat blood pressure measurement and review home readings if available. Address blood pressure control with kidney-protective goals while avoiding medications or dosing that may be unsafe with the current renal function.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications; avoid NSAIDs and other nephrotoxic exposures, and adjust renally cleared medications as appropriate.

Counseled to seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, severe weakness, rapidly worsening swelling, or markedly decreased urine output.

Return promptly after nephrology evaluation or sooner for any change in symptoms.
