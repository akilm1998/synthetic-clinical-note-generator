Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Patrick Bell is a 97-year-old man with a 42-year history of essential hypertension that has not been under active management. He has known coronary artery disease for approximately 10 years and chronic kidney disease for approximately 52 years. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. He does not smoke.

Objective

Blood pressure 159/101 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%. Height 185 cm, weight 92.4 kg, BMI 27.0 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 137 mmol/L, potassium 3.9 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated blood pressure in an elderly man with longstanding disease, coronary artery disease, and chronic kidney disease. He is currently asymptomatic, with no reported features suggesting an acute hypertensive emergency. The elevated diastolic pressure and lack of active management warrant prompt reassessment and treatment planning, with attention to renal function, coronary disease, fall risk, and avoidance of overly rapid blood pressure reduction.

Coronary artery disease, chronic, approximately 10 years’ duration.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure after a period of seated rest using an appropriately sized cuff and begin home blood pressure monitoring with a written log. Arrange prompt outpatient medication reconciliation and initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy, individualized to his age, coronary artery disease, kidney disease, orthostatic risk, and renal function.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urinalysis before or during treatment planning, along with ECG and lipid profile as clinically appropriate. Continue sodium restriction, avoid excessive alcohol intake, and encourage activity appropriate to functional status.

Seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, confusion, visual loss, or persistent markedly higher blood pressure readings. Close follow-up is recommended to reassess blood pressure and treatment tolerance.
