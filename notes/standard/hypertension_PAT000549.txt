Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Anthony Cooper is a 35-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 11 years ago. He has not been under active treatment for his blood pressure. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis.

Relevant medical history includes coronary artery disease for approximately 1 year and type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 4 years. He currently smokes. Weight is 87.1 kg at a height of 173 cm, with BMI 29.1 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 151/92 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 138 mmol/L, potassium 4.9 mmol/L.

Patient is asymptomatic at today’s visit. No additional examination findings documented.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, persistently elevated today and not currently under active management. No symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive complication at this visit. Long duration of hypertension and concurrent coronary artery disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus increase his cardiovascular risk.

Overweight status with BMI 29.1 kg/m².

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given the longstanding history of hypertension, current reading of 151/92 mmHg, diabetes, coronary artery disease, and ongoing tobacco use. Recommend confirming control with home blood pressure measurements and review of technique; patient should bring a log to the next visit.

Discussed initiation of antihypertensive therapy appropriate for his cardiovascular risk profile, with medication selection and dosing to be finalized after shared decision-making and review of his complete medication history. Serum electrolytes are currently acceptable, with potassium 4.9 mmol/L; repeat renal function and electrolytes should be obtained as clinically indicated after therapy is selected.

Counseled on reducing dietary sodium, limiting alcohol if applicable, increasing regular physical activity as tolerated, pursuing gradual weight reduction, and stopping tobacco use. Advised adherence to diabetes and coronary artery disease care.

Recommend routine cardiovascular risk assessment, including lipid profile, urinalysis, and ECG, as part of hypertension evaluation and comorbidity management. Return for blood pressure reassessment after treatment initiation or adjustment. Seek urgent care for chest pain, severe headache, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated home blood pressure readings.
