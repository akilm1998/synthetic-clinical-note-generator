Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Joseph Bailey is a 76-year-old man, 162 cm tall and weighing 64.0 kg, with a 3-year history of chronic kidney disease and associated secondary hyperparathyroidism and anemia of chronic disease. He reports persistent fatigue. He denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No interval fluid-overload symptoms are reported.

He continues to smoke. Current medication use was not provided and requires review, particularly in the setting of markedly reduced renal function.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 145/86 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Height 162 cm, weight 64.0 kg, BMI 24.4 kg/m².

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 5.12 mg/dL, BUN 62 mg/dL, estimated GFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function

Creatinine is 5.12 mg/dL with BUN 62 mg/dL and estimated GFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m². He has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, sleep disturbance, muscle cramps, edema, oliguria-type urinary change, or dyspnea to suggest clinically apparent uremic or fluid-overload symptoms at this visit. Blood pressure remains above goal at 145/86 mmHg. The degree of renal impairment warrants prompt renal specialist involvement and preparation for potential renal replacement planning, while monitoring for electrolyte, acid-base, volume, and uremic complications.

2. Secondary hyperparathyroidism

Longstanding comorbidity, reported duration 17 years, in the setting of chronic kidney disease. Current calcium, phosphorus, and parathyroid hormone values are not provided.

3. Anemia of chronic disease

Reported duration 33 years. Fatigue may be related in part to chronic anemia and advanced renal dysfunction; no current hemoglobin value is available for review.

4. Tobacco use

He continues to smoke, which increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease and discussion of renal replacement options if clinically indicated. Review prior renal trajectory and current medication list, including use of renal-dose adjustments and avoidance of nephrotoxic medications such as NSAIDs. Assess blood pressure management and home readings; treatment decisions should account for renal function and volume status.

Obtain or review a serum electrolyte panel, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, CBC, intact parathyroid hormone, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess complications and guide management. Renal ultrasound may be considered if structural assessment has not been performed or prior imaging is unavailable.

Continue monitoring for worsening fatigue, nausea, vomiting, pruritus, confusion, progressive reduction in urine output, edema, dyspnea, or other symptoms concerning for uremia or fluid overload; seek urgent evaluation if these develop. Counsel regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of over-the-counter NSAIDs. Follow up closely after renal evaluation and laboratory reassessment.
