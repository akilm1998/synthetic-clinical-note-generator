Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Brooke Snyder is a 91-year-old woman seen for routine follow-up of anemia present for approximately 42 years. She reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, palpitations, shortness of breath, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor is not reported. Anemia has not been actively managed.

Relevant chronic conditions include rheumatoid arthritis for 47 years, chronic infection for 39 years, and heart failure for 6 years. She continues to smoke. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Height 175 cm; weight 76.0 kg; BMI 24.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 125/81 mmHg, pulse 88 bpm, respiratory rate 23/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Hemoglobin 8.2 g/dL, hematocrit 24.7%, RBC 2.78 million/µL, MCV 89 fL. Serum iron 37 µg/dL, ferritin 487 ng/mL, TIBC 236 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 15.7%.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia with significant reduction in hemoglobin and hematocrit. The low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and reduced TIBC are most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of longstanding rheumatoid arthritis and chronic infection. Heart failure and advanced age increase the potential clinical impact of the anemia, although she currently denies cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms and has stable vital signs. The prolonged duration and lack of active management warrant renewed evaluation to confirm the etiology and assess for potentially reversible contributors.

Plan

Obtain or update a complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to characterize the anemia and marrow response.

Recommend vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as part of the anemia evaluation. Review for evidence of blood loss and reassess the activity and treatment of her chronic inflammatory conditions.

Given hemoglobin of 8.2 g/dL, recommend close outpatient follow-up and consideration of hematology consultation for treatment planning, including assessment of whether iron replacement or an erythropoietin-stimulating approach is appropriate in the context of chronic inflammation, heart failure, and overall goals of care. Avoid empiric iron therapy without further evaluation, as ferritin is elevated despite low circulating iron.

Continue monitoring for new fatigue, weakness, dizziness, chest discomfort, dyspnea, palpitations, syncope, or declining exercise tolerance. Seek urgent evaluation for severe or rapidly worsening cardiopulmonary symptoms.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, particularly given coexisting anemia and heart failure.
