Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension and cardiovascular risk-factor management.

Subjective

Kelsey Porter is a 50-year-old woman with an 11-year history of hypertension that has not been actively managed. She presents for interval assessment. She reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. She continues to smoke. There is no reported interval hypertensive emergency or acute cardiopulmonary symptom.

She also has a 12-year history of hyperlipidemia. No current antihypertensive or lipid-lowering medication regimen is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 156/93 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 151 cm, weight 52.2 kg, BMI 22.9 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 138 mmol/L, potassium 4.5 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled. Office blood pressure is persistently elevated at 156/93 mmHg in the setting of 11 years without active management. Intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue are reported, without symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency, including chest pain, dyspnea, severe headache, visual disturbance, or epistaxis. Sodium and potassium are within the provided reference ranges.

2. Hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 12 years. Current lipid values are not available in the supplied information. This remains an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor in the setting of hypertension and active tobacco use.

3. Tobacco use. Active smoking increases her overall cardiovascular risk and may contribute to difficulty achieving blood pressure control.

Plan

Discussed the significance of sustained blood pressure elevation and the increased long-term risk of cardiovascular and renal complications. Recommend confirmation of blood pressure control with validated home measurements, including a written log of morning and evening readings, and review of technique. Given the current reading and longstanding untreated hypertension, initiation of regular antihypertensive pharmacotherapy is recommended following shared decision-making, with adjustment based on home readings, response, and tolerance.

Recommend dietary sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and continued maintenance of a healthy body weight. Smoking cessation was strongly advised; cessation counseling and pharmacologic support options should be offered.

Recommend baseline cardiovascular risk evaluation with lipid profile, urinalysis, and electrocardiogram. Continue monitoring serum electrolytes with antihypertensive treatment, particularly if a diuretic or renin-angiotensin system agent is selected. Further assessment of the reported palpitations and dizziness is warranted if symptoms persist, worsen, or are associated with syncope, chest pain, or shortness of breath.

Follow up after a period of home blood pressure monitoring to reassess control and finalize medication titration. She was advised to seek urgent evaluation for severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, chest pain, shortness of breath, acute visual changes, or markedly elevated blood pressure accompanied by symptoms.
