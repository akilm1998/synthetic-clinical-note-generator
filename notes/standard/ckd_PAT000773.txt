Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Sarah Griffin is a 53-year-old woman with a 12-year history of chronic kidney disease, hypertension for approximately 2 years, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 4 years. She presents for review of treatment effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance. No active medication regimen is documented for review, and there is no reported use of a current CKD-directed or antihypertensive medication regimen to assess for efficacy or adverse effects.

She denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, and shortness of breath. She reports current tobacco use. Her height is 169 cm and weight is 63.4 kg, with BMI 22.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 153/100 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 3.04 mg/dL, estimated GFR 18 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 87 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function, present for approximately 12 years. Current creatinine, eGFR, and BUN are consistent with significant chronic renal impairment. She is presently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, including nausea, pruritus, cramps, fatigue, edema, dyspnea, or urinary changes. No medication effectiveness or tolerance can be determined because an active regimen is not documented.

Hypertension remains above goal today at 153/100 mmHg and represents an important modifiable risk factor for further kidney function decline. Secondary hyperparathyroidism has been present for 4 years; the current biochemical control is not established from the available results.

Tobacco use, which may further increase cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Complete medication reconciliation at the next contact, including prescription, over-the-counter, and nonprescription agents. Assess adherence, access, dosing, and adverse effects before initiating or adjusting therapy. Avoid nephrotoxic medications when possible, including NSAIDs, and review all medications for renal dosing.

Recommend prompt nephrology follow-up for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease and planning based on the trajectory of renal function. Obtain urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum electrolytes, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate to guide renal, anemia, and mineral-bone management. Renal ultrasound may be considered to evaluate renal structure if not previously performed.

Reassess and optimize blood pressure management, with home blood pressure monitoring if available. Reinforce sodium restriction, avoidance of dehydration, and adherence to the eventual prescribed regimen. Counsel regarding smoking cessation and cardiovascular risk reduction.

She should seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, persistent nausea or vomiting, marked reduction in urine output, confusion, or chest pain. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended for blood pressure and kidney disease management.
