Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Diana Black is a 31-year-old woman with a 6-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus presenting for medication review. No current diabetes medication regimen was reported for review, and she does not report medication-related adverse effects. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has a history of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 3 years. She currently smokes. No acute concerns are reported today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 123/76 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 168 cm, weight 80.2 kg, BMI 28.4 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.8%. Fasting glucose 252 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings provided.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, inadequately controlled based on HbA1c of 9.8% and fasting glucose of 252 mg/dL. She is currently asymptomatic despite marked hyperglycemia. No diabetes medication regimen or adherence history was reported, and medication effectiveness and tolerance therefore cannot be assessed at this visit.

Overweight.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, reported duration 3 years.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values are above goal and that lifestyle measures alone are unlikely to provide adequate control. Reviewed the importance of consistent medication use once therapy is initiated, home glucose monitoring as appropriate, nutrition focused on reducing refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, and regular physical activity aimed at gradual weight reduction.

Recommend initiating evidence-based pharmacologic therapy, with metformin considered if renal and hepatic status are appropriate and there are no contraindications. Given the degree of hyperglycemia, discuss need for timely treatment adjustment if control does not improve. Review renal function and hepatic status before finalizing medication selection; consider urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and fasting lipid profile as part of diabetes risk assessment.

Counseled regarding daily foot awareness and routine preventive eye and foot care. Advised smoking cessation and offered support with cessation planning.

Follow up promptly after initiation of therapy to assess adherence, tolerance, and home glucose response, with repeat HbA1c at an appropriate interval. Seek urgent care for vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, inability to maintain hydration, or marked worsening of hyperglycemic symptoms.
