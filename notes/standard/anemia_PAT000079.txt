Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for longstanding anemia.

Subjective

Eliana Matthews is a 58-year-old woman seen for routine outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 15 years. She reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or noted pallor. Her anemia has not been under active treatment. She has a 28-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and a 2-year history of chronic kidney disease, both clinically relevant contributors to chronic anemia. She continues to smoke. No current medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Height 160 cm, weight 52.5 kg, BMI 20.5 kg/m². Blood pressure 132/87 mmHg, pulse 92 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin 8.9 g/dL, hematocrit 26.2%, RBC count 2.88 million/µL, and MCV 91 fL. Serum iron is 34 µg/dL, ferritin 305 ng/mL, TIBC 242 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 14%. This is a normocytic anemia with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and reduced iron-binding capacity.

Assessment

Longstanding anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation in the setting of chronic kidney disease and longstanding diabetes mellitus. The normocytic indices and iron studies support impaired iron availability rather than straightforward absolute iron deficiency, although the low transferrin saturation warrants further evaluation. Hemoglobin is significantly reduced, but she currently denies cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms of anemia. Pulse is mildly elevated and respiratory rate is mildly increased, with preserved oxygen saturation.

Plan

Discussed the laboratory pattern and the likely contribution of chronic kidney disease and chronic inflammation. Recommend repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to assess marrow response and confirm the current pattern. Recommend renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers as part of the anemia evaluation. Review for potential sources of blood loss and nutritional factors at follow-up.

Given the degree and duration of anemia, recommend coordination with the clinician managing her chronic kidney disease to determine whether renal-related anemia treatment, including consideration of iron replacement or an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent, is appropriate after completion of the evaluation. No iron or erythropoietin therapy is initiated in this note because no current regimen or treatment decision was provided.

Counseled to seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, palpitations, marked weakness, or evidence of bleeding. Continue routine follow-up for anemia and chronic disease management.
