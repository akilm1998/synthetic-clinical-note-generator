Chief Complaint

Annual review and chronic kidney disease follow-up.

Subjective

David Parker is a 76-year-old male presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 46-year history of chronic kidney disease and a 15-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. Current management of his chronic kidney disease has not been established. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. He is a current smoker. No medication list or allergies were provided for review.

Objective

Height 188 cm; weight 97.5 kg; BMI 27.6 kg/m².

Blood pressure 148/90 mmHg, pulse 81/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 5.54 mg/dL.
BUN 73 mg/dL.
Estimated GFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m².

No edema, pallor, asterixis, or other examination findings were documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal filtration and significantly elevated creatinine and BUN. Although he is currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, these results represent advanced renal dysfunction requiring prompt specialist assessment and structured management. The reported 46-year duration and coexisting long-standing type 2 diabetes are important risk factors.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 15 years, requiring ongoing renal-protective risk-factor management.

Elevated blood pressure at today’s visit.

Current tobacco use.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation and management of severe chronic kidney disease, including discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if clinically indicated. Advise urgent medical evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, markedly reduced urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.

Obtain updated renal and metabolic assessment, including serum electrolytes, repeat creatinine and BUN, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and urinalysis. Consider renal ultrasound as part of the etiologic evaluation if not previously completed.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplemental medications at the next visit and avoid nephrotoxic agents, particularly nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. Medication dosing should be adjusted for renal function. Assess blood pressure and diabetes control closely, with treatment directed toward renal and cardiovascular risk reduction.

Counsel regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of tobacco exposure. Reinforce a kidney-conscious diet with attention to sodium intake and individualized protein, potassium, phosphorus, and fluid recommendations in conjunction with nephrology and dietary guidance.

Continue routine preventive care appropriate for age, including immunization review, cancer screening based on prior screening history and life expectancy, fall-risk assessment, and cardiovascular risk assessment. Schedule close primary care follow-up after renal evaluation and review of repeat testing.
