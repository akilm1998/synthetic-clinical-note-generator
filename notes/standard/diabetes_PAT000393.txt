Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated diabetes laboratory results.

Subjective

Charlotte Bailey is a 31-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. Recent HbA1c is 9.7% and fasting glucose is 229 mg/dL. Diabetes is not currently under active management. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet.

Relevant comorbidities include non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 5 years, dyslipidemia for 1 year, and anxiety for 2 years. She does not smoke.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 123/76 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 177 cm, weight 68.0 kg, BMI 21.7 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.7%; fasting glucose 229 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently significantly above glycemic goal based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. Patient is asymptomatic without reported neuropathic, visual, infectious, or constitutional symptoms. No acute metabolic symptoms reported.

Dyslipidemia.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Anxiety.

Plan

Discussed laboratory results and the importance of prompt active diabetes management despite absence of symptoms. Recommend timely primary care follow-up to establish a medication plan; pharmacologic therapy should be considered given the degree of hyperglycemia. Reviewed nutrition, reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, regular physical activity as tolerated, and home glucose monitoring.

Recommend evaluation of renal status with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, along with a fasting lipid profile, as clinically appropriate. Recommend routine diabetic eye and foot assessments. Educated regarding symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including persistent vomiting, abdominal pain, marked weakness, confusion, inability to maintain hydration, or rapidly worsening polyuria/polydipsia.

Continue monitoring and management of dyslipidemia and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease in primary care. Follow up promptly for diabetes treatment initiation and reassessment.
