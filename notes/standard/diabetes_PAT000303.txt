Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Jordan Spencer is an 87-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 16 years ago. Diabetes is currently not actively managed. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Recent laboratory results show HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 235 mg/dL. He has no reported symptoms of acute hyperglycemia. Relevant comorbidities include hypertension for 38 years, obstructive sleep apnea for 3 years, and anxiety for 14 years. He does not smoke.

Objective

Height 188 cm; weight 80.6 kg; BMI 22.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 142/82 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.3%; fasting glucose 235 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications. Glycemic control is significantly above goal based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. Despite the degree of hyperglycemia, he currently denies polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, or infectious complications. His BMI is within the normal range. Diabetes has been present for approximately 16 years and is not currently being actively managed.

Hypertension with blood pressure mildly elevated today.

Obstructive sleep apnea.

Anxiety.

Plan

Discussed the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose, the risks of persistent hyperglycemia, and the importance of consistent diabetes management. Confirm current medication use, adherence, allergies, dietary pattern, and home glucose monitoring at the next medication reconciliation before selecting a treatment regimen. Given his age and prolonged period of inadequate control, treatment should be individualized with attention to renal function, hypoglycemia risk, nutritional status, functional status, and patient preferences.

Recommend obtaining renal function, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and a fasting lipid profile to guide safe and comprehensive diabetes management. Encourage a balanced carbohydrate-controlled diet, regular physical activity as tolerated, adequate hydration, and home glucose monitoring if a monitoring plan is established.

Review daily foot care and advise prompt evaluation for skin breakdown, wounds, infection, new numbness, or visual changes. Recommend routine diabetic eye and foot assessment as clinically appropriate. Discuss symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including vomiting, marked weakness, confusion, inability to maintain hydration, or rapidly worsening hyperglycemic symptoms.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and address cardiovascular risk factors as part of diabetes care. Follow up after medication and laboratory review to establish an active, individualized treatment plan and reassess glycemic control.
