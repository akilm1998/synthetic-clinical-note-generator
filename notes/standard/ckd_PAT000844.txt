Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for chronic kidney disease.

History of Present Illness

David Parker is a 34-year-old man, height 190 cm, weight 97.5 kg, BMI 27.0 kg/m², presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of chronic kidney disease identified approximately one year ago. He has type 2 diabetes mellitus of approximately two years’ duration. Current renal indices are markedly abnormal, with serum creatinine 4.44 mg/dL, BUN 63 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 11 mL/min/1.73 m².

He currently denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, and shortness of breath. He is a current smoker. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath.

Cardiovascular: Denies peripheral edema.

Gastrointestinal: Denies nausea.

Genitourinary: Denies changes in urinary frequency.

Skin: Denies pruritus.

Neuromuscular: Denies muscle cramps.

Sleep: Denies sleep disturbance.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 132/89 mmHg, pulse 71 beats/min, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory data: Serum creatinine 4.44 mg/dL. BUN 63 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 11 mL/min/1.73 m².

Physical examination findings provided today are limited to the above vital signs. The patient reports no symptoms suggestive of current volume overload or uremic prodrome.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced kidney function, present for approximately one year, in the setting of type 2 diabetes mellitus. Creatinine is 4.44 mg/dL with BUN 63 mg/dL and estimated GFR 11 mL/min/1.73 m². Despite the absence of fatigue, nausea, pruritus, edema, dyspnea, urinary changes, or sleep disturbance, these laboratory findings represent advanced renal impairment requiring prompt specialist evaluation and close monitoring.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately two years ago, is a significant potential contributor to chronic kidney disease. The available information does not establish the underlying renal etiology.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for comprehensive evaluation of the markedly reduced estimated GFR and determination of the appropriate renal replacement therapy planning timeline. This should include assessment for reversible contributors, review of diabetes management, and counseling regarding kidney-protective care.

Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, and calcium, along with complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone measurement. Renal ultrasound is recommended to assess renal structure and exclude obstruction.

Review blood pressure and diabetes control closely at follow-up. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents, and review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal products with a clinician before use. Dietary counseling focused on appropriate sodium, protein, potassium, and phosphorus intake should be coordinated with nephrology and dietary services after laboratory assessment.

Smoking cessation was strongly advised because of its adverse renal and cardiovascular effects.

He was instructed to seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, markedly decreased urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest discomfort, or severe weakness. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended after nephrology evaluation and repeat laboratory testing.
