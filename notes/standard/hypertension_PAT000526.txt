Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Noah Hamilton is a 73-year-old man seen for outpatient follow-up of hypertension present for approximately 36 years. He is not currently receiving active management for his elevated blood pressure. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. He does not smoke.

He has a history of chronic kidney disease of approximately 20 years’ duration. No interval renal symptoms are reported. Current serum electrolytes show sodium 142 mmol/L and potassium 4.7 mmol/L.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 176/95 mmHg; pulse 71 bpm; respiratory rate 18/min; temperature 36.8°C; oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 160 cm, weight 54.5 kg, BMI 21.3 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Sodium 142 mmol/L. Potassium 4.7 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure, currently without reported symptoms suggestive of acute end-organ injury. Long duration of hypertension and lack of active management increase his cardiovascular and renal risk.

Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 20 years. Potassium is currently within an acceptable range at 4.7 mmol/L; renal function values are not available in today’s data. Blood pressure control is particularly important in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Plan

Discussed the significantly elevated blood pressure and the importance of prompt, consistent treatment to reduce risk of stroke, cardiovascular disease, and further kidney injury. Repeat blood pressure measurement during the visit and obtain home blood pressure readings if feasible, using a validated upper-arm cuff and recording readings for review.

Recommend initiation of antihypertensive therapy with consideration of kidney-protective treatment, selected and dosed in the context of chronic kidney disease and followed with appropriate renal function and electrolyte monitoring. Review potential medication risks and adherence at the time therapy is started.

Recommend evaluation with renal function testing, urinalysis, ECG, and lipid profile as clinically appropriate for longstanding hypertension and chronic kidney disease. Counsel on limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, and avoiding excess alcohol intake.

Because he is asymptomatic with no reported chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic deficit, or visual change, there is no current symptom-based indication of hypertensive emergency. He was instructed to seek emergency care for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, marked visual changes, or other acute concerning symptoms.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment adjustment.
