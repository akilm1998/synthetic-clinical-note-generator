Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for anemia.

Subjective

Ivy Henderson is a 67-year-old woman, height 159 cm, weight 64.0 kg, BMI 25.3 kg/m², presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of anemia. She has a 7-year history of malignancy, which is the relevant chronic comorbidity in the current assessment. Her anemia has not previously been actively managed.

She currently denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, or noticeable pallor. No smoking history. Medication and allergy information were not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 125/76 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.5 g/dL
Hematocrit 26.1%
RBC count 3.14 million/µL
MCV 83 fL
Serum iron 32 µg/dL
Ferritin 253 ng/mL
TIBC 265 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 12.1%

The anemia is normocytic with reduced serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated/normal iron stores as reflected by ferritin, and relatively low iron-binding capacity.

Assessment

Anemia of chronic disease/inflammation in the setting of a 7-year history of malignancy, currently untreated. Hemoglobin is significantly reduced at 8.5 g/dL, with corresponding low hematocrit and RBC count. The normocytic indices and iron studies, particularly low serum iron and transferrin saturation with ferritin 253 ng/mL and TIBC 265 µg/dL, are most consistent with impaired iron availability related to chronic inflammation rather than uncomplicated iron depletion. She is presently hemodynamically stable and reports no cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms.

Plan

Discussed the laboratory pattern and likely relationship between chronic disease and reduced red blood cell production.

Recommend coordination with the clinician managing her malignancy to review disease activity and determine whether anemia-directed therapy is appropriate. Consider hematology consultation, particularly given hemoglobin of 8.5 g/dL and the absence of prior management.

Recommend further outpatient evaluation with repeat CBC and reticulocyte count, peripheral blood smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12 and folate levels, and inflammatory markers. Consider assessment for occult blood loss if clinically indicated.

Iron supplementation should not be started empirically based solely on the low serum iron, given the ferritin level and overall pattern; iron replacement decisions should be guided by the malignancy and chronic disease treatment team. Depending on the underlying malignancy and broader evaluation, intravenous iron or an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent may be considered by the appropriate specialist.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, palpitations, marked weakness, or evidence of bleeding. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review additional evaluation and treatment recommendations.
