Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and abnormal renal function.

Subjective

Kayla Pierce is a 40-year-old woman presenting for urgent outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. She is not currently reporting fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No smoking history. Comorbidities include gout for 1 year and hyperlipidemia for 2 years. Current medication regimen was not provided.

Objective

Height 155 cm, weight 65.1 kg, BMI 27.1 kg/m². Blood pressure 143/91 mmHg, pulse 68 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 2.86 mg/dL, BUN 84 mg/dL, estimated GFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. Although currently asymptomatic without reported volume-overload or uremic symptoms, these results require prompt reassessment and specialist involvement. Blood pressure is above goal today. Gout and hyperlipidemia are additional cardiovascular and renal risk factors.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology referral for evaluation and ongoing management of significant renal impairment. Repeat renal function testing and obtain serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound to assess kidney structure and exclude obstructive pathology.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications with a clinician before use; avoid NSAIDs and other potential nephrotoxins. Counsel regarding blood pressure control, moderation of dietary sodium, appropriate hydration, and avoidance of unapproved supplements. Management of gout and hyperlipidemia should be coordinated with renal function in mind.

Seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness. Close primary care follow-up after repeat testing and nephrology evaluation.
