Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Charles Bennett is a 94-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 58 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. He denies chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, dizziness, or other reported cardiovascular symptoms. He notes fatty deposits involving the skin and eyelids, consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma.

He has known coronary artery disease of approximately 47 years’ duration and type 2 diabetes mellitus of approximately 11 years’ duration. He does not smoke. Current weight is 87.4 kg at 178 cm, with BMI 27.6 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 123/80 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Lipid panel: Total cholesterol 336 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 230 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 31 mg/dL, triglycerides 375 mg/dL.

Visible xanthomas and xanthelasma are reported. No active chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness reported during this encounter.

Assessment

Longstanding untreated hyperlipidemia with marked elevation of LDL cholesterol and triglycerides and low HDL cholesterol, consistent with significant mixed dyslipidemia. The LDL of 230 mg/dL is particularly concerning in the setting of established coronary artery disease and diabetes mellitus. The presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma supports prolonged exposure to elevated atherogenic lipids. Despite the absence of current ischemic symptoms, his overall cardiovascular risk is very high.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, without reported active anginal symptoms today.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the lipid results and the importance of active risk-factor reduction given the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, hypertriglyceridemia, established coronary artery disease, and diabetes. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy, with a statin-based regimen considered first if clinically tolerated, followed by addition of nonstatin therapy if LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated.

Recommend review of potential secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and confirmation of adherence to dietary and treatment recommendations before adjustment of therapy. A repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment would be appropriate to assess response. Consider measurement of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a) for additional risk characterization if this would influence management; coronary calcium scoring is unlikely to add meaningful information in the setting of established coronary artery disease.

Counseled regarding reduction of saturated and trans fats, limitation of refined carbohydrates and excess dietary sugars, maintenance of regular physical activity as tolerated, and weight control. Continue avoidance of tobacco.

Advised to seek urgent evaluation for new or persistent chest discomfort, dyspnea, syncope, or exertional leg pain. Continue coordinated management of coronary artery disease and diabetes mellitus. Follow up after treatment discussion and repeat lipid assessment.
