Chief Complaint

Annual review of longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Morgan Oliver is a 64-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease assessment. She has a 12-year history of essential hypertension that is currently not under active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. She is a nonsmoker. Weight is 48.5 kg at a height of 152 cm, with BMI 21.0 kg/m².

Her medical history is also notable for coronary artery disease of approximately 3 years’ duration and chronic kidney disease of approximately 6 years’ duration. No current antihypertensive regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Blood pressure is 175/100 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, and oxygen saturation 96%. Serum sodium is 140 mmol/L and potassium is 4.8 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms are reported today. A complete examination should focus on volume status, cardiac findings, peripheral pulses, and evidence of hypertensive end-organ involvement.

Assessment

Longstanding essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure and no reported symptoms suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency. Given the duration of untreated or inadequately managed hypertension, coexisting coronary artery disease, and chronic kidney disease, cardiovascular and renal risk is substantial. The potassium level of 4.8 mmol/L is within the reported range but warrants consideration when selecting therapies that may further increase serum potassium.

Coronary artery disease, established comorbidity, requiring coordinated risk-factor management.

Chronic kidney disease, approximately 6 years’ duration, requiring renal-protective blood pressure management and monitoring of renal function and electrolytes.

Plan

Repeat the blood pressure measurement after seated rest using an appropriately sized cuff and confirm the degree of elevation with home or ambulatory readings if feasible. Given the current reading and significant cardiovascular and renal comorbidity, prompt clinician-directed initiation or re-establishment of antihypertensive therapy is recommended rather than continued observation alone. Treatment selection should account for coronary artery disease, chronic kidney disease, renal function, and potassium level; an ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker may be considered when clinically appropriate, with close monitoring of renal function and potassium after initiation or dose adjustment. A calcium-channel blocker or thiazide-type diuretic may also be considered based on renal function, volume status, and overall regimen.

Obtain or review renal function, repeat serum electrolytes after treatment changes, urinalysis, lipid profile, and an ECG as part of cardiovascular and hypertensive end-organ assessment. Continue coordinated management of coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease.

Counseled regarding a low-sodium dietary pattern, avoidance of excessive alcohol, regular physical activity as tolerated, and adherence to a structured blood pressure-monitoring plan. Her normal BMI and nonsmoking status are favorable factors.

She should seek urgent medical attention for new chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, focal neurologic symptoms, visual change, confusion, or markedly higher blood pressure readings. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after blood pressure reassessment and treatment initiation or adjustment, rather than waiting for the next annual visit. Age-appropriate preventive care, including immunizations and recommended cancer, bone health, and cardiovascular risk screening, should be reviewed and updated as appropriate.
