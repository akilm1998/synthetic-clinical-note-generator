Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated cholesterol and triglycerides.

Subjective

Hayden Johnston is a 77-year-old male presenting for focused follow-up of hyperlipidemia present for approximately 19 years and currently not under active treatment. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 334 mg/dL, LDL-C 220 mg/dL, HDL-C 36 mg/dL, and triglycerides 389 mg/dL. He denies chest discomfort, dizziness, or intermittent claudication. He reports xanthomas and xanthelasma.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 31 years, hypertension for 32 years, and obesity for 28 years. He continues to smoke. Weight is 104.3 kg at 167 cm. No medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 147/90 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respirations 21/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air. BMI 37.4 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Total cholesterol 334 mg/dL; LDL-C 220 mg/dL; HDL-C 36 mg/dL; triglycerides 389 mg/dL.

Cutaneous findings by history include xanthomas and xanthelasma. No active chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness reported.

Assessment

Severe untreated mixed dyslipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and triglycerides and low HDL-C. The degree and chronicity of LDL elevation, together with xanthomas/xanthelasma, are concerning for a substantial atherogenic lipid burden. He has established coronary artery disease and multiple additional cardiovascular risk factors, including advanced age, active tobacco use, hypertension, and obesity.

Hypertension with current blood pressure above goal.

Severe obesity.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Recommend prompt initiation of guideline-directed high-intensity lipid-lowering therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after medication reconciliation and review of contraindications. Given established coronary artery disease and LDL-C of 220 mg/dL, treatment should target a substantial LDL reduction; ezetimibe and/or additional specialty therapy may be considered if LDL remains elevated or statin therapy is not tolerated.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid testing after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, with consideration of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk characterization. Evaluate for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia with appropriate metabolic and hepatic testing.

Counseled on a heart-healthy, low-saturated-fat and low-refined-carbohydrate diet, weight reduction, and regular activity as tolerated. Strongly advised smoking cessation. Blood pressure management should be reassessed concurrently.

Because of his coronary artery disease and markedly abnormal untreated lipid profile, close outpatient follow-up is recommended after treatment is initiated. He should seek urgent evaluation for new chest discomfort, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain.
