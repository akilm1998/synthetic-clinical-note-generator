Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Zoe Butler is a 49-year-old woman, BMI 27.2 kg/m², presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 9 years ago. She reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, or shortness of breath. No uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported at this visit.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed 1 year ago, gout for 9 years, and anemia of chronic disease for 5 years. She currently smokes. Current CKD management is not established as active. Medication use, allergies, and other social history were not provided.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 146/87 mmHg, pulse 87 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 158 cm; weight 67.9 kg; BMI 27.2 kg/m².

Laboratory data:
Serum creatinine 3.05 mg/dL.
Estimated GFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m².
BUN 86 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of approximately 9 years’ duration with markedly reduced estimated filtration and significant azotemia. Although she is currently asymptomatic and has no reported edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, or sleep disturbance, the renal indices indicate advanced kidney dysfunction requiring prompt structured evaluation and active management.

Blood pressure is elevated at today’s visit and may contribute to ongoing renal injury. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, gout, and anemia of chronic disease are relevant comorbidities requiring coordinated management in the context of renal impairment. Ongoing tobacco use and overweight status are additional modifiable cardiovascular and renal risk factors.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management and discussion of renal replacement planning if clinically indicated by trajectory and subsequent assessment.

Repeat renal function and serum electrolytes promptly, with monitoring of creatinine, eGFR, and BUN trends. Obtain urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to quantify proteinuria. Consider renal ultrasound, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone assessment as part of CKD evaluation and complication surveillance.

Review and optimize blood pressure and diabetes management with renal dosing of any medications. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents; review all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use. Gout therapy should be reassessed for renal appropriateness.

Evaluate and follow anemia of chronic disease in the context of CKD. Counsel regarding smoking cessation, moderation of dietary sodium, avoidance of dehydration, and maintenance of a kidney-appropriate diet pending formal dietary guidance.

She should seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, edema, markedly reduced urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after repeat testing and nephrology assessment.
