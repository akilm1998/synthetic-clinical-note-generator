Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Claire PriceLucy Sanders is an 87-year-old woman seen for outpatient follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 20 years. Her lipid disorder is currently not under active treatment. Recent results show markedly elevated cholesterol and triglycerides. She denies chest discomfort, dizziness, or intermittent claudication. She has visible xanthomas and xanthelasma. She continues to smoke.

Relevant history includes coronary artery disease for approximately 20 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 47 years. No medication regimen or allergies were provided for review today.

Objective

Height 168 cm; weight 71.1 kg; BMI 25.2 kg/m². Blood pressure 134/76 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 94%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 346 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 252 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 39 mg/dL, triglycerides 273 mg/dL.

Xanthomas and xanthelasma are reported. No chest discomfort, dizziness, or exertional leg pain reported at this visit.

Assessment

Severe untreated hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, in the setting of longstanding coronary artery disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus. HDL cholesterol is low. The presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma is consistent with significant chronic lipid elevation. She is currently asymptomatic from an anginal or claudication standpoint, but her age, ongoing tobacco use, diabetes, and known coronary artery disease place her at very high cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the substantially elevated LDL cholesterol and triglycerides and the importance of lipid-lowering therapy for secondary cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of a statin, with consideration of a moderate- or high-intensity regimen as tolerated given her age and established coronary artery disease. If LDL cholesterol remains significantly elevated despite tolerated statin therapy, addition of ezetimibe or referral for further lipid management may be considered.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment is initiated or adjusted to assess response. Consider evaluation for additional risk markers, including apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a), if results would affect management. Coronary calcium scoring is unlikely to add meaningful information in the setting of established coronary artery disease.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy diet low in saturated and trans fats, avoidance of tobacco, regular physical activity as tolerated, and maintenance of current healthy weight. Smoking cessation counseling provided. Reviewed warning symptoms, including new chest discomfort, shortness of breath, dizziness, or exertional leg pain, which should prompt urgent medical evaluation.

Follow up after treatment discussion and repeat lipid testing to reassess tolerance, adherence, and lipid response.
