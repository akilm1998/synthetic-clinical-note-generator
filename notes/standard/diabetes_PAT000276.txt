Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Riley Benson is an 89-year-old man with a 14-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension for 22 years, and chronic kidney disease for 32 years. He presents for outpatient review of diabetes treatment effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance. No specific current diabetes medication regimen is identified in the available treatment information, so medication effectiveness or adverse effects cannot be attributed to a particular agent.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet. He continues to smoke. There are no reported medication-related symptoms.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 157/83 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 173 cm, weight 66.1 kg, BMI 22.1 kg/m².

Recent laboratory results show HbA1c 9.7% and fasting glucose 210 mg/dL, consistent with persistent hyperglycemia. No additional laboratory results are available for review today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently inadequately controlled based on HbA1c of 9.7% and fasting glucose of 210 mg/dL. Despite the degree of hyperglycemia, he reports no classic hyperglycemic symptoms and no symptoms suggestive of peripheral neuropathy or active infection. Given his advanced age and longstanding chronic kidney disease, treatment should be individualized with attention to renal function, hypoglycemia risk, treatment burden, and tolerability.

Hypertension with blood pressure above goal today.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding; current renal function is important for diabetes medication selection and dosing.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Complete a careful medication reconciliation with the patient and pharmacy, including confirmation of any current diabetes, blood pressure, and kidney-related medications, adherence, dosing, and tolerance. Because the diabetes regimen is not established in the current record and glycemic control is poor, discuss initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy using a gradual, individualized approach. Avoid hypoglycemia-prone treatment whenever possible and review renal function before selecting or dosing medications, particularly metformin or other agents affected by kidney function.

Review home glucose monitoring technique and establish a practical monitoring schedule appropriate for his treatment plan. Educate regarding symptoms of hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia, regular meals, hydration, and when to seek urgent care.

Encourage a balanced carbohydrate-conscious diet and continued physical activity as safely tolerated, with attention to maintaining his current healthy body weight. Reinforce smoking cessation and offer cessation support.

Reassess blood pressure and review adherence and tolerance of antihypertensive therapy during medication reconciliation. Coordinate diabetes and kidney disease management with the patient’s treating clinicians as clinically appropriate.

Follow up after medication reconciliation and treatment adjustment to assess glucose readings, adherence, tolerance, and safety.
