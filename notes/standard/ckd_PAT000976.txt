Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease and other chronic conditions.

Subjective

Sydney Fuller is an 81-year-old woman, height 152 cm, weight 66.3 kg, BMI 28.7 kg/m², presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 37-year history of chronic kidney disease, with longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus for 19 years, hyperlipidemia for 45 years, and anemia of chronic disease for 26 years. She reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, or shortness of breath. She does not smoke. No medication list or active renal treatment regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 143/85 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory data: Serum creatinine 3.71 mg/dL, estimated glomerular filtration rate 16 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 71 mg/dL.

No additional physical examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of longstanding duration with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN and creatinine. She is currently clinically without reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms, but the degree of renal impairment requires close monitoring and active management. Blood pressure is above an ideal renal-protective target.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for 19 years, an important contributor to chronic kidney disease risk. Current glycemic control cannot be assessed from the available information.

Hypertension by current blood pressure reading, with chronic kidney disease increasing cardiovascular and renal risk.

Anemia of chronic disease, present for 26 years. Current hemoglobin and iron indices are not available for assessment.

Hyperlipidemia, present for 45 years, contributing to elevated cardiovascular risk.

Overweight by BMI at 28.7 kg/m².

Plan

Arrange prompt comprehensive renal assessment, including repeat serum creatinine, eGFR, BUN, electrolytes, bicarbonate, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously performed or if clinically indicated. Monitor for development of edema, dyspnea, nausea, vomiting, pruritus, confusion, muscle cramps, or significant change in urine output; seek urgent evaluation if these occur.

Recommend nephrology referral for management of significantly reduced renal function, assessment of complications, medication and dietary review, and discussion of future renal replacement planning if appropriate. Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal products; avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed. Ensure medication dosing is adjusted for renal function.

Reassess blood pressure with home readings if feasible and address lifestyle measures including sodium restriction, avoidance of excess processed foods, and appropriate physical activity. Antihypertensive therapy should be reviewed or initiated based on medication reconciliation, renal status, potassium level, and overall goals of care.

Review diabetes management and obtain appropriate glycemic monitoring, including hemoglobin A1c, while individualizing targets for age, comorbidity burden, and renal function. Continue attention to nutrition and avoidance of hypoglycemia.

Evaluate chronic anemia with CBC, iron studies, vitamin B12, and folate as clinically appropriate. Treatment should be guided by results and renal specialist input.

Review cardiovascular risk reduction, including lipid management, diet, activity, and confirmation of medication use. Encourage gradual weight management as tolerated.

Complete age-appropriate preventive care review, including immunization status, fall-risk assessment, vision and hearing care, bone health, and individualized cancer screening based on overall health and preferences. Follow up after renal and chronic disease laboratory evaluation, or sooner for any new symptoms.
