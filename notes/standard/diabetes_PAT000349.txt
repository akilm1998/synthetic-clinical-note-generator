Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Cynthia Montgomery is a 94-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 17 years ago. Diabetes is currently not actively managed. Recent HbA1c is 9.3% and fasting glucose is 205 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimal glycemic control.

She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No hypoglycemic symptoms are reported. She does not smoke.

Comorbidity includes non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, present for approximately 48 years.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 127/79 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 176 cm; weight 90.8 kg; BMI 29.3 kg/m².

Laboratory data: HbA1c 9.3%. Fasting glucose 205 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, present for approximately 17 years, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 205 mg/dL. Despite biochemical hyperglycemia, she reports no classic hyperglycemic symptoms, constitutional symptoms, neuropathic complaints, or wound-related concerns.

At age 94, treatment goals should be individualized with emphasis on avoiding symptomatic hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia, minimizing treatment burden, and considering functional status and overall health. Renal function is relevant before selecting or dosing glucose-lowering therapy. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease is a longstanding comorbidity.

Overweight by BMI, with BMI 29.3 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of re-establishing active diabetes management. Recommend a simplified, individualized treatment approach appropriate for advanced age, with avoidance of overly aggressive glycemic lowering.

Review renal function before initiating or adjusting diabetes medication. Metformin may be considered if renal function and overall tolerance permit; medication selection should also account for nutritional intake, frailty, gastrointestinal tolerance, and hypoglycemia risk. Agents with substantial hypoglycemia risk should be used cautiously.

Encourage a consistent, carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, adequate hydration, and physical activity appropriate to her mobility and safety. Avoid restrictive dieting or weight-loss strategies that could compromise nutrition.

Recommend home glucose monitoring if feasible, particularly during treatment initiation or adjustment, with education regarding symptoms of hypoglycemia and marked hyperglycemia. Seek prompt medical attention for confusion, inability to maintain oral intake, persistent vomiting, severe weakness, or other acute change in condition.

Recommend assessment for diabetes-related complications, including a comprehensive foot examination, retinal evaluation, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, and fasting lipid profile. Continue monitoring of liver disease in the context of diabetes management.

Follow up after treatment planning and review of recommended baseline assessments to reassess glycemic control, tolerability, and safety.
