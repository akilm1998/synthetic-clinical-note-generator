Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Emma Carter is a 78-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 32 years. She reports intermittent headache but denies current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. No acute cardiopulmonary concerns are reported.

Her medical history is notable for inflammatory bowel disease of approximately 15 years’ duration and malignancy of approximately 22 years’ duration, both of which may contribute to chronic inflammation and anemia. She currently smokes. No anemia-directed medication regimen is documented.

Objective

Height 176 cm; weight 79.6 kg; BMI 25.7 kg/m².

Blood pressure 123/81 mmHg, pulse 84 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Patient appears clinically stable. Pallor is present. Cardiovascular examination is normal without tachycardia. Respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 9.3 g/dL
Hematocrit 27.2%
RBC count 3.28 million/µL
MCV 83 fL
Serum iron 34 µg/dL
Ferritin 477 ng/mL
TIBC 209 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 16.3%

Assessment

Chronic anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/disease. The anemia is normocytic with low serum iron and TIBC and elevated ferritin, a pattern supporting inflammation-related iron restriction rather than uncomplicated iron deficiency. Hemoglobin and hematocrit remain reduced. Her long-standing inflammatory bowel disease and malignancy are relevant potential contributors. She is currently hemodynamically stable without reported fatigue, dyspnea, palpitations, or exercise limitation, though pallor and headache are present.

Plan

Review the anemia and laboratory pattern with the patient. Recommend completion or review of a CBC with reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the anemia and identify reversible contributors.

Recommend assessment of current inflammatory bowel disease activity and malignancy-related disease burden, including review with the clinicians managing those conditions. Consider hematology consultation if anemia persists, worsens, becomes symptomatic, or the etiology remains unclear.

Do not begin empiric iron therapy solely on the basis of the low serum iron given the elevated ferritin and low TIBC; iron replacement decisions should be guided by the broader clinical evaluation and trend in iron studies. Monitor for worsening headache, fatigue, weakness, dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, or reduced exercise tolerance, with urgent evaluation for significant or rapidly progressive symptoms.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of continued tobacco exposure. Arrange outpatient follow-up after repeat evaluation of the anemia and sooner for new or worsening symptoms.
