Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Joseph Bailey is an 86-year-old man, height 180 cm, weight 74.8 kg, BMI 23.1 kg/m², presenting for routine follow-up of longstanding hypertension diagnosed approximately 11 years ago. Blood pressure control has not been actively managed. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease of approximately 31 years’ duration and hyperlipidemia of approximately 29 years’ duration. He currently smokes. No medication regimen is documented for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 159/92 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory data: Sodium 135 mmol/L, potassium 5.1 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal at 159/92 mmHg. He is asymptomatic without features suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency. Given his advanced age and coronary artery disease, blood pressure reduction should be gradual and monitored closely. The mildly elevated potassium is relevant when selecting therapy.

2. Coronary artery disease, longstanding. He reports no current chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, or exercise-related symptoms.

3. Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

4. Tobacco use. Continued smoking increases cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of hypertension and coronary artery disease.

5. Mild hyperkalemia with potassium 5.1 mmol/L. This should be reassessed before initiating or titrating agents that may further increase potassium.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management, dietary sodium reduction, regular activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, including seated readings obtained under consistent conditions.

Recommend initiating or adjusting antihypertensive therapy after medication reconciliation and review of renal function, with cautious dosing and reassessment given his age, coronary artery disease, and potassium level. Repeat serum electrolytes and renal function should be obtained to guide treatment and monitor potassium. Avoid empiric use of potassium-raising therapy until the potassium result is rechecked.

Recommend cardiovascular risk assessment including ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as clinically appropriate. Continue surveillance for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly elevated blood pressure; seek urgent care if these occur.

Smoking cessation was strongly encouraged. Follow up after home blood pressure readings and laboratory reassessment to update the treatment plan.
