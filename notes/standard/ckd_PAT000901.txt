Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Brooklyn Barnes is a 38-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 11 years. Current management has not been established. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, changes in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath.

Her medical history is notable for anemia of chronic disease for approximately 1 year, gout for approximately 1 year, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 2 years. She does not smoke. Height is 171 cm and weight is 69.6 kg, with BMI 23.8 kg/m². No medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 138/88 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory data: Serum creatinine 4.25 mg/dL, estimated GFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 69 mg/dL.

No peripheral edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, or other uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported. No additional examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced kidney function, present for approximately 11 years, currently without reported symptoms of fluid overload or overt uremic syndrome. Creatinine is 4.25 mg/dL, eGFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 69 mg/dL. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction and/or her known anemia of chronic disease. The cause of the chronic kidney disease is not established from the available history. Diabetes mellitus has been present for approximately 2 years and may be a contributing risk factor, although the renal disease predates the reported diabetes duration. Blood pressure is mildly elevated.

Anemia of chronic disease, previously identified, requiring correlation with current blood counts and iron indices.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration approximately 2 years, requiring assessment of current glycemic control and renal-protective management.

Gout, duration approximately 1 year, in the setting of significant chronic kidney disease.

Plan

Given the substantially reduced eGFR and long duration of kidney disease, arrange prompt nephrology consultation for comprehensive evaluation, renal-protective management, and education regarding kidney replacement options if kidney function remains at this level or declines further. Review goals of care and symptoms that should prompt urgent evaluation, including worsening shortness of breath, new edema, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, chest pain, or substantially reduced urine output.

Obtain or review a serum electrolyte panel, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, complete blood count, iron studies, and intact parathyroid hormone to evaluate complications of chronic kidney disease and anemia. Check urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and urinalysis for proteinuria and evidence of an underlying renal process. Recommend renal ultrasound to assess renal size and structural abnormalities.

Optimize diabetes and blood pressure management with attention to kidney-safe therapy. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents; review all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use before treatment decisions. Coordinate gout management with renal function in mind and avoid empiric medication changes without medication reconciliation.

Monitor fatigue and symptoms of uremia or fluid retention. Continue close outpatient follow-up after laboratory evaluation and nephrology review.
