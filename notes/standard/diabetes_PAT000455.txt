Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for diabetes management.

Subjective

Denise Wheeler is a 49-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 17 years ago. She is not currently engaged in active diabetes management. Recent laboratory testing shows hemoglobin A1c 9.4% and fasting glucose 195 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimally controlled diabetes. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. She does not smoke. She has a 10-year history of anxiety.

Objective

Vital signs: blood pressure 124/77 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 176 cm, weight 90.1 kg, BMI 29.1 kg/m². No acute cardiopulmonary concerns evident from the available assessment.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently inadequately controlled based on hemoglobin A1c of 9.4% and fasting glucose of 195 mg/dL. She is asymptomatic today, without reported neuropathic, visual, constitutional, or infectious symptoms. Overweight status is a contributing risk factor. Longstanding anxiety may affect adherence and self-management.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated A1c and fasting glucose and the need to re-engage in active diabetes care. Recommend prompt review of lifestyle measures, including a carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, portion control, regular physical activity as tolerated, and weight reduction.

Recommend initiating or intensifying glucose-lowering therapy after shared decision-making, with medication selection based on renal function, cardiovascular risk, tolerability, cost, and patient preference. She should begin home glucose monitoring if feasible and bring readings to follow-up. Recommend assessment of renal function, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes preventive care, including dilated eye examination and comprehensive foot examination.

Reviewed symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including persistent vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, confusion, marked weakness, or rapidly worsening hyperglycemic symptoms. Continue attention to anxiety management and reassess its impact on diabetes self-care. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review glucose readings and response to the treatment plan.
