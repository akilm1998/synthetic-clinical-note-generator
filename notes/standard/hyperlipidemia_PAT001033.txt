Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Tristan Burke is a 92-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 53 years and currently without documented active lipid-lowering management. He denies chest discomfort, exertional calf pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. He has known coronary artery disease of approximately 6 years’ duration. He continues to smoke. Obesity has been present for approximately 38 years.

Recent lipid results were reviewed: total cholesterol 311 mg/dL, LDL-C 220 mg/dL, HDL-C 33 mg/dL, and triglycerides 292 mg/dL.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 127/77 mmHg, pulse 86/min, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 184 cm; weight 123.9 kg; BMI 36.6 kg/m².

No lipid-related physical findings or cardiopulmonary symptoms are reported today. No additional examination findings provided.

Assessment

Severe mixed dyslipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and elevated triglycerides, along with low HDL-C. The degree and longstanding nature of LDL elevation, in the setting of established coronary artery disease, indicate a substantially increased atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk. Obesity and ongoing tobacco use are additional modifiable risk factors. Despite the severity of the laboratory abnormalities, he is currently asymptomatic from an ischemic and peripheral vascular standpoint.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid profile and the potential benefit of lipid-lowering therapy for secondary cardiovascular prevention. Given his age, coronary artery disease, overall goals of care, and treatment preferences, recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of a statin, with consideration of a moderate- or high-intensity regimen if clinically appropriate and tolerated. If LDL-C remains substantially elevated despite tolerated statin therapy, addition of ezetimibe may be considered.

Recommend a heart-healthy dietary pattern emphasizing reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess calories, with increased intake of vegetables, fiber, and unsaturated fats. Encourage gradual physical activity as tolerated and weight-management efforts.

Strongly counsel smoking cessation and offer evidence-based cessation support. Recommend review of potential secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and medication tolerance before treatment initiation. Repeat a fasting lipid panel after an appropriate interval following any therapy or major lifestyle change to assess response. Continue clinical monitoring for anginal symptoms, exertional leg pain, or other manifestations of atherosclerotic disease.
