Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Matthew Reed is a 98-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of a 7-year history of hyperlipidemia that has not been under active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid testing demonstrates markedly elevated cholesterol and triglycerides. He denies chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness. He reports xanthelasma. He continues to smoke. Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus of 32 years’ duration and coronary artery disease diagnosed 6 years ago.

Objective

Height 185 cm; weight 100.3 kg; BMI 29.3 kg/m². Blood pressure 132/76 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Laboratory results:
Total cholesterol 353 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 258 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 32 mg/dL
Triglycerides 316 mg/dL

Xanthelasma is reported. No current chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe elevation of LDL cholesterol and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia, currently untreated. The lipid profile represents a markedly atherogenic pattern in the setting of established coronary artery disease, longstanding type 2 diabetes, tobacco use, and overweight status. Absence of current ischemic symptoms is reassuring but does not lessen the need for aggressive risk-factor management. Xanthelasma is consistent with the longstanding dyslipidemia.

Plan

Discussed the substantially increased cardiovascular risk associated with the current lipid levels and comorbid coronary artery disease and diabetes. Recommend initiation of lipid-lowering therapy, typically with a high-intensity statin if tolerated, with treatment selection and intensity individualized to his age, overall health, preferences, and goals of care. If adequate LDL reduction is not achieved or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of nonstatin therapy such as ezetimibe should be considered.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, along with evaluation for potentially reversible secondary contributors to dyslipidemia as clinically appropriate. Dietary counseling provided regarding reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol, with emphasis on a heart-healthy eating pattern. Encourage physical activity appropriate to functional capacity and safety.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered discussion of cessation support. Continue coordinated management of coronary artery disease and diabetes. Follow up after lipid reassessment to review treatment tolerance, adherence, and degree of lipid reduction.
