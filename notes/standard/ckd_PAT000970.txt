Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Philip Donovan is a 46-year-old man with a 13-year history of chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 3 years, anemia of chronic disease for 3 years, and gout for 1 year. He is here for outpatient medication review. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. He does not smoke.

Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance were reviewed in the context of his current care needs. A complete active medication regimen is not documented today; medication reconciliation, including any over-the-counter medications and gout treatments, is important given his degree of renal impairment. No medication-related symptoms are reported.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 143/88 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respirations 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 175 cm, weight 69.2 kg, BMI 22.6 kg/m².

Laboratory results: serum creatinine 3.77 mg/dL, BUN 73 mg/dL, estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, present for approximately 13 years. Current creatinine, BUN, and eGFR indicate advanced renal impairment. He is currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms. Chronic kidney disease is not receiving documented active medication management today.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 3 years, a significant renal risk factor.

Anemia of chronic disease, duration 3 years.

Gout, duration 1 year; medication selection and dosing require renal adjustment.

Blood pressure above goal at today’s visit.

Plan

Complete medication reconciliation at the next contact, including prescription medications, supplements, and over-the-counter analgesics. Assess adherence, treatment response, and adverse effects for each medication. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and ensure all medications are renally dosed; review any gout therapy specifically for renal safety.

Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation for advanced chronic kidney disease and assistance with renal-protective management and planning. Obtain or arrange monitoring of serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone, with renal ultrasound as clinically indicated.

Continue diabetes and blood pressure management with attention to kidney protection. Reassess blood pressure and review home readings if available. Counsel regarding avoidance of dehydration and nephrotoxic medications.

Reviewed symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including new shortness of breath, progressive swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, or severe weakness. Follow up promptly after medication reconciliation and renal evaluation.
