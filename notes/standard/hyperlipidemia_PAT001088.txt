Chief Complaint

Annual review and evaluation of longstanding elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Sydney Fuller is a 74-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease follow-up. She has a 28-year history of hyperlipidemia that has not been actively managed. Recent lipid results show markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with elevated triglycerides. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, or other reported cardiovascular symptoms. She reports xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

Relevant chronic conditions include type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 20 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 14 years. No current lipid-lowering medications were provided.

Objective

Height 180 cm, weight 73.2 kg, BMI 22.6 kg/m². Blood pressure 126/77 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Lipid profile: total cholesterol 352 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 251 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 43 mg/dL, triglycerides 290 mg/dL. Xanthelasma reported. No chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness reported. Nonsmoker.

Assessment

Longstanding severe hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, currently without active management. The lipid pattern is associated with substantially increased lifetime atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of advanced age and longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus. She currently reports no anginal or claudication symptoms. Xanthelasma is consistent with chronic lipid elevation.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, approximately 20 years’ duration.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, approximately 14 years’ duration. This should be considered when selecting and monitoring lipid-lowering therapy but is not, by itself, a contraindication to statin treatment.

Plan

Discussed the importance of initiating evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy given the LDL cholesterol of 251 mg/dL and coexisting longstanding diabetes. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of a high-intensity statin such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after reviewing medication history, contraindications, and patient preference. If statin therapy is not tolerated or LDL reduction is inadequate, consider additional therapy such as ezetimibe and, depending on response and overall risk, referral for consideration of other lipid-lowering options.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Recommend baseline hepatic assessment and review for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia, including thyroid disease, renal disease, diet, and other medications, before or at the time of treatment planning. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk characterization if clinically useful.

Counseled on a Mediterranean-style eating pattern, reduction of saturated and trans fats, limitation of refined carbohydrates and alcohol, regular physical activity as tolerated, and continued avoidance of tobacco. Her current BMI is in the healthy range.

Continue comprehensive diabetes care, including review of glycemic monitoring and diabetes-related preventive measures. Review preventive screening history and immunizations at this annual visit, including age-appropriate cancer screening, influenza vaccination, COVID vaccination, pneumococcal vaccination, and shingles vaccination as indicated by prior records and clinical history.

Follow up after lipid treatment is initiated or within approximately 4–12 weeks to review tolerance and repeat lipid results, with ongoing chronic disease follow-up thereafter. Advise prompt evaluation for new chest discomfort, exertional shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain.
