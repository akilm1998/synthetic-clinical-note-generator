Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Jacob Long is a 70-year-old man with approximately 20 years of hypertension, hyperlipidemia for 20 years, and coronary artery disease for 19 years. His hypertension has not been under active management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. No interval hypertensive symptoms are reported.

He continues to smoke. Current antihypertensive and lipid-lowering medications were not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 161/88 mmHg; pulse 78 bpm; respiratory rate 14/min; temperature 36.8°C; oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 182 cm; weight 70.6 kg; BMI 21.3 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Sodium 145 mmol/L; potassium 5.0 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled. Office blood pressure is markedly elevated at 161/88 mmHg despite absence of symptoms or findings suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency. His age and established coronary artery disease increase the importance of sustained blood pressure control. Potassium is at the upper end of the reported range and should be considered when selecting or titrating therapy.

2. Coronary artery disease, longstanding, without reported angina or cardiopulmonary symptoms today.

3. Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

4. Active tobacco use, an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

Plan

Discussed the significance of persistent systolic hypertension and the increased cardiovascular risk associated with his coronary artery disease and smoking. Recommend initiation or re-establishment of a structured antihypertensive treatment plan, with medication selection and titration guided by repeat blood pressure measurements, coronary disease history, and potassium level. Avoid empiric escalation of agents that may further increase potassium until the electrolyte profile is reassessed.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a validated upper-arm cuff, recording seated readings at consistent times, and bringing the log to follow-up. Reinforced a lower-sodium eating pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, moderation of alcohol if applicable, and continued attention to cardiovascular risk reduction.

Recommend repeat serum electrolytes and assessment of renal function before or shortly after starting or adjusting antihypertensive therapy. Recommend ECG and lipid profile as clinically appropriate for cardiovascular risk assessment and ongoing coronary artery disease management.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered counseling regarding cessation strategies. Continue routine follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment adjustment. Seek urgent medical attention for chest pain, new shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, or visual changes.
