Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Connor Walters is a 90-year-old man presenting for follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 18 years ago. Hypertension is currently not actively managed. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. No acute hypertensive symptoms reported.

He also has a history of type 2 diabetes mellitus for 48 years and hyperlipidemia for 58 years. He does not smoke. Weight is 75.2 kg at 177 cm, with BMI 24.0 kg/m².

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 157/86 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory Data: Sodium 137 mmol/L; potassium 3.9 mmol/L.

General: No acute symptoms reported today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with elevated office blood pressure, currently without documented antihypertensive management. Patient is asymptomatic, with no reported features concerning for hypertensive emergency.

Longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus and hyperlipidemia increase overall cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the importance of blood pressure control, particularly given his advanced age, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia. Recommend confirming the pattern of elevation with properly obtained home blood pressure readings and review of readings at follow-up. Assess for orthostatic symptoms and blood pressure response before intensifying treatment.

Recommend reduced dietary sodium, regular tolerated physical activity, and avoidance of excessive alcohol intake. Medication therapy should be discussed and initiated through shared decision-making, with attention to renal function, electrolyte status, fall risk, and orthostatic blood pressure in this 90-year-old patient.

Continue monitoring electrolytes with treatment changes; current sodium and potassium are within the provided reference context. Return for blood pressure reassessment after home readings are obtained, or sooner for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, or vision changes.
