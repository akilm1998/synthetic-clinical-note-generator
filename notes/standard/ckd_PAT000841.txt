Chief Complaint

Initial outpatient consultation for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Denise Wheeler is a 57-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 10 years. Recent laboratory results show creatinine 3.86 mg/dL, estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 80 mg/dL. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath.

Past medical history is significant for hypertension for 22 years, anemia of chronic disease for 12 years, and hyperlipidemia for 1 year. Current medication list was not provided for review. She does not smoke.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 153/94 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 154 cm, weight 68.1 kg, BMI 28.7 kg/m².

Available laboratory data: serum creatinine 3.86 mg/dL; estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m²; BUN 80 mg/dL.

No peripheral edema or other physical examination findings are documented today. Patient reports no shortness of breath, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, urinary frequency changes, or sleep disturbance.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, present for approximately 10 years. Current creatinine, BUN, and estimated GFR are significantly abnormal and warrant prompt specialist evaluation and close monitoring. Fatigue may be related to chronic kidney disease and/or her longstanding anemia of chronic disease.

Hypertension, longstanding and above goal today at 153/94 mmHg. Poor blood pressure control may contribute to ongoing renal disease progression.

Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding for 12 years; current hemoglobin and blood counts were not provided.

Hyperlipidemia, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for comprehensive evaluation of advanced chronic kidney disease, discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if indicated, and ongoing management.

Recommend serum electrolyte panel, repeat renal function testing, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as part of the initial renal evaluation. Results should be reviewed promptly when available.

Perform complete medication reconciliation at the next contact. Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal products. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically approved by a clinician. Medication dosing should be adjusted for current renal function.

Address blood pressure promptly with home blood pressure monitoring and review of current antihypertensive therapy once the medication list is confirmed. Encourage a renal-conscious, lower-sodium eating pattern and avoidance of excess dietary sodium.

Evaluate fatigue with CBC and renal-related laboratory assessment. Management of anemia should be guided by the results and nephrology recommendations; do not start or adjust anemia-directed therapy without laboratory review.

Discussed urgent precautions: seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, marked weakness, inability to urinate, or rapidly developing swelling.

Follow up promptly after laboratory testing and nephrology evaluation, with earlier reassessment for worsening symptoms or persistently elevated blood pressure.
