Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Michelle Cross is an 83-year-old woman, 160 cm tall and weighing 64.0 kg, with a 40-year history of hyperlipidemia and hypertension for approximately 5 years. Her lipid disorder has not been actively managed. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, or other reported cardiovascular symptoms. She reports fatty skin deposits involving the skin and eyelids, consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 138/81 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air. BMI 25.0 kg/m².

Recent lipid panel: Total cholesterol 371 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 276 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 39 mg/dL, triglycerides 281 mg/dL.

Reported physical findings include xanthomas and xanthelasma. No chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness reported.

Assessment

Severe longstanding hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia. The presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma supports prolonged exposure to elevated lipid levels. This represents a significant atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly given her advanced age and coexisting hypertension, although she currently reports no anginal or claudication symptoms.

Hypertension, approximately 5 years in duration, with current blood pressure 138/81 mmHg.

Plan

Discussed the substantially elevated cardiovascular risk associated with an LDL cholesterol of 276 mg/dL and total cholesterol of 371 mg/dL. Recommend initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy after reviewing goals of care, potential benefits, adverse effects, and patient preference. A high-intensity statin such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin would generally be appropriate if tolerated; additional therapy such as ezetimibe may be considered if LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated.

Recommend evaluation for potentially reversible secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and review of diet, exercise, weight trajectory, and any over-the-counter supplements. Counsel regarding reduction of saturated and trans fats, increased soluble fiber, and regular physical activity as tolerated. She should continue avoiding tobacco.

Recommend follow-up lipid testing after treatment is initiated or adjusted to assess response and tolerability. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for additional risk assessment, with further cardiovascular risk evaluation guided by shared decision-making.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and reinforce adherence to a heart-healthy lifestyle. Return for outpatient follow-up to review treatment decisions, lipid response, and any new cardiovascular symptoms.
