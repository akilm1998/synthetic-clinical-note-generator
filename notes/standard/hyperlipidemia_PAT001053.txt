Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly abnormal cholesterol levels.

Subjective

Liam Matthews is a 65-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 33 years and not currently under active treatment. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain/claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. He does not smoke.

He has longstanding obesity, approximately 40 years in duration. Current weight is 105.1 kg at 173 cm, BMI 35.1 kg/m². No lipid-lowering medication is reported.

Objective

Vital Signs: BP 125/82 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory Results:
Total cholesterol 332 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 243 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 32 mg/dL
Triglycerides 284 mg/dL

Assessment

Severe untreated hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, elevated triglycerides, and low HDL cholesterol. The degree and longstanding duration of elevation confer significant future atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, despite absence of current chest discomfort or claudication. Obesity is an important contributing cardiometabolic risk factor.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the importance of initiating evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy. Recommend starting a high-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after shared decision-making and review of contraindications.

Counseled on reducing saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates and alcohol, increasing dietary fiber, pursuing gradual weight loss, and increasing regular physical activity as tolerated.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation to assess response and adherence. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors and additional risk stratification, including ApoB and lipoprotein(a), as clinically appropriate. Given the LDL level and long history of untreated disease, discuss assessment for premature atherosclerotic disease and consideration of coronary artery calcium imaging if it will assist treatment decisions.

Advised to seek urgent care for new or persistent chest pain, shortness of breath, syncope, or acute neurologic symptoms. Close outpatient follow-up recommended to confirm treatment initiation and review lipid response.
