Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective
Owen Myers is a 78-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. Diabetes has not been actively managed. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He continues to smoke. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 21 years’ duration, depression of 51 years’ duration, and obstructive sleep apnea of 29 years’ duration. No current diabetes medication regimen is documented.

Objective
Height 188 cm; weight 100.0 kg; BMI 28.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 144/85 mmHg, pulse 70 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent laboratory results: hemoglobin A1c 9.2%; fasting glucose 214 mg/dL.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on elevated hemoglobin A1c and fasting glucose. Despite significant hyperglycemia, he reports no osmotic, constitutional, visual, neuropathic, or infectious symptoms.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Renal function is particularly relevant to selection and dosing of diabetes therapy.

Overweight, BMI 28.3 kg/m².

Tobacco use.

Depression and obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding comorbidities.

Plan
Discussed that current glycemic control is above goal and requires active treatment. Reviewed nutrition, reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sweetened beverages, portion control, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Given his age and chronic kidney disease, treatment goals should be individualized with emphasis on avoiding both persistent hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia.

Review renal function before selecting or dosing glucose-lowering medication. Discuss initiation of pharmacologic therapy, with medication choice guided by kidney function, comorbidities, cost, tolerability, and hypoglycemia risk. Encourage home glucose monitoring if medication therapy is started.

Recommend assessment of kidney involvement with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, along with a fasting lipid profile as part of diabetes risk assessment. Recommend routine diabetic retinal evaluation and comprehensive foot examination, including assessment of sensation and pulses.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and offered discussion of cessation strategies. Continue management of chronic kidney disease, depression, and obstructive sleep apnea in coordination with his established care plan.

Follow up in approximately 4–6 weeks to review glucose readings, renal assessment, medication tolerance if treatment is initiated, and blood pressure.
