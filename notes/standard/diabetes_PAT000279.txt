Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Elizabeth Perry is a 42-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 3 years ago. Diabetes has not been under active management. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has longstanding obesity for approximately 15 years and known non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 3 years. Current weight is 118.9 kg at 176 cm, with BMI 38.4 kg/m². She reports current tobacco use.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 116/78 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Recent laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.9%. Fasting glucose 216 mg/dL.

No acute distress or diabetes-related complications are reported today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose indicating inadequate glycemic control. Patient is currently asymptomatic but requires prompt initiation of active management.

Severe obesity, longstanding.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed laboratory results and the importance of active diabetes management, including nutrition changes, portion control, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, and gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated. Weight reduction was strongly encouraged given the impact on glycemic control and fatty liver disease.

Discussed initiating first-line pharmacologic therapy, typically metformin if no contraindication, with review of renal function and tolerability before prescribing. Additional diabetes therapy may be needed if glycemic control does not improve.

Recommend home glucose monitoring and maintaining a log for review at follow-up. Reviewed symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and advised earlier evaluation for worsening thirst or urination, vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, or inability to maintain oral intake.

Obtain urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, and fasting lipid profile as part of diabetes risk assessment. Recommend comprehensive foot examination and dilated retinal evaluation. Counseling provided regarding smoking cessation.

Follow up promptly after initiation of treatment and review of recommended testing to reassess glycemic control and adjust therapy.
