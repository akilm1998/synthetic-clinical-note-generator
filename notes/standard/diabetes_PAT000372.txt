Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for management of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Patrick Bell is a 32-year-old man presenting as a new patient for outpatient evaluation of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 11 years ago. Current diabetes management has not been established, and he is not presenting with an active treatment regimen. Recent laboratory results show HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 224 mg/dL.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No acute concerns reported today.

Relevant comorbidities include dyslipidemia of approximately 1 year and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of approximately 2 years. He does not smoke. Height is 177 cm and weight is 67.4 kg, with BMI 21.5 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: blood pressure 125/80 mmHg, pulse 68 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 177 cm; weight 67.4 kg; BMI 21.5 kg/m².

Laboratory results:
HbA1c 9.3%.
Fasting glucose 224 mg/dL.

No diabetic symptoms or peripheral sensory complaints reported. No abnormal physical examination findings provided today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, diagnosed approximately 11 years ago, currently with significantly elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose consistent with inadequate glycemic control. He is currently asymptomatic, without reported polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, recurrent infections, or impaired wound healing. His normal BMI is noted despite persistent hyperglycemia.

Dyslipidemia, duration approximately 1 year.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, duration approximately 2 years.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of establishing consistent diabetes care. Recommend prompt initiation of an evidence-based glucose-lowering medication regimen, with selection guided by renal and hepatic status, prior treatment history, tolerability, cost, and shared decision-making. Metformin would generally be considered if appropriate after review of kidney and liver function; medication reconciliation and allergy review should be completed before prescribing.

Recommend nutrition counseling focused on consistent carbohydrate intake, limiting sugar-sweetened beverages and refined carbohydrates, and maintaining adequate protein and balanced meals. Encourage regular aerobic and resistance physical activity as tolerated, while recognizing that he is not overweight and avoiding unintended weight loss.

Recommend assessment of renal function with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment, and review of liver status in the context of known non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Recommend a comprehensive diabetic foot examination and dilated retinal examination. Review immunization status and provide preventive care appropriate for adults with diabetes.

Discussed home glucose monitoring, including fasting and occasional postprandial measurements, particularly while treatment is being established. Reviewed symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and indications for urgent evaluation, including vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, marked weakness, inability to maintain hydration, or rapidly worsening symptoms.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after initiation of the management plan to review glucose readings, treatment tolerance, and laboratory evaluation, with repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval to assess response.
