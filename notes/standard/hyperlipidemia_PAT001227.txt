Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective
Martin Shaw is a 64-year-old male presenting for routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 29 years and currently without an active lipid-lowering management regimen documented. Recent lipid testing remains markedly abnormal. He denies chest discomfort, dizziness, and exertional calf pain. He reports xanthelasma but no fatty skin deposits elsewhere. He is a nonsmoker. Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease, present for approximately 19 years, and obesity.

Objective
Vital signs: Blood pressure 131/82 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 166 cm, weight 84.6 kg, BMI 30.7 kg/m².

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 380 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 271 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 36 mg/dL, triglycerides 367 mg/dL.

Xanthelasma is reported. No chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness reported.

Assessment
Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, currently inadequately controlled. The degree and longstanding nature of LDL elevation, together with established coronary artery disease, confer substantially increased atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk. Low HDL and obesity are additional adverse risk factors. Xanthelasma is also present. No current symptoms suggestive of active angina or symptomatic peripheral arterial disease.

Coronary artery disease, present for approximately 19 years, clinically stable by current symptom report. Secondary prevention and aggressive lipid reduction are indicated.

Obesity with BMI 30.7 kg/m².

Plan
Discussed the significance of the current lipid results and the importance of consistent lipid-lowering therapy in the setting of coronary artery disease. Recommend initiation of a high-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after reviewing contraindications, potential adverse effects, and patient preference. If LDL cholesterol remains substantially above goal despite adherence and tolerated statin therapy, recommend addition of ezetimibe and consideration of a PCSK9 inhibitor in the appropriate clinical setting.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, lean proteins, and replacement of saturated fats with unsaturated fats; avoid trans fats and limit refined carbohydrates, alcohol, and excess dietary sugars given the elevated triglycerides. Encourage gradual, regular aerobic activity as tolerated and weight reduction.

Recommend evaluation for potentially contributory secondary causes of severe dyslipidemia and review of family history for premature cardiovascular disease. Consider measurement of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) for further risk assessment. A fasting lipid panel should be repeated after treatment is initiated and adherence is established to assess response.

Continue monitoring for exertional chest pressure, dyspnea, dizziness, or leg pain with walking. Seek urgent medical attention for persistent or severe chest discomfort or other acute cardiovascular symptoms. Follow up after repeat lipid assessment to update management.
