Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective
Margaret Walsh is a 36-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of anemia present for approximately 7 years. She reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or known pallor. She does not smoke. Relevant comorbidities include rheumatoid arthritis for 3 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 2 years, and a chronic infection for 1 year. No current anemia-directed treatment is documented.

Objective
Height 160 cm, weight 63.0 kg, BMI 24.6 kg/m². Blood pressure 120/78 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results: hemoglobin 8.5 g/dL, hematocrit 26.1%, RBC 3.03 million/µL, MCV 86 fL, serum iron 40 µg/dL, ferritin 363 ng/mL, TIBC 256 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 15.6%.

Assessment
Chronic normocytic anemia with significant reduction in hemoglobin and hematocrit. The pattern of low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC is most consistent with anemia associated with chronic inflammation, in the setting of rheumatoid arthritis and chronic infection. She is currently hemodynamically stable and denies cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms. The duration and degree of anemia warrant further evaluation and active management.

Plan
Recommend prompt repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to confirm persistence and assess marrow response. Recommend evaluation of vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers, with continued assessment and treatment of underlying rheumatoid arthritis and chronic infection. Consider hematology referral given hemoglobin of 8.5 g/dL, chronicity, and unclear response to prior care. Iron supplementation should be guided by the overall evaluation and underlying inflammatory status rather than ferritin alone.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including new shortness of breath, chest pain, palpitations, syncope, marked weakness, dizziness, or bleeding. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after repeat testing and reassessment.
