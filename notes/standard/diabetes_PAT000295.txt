Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective
Jasmine Spencer is a 98-year-old woman with a 59-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus presenting for routine outpatient follow-up and review of disease control. Recent HbA1c is 9.7% and fasting glucose is 232 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimal glycemic control. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. She reports tingling in the hands or feet without reported numbness.

Her diabetes is currently not receiving active management according to the available history; no medication regimen is provided. Comorbidities include dyslipidemia of 38 years’ duration, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of 11 years’ duration, and depression of 3 years’ duration. She does not smoke.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 130/82 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 170 cm; weight 59.2 kg; BMI 20.5 kg/m².

Laboratory data: HbA1c 9.7%; fasting glucose 232 mg/dL.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose. Despite the degree and duration of hyperglycemia, she denies osmotic symptoms, constitutional symptoms, visual complaints, wounds, or recurrent infections. Distal tingling is present and warrants further assessment for a possible diabetes-related sensory process, although no formal complication is established today.

Dyslipidemia, longstanding.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Depression.

Plan
Discussed that current glycemic indices are above individualized treatment targets and that management should be reassessed promptly. Given her age, body habitus, long duration of diabetes, comorbidities, and need to minimize hypoglycemia, treatment goals should be individualized with emphasis on safety, function, and avoidance of symptomatic hyperglycemia.

Review and establish a feasible diabetes treatment plan, including consideration of pharmacologic therapy if consistent with her goals of care and overall clinical status. Medication selection and dosing should account for renal function, nutritional status, hepatic history, frailty risk, and hypoglycemia potential. No current diabetes medications are documented.

Encourage consistent nutrition, adequate hydration, and activity appropriate to her functional capacity. If home glucose monitoring is used, review technique and glucose patterns; provide education regarding symptoms of hypoglycemia and significant hyperglycemia.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin excretion, fasting lipid profile, and other routine diabetes monitoring to guide safe treatment and cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend periodic retinal evaluation and comprehensive foot examination, including assessment of sensation and pulses, particularly in view of reported tingling.

Continue clinical monitoring of dyslipidemia, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and depression as part of comprehensive diabetes care. Reassess glycemic response, tolerability, nutritional status, and sensory symptoms at follow-up.
