Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Tristan Burke is a 72-year-old man seen for routine diabetes follow-up. He has a 37-year history of type 2 diabetes that is currently not under active management. Recent laboratory results show HbA1c 9.4% and fasting glucose 220 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has a history of depression for approximately 37 years. Smoking status: never smoker. No current diabetes medications or other treatment regimen are provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 121/81 mmHg, pulse 72/min, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 178 cm; weight 80.5 kg; BMI 25.4 kg/m².

Laboratory data: HbA1c 9.4%. Fasting glucose 220 mg/dL.

No diabetes-related physical examination abnormalities are provided. The patient reports no current sensory symptoms, visual symptoms, wounds, or infectious complications.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently inadequately controlled based on HbA1c of 9.4% and fasting glucose of 220 mg/dL. Despite significant biochemical hyperglycemia, he denies osmotic, constitutional, visual, neuropathic, or infectious symptoms.

Long-standing diabetes mellitus, approximately 37 years’ duration, with increased risk for microvascular and macrovascular complications.

Depression, longstanding, approximately 37 years’ duration.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of re-establishing active diabetes management. Recommend an individualized treatment plan incorporating nutrition counseling, regular physical activity as tolerated, home glucose monitoring, and initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy after review of contraindications, renal function, prior treatment history, and patient preferences.

Recommend assessment of renal status with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive diabetic foot examination, and a dilated retinal examination. Continue periodic HbA1c monitoring to assess response to treatment.

Reviewed symptoms of marked hyperglycemia and advised prompt medical evaluation for persistent vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, confusion, severe weakness, or rapidly worsening glucose readings.

Address ongoing depression and incorporate mood, adherence, and functional status into diabetes management. Follow up after treatment initiation or adjustment to review glucose data, tolerance, and glycemic response.
