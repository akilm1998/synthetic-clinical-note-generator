Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Patrick Bell is a 77-year-old man, 185 cm tall and weighing 81.5 kg (BMI 23.8 kg/m²), presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 45 years ago. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. Chronic kidney disease has not been under active management. He has a 6-year history of secondary hyperparathyroidism. He continues to smoke.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 146/85 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 3.55 mg/dL, BUN 77 mg/dL, estimated GFR 20 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. Despite significant biochemical impairment, he currently denies uremic, fluid-overload, or constitutional symptoms. The prolonged disease history and lack of active management warrant structured evaluation and renal-protective care.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, present for approximately 6 years, in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Blood pressure is elevated at today’s visit and should be reassessed as part of overall renal and cardiovascular risk reduction.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Obtain urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum electrolytes, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone level to further assess kidney disease complications. Recommend renal ultrasound to evaluate renal structure.

Recommend nephrology referral for comprehensive chronic kidney disease management, monitoring of renal function and complications, and discussion of future renal replacement planning if clinically appropriate.

Repeat blood pressure measurements and address persistent elevation with a kidney-protective treatment strategy. Review all prescribed and over-the-counter medications for renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and the importance of ongoing renal follow-up. Educated to seek prompt care for new or worsening shortness of breath, edema, nausea, confusion, marked change in urine output, or other acute concerns.
