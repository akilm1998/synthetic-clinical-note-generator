Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Gabriella Holland is a 58-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 18 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, and dizziness. Xanthelasma is present. She does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 11 years, chronic kidney disease for 5 years, and longstanding obesity for approximately 33 years. Current weight is 85.5 kg at 150 cm, with BMI 38.0 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 136/82 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Recent lipid panel: Total cholesterol 349 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 248 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 49 mg/dL, triglycerides 258 mg/dL.

Xanthelasma noted. No reported chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol and moderately elevated triglycerides. The degree and long duration of elevation, in the setting of known coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease, confer substantially increased cardiovascular risk. Lipid control is currently inadequate and requires active management.

Obesity with BMI 38.0 kg/m².

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, without reported current anginal symptoms.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the need for prompt evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy given the LDL cholesterol of 248 mg/dL and history of coronary artery disease. Perform medication reconciliation and review prior lipid-lowering treatment history, intolerance, and adherence before selecting therapy. A high-intensity statin would generally be indicated; consider addition of ezetimibe or other therapy if LDL reduction remains inadequate or statin intolerance limits treatment.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing reduction of saturated and trans fats, increased soluble fiber, and avoidance of excess refined carbohydrates. Encourage gradual, sustainable physical activity as tolerated and weight reduction efforts. Continue tobacco avoidance.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid testing after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to severe dyslipidemia and measurement of additional cardiovascular risk markers, including apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a), as clinically appropriate. Medication selection and dosing should account for chronic kidney disease.

Continue monitoring for new chest discomfort, exertional dyspnea, dizziness, or leg pain with walking. Seek urgent evaluation for persistent chest pain or other acute cardiovascular symptoms. Follow up after medication reconciliation and lipid management initiation to reassess tolerance, adherence, and lipid response.
