Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for diabetes and elevated glucose measurements.

Subjective

Anna Cook is a 95-year-old woman seen for focused follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 29 years. Diabetes has not been actively managed recently. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No acute diabetic symptoms are reported.

She has longstanding obesity of approximately 22 years and anxiety for approximately 3 years. She does not smoke.

Objective

Height 153 cm, weight 83.1 kg, BMI 35.5 kg/m². Blood pressure 130/82 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent HbA1c is 9.3% and fasting glucose is 232 mg/dL, consistent with significant hyperglycemia. No diabetic complications are reported in the available history.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently substantially above individualized glycemic targets based on HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 232 mg/dL. She is currently asymptomatic but requires prompt outpatient management. Advanced age and comorbid obesity warrant a cautious, individualized approach that avoids hypoglycemia.

Obesity, BMI 35.5 kg/m².

Anxiety, longstanding approximately 3 years.

Plan

Review diabetes goals with the patient, emphasizing individualized targets appropriate for her age, functional status, and risk of hypoglycemia. Complete medication reconciliation and establish a practical glucose-lowering regimen under close clinical supervision; treatment should be initiated or intensified cautiously rather than relying on dietary management alone.

Discuss consistent nutrition, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages and concentrated sweets, appropriate portion sizes, and activity as tolerated with attention to fall prevention. Encourage adequate oral hydration.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before selecting or adjusting therapy, along with a fasting lipid profile. Arrange close follow-up to review home glucose readings and tolerance of treatment, with repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval.

Seek urgent evaluation for persistent vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, confusion, marked weakness, or worsening polyuria or polydipsia.
