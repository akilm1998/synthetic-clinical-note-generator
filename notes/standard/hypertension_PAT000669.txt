Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of hypertension.

Subjective

Brandon Cox is a 70-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease follow-up. He has a 3-year history of hypertension that is currently not actively managed. He reports no headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. No acute cardiovascular or neurologic symptoms are reported today.

Relevant medical history includes type 2 diabetes mellitus for 16 years, coronary artery disease for 10 years, and chronic kidney disease for 5 years. He does not smoke. Current weight is 72.3 kg at a height of 163 cm, with BMI 27.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 158/92 mmHg; pulse 76 bpm; respiratory rate 17/min; temperature 36.8°C; oxygen saturation 96%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 138 mmol/L, potassium 4.2 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Persistent elevated blood pressure consistent with inadequately controlled essential hypertension. He is asymptomatic without features of hypertensive emergency, but his age and comorbid coronary artery disease, chronic kidney disease, and long-standing type 2 diabetes increase cardiovascular and renal risk.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 16-year history.

Coronary artery disease, 10-year history.

Chronic kidney disease, 5-year history.

Overweight by BMI at 27.2 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management and reduction of cardiovascular and renal risk. Recommend confirming blood pressure control with properly seated office measurements and/or home blood pressure monitoring, with a written log for review. Given today’s reading and significant comorbidities, recommend prompt follow-up to establish an antihypertensive treatment plan appropriate for his kidney disease, diabetes, and coronary artery disease. Medication selection and dosing should be guided by renal function, cardiovascular history, and review of the complete medication and allergy history.

Continue dietary measures emphasizing reduced sodium intake, balanced nutrition, weight management, and regular activity as tolerated. Avoid tobacco exposure and limit alcohol if used.

Recommend monitoring renal function and electrolytes as clinically appropriate when antihypertensive therapy is initiated or adjusted; current sodium and potassium are within the provided reference context. Continue coordinated management of diabetes, coronary artery disease, and chronic kidney disease.

Preventive care reviewed in the context of annual follow-up, including age-appropriate immunizations, diabetes-related eye and foot care, cardiovascular risk assessment, and routine cancer screening based on prior screening history and patient preference.

Seek urgent medical attention for severe or persistent headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, marked visual change, or other signs of acute blood pressure-related illness. Arrange follow-up soon for blood pressure reassessment and chronic disease management.
