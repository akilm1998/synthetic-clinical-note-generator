Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Sophia Turner is an 84-year-old woman with a 14-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus presenting for routine outpatient follow-up and review of disease control. Recent laboratory results show HbA1c 9.5% and fasting glucose 210 mg/dL, consistent with inadequate glycemic control. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, unintentional weight loss, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet.

Her medical history is also notable for hypertension of 48 years’ duration, obstructive sleep apnea for 18 years, and depression for 21 years. She currently smokes. No diabetes medications or other active treatment regimen are provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 150/83 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 94%.

Height 165 cm, weight 60.7 kg, BMI 22.3 kg/m².

Laboratory data: HbA1c 9.5%. Fasting glucose 210 mg/dL.

No acute hyperglycemic symptoms reported. No examination findings beyond the documented vital signs and anthropometric measurements are available from this encounter.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently inadequately controlled, based on HbA1c of 9.5% and fasting glucose of 210 mg/dL. Despite significant hyperglycemia, she denies osmotic, constitutional, visual, infectious, or neuropathic symptoms. At age 84, glycemic targets should be individualized according to functional status, comorbidity burden, hypoglycemia risk, and treatment preferences, while avoiding symptomatic hyperglycemia.

Longstanding hypertension with blood pressure above an appropriate outpatient target today.

Longstanding obstructive sleep apnea.

Longstanding depression.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the need to re-establish an active, sustainable diabetes management plan. Review current medications, adherence, dietary pattern, glucose-monitoring practices, renal function, and hypoglycemia risk before selecting or intensifying therapy. Pharmacologic treatment should be individualized for her age and comorbidities, with gradual improvement in glycemic control and avoidance of hypoglycemia.

Recommend home glucose monitoring as clinically appropriate and review of readings at follow-up. Counsel regarding consistent carbohydrate intake, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, balanced nutrition, and regular physical activity within her functional limitations.

Recommend assessment of renal status with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, along with a fasting lipid profile as part of diabetes risk assessment. Recommend routine diabetic retinal evaluation and comprehensive foot examination, including sensory and vascular assessment.

Blood pressure should be reassessed and antihypertensive management reviewed in the context of diabetes and age. Continue to address tobacco cessation using a patient-centered approach. Ongoing management of obstructive sleep apnea and depression should be incorporated into the overall care plan.

Follow up after medication and glucose-monitoring review to reassess glycemic control and treatment tolerability.
