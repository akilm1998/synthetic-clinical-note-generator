Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Owen Myers is a 51-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 7 years ago. He reports ongoing fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, changes in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath.

Chronic kidney disease has not been actively managed recently. Available laboratory results show creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, eGFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 75 mg/dL. No current medication list was provided.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 2 years, hyperlipidemia for 13 years, and gout for 9 years. He currently smokes. Height is 164 cm, weight 75.0 kg, BMI 27.9 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 146/87 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Laboratory data:
Serum creatinine: 6.0 mg/dL
Estimated GFR: 5 mL/min/1.73 m²
BUN: 75 mg/dL

No peripheral edema or respiratory symptoms are reported today. No additional examination findings are documented.

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. The patient has a 7-year history of CKD and current creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, eGFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 75 mg/dL. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction. Although he does not currently report nausea, pruritus, edema, reduced urination, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, or dyspnea, the laboratory findings require prompt specialist evaluation and active management.

2. Elevated blood pressure reading, 146/87 mmHg, in the setting of CKD and coronary artery disease.

3. Coronary artery disease, present for 2 years. Current treatment details are not available.

4. Hyperlipidemia, present for 13 years. Current treatment details are not available.

5. Gout, present for 9 years.

6. Tobacco use. Ongoing smoking increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

7. Overweight, BMI 27.9 kg/m².

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of severe renal impairment and assessment for renal replacement therapy planning as clinically indicated. The patient should seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, marked weakness, persistent nausea or vomiting, new swelling, significant change in urination, or other acute deterioration.

Obtain updated renal and CKD-related evaluation, including serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound, as clinically appropriate. Review for complications of CKD, including anemia, electrolyte disturbance, mineral and bone disorder, and fluid retention.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications with particular attention to renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents such as NSAIDs unless specifically directed by a clinician. Medication reconciliation and cardiovascular risk management are needed, including review of therapy for blood pressure, coronary artery disease, hyperlipidemia, and gout.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, avoidance of excess dietary sodium, and maintaining appropriate hydration without excessive fluid intake. Close outpatient follow-up should occur promptly after nephrology assessment or sooner for any new symptoms.
