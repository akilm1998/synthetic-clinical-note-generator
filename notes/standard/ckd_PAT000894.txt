Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Ian Lawson is a 77-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 12 years ago. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. There is no reported interval symptom burden suggestive of uremic prodrome or fluid overload.

His medical history is notable for longstanding hypertension for approximately 21 years and coronary artery disease for approximately 29 years. He continues to smoke. Current medication regimen and adherence were not provided for review today.

Objective

Height 162 cm, weight 57.5 kg, BMI 21.9 kg/m². Blood pressure is 154/95 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, and oxygen saturation 95% on room air.

Recent laboratory results show serum creatinine 3.53 mg/dL, estimated GFR 18 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 80 mg/dL. No additional laboratory or imaging findings are available for review.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function and azotemia, present for approximately 12 years. He is currently clinically without reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms, but the degree of renal impairment warrants close monitoring and active management. Contributing risk factors include longstanding hypertension, coronary artery disease, advanced age, and ongoing tobacco use.

Hypertension, currently above goal at 154/95 mmHg, with importance increased in the setting of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Review and reconcile all current medications, including over-the-counter agents and supplements, with particular attention to avoidance of NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications. Management should include an individualized blood pressure regimen appropriate for renal disease and coronary artery disease, with home blood pressure monitoring if feasible.

Recommend nephrology evaluation for ongoing management of advanced chronic kidney disease, assessment of progression, and discussion of renal replacement planning if clinically indicated. Obtain serial renal function and electrolyte testing, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and mineral-bone parameters including intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Renal ultrasound may be considered if not previously performed or if there is concern for structural disease.

Counseled regarding low-sodium nutrition, avoidance of dehydration, and smoking cessation. Monitor for new or worsening edema, shortness of breath, nausea, vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, itching, muscle cramps, or significant changes in urine output; seek prompt medical attention if these occur.

Continue close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure control, renal function surveillance, and coordination of care for chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease.
