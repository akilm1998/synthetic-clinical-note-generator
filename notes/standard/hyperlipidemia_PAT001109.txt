Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Patrick Harper is a 62-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 13-year history of hyperlipidemia that has not been actively managed. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or claudication, and dizziness. He reports xanthomas and xanthelasma. He is a nonsmoker.

Past medical history is notable for coronary artery disease for approximately 4 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 34 years. No current medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Height 180 cm, weight 86.2 kg, BMI 26.6 kg/m². Blood pressure 126/78 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Laboratory results: total cholesterol 376 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 269 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 34 mg/dL, triglycerides 367 mg/dL.

Xanthomas and xanthelasma are reported. No active chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness reported today.

Assessment

Severe mixed dyslipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and triglycerides and low HDL cholesterol, currently without active lipid-lowering management. The presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma is consistent with longstanding significant lipid elevation. His known coronary artery disease places him at particularly high cardiovascular risk despite the absence of current symptoms.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding. Lipid treatment should be coordinated with monitoring of liver status; fatty liver disease alone is not generally a reason to defer indicated statin therapy.

BMI 26.6 kg/m², in the overweight range.

Plan

Discussed the substantially increased cardiovascular risk associated with the current lipid profile and known coronary artery disease. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after shared decision-making and medication reconciliation. If LDL cholesterol remains above goal or statin therapy is not tolerated, recommend consideration of ezetimibe and, given the severity of elevation and established coronary disease, possible specialist-directed additional therapy.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, with consideration of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk characterization. Evaluate for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia as clinically appropriate. Triglycerides are elevated but below the range typically associated with pancreatitis; prioritize LDL reduction, dietary modification, and improved overall cardiometabolic risk control.

Counseled on a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, legumes, whole grains, fish, and unsaturated fats while limiting saturated fats, trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol. Encourage regular aerobic and resistance activity as tolerated and continued avoidance of tobacco.

Recommend coordination of cardiovascular prevention with his established coronary artery disease, including review of current cardiology care and preventive therapies. Seek urgent evaluation for new or persistent chest pain, shortness of breath, or exertional symptoms.

Continue routine monitoring of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and liver health while addressing the dyslipidemia. Review and update age-appropriate cancer screening, immunizations, and other preventive care during the annual visit. Follow up after repeat lipid testing to assess response and finalize long-term management.
