Chief Complaint

Annual review and chronic kidney disease follow-up.

Subjective

Owen Myers is a 59-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 20-year history of chronic kidney disease, longstanding hypertension for approximately 18 years, anemia of chronic disease for 15 years, and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. He does not smoke.

He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, changes in urination, or shortness of breath. No current uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported. Chronic kidney disease has not been under active ongoing management, and current medications were not provided for review.

Objective

Height 163 cm; weight 60.8 kg; BMI 22.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 161/93 mmHg
Pulse 83 bpm
Respiratory rate 15/min
Temperature 36.6°C
Oxygen saturation 98%

Available laboratory results:
Creatinine 3.45 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 20 mL/min/1.73 m²
BUN 74 mg/dL

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function

Longstanding chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 20 years, with creatinine 3.45 mg/dL, eGFR 20 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 74 mg/dL. He is currently asymptomatic without reported edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, cramps, or urinary changes. The degree of renal impairment requires re-establishment of active monitoring and specialist-directed care. No formal disease stage is assigned today.

2. Hypertension

Blood pressure is elevated at 161/93 mmHg in the setting of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease. Current antihypertensive regimen is not available for review.

3. Anemia of chronic disease

History of anemia for approximately 15 years. No current hemoglobin or hematocrit is available, and he denies fatigue. Further assessment is needed to characterize current severity and management needs.

4. Coronary artery disease

History of coronary artery disease for approximately 1 year. No anginal symptoms or shortness of breath reported today. Cardiovascular risk is increased by uncontrolled blood pressure and chronic kidney disease.

5. Preventive health maintenance

Annual preventive review completed. He has a normal BMI of 22.9 kg/m² and reports no tobacco use.

Plan

1. Chronic kidney disease

Arrange prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation and ongoing management.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, renal function testing, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Consider renal ultrasound as part of evaluation of longstanding renal disease.

Review for potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications, particularly NSAIDs, and avoid their use unless specifically directed. Encourage appropriate hydration while avoiding excessive salt intake. Discuss monitoring for new edema, shortness of breath, nausea, persistent itching, marked reduction in urination, confusion, or worsening fatigue, with urgent assessment if these occur.

2. Hypertension

Repeat blood pressure measurement and review home readings if available. Initiate or optimize antihypertensive treatment after medication reconciliation, with a renal- and cardiovascular-protective strategy coordinated with nephrology and cardiology as appropriate. Reinforce reduced dietary sodium intake.

3. Anemia of chronic disease

Repeat CBC and assess for worsening anemia or other contributing causes. Coordinate treatment decisions with nephrology; no erythropoietin or other anemia therapy is documented today.

4. Coronary artery disease

Review current cardiac medications, symptoms, and prior cardiac follow-up. Continue aggressive blood pressure and cardiovascular risk-factor management. Advise urgent evaluation for chest pain, new dyspnea, syncope, or exertional decline.

5. Preventive care

Review immunization status, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, and other age-appropriate vaccines, with consideration of chronic kidney disease in vaccine planning. Confirm completion of age-appropriate colorectal cancer screening and other preventive screenings based on prior results and shared decision-making. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated, balanced nutrition, and avoidance of tobacco.

Follow-up

Follow up after updated renal studies and medication reconciliation, with close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure and kidney disease management.
