Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Audrey Jenkins is a 40-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 11 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She continues to smoke. She also has type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Height 151 cm, weight 46.7 kg, BMI 20.5 kg/m². Blood pressure 123/80 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Recent lipid profile: total cholesterol 381 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 269 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 40 mg/dL, and triglycerides 361 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding and markedly uncontrolled. LDL cholesterol is 269 mg/dL with concomitant hypertriglyceridemia and low-normal HDL cholesterol. The degree and duration of elevation confer substantial lifetime atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of active tobacco use and type 2 diabetes mellitus, despite a normal BMI and currently normal blood pressure. She has no reported symptoms suggestive of current angina or claudication and no reported xanthomas or xanthelasma.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 2 years’ duration. Glycemic control cannot be assessed from the information available today. Diabetes further strengthens the indication for aggressive cardiovascular risk reduction.

Tobacco use. Ongoing smoking is an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor and may worsen overall lipid-related risk.

Plan

Discussed the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and triglycerides and the importance of initiating active treatment rather than continued observation. Recommend initiation of a high-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after review of contraindications, potential adverse effects, pregnancy considerations, and patient preferences. If LDL cholesterol remains substantially elevated despite adherence and maximally tolerated statin therapy, addition of ezetimibe and further risk-based therapy can be considered.

Recommend a fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation and dose stabilization to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and assessment of lifetime cardiovascular risk, including consideration of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a) measurement when clinically appropriate. Given the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and long duration of disease, a detailed family history of premature cardiovascular disease should be obtained at a future visit.

Counseled on reducing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and added sugars; maintaining regular aerobic and resistance exercise; and avoiding excess alcohol, particularly given the triglyceride level of 361 mg/dL. Her normal BMI does not eliminate the need for dietary and pharmacologic risk reduction.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered discussion of behavioral and pharmacologic cessation support.

Coordinate lipid management with diabetes care and reinforce adherence to diabetes treatment and routine monitoring. She should seek urgent evaluation for new chest pressure, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain. Follow up after initiation or adjustment of lipid-lowering therapy to review tolerance and repeat lipid results.
