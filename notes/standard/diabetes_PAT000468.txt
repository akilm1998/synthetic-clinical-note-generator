Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for elevated diabetes laboratory values.

Subjective
Emma Carter is a 51-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 8 years ago. Recent testing shows HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 247 mg/dL. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No smoking history. Comorbidity includes obstructive sleep apnea of approximately 8 years’ duration. Current diabetes treatment regimen is not documented.

Objective
Height 162 cm; weight 56.4 kg; BMI 21.5 kg/m². Blood pressure 124/81 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%. HbA1c 9.3%. Fasting glucose 247 mg/dL.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently markedly above glycemic target based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. Despite the degree of hyperglycemia, she is presently asymptomatic and has a normal BMI. No neuropathic, infectious, constitutional, or visual symptoms are reported.

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding.

Plan
Discussed that the current glucose values indicate inadequate glycemic control and require prompt treatment planning rather than continued observation. Arrange timely outpatient review to establish or intensify a diabetes regimen; medication selection should account for renal function, contraindications, hypoglycemia risk, and patient preferences. Reinforce carbohydrate-conscious nutrition, regular physical activity as tolerated, and home glucose monitoring with documentation of readings.

Recommend evaluation of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and assessment for diabetes-related eye and foot complications as clinically appropriate. Review immunization and cardiovascular risk-reduction needs at follow-up.

Given the fasting glucose of 247 mg/dL, advise urgent evaluation for worsening hyperglycemia if she develops marked thirst or urination, vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, weakness, or inability to maintain oral intake. Close follow-up is recommended after treatment is initiated or adjusted.
