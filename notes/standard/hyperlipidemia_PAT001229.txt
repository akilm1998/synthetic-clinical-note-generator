Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Blake Hunter is a 63-year-old man seen for routine follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 26 years. The condition has not been actively managed. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. He does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidity includes non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, present for approximately 34 years. No current lipid-lowering medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Height 161 cm; weight 67.4 kg; BMI 26.0 kg/m².

Blood pressure 130/78 mmHg, pulse 68 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Recent lipid profile: total cholesterol 296 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 201 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 34 mg/dL, and triglycerides 305 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, accompanied by low HDL cholesterol. The degree and long duration of elevation warrant active treatment and cardiovascular risk assessment. He currently reports no symptoms suggestive of angina or intermittent claudication and has no reported xanthomas or xanthelasma.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding. This should be considered when monitoring therapy, but is not by itself a reason to leave significant hyperlipidemia untreated.

Plan

Discussed the importance of initiating evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy given LDL cholesterol of 201 mg/dL and the prolonged history of untreated dyslipidemia. Recommend shared decision-making regarding a high-intensity statin, with review of potential adverse effects and medication precautions before starting.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, increased soluble fiber, and limitation of refined carbohydrates and excess alcohol. Encourage regular aerobic and resistance activity as tolerated and maintenance of a healthy weight.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid testing after treatment is initiated to assess response, along with appropriate monitoring of liver enzymes in the context of longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Consider evaluation for additional cardiovascular risk markers, including lipoprotein(a) or apolipoprotein B, if results would affect risk discussion or treatment intensity. Coronary artery calcium imaging may be considered if treatment decision-making remains uncertain, although the LDL level warrants serious consideration of therapy regardless.

Return for lipid management follow-up after medication and lifestyle decisions are made, or sooner for medication concerns or development of chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or other concerning cardiovascular symptoms.
