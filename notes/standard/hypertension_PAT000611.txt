Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Hailey Johnston is a 55-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of a 12-year history of essential hypertension, currently not under active management. She reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis.

She has known coronary artery disease of approximately 6 years’ duration. No current antihypertensive regimen is provided. She does not smoke. Height is 172 cm and weight is 73.7 kg, with BMI 24.9 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 150/89 mmHg; pulse 76 bpm; respiratory rate 17/min; temperature 36.7°C; oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory data: Sodium 141 mmol/L; potassium 5.1 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent elevation at today’s visit in the setting of 12 years of disease and no active management. Associated dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue are reported without current symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Given the coexisting coronary artery disease, sustained blood pressure control and cardiovascular risk reduction are particularly important.

Potassium is mildly elevated at 5.1 mmol/L, which should be considered when selecting antihypertensive therapy and warrants confirmation before initiating agents that may further increase potassium.

Coronary artery disease, established approximately 6 years ago, without reported angina or dyspnea today.

Plan

Discussed the importance of initiating a structured hypertension management plan and monitoring blood pressure regularly. Recommend home blood pressure measurements using a validated upper-arm cuff, with a written log for review.

Recommend repeat serum electrolytes, including potassium, and assessment of renal function before selecting and titrating antihypertensive therapy. Given the potassium level and coronary artery disease, medication selection should be individualized and monitored closely; avoid empiric initiation of a potassium-raising agent until the potassium result is reassessed.

Recommend cardiovascular evaluation with ECG and review of lipid profile and urinalysis as part of hypertension and coronary artery disease risk assessment. Reinforce a low-sodium eating pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, moderation of alcohol if applicable, and maintenance of her current healthy weight.

Advise urgent evaluation for new chest pain, severe headache, acute neurologic symptoms, significant shortness of breath, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after home blood pressure readings and repeat laboratory assessment to update treatment.
