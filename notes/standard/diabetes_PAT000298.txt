Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Julian Holland is a 54-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 22 years ago. Diabetes control is currently poor, with recent HbA1c of 9.2% and fasting glucose of 237 mg/dL. He reports increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, and frequent infections are all absent. He does report numbness and tingling in the hands and feet.

He has a history of chronic kidney disease for approximately 11 years, obesity for approximately 30 years, and obstructive sleep apnea diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. He does not smoke. No current diabetes medications or other medication regimen were provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 134/82 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 161 cm, weight 101.6 kg, BMI 39.2 kg/m².

Recent laboratory results: HbA1c 9.2%. Fasting glucose 237 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings provided.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented acute complication, currently inadequately controlled based on HbA1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 237 mg/dL. He denies osmotic or constitutional symptoms but reports distal numbness and tingling, concerning for possible diabetic peripheral neuropathic symptoms in the setting of long-standing diabetes.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Renal function and albuminuria should be considered when selecting and dosing diabetes therapy.

Severe obesity with BMI 39.2 kg/m².

Obstructive sleep apnea.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the increased risk of microvascular and cardiovascular complications with persistent hyperglycemia. Review current dietary pattern, physical activity, home glucose monitoring, and barriers to treatment at the next medication-focused discussion.

Recommend initiation or intensification of evidence-based diabetes pharmacotherapy, with medication selection and dosing guided by current kidney function and renal safety considerations. Obtain updated serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before finalizing therapy, given the history of chronic kidney disease. Consider an agent with weight and cardiovascular/renal benefit if clinically appropriate.

Recommend nutrition counseling focused on reducing concentrated carbohydrates and calorie-dense foods, portion control, and gradual sustainable weight reduction. Encourage activity appropriate for his functional status, with gradual progression toward regular aerobic and resistance exercise.

Review daily foot care, avoidance of barefoot walking, and prompt evaluation of skin breaks or nonhealing wounds. Perform a comprehensive diabetic foot and sensory examination at follow-up. Recommend routine dilated retinal examination given the duration of diabetes and current poor glycemic control.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and cardiovascular risk factors. Reinforce adherence to sleep apnea treatment if prescribed, although no current regimen was provided.

Follow up promptly after medication and renal assessment review to establish an active diabetes treatment plan and reassess glycemic control.
