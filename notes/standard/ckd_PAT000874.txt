Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and abnormal renal function.

Subjective
Charlotte Bailey is a 64-year-old woman, 180 cm and 78.4 kg (BMI 24.2 kg/m²), presenting for focused follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 15 years. She is not currently receiving documented management for this condition. She denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. She has a 22-year history of anemia of chronic disease. She continues to smoke.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 153/85 mmHg, pulse 79/min, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 3.37 mg/dL
eGFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m²
BUN 80 mg/dL

No acute cardiopulmonary or uremic symptoms reported today.

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine, present for approximately 15 years and currently without active documented management. Although she is clinically asymptomatic, the degree of renal impairment warrants prompt specialist evaluation and close monitoring for electrolyte, acid-base, volume, anemia, and uremic complications.

Hypertension, currently above goal at 153/85 mmHg, requiring reassessment and treatment planning in the context of chronic kidney disease.

Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding for approximately 22 years; current severity and contribution to symptoms cannot be assessed from the available results.

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Plan
Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive chronic kidney disease management and discussion of renal replacement planning if clinically indicated.

Obtain repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium and phosphorus, along with CBC, urinalysis, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound to assess kidney structure and chronicity.

Review blood pressure promptly and initiate or adjust antihypertensive therapy based on repeat measurements, volume status, potassium, and renal specialist guidance. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents; review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications.

Address longstanding anemia after CBC and iron assessment; treatment should be guided by laboratory findings and nephrology recommendations.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of dehydration. Seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, marked weakness, rapidly worsening edema, or substantial change in urination. Close outpatient follow-up is needed after laboratory reassessment and nephrology review.
