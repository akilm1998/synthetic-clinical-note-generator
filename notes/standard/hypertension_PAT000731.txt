Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of hypertension.

Subjective

Janet McKenzie is a 68-year-old woman with a 32-year history of essential hypertension, currently without documented active management. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for 21 years, coronary artery disease for 14 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 17 years. She reports fatigue but denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or nosebleeds. She does not smoke.

No specific antihypertensive regimen is documented for review today; therefore, medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be assessed against an established regimen. No medication-related symptoms are reported.

Objective

Blood pressure 162/96 mmHg. Pulse 74 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 161 cm, weight 58.1 kg, BMI 22.4 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 138 mmol/L, potassium 4.1 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and no documented active treatment plan. She is currently asymptomatic for hypertensive emergency, with no chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic or visual symptoms. Longstanding chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease, and diabetes mellitus increase her cardiovascular and renal risk. Electrolytes are currently within expected ranges.

Fatigue, nonspecific.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given the current reading and her chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease, and diabetes. Confirm blood pressure with repeat office measurement and home monitoring using appropriate technique.

Establish or reconcile an antihypertensive regimen promptly, with medication selection and dosing tailored to renal function, diabetes, and coronary disease. Review adherence, effectiveness, and adverse effects at the next medication-focused visit. Monitor renal function and electrolytes after initiation or adjustment of therapy.

Recommend limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, and avoiding excess alcohol. Continue risk-factor management for diabetes and coronary artery disease.

Obtain or review an ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as clinically appropriate for cardiovascular and renal risk assessment; no new results are available today.

Seek urgent medical attention for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, vision changes, or markedly worsening blood pressure. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning.
