Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Violet Murphy is a 45-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 9 years ago. She reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, change in urination, peripheral edema, or shortness of breath. No interval symptoms suggestive of uremia or fluid overload are reported.

Her comorbidities include secondary hyperparathyroidism for 1 year, anemia of chronic disease for 2 years, and coronary artery disease for 1 year. She currently smokes. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

Objective

Height 159 cm, weight 55.4 kg, BMI 21.9 kg/m². Blood pressure 143/87 mmHg, pulse 84 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 2.6 mg/dL, estimated GFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 88 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with significantly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. She is currently clinically without reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms. Blood pressure is above goal today. The condition has not been actively managed recently and requires prompt reassessment and coordinated renal care.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism associated with chronic kidney disease.

Anemia of chronic disease associated with chronic kidney disease.

Coronary artery disease.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of re-establishing active management of her chronic kidney disease given the current renal indices, even in the absence of symptoms. Recommend prompt nephrology referral for evaluation and ongoing management, including discussion of renal replacement planning if appropriate.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, renal function testing, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Consider renal ultrasound as part of structural evaluation if not recently completed. Review results promptly and monitor for worsening renal function, hyperkalemia, acidosis, anemia, or mineral-bone abnormalities.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications when available, avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents, and use renal dosing for medications. Counsel regarding blood pressure control, low-sodium intake, smoking cessation, and avoidance of dehydration. Continue cardiovascular risk reduction appropriate for her coronary artery disease.

She was advised to seek urgent care for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness. Follow up promptly after laboratory testing and nephrology evaluation.
