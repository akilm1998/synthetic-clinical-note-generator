Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and markedly abnormal renal function.

Subjective

Lily Peterson is a 37-year-old woman, 175 cm, 70.4 kg, BMI 23.0 kg/m², presenting for urgent outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 11 years ago. Current management has not been established. She reports fatigue but denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. She smokes. Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease diagnosed 1 year ago and hyperlipidemia for 6 years. No current medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 137/83 mmHg, pulse 81/min, respirations 17/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory results: serum creatinine 5.85 mg/dL, eGFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 66 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. The patient has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, fluid retention, dyspnea, or urinary changes. Given the degree of renal impairment and lack of established management, this requires urgent specialist evaluation and close monitoring for complications.

Coronary artery disease, diagnosed 1 year ago. No active cardiopulmonary symptoms reported.

Hyperlipidemia, diagnosed 6 years ago.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning. Recommend prompt assessment of serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium/phosphorus, CBC, intact parathyroid hormone, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and renal ultrasound. These results are needed to evaluate for metabolic complications, anemia, proteinuria, and potentially reversible or structural contributors.

Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications; review all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use at the next contact. Counsel regarding avoidance of dehydration and the importance of coordinated renal and cardiovascular care.

Proceed to the emergency department immediately for worsening fatigue, nausea or vomiting, confusion, new weakness, chest pain, shortness of breath, swelling, markedly reduced urination, or other acute deterioration.

Smoking cessation counseling provided. Close follow-up is required after nephrology assessment and repeat renal-related laboratory evaluation.
