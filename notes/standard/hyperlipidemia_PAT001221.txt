Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Christopher Perry is a 70-year-old male seen for follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 9 years and not currently under active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid results remain markedly elevated: total cholesterol 355 mg/dL, LDL-C 241 mg/dL, HDL-C 42 mg/dL, and triglycerides 358 mg/dL.

He denies chest discomfort, dizziness, or intermittent claudication. He reports fatty skin deposits consistent with xanthomas. No xanthelasma reported. His history is notable for coronary artery disease for approximately 7 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 25 years. He continues to smoke. No interval cardiovascular symptoms reported.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 128/76 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 189 cm, weight 98.6 kg, BMI 27.6 kg/m².

Lipid panel:
Total cholesterol: 355 mg/dL
LDL-C: 241 mg/dL
HDL-C: 42 mg/dL
Triglycerides: 358 mg/dL

Patient reports xanthomas. No xanthelasma reported. No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe elevation of LDL cholesterol and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia, longstanding and currently untreated. The LDL-C of 241 mg/dL is particularly concerning in the setting of known coronary artery disease. Reported xanthomas further support a prolonged, significant lipid burden. He is presently without angina, claudication, or other reported ischemic symptoms.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, without reported interval chest discomfort.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significantly abnormal lipid profile and increased cardiovascular risk, particularly given his coronary artery disease and ongoing smoking. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after review of contraindications, medication interactions, and shared decision-making. Given the degree of LDL elevation and established coronary disease, additional therapy such as ezetimibe may be needed if LDL remains substantially elevated despite maximally tolerated statin therapy.

Recommend repeating a fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation and adjustment to assess response. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk assessment, along with evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia. Liver enzymes should be reviewed before and during therapy as clinically indicated in the context of his known fatty liver disease.

Counseled on a heart-healthy diet emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates and alcohol, increased dietary fiber, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encouraged weight maintenance and gradual improvement in exercise. Strongly advised smoking cessation; discussed that cessation is particularly important given his coronary artery disease.

Reviewed warning symptoms, including new or worsening chest discomfort, shortness of breath, dizziness, or exertional leg pain, for which urgent evaluation is appropriate. Continue outpatient follow-up to review treatment tolerance and lipid response.
