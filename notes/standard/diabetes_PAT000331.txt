Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Evan Bryant is a 69-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 9 years ago. He is not currently engaged in a documented diabetes treatment regimen. Recent laboratory testing shows poor glycemic control with hemoglobin A1c of 9.8% and fasting glucose of 233 mg/dL.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or frequent infections. He reports tingling in the hands or feet, without reported numbness. No medication use or adverse medication effects are reported.

Past medical history is also notable for dyslipidemia for approximately 13 years and depression for approximately 32 years. He currently smokes. No additional interval concerns are reported.

Objective

Height 167 cm. Weight 78.1 kg. BMI 28.0 kg/m².

Blood pressure 129/77 mmHg. Pulse 76 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 37.0°C. Oxygen saturation 97%.

Recent laboratory results: hemoglobin A1c 9.8%; fasting glucose 233 mg/dL.

No abnormal physical examination findings are provided for today’s visit.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with significantly above-goal glycemic control based on hemoglobin A1c of 9.8% and fasting glucose of 233 mg/dL. He does not report osmotic or constitutional symptoms, but the elevated values require active treatment and closer monitoring. Tingling in the hands or feet raises concern for possible peripheral sensory involvement; further examination is appropriate before attributing this to diabetic neuropathy.

Dyslipidemia, longstanding, requiring reassessment and cardiovascular risk reduction in the setting of diabetes and tobacco use.

Depression, longstanding. Current symptom burden and treatment status were not addressed in the available interval history and should be reviewed.

Overweight with BMI 28.0 kg/m².

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of initiating a structured diabetes management plan, including nutrition focused on reducing concentrated carbohydrates and portion sizes, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight management, and home glucose monitoring if available. Given the degree of hyperglycemia and lack of current management, pharmacologic therapy should be initiated after reviewing contraindications and baseline renal function; metformin would be a reasonable initial option if renal function and gastrointestinal tolerance permit. Escalation to additional therapy should be considered if glycemic control remains inadequate.

Repeat hemoglobin A1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response. Recommend fasting lipid profile, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio for diabetes risk assessment and medication planning. Recommend routine diabetic retinal evaluation and a complete foot examination, including assessment of protective sensation and peripheral pulses. Counsel regarding daily foot inspection and prompt evaluation of wounds.

Assess the reported distal tingling further at follow-up and review for progression, weakness, balance difficulty, or loss of protective sensation.

Address dyslipidemia with updated lipid testing and discussion of cardiovascular risk reduction, including lipid-lowering therapy as clinically appropriate.

Counseled on smoking cessation and offered support with a specific cessation plan at the next visit. Review depressive symptoms, safety, and current treatment needs, with treatment or behavioral health support as appropriate.

Follow up promptly after initiation of diabetes therapy to review glucose readings and tolerance, with repeat glycemic assessment within approximately three months.
