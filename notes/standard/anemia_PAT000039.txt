Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Penelope Long is an 81-year-old woman, 170 cm tall and 61.8 kg, with BMI 21.4 kg/m², presenting for outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 15 years. She reports no current fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, headache, palpitations, shortness of breath, reduced exercise tolerance, or recognized pallor. No interval anemia-related symptoms are reported.

She has a 22-year history of heart failure, which is a potential contributor to chronic inflammatory anemia. She continues to smoke. No current anemia-directed medication regimen is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 138/80 mmHg, pulse 92 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory studies: Hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL, hematocrit 25.8%, RBC count 3.0 million/µL, MCV 86 fL. Serum iron 38 µg/dL, ferritin 154 ng/mL, TIBC 250 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 15.2%.

Assessment

Anemia of chronic disease/inflammation, longstanding and currently without reported symptoms. The anemia is normocytic with reduced serum iron and transferrin saturation, a relatively preserved ferritin level, and low-normal TIBC, a pattern compatible with impaired iron availability in chronic inflammation. Her longstanding heart failure may be contributory. The hemoglobin remains significantly reduced, and the condition has not been under active management.

Heart failure, longstanding. No cardiopulmonary symptoms are reported today; oxygen saturation is 96%, with mildly increased respiratory rate at 22/min and pulse of 92 bpm. Current volume status and heart failure treatment are not assessed in the available information.

Tobacco use. Continued smoking may adversely affect cardiopulmonary reserve and overall health.

Plan

Further characterize the anemia with repeat CBC, reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers. Review the trend in hemoglobin and assess for additional contributors to anemia, including occult blood loss or nutritional deficiency, as clinically appropriate.

Given hemoglobin of 8.8 g/dL and the prolonged period without active management, arrange timely outpatient reassessment and consideration of hematology involvement. Iron replacement should not be initiated solely on the ferritin value; the low transferrin saturation should be interpreted in the context of renal function, inflammatory status, and any evidence of blood loss. Intravenous iron or an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent may be considered only if a specific indication is established after evaluation.

Monitor for development of fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, chest discomfort, syncope, or declining exercise tolerance. Seek urgent evaluation for chest pain, significant shortness of breath, syncope, or acute clinical deterioration.

Continue attention to heart failure symptoms and coordinate anemia management with ongoing cardiac care. Smoking cessation was advised. Reassess after the recommended laboratory evaluation to update treatment planning.
