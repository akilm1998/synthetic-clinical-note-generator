Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Simon Bishop is a 51-year-old man with a 14-year history of chronic kidney disease, hypertension for 3 years, secondary hyperparathyroidism for 6 years, and anemia of chronic disease for 2 years. He presents for outpatient medication review. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. He continues to smoke. Current medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance require reconciliation against his active treatment regimen before adjustments are made.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 156/93 mmHg, pulse 80/min, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 182 cm, weight 70.2 kg, BMI 21.2 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 5.58 mg/dL, BUN 69 mg/dL, estimated GFR 9 mL/min/1.73 m².

No reported symptoms suggestive of current uremic prodrome or clinically evident fluid overload.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, present for approximately 14 years. Current creatinine is 5.58 mg/dL, BUN 69 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 9 mL/min/1.73 m². He is presently without reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms, but the degree of renal impairment warrants prompt specialist management and preparation for potential renal replacement therapy if clinically indicated.

Hypertension remains suboptimally controlled at 156/93 mmHg. Medication efficacy and adherence cannot be adequately judged until the active medication regimen, dosing, and use pattern are directly reconciled.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, chronic comorbidity associated with his kidney disease.

Anemia of chronic disease, chronic comorbidity associated with his kidney disease.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Perform complete medication reconciliation, including prescribed, over-the-counter, and nonprescription agents. Confirm adherence, dosing schedule, therapeutic response, and adverse effects before making medication changes. Review for nephrotoxic medications and avoid NSAIDs unless specifically directed by a treating clinician.

Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation for advanced chronic kidney disease management, assessment for complications, and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning based on symptoms, laboratory trends, and overall clinical status.

Recommend monitoring serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone. Consider renal ultrasound as part of structural assessment if not previously completed.

Address blood pressure control after medication reconciliation, with individualized targets and renal-appropriate therapy selection. Reinforce sodium restriction and avoidance of dehydration.

Discuss smoking cessation and provide cessation support. Advise immediate medical evaluation for new shortness of breath, progressive edema, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, or significant reduction in urine output.
