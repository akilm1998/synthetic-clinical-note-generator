Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Savannah Bell is a 60-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 24 years ago. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No tobacco use.

Her medical history is also significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 15 years, gout for 10 years, and coronary artery disease for 13 years. Chronic kidney disease is not currently under active management. No current medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Height 167 cm; weight 74.2 kg; BMI 26.6 kg/m².

Blood pressure 142/84 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Recent laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 3.76 mg/dL
BUN 84 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m²

No edema, respiratory distress, or other abnormal physical findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with significant reduction in renal function and marked azotemia. The patient has a longstanding history of kidney disease in the setting of diabetes, mildly elevated blood pressure, gout, and coronary artery disease. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction; anemia and other metabolic complications should be assessed. She currently denies symptoms suggestive of clinically overt volume overload or uremic syndrome, including nausea, pruritus, sleep disturbance, dyspnea, or edema.

Diabetes mellitus, longstanding, with chronic kidney disease.

Coronary artery disease.

Gout.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney dysfunction and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if clinically indicated. Review goals of care and options for future renal support based on symptoms, laboratory trends, and overall health status.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, complete blood count, intact parathyroid hormone, urinalysis, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess for metabolic complications, anemia, mineral-bone disorder, and ongoing proteinuria.

Recommend renal ultrasound to evaluate kidney structure and exclude obstructive or other structural contributors.

Optimize blood pressure and diabetes management with attention to renal and cardiovascular risk reduction. Medication reconciliation is needed before initiating or adjusting therapy; avoid nephrotoxic medications and adjust renally cleared medications as appropriate, particularly in the setting of gout and coronary artery disease.

Counseled regarding prompt medical evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, edema, markedly decreased urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or other acute change in condition. Close follow-up after nephrology assessment and repeat laboratory evaluation.
