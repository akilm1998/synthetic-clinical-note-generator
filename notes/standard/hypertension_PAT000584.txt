Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Jasmine Spencer is an 89-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 16-year history of essential hypertension that is currently not actively managed. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for approximately 35 years, chronic kidney disease for approximately 26 years, and hyperlipidemia for approximately 12 years. She does not smoke. Height is 160 cm and weight is 62.0 kg, with BMI 24.2 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure 169/94 mmHg. Pulse 75 bpm. Respiratory rate 20/min. Temperature 36.8°C. Oxygen saturation 95%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 139 mmol/L, potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with a markedly elevated office blood pressure in the setting of longstanding disease and no current active management. She is asymptomatic without features suggestive of hypertensive emergency today. Her coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease increase the clinical importance of achieving careful blood pressure control. Electrolytes are currently within the reported reference range.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

Preventive health and chronic disease review completed.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure during the visit using appropriate cuff size and standardized technique, with assessment of home blood pressure readings if available. Given the persistent elevation and her coronary and renal comorbidities, discuss initiation of an individualized antihypertensive regimen, beginning cautiously because of advanced age and monitoring for orthostatic symptoms, falls, renal function changes, and electrolyte abnormalities. Medication selection should account for her chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease.

Recommend renal function testing and urinalysis, including assessment for proteinuria, before and after treatment initiation as clinically indicated. Repeat serum electrolytes should accompany antihypertensive management. Recommend ECG and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk and hypertension assessment.

Counseled regarding a lower-sodium diet, avoidance of excessive alcohol, regular activity appropriate to functional capacity, and maintenance of healthy weight. Review adherence, home blood pressure technique, and tolerance of any prescribed therapy at close follow-up rather than waiting for the next annual visit.

Seek urgent medical attention for chest pain, new shortness of breath, severe headache, acute visual change, focal weakness, confusion, or other sudden neurologic symptoms.

Continue comprehensive preventive care review, including immunizations, fall-risk assessment, medication reconciliation, and age-appropriate screening based on overall health status and patient preferences.
