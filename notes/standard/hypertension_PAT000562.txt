Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Monica Shaw is a 57-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately two years ago. Her blood pressure has not been actively managed with a documented treatment regimen. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. She has no acute cardiovascular or neurologic complaints today.

She has type 2 diabetes mellitus of approximately seven years’ duration. She does not smoke. Her current weight is 67.1 kg at a height of 180 cm, corresponding to a BMI of 20.7 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 143/89 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 145 mmol/L, potassium 4.4 mmol/L.

No additional physical examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension with blood pressure above goal at today’s visit. She is asymptomatic without features concerning for hypertensive emergency. The duration of hypertension is approximately two years, and the absence of active management warrants structured reassessment and initiation of an evidence-based management plan, particularly in the context of coexisting type 2 diabetes mellitus. Electrolytes are currently within an acceptable range, with sodium 145 mmol/L and potassium 4.4 mmol/L.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for seven years, relevant to overall cardiovascular and renal risk assessment.

Plan

Confirm the blood pressure measurement with repeat office readings and encourage home blood pressure monitoring using a validated upper-arm cuff, with a written log for review. Discuss initiation of antihypertensive pharmacotherapy based on confirmed readings and individualized cardiovascular and renal risk; medication selection should account for her diabetes and baseline electrolytes.

Recommend a heart-healthy, reduced-sodium dietary pattern, regular aerobic and resistance physical activity as tolerated, and moderation of alcohol intake. Her BMI is currently within the healthy range, so weight reduction is not indicated.

Recommend assessment of cardiovascular and end-organ risk with an ECG, lipid profile, urinalysis, and evaluation of renal function as clinically appropriate. Continue coordinated management of diabetes and reinforce adherence to routine diabetes care.

She should seek urgent evaluation for severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, chest pain, shortness of breath, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Arrange outpatient follow-up after home blood pressure readings are obtained to reassess control and finalize treatment.
