Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Charlie Brown is a 53-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, reported to have been present for approximately 21 years. His history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus of 15 years’ duration and ongoing tobacco use. He has not had active management of his kidney disease. He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, changes in urination, or shortness of breath.

Objective

Height 167 cm, weight 60.2 kg, BMI 21.6 kg/m². Blood pressure is 135/84 mmHg, pulse 80/min, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, and oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Available laboratory results show serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 76 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 6 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced kidney function, creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 76 mg/dL, and eGFR 6 mL/min/1.73 m². Although he is currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, these results represent advanced renal impairment requiring prompt specialist assessment and active management. The long duration of kidney disease and longstanding diabetes are important contributing risk factors. Current blood pressure is reasonably controlled at today’s visit. No medication regimen or renal replacement therapy plan is documented.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for 15 years, with chronic kidney disease. Glycemic control data are not provided today.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning, including dialysis and transplantation options as clinically appropriate.

Obtain or update a serum electrolyte panel, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, complete blood count, intact parathyroid hormone, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and renal ultrasound as part of the recommended renal evaluation. Repeat renal function testing promptly and monitor for hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, anemia, mineral-bone abnormalities, and worsening uremic or volume-related symptoms.

Perform formal medication reconciliation at the next clinical contact and avoid nephrotoxic medications, including nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, unless specifically directed by a clinician. Medication dosing should be reviewed in the context of the markedly reduced eGFR.

Continue management of diabetes and blood pressure with attention to kidney-safe treatment choices and individualized glycemic targets. Encourage avoidance of excess dietary sodium and prompt reporting of reduced urine output, new edema, dyspnea, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, or chest pain.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, given its adverse effects on cardiovascular and renal risk. Close follow-up is recommended after the renal evaluation rather than routine annual monitoring.
