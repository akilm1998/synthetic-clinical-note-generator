Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Hunter Ford is a 67-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 12 years and currently without documented lipid-lowering management. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. He does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 23 years’ duration, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of approximately 27 years’ duration, and obesity for approximately 15 years. Current weight is 98.7 kg at a height of 177 cm, with BMI 31.5 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 140/82 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory results: Total cholesterol 294 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 204 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 45 mg/dL, triglycerides 224 mg/dL.

No xanthomas or xanthelasma are reported. No cardiovascular or claudication symptoms are reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with marked elevation of LDL cholesterol and moderate hypertriglyceridemia. Current lipid profile demonstrates total cholesterol 294 mg/dL, LDL 204 mg/dL, HDL 45 mg/dL, and triglycerides 224 mg/dL. The degree and chronicity of LDL elevation, in the setting of advanced age, obesity, chronic kidney disease, and fatty liver disease, confer substantially increased atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk despite the absence of current symptoms. He is not currently described as receiving lipid-lowering therapy.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. This is an important cardiovascular risk-enhancing comorbidity and should be considered when selecting and monitoring therapy.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding. Lipid treatment should be coordinated with attention to hepatic status; fatty liver disease alone is not a reason to defer indicated statin therapy.

Obesity with BMI 31.5 kg/m².

Blood pressure is mildly elevated at today’s visit at 140/82 mmHg; this should be reassessed over time but does not establish a separate diagnosis based on this single reading.

Plan

Discussed the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and associated cardiovascular risk. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, following shared decision-making and review of prior treatment history, contraindications, and potential adverse effects. The treatment goal should be a substantial reduction in LDL cholesterol, with further intensification considered if response is inadequate.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid assessment after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response and adherence. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia, with selection of testing guided by his chronic kidney disease and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. ApoB and lipoprotein(a) may provide additional risk stratification if clinically useful. Coronary artery calcium imaging may be considered if treatment decisions remain uncertain, although the LDL level itself strongly supports pharmacologic therapy.

Counseled regarding reduction of saturated and trans fats, emphasis on vegetables, whole grains, legumes, lean proteins, and increased dietary fiber, along with weight reduction and regular physical activity as tolerated. Continue avoidance of tobacco.

Coordinate lipid management with ongoing care for chronic kidney disease and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Monitor for development of chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or other symptoms concerning for atherosclerotic disease and seek prompt evaluation if these occur.

Follow up after initiation or adjustment of lipid-lowering therapy to review tolerance, adherence, and repeat lipid results.
