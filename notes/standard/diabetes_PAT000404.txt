Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Charles Bennett is a 62-year-old man seen for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 28 years ago. His diabetes has not been actively managed recently. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. He has no reported symptoms suggestive of an acute hyperglycemic complication.

He also has longstanding dyslipidemia, present for approximately 32 years, and anxiety of approximately 33 years’ duration. He currently smokes. No medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Height is 161 cm, weight 59.9 kg, and BMI 23.1 kg/m². Blood pressure is 130/80 mmHg, pulse 75 beats/min, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.6°C, and oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin A1c 10.0% and fasting glucose 257 mg/dL, consistent with substantially elevated average and fasting glucose levels.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, with markedly inadequate glycemic control based on hemoglobin A1c of 10.0% and fasting glucose of 257 mg/dL. Despite the absence of hyperglycemic symptoms, these values indicate persistent significant hyperglycemia and require prompt re-engagement in treatment.

Dyslipidemia, longstanding.

Anxiety, longstanding.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated hemoglobin A1c and fasting glucose and the risk of microvascular and macrovascular complications with prolonged hyperglycemia. Recommend prompt initiation or re-establishment of a structured diabetes treatment plan, including medication therapy appropriate to his overall health and renal function, to be reviewed and prescribed after medication reconciliation and shared decision-making.

Counseled regarding reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and refined sugars, portion control, regular physical activity as tolerated, and continued attention to weight and nutrition. Although his BMI is 23.1 kg/m², lifestyle measures remain important for glycemic and cardiovascular risk reduction.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with documentation of fasting and, when appropriate, postprandial readings to guide treatment adjustment. Repeat hemoglobin A1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response.

Recommend diabetes preventive care, including urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive foot examination, and dilated retinal examination. Smoking cessation was strongly advised, with discussion of its importance in reducing cardiovascular and diabetes-related vascular risk.

Dyslipidemia and anxiety should be reassessed as part of comprehensive cardiometabolic care. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended to review glucose readings, complete medication reconciliation, and advance therapy until glycemic control improves.
