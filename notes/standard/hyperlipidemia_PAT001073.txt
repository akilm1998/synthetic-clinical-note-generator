Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Cameron West is a 75-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 3 years and not actively managed. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness. Reports xanthomas and xanthelasma. He does not smoke.

Relevant history includes coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, hypertension for 26 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 35 years. No current lipid-lowering medication regimen is documented.

Objective

Vital Signs: BP 141/90 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respirations 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 165 cm, weight 62.9 kg, BMI 23.1 kg/m².

Laboratory Results:
Total cholesterol 286 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 196 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 44 mg/dL
Triglycerides 229 mg/dL

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, currently without active treatment. Presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma supports longstanding lipid elevation. Given known coronary artery disease, the lipid profile represents a significant cardiovascular risk. No current ischemic or claudication symptoms reported.

Hypertension with mildly elevated office blood pressure today.

History of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Plan

Discussed the markedly abnormal lipid profile and increased cardiovascular risk in the setting of known coronary artery disease. Recommend initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy, preferably a high-intensity statin if tolerated, after shared decision-making. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, consider additional lipid-lowering therapy.

Counseled on a heart-healthy diet emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, increased fiber, and limitation of refined carbohydrates and alcohol. Continue regular physical activity as tolerated. Smoking avoidance reinforced.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider assessment of liver enzymes as clinically appropriate given longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and planned lipid therapy.

Monitor blood pressure and continue routine cardiovascular follow-up. Advised to seek urgent evaluation for new chest discomfort, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain.
