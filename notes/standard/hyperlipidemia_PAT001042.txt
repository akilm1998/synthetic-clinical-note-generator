Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Spencer Lawson is a 62-year-old man presenting for follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 32 years and currently not under active treatment. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, and dizziness. He reports fatty-appearing deposits involving the skin and eyelids, consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma. He does not smoke.

He has longstanding obesity, present for approximately 41 years. Current weight is 95.5 kg at a height of 164 cm.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 123/78 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

BMI: 35.5 kg/m².

Recent lipid panel: Total cholesterol 351 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 268 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 35 mg/dL, triglycerides 240 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol, low HDL cholesterol, and elevated triglycerides. Disease has been present for approximately 32 years and is currently untreated. Xanthomas and xanthelasma are reported. He has no current chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness. The LDL level is substantially above the threshold at which pharmacologic therapy is strongly indicated for cardiovascular risk reduction.

Obesity, BMI 35.5 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the significant cardiovascular risk associated with the current lipid levels and the importance of active treatment. Recommend initiation of a high-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, following shared decision-making and review of contraindications and potential adverse effects. Medication use is not currently documented.

Counseled regarding reduction of saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates and processed foods, increasing dietary fiber, and emphasizing vegetables, legumes, whole grains, and lean protein sources. Encouraged gradual, regular physical activity as tolerated and weight reduction.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment is initiated to assess response and adherence. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to severe dyslipidemia and additional risk stratification, including ApoB and lipoprotein(a), as clinically appropriate. Coronary artery calcium imaging may be considered if treatment decisions remain uncertain after discussion.

Advised to seek urgent evaluation for new chest pain, exertional shortness of breath, sudden neurologic symptoms, or significant leg pain with walking. Continue outpatient follow-up to review treatment decisions and lipid response.
