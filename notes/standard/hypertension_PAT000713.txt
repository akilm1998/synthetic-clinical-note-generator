Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Thomas Foster is a 38-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 12 years ago. Blood pressure management has not been established recently. He reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 7 years, hyperlipidemia for 6 years, and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. He does not smoke. Current medication use, allergies, and home blood pressure readings were not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 135/91 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 181 cm; weight 73.1 kg; BMI 22.3 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 144 mmol/L, potassium 3.7 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension of approximately 12 years’ duration with persistent diastolic elevation on today’s reading. The reported dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue are nonspecific but warrant correlation with blood pressure pattern and cardiac risk profile. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency.

Hypertension is clinically important in the setting of type 2 diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, and established coronary artery disease. Longitudinal control and cardiovascular risk reduction require structured follow-up and active treatment planning.

Plan

Obtain and review standardized home blood pressure measurements, including morning and evening readings, to clarify the out-of-office blood pressure pattern. Reinforce sodium reduction, regular aerobic physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excessive alcohol intake, and continued weight maintenance; current BMI is 22.3 kg/m².

Given persistent diastolic elevation and the patient’s diabetes and coronary artery disease, discuss initiation or re-establishment of antihypertensive therapy after review of current medications, contraindications, and home readings. Choice of agent should account for cardiovascular and diabetic comorbidity. The patient should not self-adjust or start medication without clinician-directed review.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin, repeat lipid evaluation, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular and hypertension risk assessment. Recommend a 12-lead ECG given palpitations and known coronary artery disease. Continue coordinated management of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and coronary artery disease.

Seek urgent evaluation for chest pain, new shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, severe headache, syncope, or sustained markedly elevated blood pressure. Arrange outpatient reassessment after a period of home monitoring to review blood pressure control, symptoms, and treatment plan.
