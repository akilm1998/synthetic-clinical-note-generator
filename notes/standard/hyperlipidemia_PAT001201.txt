Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Eliana Matthews is a 36-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 6 years and currently without an established treatment regimen. She denies chest discomfort, exertional calf pain, dizziness, or other reported cardiovascular symptoms. She reports fatty-appearing deposits involving the skin and eyelids, consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma.

She continues to smoke. Her comorbid conditions include obesity and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, each documented for approximately 3–4 years. Current weight is 89.0 kg at a height of 153 cm, with BMI 38.0 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 116/79 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results: Total cholesterol 362 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 269 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 35 mg/dL, triglycerides 288 mg/dL.

Reported clinical findings include xanthomas and xanthelasma. No chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia and low HDL cholesterol. The LDL level of 269 mg/dL represents severe untreated hypercholesterolemia and, together with the reported xanthomas and xanthelasma, warrants prompt risk reduction and evaluation for contributing causes. She has no reported anginal or claudication symptoms at this visit.

Obesity, BMI 38.0 kg/m², contributing to cardiometabolic risk and likely relevant to her dyslipidemic pattern.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, established for approximately 4 years. This is not a contraindication to statin therapy in the absence of evidence of active liver injury.

Active tobacco use, an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and the need for active treatment rather than observation alone. Recommend initiation of high-intensity lipid-lowering therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after medication reconciliation and confirmation of no contraindication. Given her age and potential for pregnancy, pregnancy status and reproductive plans should be reviewed before starting therapy.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess calories, with increased dietary fiber and regular physical activity as tolerated. Weight reduction was encouraged as part of management of obesity, hypertriglyceridemia, and fatty liver disease.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered discussion of evidence-based cessation support.

Recommend evaluation for secondary contributors to severe dyslipidemia and further risk characterization, including a fasting lipid panel, apolipoprotein B, lipoprotein(a), and assessment of glycemic and thyroid status as clinically appropriate. Consider coronary artery calcium assessment if treatment decisions or long-term risk discussion remain uncertain; this should not delay treatment of the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol.

Plan to reassess tolerance and adherence after treatment initiation and repeat lipid measurements to assess response and guide further intensification, including consideration of add-on therapy if LDL reduction remains inadequate. Follow up after initiation of therapy for review of response and ongoing cardiovascular risk-factor management.
