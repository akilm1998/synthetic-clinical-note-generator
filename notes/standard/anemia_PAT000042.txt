Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for longstanding anemia.

Subjective

Simone Bishop is a 75-year-old woman, height 165 cm, weight 70.0 kg, BMI 25.7 kg/m², presenting for routine follow-up of anemia present for approximately 44 years. She reports headache but denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor. There is no reported interval worsening of anemia-related symptoms. She currently smokes.

Relevant comorbidities include malignancy of 7 years’ duration and type 2 diabetes mellitus of 12 years’ duration. No current anemia-directed treatment is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 134/81 mmHg, pulse 90 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Recent laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 9.3 g/dL
Hematocrit 27.9%
RBC count 3.13 million/µL
MCV 89 fL
Serum iron 22 µg/dL
Ferritin 226 ng/mL
TIBC 268 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 8.2%

Assessment

Longstanding normocytic anemia with moderate reduction in hemoglobin and hematocrit. The low serum iron and markedly reduced transferrin saturation in the setting of an elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC are most consistent with impaired iron availability associated with chronic inflammation or chronic disease. Her known malignancy may be contributory. The chronicity and absence of cardiopulmonary symptoms suggest current clinical stability, although the headache may be anemia-related or multifactorial.

The pattern does not establish isolated absolute iron deficiency; concomitant iron deficiency should be considered if supported by the broader clinical evaluation. Further characterization is appropriate, particularly given her age, longstanding anemia, malignancy, and diabetes.

Plan

Recommend completion or review of a CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to assess marrow response and red-cell morphology. Obtain vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate to evaluate reversible contributors and the contribution of chronic disease.

Review the activity and treatment status of the underlying malignancy and assess for other chronic inflammatory or infectious contributors. Consider evaluation for occult blood loss if clinically indicated based on history and examination.

Do not initiate empiric iron replacement solely on the basis of the low serum iron, given the elevated ferritin and pattern suggestive of inflammation-related iron sequestration. If additional evaluation supports functional or absolute iron deficiency, oral or intravenous iron can be considered. Erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy would require specialist assessment of the underlying disease, renal function, treatment intent, and risk-benefit profile.

Monitor for worsening fatigue, weakness, dyspnea, palpitations, dizziness, reduced exercise tolerance, or progressive headache. Continue outpatient hematology-focused follow-up after the recommended evaluation.
