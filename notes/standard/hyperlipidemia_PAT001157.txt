Chief Complaint

Annual review and management of longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Caroline Rivera is an 85-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 34-year history of hyperlipidemia that has not been actively managed. Recent lipid results show markedly elevated total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, and triglycerides. She reports fatty skin deposits consistent with xanthomas. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, or xanthelasma.

Medical history is significant for chronic kidney disease for approximately 30 years, hypertension for 54 years, and obesity for 14 years. She continues to smoke. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Height 160 cm; weight 99.1 kg; BMI 38.7 kg/m².

Blood pressure 159/92 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Lipid panel:
Total cholesterol 317 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 196 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 48 mg/dL
Triglycerides 364 mg/dL

Xanthomas are reported. No chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe elevation of LDL cholesterol and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia, longstanding for approximately 34 years and currently without active management. The lipid pattern, obesity, tobacco use, hypertension, chronic kidney disease, and advanced age confer substantially increased atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, despite the absence of current anginal or claudication symptoms. Reported xanthomas support prolonged lipid elevation.

Hypertension with blood pressure above goal at today’s visit. Chronic kidney disease and longstanding hypertension increase overall cardiovascular risk and should be considered when selecting lipid-lowering therapy.

Class II obesity with BMI 38.7 kg/m².

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the increased cardiovascular risk associated with longstanding hyperlipidemia, smoking, hypertension, obesity, and chronic kidney disease. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of lipid-lowering therapy, with consideration of a statin appropriate for her age, renal status, overall health, and goals of care. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, additional lipid-lowering options can be considered.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid assessment after treatment decisions are made and periodic monitoring of lipid response and treatment safety. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and measurement of apolipoprotein B or lipoprotein(a) if results would change management. Coronary artery calcium imaging is unlikely to be necessary if treatment is otherwise clearly indicated, but could be discussed only if treatment decisions remain uncertain.

Counseled on a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, fish, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol. Encourage gradual activity appropriate to functional capacity and joint tolerance, with weight reduction as a long-term goal.

Strongly advised smoking cessation; discussed cessation support and pharmacologic options as appropriate after review of her current medications and preferences.

Recommend prompt reassessment of blood pressure and review of her antihypertensive regimen, with attention to chronic kidney disease and avoidance of excessive dietary sodium. Continue routine monitoring of renal function as part of chronic kidney disease care.

Reviewed preventive health priorities for an older adult, including immunizations, fall-risk reduction, physical activity, nutrition, and age- and preference-appropriate cancer screening. Follow up after lipid and blood pressure management decisions, or sooner for chest discomfort, new exertional leg pain, neurologic symptoms, or other concerning symptoms.
