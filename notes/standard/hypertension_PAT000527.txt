Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Chloe Cox is a 78-year-old woman seen for routine outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension, identified approximately 1 year ago. Blood pressure remains elevated today, and she is not currently receiving documented active antihypertensive management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, or epistaxis. She reports palpitations but no associated chest pain, dyspnea, or dizziness.

Relevant history includes coronary artery disease of approximately 26 years’ duration. She does not smoke. Weight is stable at 50.1 kg; height is 153 cm, with BMI 21.4 kg/m².

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 155/99 mmHg; pulse 71 bpm; respiratory rate 17/min; temperature 36.9°C; oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 153 cm; weight 50.1 kg; BMI 21.4 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Sodium 144 mmol/L; potassium 5.0 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure at 155/99 mmHg. She is currently asymptomatic, without features suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Given her age and history of coronary artery disease, blood pressure reduction should be gradual and individualized.

Palpitations without associated chest pain, shortness of breath, dizziness, or syncope reported today.

Potassium is at the upper end of the reported range at 5.0 mmol/L, which is relevant when selecting antihypertensive therapy that may further increase potassium.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding by history.

Plan

Discussed the importance of blood pressure control, dietary sodium reduction, regular activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Continue home blood pressure monitoring with a log for review, including seated readings after several minutes of rest.

Recommend repeat serum electrolytes and renal function assessment before or shortly after initiating therapy, with particular attention to potassium. Given the elevated potassium and coronary artery disease, consider a low-dose calcium-channel blocker such as amlodipine as an initial treatment option rather than empirically starting a potassium-raising agent; final selection should be individualized after medication reconciliation and review of home readings.

Recommend ECG evaluation for reported palpitations. Advise urgent evaluation for chest pain, new shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, syncope, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up in the near term to review home blood pressure readings, laboratory results, palpitations, and response to treatment.
