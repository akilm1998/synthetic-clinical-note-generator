Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Faith Hawkins is an 88-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of anemia present for approximately 25 years. She reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or other reported anemia-related symptoms. No pallor is reported. She does not smoke.

Her relevant comorbidity is inflammatory bowel disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. Anemia has not been actively managed to date. No current medications or treatment regimen are provided.

Objective

Height 151 cm; weight 49.9 kg; BMI 21.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 129/77 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Hemoglobin 9.2 g/dL, hematocrit 27.5%, RBC 3.09 million/µL, MCV 89 fL. Serum iron 33 µg/dL, ferritin 314 ng/mL, TIBC 243 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 13.6%.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, longstanding for approximately 25 years, currently asymptomatic and hemodynamically stable. The pattern of low hemoglobin and hematocrit with normal MCV, low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and relatively low TIBC is most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation, likely occurring in the setting of inflammatory bowel disease. Iron-restricted erythropoiesis may coexist despite preserved iron stores. There is no reported cardiopulmonary compromise or symptomatic deterioration today.

Inflammatory bowel disease, present for approximately 1 year, is a clinically relevant potential contributor to ongoing inflammation and anemia.

Plan

Review the anemia and iron indices with the patient and emphasize the importance of addressing the underlying inflammatory bowel disease as part of anemia management.

Recommend obtaining a CBC with reticulocyte count, peripheral blood smear, vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the chronic anemia and assess for additional contributors. Continue monitoring hemoglobin and hematocrit over time.

Given the low transferrin saturation, discuss evaluation for iron-restricted erythropoiesis and consideration of iron supplementation, with the route and intensity guided by inflammatory bowel disease activity, gastrointestinal tolerance, renal function, and repeat laboratory assessment.

Coordinate ongoing management of inflammatory bowel disease with the treating clinician. Consider hematology evaluation if the anemia worsens, becomes symptomatic, or remains unexplained after initial evaluation.

Advise prompt assessment for new shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, bleeding, or marked decline in exercise tolerance.
