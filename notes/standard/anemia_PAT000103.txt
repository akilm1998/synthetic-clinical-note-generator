Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Julian Holland is a 60-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of longstanding anemia, present for approximately 20 years. He has a history of inflammatory bowel disease for 14 years and chronic infection for 3 years, both of which may contribute to chronic inflammation and impaired iron utilization.

He currently denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, or perceived pallor. No tobacco use. Current anemia-directed treatment is not documented.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 131/77 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respirations 19/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 169 cm, weight 76.3 kg, BMI 26.7 kg/m².

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL
Hematocrit 26.5%
RBC count 3.15 million/µL
MCV 84 fL
Serum iron 31 µg/dL
Ferritin 304 ng/mL
TIBC 239 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 13%

Assessment

1. Chronic normocytic anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation. Hemoglobin and hematocrit remain significantly reduced, with normal MCV, low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and low TIBC. This pattern is compatible with inflammation-associated iron restriction in the setting of longstanding inflammatory bowel disease and chronic infection. He is presently hemodynamically stable and reports no cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms.

2. Inflammatory bowel disease, longstanding, approximately 14 years. Ongoing inflammatory activity may be contributing to the anemia.

3. Chronic infection, approximately 3 years. Chronic inflammatory burden may also be contributing to impaired erythropoiesis.

Plan

Review the anemia and iron indices with the treating clinicians managing his inflammatory bowel disease and chronic infection, with emphasis on controlling underlying inflammatory conditions.

Obtain or review a repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to assess persistence and marrow response. Additional evaluation should include renal function, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers. Continue assessment for gastrointestinal blood loss or active inflammatory bowel disease as clinically indicated.

Given the hemoglobin of 8.6 g/dL and longstanding anemia, consider hematology consultation for guidance regarding further evaluation and suitability of iron replacement or an erythropoiesis-stimulating approach. Iron therapy should be selected after considering inflammatory activity, gastrointestinal tolerance, and the overall cause of anemia; no treatment regimen is initiated or documented today.

Discussed seeking urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, palpitations, syncope, marked dizziness, bleeding, or rapidly worsening weakness. Routine outpatient follow-up after the above evaluation and review of disease control.
