Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for poorly controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Natalie Ross is a 42-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 12 years ago. Current diabetes management has been limited, with recent laboratory testing showing elevated glucose values. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No acute hyperglycemic symptoms reported today.

Relevant history includes obesity for approximately 5 years, dyslipidemia for 12 years, and depression for 22 years. She reports current tobacco use. Medication use and allergies were not provided.

Objective

Height 150 cm, weight 87.3 kg, BMI 38.8 kg/m². Blood pressure 116/77 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Hemoglobin A1c 9.6%. Fasting glucose 206 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently above goal based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. She is clinically stable without reported polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, or impaired wound healing. Twelve-year disease duration and suboptimal current management increase risk for future microvascular and cardiovascular complications.

Obesity, BMI 38.8 kg/m².

Dyslipidemia.

Depression, longstanding.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed that the current HbA1c and fasting glucose indicate inadequate glycemic control despite absence of acute symptoms. Recommend prompt initiation or intensification of evidence-based diabetes therapy after review of medication history, renal function, contraindications, and patient preferences; metformin-based therapy would be reasonable if appropriate, with consideration of additional glucose-lowering therapy if needed. Review home glucose monitoring technique and establish fasting and postprandial monitoring.

Counseled on reducing refined carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated, and weight reduction. Address tobacco cessation at follow-up.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and comprehensive diabetes complication screening, including foot examination and dilated retinal evaluation. Continue management of dyslipidemia and depression in the context of overall cardiometabolic risk.

Seek urgent care for persistent vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, marked weakness, inability to maintain hydration, or markedly elevated home glucose readings. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review glucose readings and treatment response.
