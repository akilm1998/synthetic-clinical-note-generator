Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Leo Davidson is a 50-year-old man presenting for outpatient review of longstanding anemia. The anemia has been present for approximately 15 years and is not currently under active treatment. He denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor. There is no reported smoking history. He has type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. No anemia-directed medication is currently being used, so medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be assessed today.

Objective

Height 171 cm, weight 67.5 kg, BMI 23.1 kg/m². Blood pressure 121/82 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin 8.1 g/dL, hematocrit 24.8%, RBC count 2.82 million/µL, and MCV 88 fL. Serum iron is 37 µg/dL, ferritin 188 ng/mL, TIBC 252 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 14.7%. The pattern is a significant normocytic anemia with low serum iron and transferrin saturation in the setting of a relatively preserved ferritin, which is compatible with anemia associated with chronic inflammation or chronic disease.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, longstanding for approximately 15 years, currently untreated. Hemoglobin is substantially reduced, although he is presently without reported cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms and has stable vital signs. Iron indices do not clearly support isolated iron deficiency and are more consistent with impaired iron availability associated with chronic disease or inflammation. The degree and duration of anemia warrant further clarification and active management rather than observation alone.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 4 years in duration.

Plan

Discussed the laboratory findings and the importance of addressing the anemia despite the absence of current symptoms. No anemia-directed medication was initiated or being reviewed today. Recommend evaluation with repeat CBC, reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers to help clarify the cause and guide treatment. Review for chronic inflammatory, renal, infectious, autoimmune, or other underlying contributors should be incorporated into follow-up care.

Iron therapy should not be started empirically without further assessment, given the normal-range ferritin and pattern suggesting inflammation or chronic disease; oral or intravenous iron may be considered later if additional evaluation supports benefit. If chronic kidney disease or another inflammatory condition is identified, treatment should be directed at the underlying disorder, with consideration of specialist-guided therapy when appropriate.

He was advised to seek urgent care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, marked weakness, or bleeding. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review the anemia evaluation and establish an active management plan.
