Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Spencer Lawson is a 70-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 35 years. He reports ongoing fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath. No acute cardiopulmonary or uremic symptoms are reported today.

Relevant comorbidities include hyperlipidemia for 23 years, secondary hyperparathyroidism for 21 years, and gout for 12 years. He is a current smoker. Current medication use was not provided.

Objective

Height 161 cm, weight 63.5 kg, BMI 24.5 kg/m².

Blood pressure 144/81 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 5.93 mg/dL.
Estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m².
BUN 81 mg/dL.

No physical examination findings beyond the documented vital signs were provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. The patient has a longstanding history of kidney disease and currently reports fatigue but no nausea, pruritus, edema, dyspnea, urinary change, or other reported symptoms suggestive of acute fluid overload or symptomatic uremia. Current renal indices warrant prompt reassessment and active renal specialty management.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, longstanding.

Gout, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning, as clinically appropriate.

Repeat renal function testing and serum electrolytes promptly, along with urinalysis and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. Recommend CBC and intact parathyroid hormone testing as part of CKD monitoring. Consider renal ultrasound if not recently completed.

Review blood pressure control, renal-protective measures, dietary sodium and protein intake, and avoidance of NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications. Medication reconciliation is needed before making specific treatment recommendations.

Continue monitoring for symptoms of worsening uremia or fluid overload, including increasing fatigue, nausea or vomiting, pruritus, confusion, reduced urine output, new edema, or shortness of breath. Seek urgent or emergency care for significant breathing difficulty, chest pain, confusion, or inability to maintain oral intake.

Address ongoing tobacco use and provide smoking-cessation counseling. Continue follow-up for hyperlipidemia, gout, and secondary hyperparathyroidism in coordination with renal care.
