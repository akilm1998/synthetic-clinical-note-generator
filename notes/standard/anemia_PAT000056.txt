Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Claire PriceLucy Sanders is a 68-year-old woman, height 158 cm, weight 69.4 kg, BMI 27.8 kg/m², presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of anemia present in the setting of a 27-year history of inflammatory bowel disease. She reports headache but denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor. She does not smoke. There is no documented active anemia-directed treatment.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 125/75 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

General: No pallor reported.

Cardiovascular: Normal cardiovascular examination; regular pulse at 80 bpm.

Respiratory: Normal respiratory examination.

Laboratory data:
Hemoglobin 8.2 g/dL
Hematocrit 24.0%
RBC count 3.0 million/µL
MCV 80 fL
Serum iron 35 µg/dL
Ferritin 254 ng/mL
TIBC 191 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 18.3%

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of longstanding inflammatory bowel disease. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are substantially reduced, with a low RBC count and borderline normocytic indices. The combination of low serum iron and TIBC with preserved-to-elevated ferritin supports impaired iron utilization associated with chronic inflammation rather than isolated absolute iron depletion, although coexisting iron deficiency or chronic gastrointestinal blood loss should be considered. She is hemodynamically stable and denies cardiopulmonary symptoms, but the reported headache may be anemia-related and warrants clinical monitoring.

Plan

Obtain a repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to further characterize the anemia and assess marrow response.

Recommend evaluation of vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers. Consider assessment for ongoing gastrointestinal blood loss and current inflammatory bowel disease activity, with coordination of care with the clinician managing her inflammatory bowel disease.

Review iron status in clinical context; oral or intravenous iron therapy should be considered only after assessment of inflammatory activity, gastrointestinal tolerance, and the likelihood of concurrent absolute iron deficiency. Erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy is not initiated from the information available and would require confirmation of the underlying cause, particularly renal function and inflammatory disease status.

Monitor for worsening fatigue, dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, or exertional intolerance. Urgent evaluation is advised if such symptoms develop or if there is evidence of significant gastrointestinal bleeding. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after the recommended evaluation to reassess hemoglobin and treatment needs.
