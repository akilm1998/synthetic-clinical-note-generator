Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Elliot Chandler is a 78-year-old man presenting for follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 19 years. Blood pressure has not been actively managed recently. He reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or nosebleeds. He does not smoke.

Relevant medical history includes chronic kidney disease for 5 years, coronary artery disease for 16 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 36 years. Current weight is 85.4 kg at a height of 171 cm, with BMI 29.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 177/98 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 138 mmol/L, potassium 5.1 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic findings are documented today.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently poorly controlled. Blood pressure is markedly elevated at 177/98 mmHg in the setting of chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease, and diabetes. He reports dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue but has no chest pain, dyspnea, severe headache, visual change, or epistaxis to suggest an acute hypertensive emergency based on today’s history. Potassium is mildly elevated at 5.1 mmol/L, which is relevant when selecting antihypertensive therapy in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

2. Chronic kidney disease, duration 5 years. Requires consideration in blood pressure treatment and medication monitoring.

3. Coronary artery disease, duration 16 years. No current angina or shortness of breath reported.

4. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 36 years.

5. Overweight, BMI 29.2 kg/m².

Plan

Repeat blood pressure after seated rest and confirm with appropriate cuff size. Begin active antihypertensive management promptly, with medication selection guided by chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease, diabetes, and potassium of 5.1 mmol/L. Review renal function and repeat electrolytes after treatment is initiated or adjusted; avoid medications that could further increase potassium until renal status and the full medication list are reviewed.

Obtain an ECG for evaluation of palpitations and cardiovascular effects of longstanding hypertension. Recommend urinalysis and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular and renal risk assessment. Encourage home blood pressure monitoring with a written log.

Discuss reduction of dietary sodium, gradual physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Reinforce adherence to follow-up and treatment once established.

Seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, severe headache, acute visual changes, confusion, or persistently rising blood pressure with symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and medication titration.
