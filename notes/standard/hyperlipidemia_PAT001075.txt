Chief Complaint

Annual review of chronic conditions and markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Benjamin Murphy is a 67-year-old man seen for comprehensive annual review. He has a 34-year history of hyperlipidemia that has not been actively treated. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 319 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 221 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 31 mg/dL, and triglycerides 333 mg/dL. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma.

He has a 20-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and a 13-year history of hypertension. He reports no tobacco use. Medication list, allergies, and home glucose or blood pressure readings were not provided for review today.

Objective

Height 187 cm. Weight 88.8 kg. BMI 25.4 kg/m².

Blood pressure 144/86 mmHg. Pulse 69 bpm. Respiratory rate 18/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 98%.

Laboratory results: total cholesterol 319 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 221 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 31 mg/dL, triglycerides 333 mg/dL.

No abnormal physical examination findings were provided. Patient reports no clinical symptoms suggestive of angina, claudication, or cholesterol deposition.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with a markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, accompanied by low HDL cholesterol. This represents a high atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk lipid profile, particularly in the setting of longstanding type 2 diabetes and hypertension. The condition has been untreated or insufficiently addressed for many years. He is currently asymptomatic from a cardiovascular standpoint.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding for approximately 20 years. Glycemic control cannot be assessed from the information available today.

Hypertension, longstanding for approximately 13 years, with an elevated office blood pressure of 144/86 mmHg today.

BMI 25.4 kg/m², in the upper normal range.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid profile and the increased cardiovascular risk associated with LDL cholesterol of 221 mg/dL in the setting of diabetes and hypertension. Recommend initiation of guideline-directed high-intensity statin therapy after review of medication history, contraindications, and patient preferences. If LDL reduction remains inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of nonstatin therapy such as ezetimibe should be considered. A fasting lipid panel should be repeated after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and measurement of additional cardiovascular risk markers when clinically appropriate.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, fish, and unsaturated fats while limiting saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol. Encourage regular aerobic and resistance exercise as tolerated. Continue tobacco avoidance.

Review current diabetes treatment, home glucose monitoring, glycemic control, renal risk assessment, annual dilated eye examination, diabetic foot assessment, and nutrition counseling. Obtain diabetes monitoring studies as clinically indicated, including glycemic and kidney assessment, without assuming current control.

Repeat blood pressure measurement and review home readings if available. Reinforce dietary sodium reduction, regular physical activity, and adherence to any established antihypertensive regimen after medication reconciliation. Adjust antihypertensive management if blood pressure remains above individualized goal.

Review preventive care at the annual visit, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, shingles, and tetanus immunization status, as well as age-appropriate colorectal cancer screening and other preventive services. Counsel regarding prompt evaluation for new chest discomfort, exertional dyspnea, syncope, or leg pain with walking.

Follow up after medication reconciliation and repeat lipid assessment to finalize long-term cardiovascular risk-reduction management.
