Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Morgan Oliver is a 44-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. Glycemic control has not been actively managed recently. Most recent HbA1c is 9.5% and fasting glucose is 214 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet.

Comorbidities include dyslipidemia for approximately 4 years, obstructive sleep apnea for 2 years, and chronic kidney disease for 2 years. Current medication regimen was not provided. She does not smoke.

Objective

Vital signs: blood pressure 125/87 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 162 cm, weight 65.3 kg, BMI 24.9 kg/m².

Laboratory data: HbA1c 9.5%. Fasting glucose 214 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, present for approximately 2 years, with significant hyperglycemia based on HbA1c 9.5% and fasting glucose 214 mg/dL. She is currently asymptomatic despite poor biochemical control. No neuropathic or infectious symptoms are reported. BMI is 24.9 kg/m². Chronic kidney disease is a relevant comorbidity and will affect selection and dosing of glucose-lowering therapy; no renal function values are available in the information reviewed today.

Dyslipidemia.

Obstructive sleep apnea.

Chronic kidney disease, stage not specified.

Plan

Discussed that current glycemic indices are above goal and warrant active treatment and close follow-up, even in the absence of symptoms. Review current medication use, adherence, prior intolerance, dietary pattern, and home glucose monitoring at the next visit. Recommend initiation or adjustment of evidence-based glucose-lowering therapy after reviewing renal function and contraindications; metformin and/or a kidney- and cardiovascular-beneficial agent such as an SGLT2 inhibitor may be considered when clinically appropriate. Avoid assuming or continuing a specific regimen until the medication list and renal function are confirmed.

Recommend diabetes-focused nutrition counseling emphasizing reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, regular physical activity as tolerated, and consistent meal planning. Given the elevated HbA1c, provide or arrange home glucose monitoring with review of fasting and occasional postprandial readings.

Obtain or review serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to guide therapy and assess diabetic kidney involvement. Recommend repeat HbA1c after an interval sufficient to assess treatment response. Consider fasting lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk management.

Recommend comprehensive diabetes preventive care, including dilated retinal examination, complete foot examination with neuropathy and vascular assessment, and ongoing renal monitoring. Counsel regarding daily foot inspection and prompt evaluation of wounds or infection.

Continue management of dyslipidemia, obstructive sleep apnea, and chronic kidney disease in coordination with the appropriate clinicians. Return for diabetes follow-up after medication and renal function review, with earlier evaluation for symptomatic hyperglycemia, vomiting, dehydration, confusion, or other acute concerns.
