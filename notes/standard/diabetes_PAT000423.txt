Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for diabetes management.

Subjective

Andrew Morris is a 32-year-old man with an 11-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus who presents for focused follow-up after markedly elevated glucose measurements. He is not currently engaged in active diabetes management. HbA1c is 9.1% and fasting glucose is 238 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has an 8-year history of anxiety. He currently smokes. No medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Height 187 cm, weight 97.2 kg, BMI 27.8 kg/m². Blood pressure 115/81 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%. HbA1c 9.1%. Fasting glucose 238 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently significantly above glycemic goal based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. Although asymptomatic today, the degree and duration of hyperglycemia warrant prompt treatment and monitoring.

Anxiety, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose, including the risk of future microvascular and cardiovascular complications even in the absence of current symptoms. Arrange prompt outpatient diabetes management to initiate or restart appropriate glucose-lowering therapy; medication selection should account for renal function, comorbidities, contraindications, cost, and patient preference.

Recommend structured nutrition counseling, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, regular physical activity as tolerated, and weight management. Encourage home glucose monitoring if a monitoring plan is established.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive foot examination, and diabetic retinal screening as part of complication surveillance. Review immunizations and cardiovascular risk reduction at follow-up.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and offered support with a cessation plan. Anxiety should be reassessed during ongoing primary care follow-up.

Return promptly for worsening thirst or urination, vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, marked weakness, or other acute change. Close follow-up is recommended to review treatment response and glucose monitoring.
