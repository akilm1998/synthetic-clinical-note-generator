Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Ellie Wood is a 62-year-old woman seen for outpatient review of chronic anemia, present for approximately 8 years. She has a history of malignancy for approximately 4 years. No anemia-directed medication regimen is documented for review today; therefore, medication adherence and treatment tolerance cannot be meaningfully assessed. She is not reporting fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. She does report headache. No bleeding symptoms are provided.

She continues to smoke. Height is 160 cm, weight 75.8 kg, BMI 29.6 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 127/80 mmHg, pulse 89 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 9.3 g/dL
Hematocrit 27.3%
RBC 3.14 million/µL
MCV 87 fL
Serum iron 34 µg/dL
Ferritin 214 ng/mL
TIBC 199 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 17.1%

Assessment

1. Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation in the setting of longstanding anemia and malignancy. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are reduced, with normal MCV. Low serum iron and TIBC with preserved/elevated ferritin support impaired iron utilization associated with chronic inflammation rather than uncomplicated iron depletion. Transferrin saturation is borderline low. She is clinically stable today without cardiopulmonary symptoms or tachycardia, although headache may be associated with the anemia.

2. Malignancy, longstanding approximately 4 years, likely contributing to the chronic inflammatory state. No current malignancy treatment or disease status is provided.

3. Tobacco use. Smoking cessation remains advisable, particularly given her anemia and history of malignancy.

Plan

Review current medications at the next visit and clarify whether she is taking any oral or intravenous iron or other anemia-directed therapy. If treatment is initiated, assess adherence, gastrointestinal or other adverse effects, and symptomatic response.

Repeat CBC with reticulocyte count to monitor trend. Recommend peripheral smear, vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate to confirm the anemia pattern and assess potentially reversible contributors.

Discuss the results with the clinician managing her malignancy to coordinate evaluation of the underlying chronic disease. Iron supplementation should not be started routinely based on the current ferritin alone; consider iron replacement only after clinical review of the full context and evidence for functional or absolute iron deficiency. An erythropoiesis-stimulating agent or intravenous iron would require individualized risk-benefit assessment in the setting of malignancy and should not be initiated empirically today.

Advise prompt evaluation for worsening headache, new shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, marked weakness, or bleeding. Continue smoking cessation counseling. Follow up after repeat laboratory monitoring or sooner if symptoms develop.
