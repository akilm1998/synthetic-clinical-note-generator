Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Alan Wheeler is a 54-year-old male presenting for routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia present for approximately 18 years. His dyslipidemia has not been actively managed. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 300 mg/dL, LDL-C 191 mg/dL, HDL-C 30 mg/dL, and triglycerides 396 mg/dL.

He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. He does not smoke. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for approximately 2 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 3 years. Weight is 75.6 kg at a height of 176 cm, with BMI 24.4 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 136/84 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory results:
Total cholesterol 300 mg/dL
LDL-C 191 mg/dL
HDL-C 30 mg/dL
Triglycerides 396 mg/dL

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and triglycerides and low HDL-C, currently untreated. The LDL-C level is significantly elevated and, together with chronic kidney disease, represents increased long-term cardiovascular risk despite the absence of current chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness. Triglycerides are also substantially elevated, though below the level typically associated with the greatest pancreatitis risk.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, chronic, noted as a relevant comorbidity when considering lipid management.

Chronic kidney disease, approximately 2 years’ duration.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active treatment for the significant lipid abnormalities and cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after shared decision-making and review of renal and hepatic status. If LDL-C remains above goal despite adherence and tolerated statin therapy, consider addition of ezetimibe.

Counseled on a heart-healthy diet emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, limitation of refined carbohydrates and alcohol, increased soluble fiber, and avoidance of excess dietary sugars. Encourage regular aerobic physical activity as tolerated and continued avoidance of tobacco.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Review for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia, including thyroid disease, glycemic abnormalities, renal disease, liver disease, and dietary factors, using clinically appropriate laboratory assessment. Consider measurement of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a) for additional risk assessment. Coronary artery calcium imaging may be considered if treatment decisions remain uncertain, though the LDL-C level itself supports pharmacologic therapy.

Continue routine monitoring of chronic kidney disease and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease when selecting and monitoring lipid-lowering therapy. Follow up after laboratory reassessment to review tolerance, adherence, and lipid response.
