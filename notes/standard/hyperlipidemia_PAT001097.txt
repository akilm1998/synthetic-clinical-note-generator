Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Peter Greene is a 49-year-old man seen for routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 2 years and not currently under active management. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, fatty skin deposits, and yellow plaques around the eyelids. He is a nonsmoker.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 16 years, hypertension for 18 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 13 years. Current lipid results were reviewed, showing markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with low HDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 146/89 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 161 cm. Weight 56.8 kg. BMI 21.9 kg/m².

Laboratory Results: Total cholesterol 327 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 235 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 32 mg/dL, triglycerides 302 mg/dL.

No xanthomas or xanthelasma are reported. No cardiovascular or claudication symptoms are reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe LDL elevation and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia and low HDL cholesterol. The LDL level of 235 mg/dL represents a substantial atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of longstanding type 2 diabetes and hypertension. He is currently asymptomatic from a cardiovascular standpoint, without reported chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness. There are no reported cutaneous lipid deposits.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, 16 years. This further increases cardiovascular risk and supports aggressive lipid risk reduction.

Hypertension, longstanding, 18 years. Blood pressure is elevated today at 146/89 mmHg.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding, 13 years. This should be considered when monitoring therapy, but is not by itself a contraindication to statin treatment.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and overall cardiovascular risk. Recommend initiation of guideline-directed high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after medication reconciliation and shared decision-making. If LDL reduction remains inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe and subsequent lipid-specialist-directed therapy may be considered.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern with reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess dietary cholesterol; increased intake of vegetables, legumes, whole grains, and soluble fiber; and regular aerobic physical activity as tolerated. His BMI is currently 21.9 kg/m², so weight loss is not the primary objective.

Repeat a fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to the lipid pattern and measurement of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a), particularly given the LDL level above 190 mg/dL. Review for a possible inherited lipid disorder if the elevation persists or additional supporting history becomes available.

Continue cardiovascular risk reduction through careful management of diabetes and blood pressure. Reassess blood pressure outside the office and review his antihypertensive regimen at follow-up. Monitor hepatic status in the context of known non-alcoholic fatty liver disease when initiating lipid-lowering therapy.

Return for follow-up after repeat lipid testing, or sooner for new chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dyspnea, or other concerning cardiovascular symptoms.
