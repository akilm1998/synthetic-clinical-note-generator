Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Sophia Turner is a 65-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 19 years ago. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet. Despite the absence of significant symptoms, current laboratory results indicate suboptimal glycemic control.

Her medical history is also notable for hypertension diagnosed 7 years ago, obstructive sleep apnea for approximately 27 years, and anxiety for approximately 45 years. She currently smokes. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 142/80 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 160 cm, weight 67.3 kg, BMI 26.3 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Hemoglobin A1c 9.5%. Fasting glucose 217 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently inadequately controlled based on HbA1c of 9.5% and fasting glucose of 217 mg/dL. She denies osmotic, constitutional, visual, infectious, or neuropathic symptoms, but the degree and duration of hyperglycemia increase her risk for future microvascular and cardiovascular complications.

Hypertension with blood pressure above goal at today’s visit.

Overweight, with BMI 26.3 kg/m².

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding.

Anxiety, longstanding.

Active tobacco use, which increases her cardiovascular risk and may further complicate management of diabetes and hypertension.

Plan

Discussed that the current glucose values reflect persistent hyperglycemia despite the absence of symptoms. Recommend re-engagement with an active diabetes treatment plan, including review of prior therapies and consideration of initiation or intensification of glucose-lowering medication, such as metformin when clinically appropriate and renal function permits. Medication selection should account for comorbid hypertension, cardiovascular risk, renal function, tolerance, cost, and patient preference.

Counseled regarding a consistent carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, reduction of processed sugars and refined carbohydrates, portion control, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage home glucose monitoring if a monitoring device is available or prescribed, with review of readings at follow-up.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes-related eye and foot evaluation to screen for complications. Continue surveillance for neuropathy, skin breakdown, visual changes, and symptoms of significant hyperglycemia.

Blood pressure should be reassessed and antihypertensive therapy reviewed or adjusted as clinically indicated. Reinforced the importance of smoking cessation; offered counseling and pharmacologic cessation support as appropriate.

Obstructive sleep apnea and anxiety should remain part of ongoing chronic disease management. Return for closer diabetes follow-up after treatment changes, with repeat glycemic assessment at an appropriate interval.
