Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of longstanding anemia.

Subjective

Vanessa Warren is a 61-year-old woman, height 171 cm, weight 72.2 kg, BMI 24.7 kg/m², presenting for review of anemia management. Her anemia has been present for approximately 27 years and is currently not under active treatment. She denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or other reported cardiopulmonary symptoms. Pallor is noted.

Medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance were reviewed. No active anemia-directed medication regimen is documented for this encounter. She does not smoke. Relevant chronic conditions include malignancy for approximately 20 years, rheumatoid arthritis for 18 years, and chronic infection for 12 years.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 128/80 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Patient is hemodynamically stable. Pallor is present. Cardiovascular examination is otherwise normal without reported tachycardia. Respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin 8.0 g/dL, hematocrit 24.3%, RBC count 2.86 million/µL, MCV 85 fL, serum iron 33 µg/dL, ferritin 477 ng/mL, TIBC 252 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 13.1%.

Assessment

Longstanding normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation. The low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC support iron-restricted erythropoiesis in the setting of chronic inflammation rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Her rheumatoid arthritis, chronic infection, and malignancy are all potential contributors. Hemoglobin is substantially reduced, although she is currently without reported fatigue, dyspnea, chest symptoms, dizziness, or tachycardia.

Anemia is presently not being actively managed. There is no documented evidence of treatment benefit, adherence, or medication intolerance because no anemia-directed regimen is identified today.

Plan

Obtain a repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to further characterize the anemia and assess marrow response. Recommend serum vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as part of the evaluation.

Review the activity and treatment status of the underlying rheumatoid arthritis, chronic infection, and malignancy, as ongoing inflammation may be suppressing erythropoiesis. Coordinate management with the clinicians caring for these conditions and consider hematology evaluation given the severity and 27-year duration of the anemia.

Do not start empiric oral iron solely on the basis of the low serum iron; the elevated ferritin and low TIBC suggest an inflammatory pattern. Iron replacement, including intravenous iron, should be considered only after confirmation of the overall iron status and clinical context. An erythropoiesis-stimulating agent may be considered by a specialist if clinically appropriate after evaluation of the underlying disease and other reversible causes.

No medication effectiveness or tolerance concerns can be assessed beyond the absence of a documented active anemia regimen. Counsel regarding prompt evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, syncope, dizziness, marked weakness, or bleeding. Follow up after the recommended evaluation to review results and establish an active treatment plan.
