Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Rachel Graham is a 45-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 5-year history of anemia associated with chronic disease/inflammation. She currently denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, palpitations, shortness of breath, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for approximately 2 years, chronic infection for 8 years, and malignancy diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. Her anemia has not been under active treatment. No current medications or allergies were provided for review.

She is a current smoker. Height is 153 cm and weight is 66.0 kg, with BMI 28.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 125/80 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.3 g/dL
Hematocrit 24.1%
RBC count 2.59 million/µL
MCV 93 fL
Serum iron 48 µg/dL
Ferritin 439 ng/mL
TIBC 200 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 24%

Assessment

Anemia consistent with chronic disease/chronic inflammation, longstanding for approximately 5 years and currently untreated. The anemia is significant, with normocytic indices and low serum iron with elevated ferritin and reduced TIBC, a pattern favoring inflammation-associated iron restriction rather than uncomplicated iron deficiency. Chronic kidney disease, chronic infection, and malignancy may all be contributing. She is presently without reported cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms, and vital signs are stable.

Chronic kidney disease, approximately 2 years’ duration, requiring review as a potential contributor to reduced erythropoiesis.

Chronic infection, longstanding, and malignancy of approximately 1 year’s duration; both may contribute to ongoing inflammatory anemia. Further management should be coordinated with the clinicians overseeing these conditions.

Overweight, BMI 28.2 kg/m².

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Review the anemia promptly with repeat CBC, reticulocyte count, and peripheral blood smear. Recommend assessment of vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers to clarify contributing factors. Review the trend in hemoglobin and assess for any evidence of blood loss or progression of the underlying chronic conditions.

Given hemoglobin of 8.3 g/dL and multiple potential contributors, recommend coordinated management with the appropriate renal, oncology, and infectious disease teams as clinically indicated. Consideration of erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy or iron replacement should be based on renal function, inflammatory status, overall treatment goals, and specialist assessment; no treatment is initiated from the information available today.

Discussed warning symptoms requiring urgent assessment, including new or worsening shortness of breath, chest discomfort, syncope, palpitations, marked weakness, or bleeding.

Provide smoking-cessation counseling and offer evidence-based cessation support. Encourage balanced nutrition, regular activity as tolerated, and weight management. Continue routine age-appropriate preventive care, including vaccination review and recommended cancer screening in the context of her existing malignancy. Arrange follow-up after repeat laboratory assessment or sooner if symptoms develop.
