Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of longstanding anemia.

Subjective

Rebecca Burke is a 51-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 17-year history of anemia and a 6-year history of chronic infection, with anemia currently not under active management. She also has type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 7 years ago.

She reports headache and has noted pallor. She denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. No bleeding symptoms or other new concerns are provided.

She currently smokes. Height is 159 cm and weight is 69.5 kg, with BMI 27.5 kg/m².

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 128/78 mmHg, pulse 89 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Physical Examination: Pallor is present. Cardiovascular examination is normal. Respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory Data: Hemoglobin 9.3 g/dL, hematocrit 27.7%, RBC count 3.42 million/µL, MCV 81 fL, serum iron 38 µg/dL, ferritin 295 ng/mL, TIBC 190 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 20.0%.

Assessment

1. Chronic anemia consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are reduced with a low RBC count and near-normocytic indices. Low serum iron with elevated ferritin and reduced TIBC supports iron restriction related to chronic inflammation, particularly in the setting of a longstanding chronic infection. The 17-year duration and current lack of active management warrant reassessment of the anemia and its underlying contributors. Headache and pallor may be related to the anemia, although she has no cardiopulmonary or functional symptoms at present.

2. Chronic infection, present for approximately 6 years, likely contributing to ongoing inflammatory anemia. The underlying infection should be reviewed and managed as clinically appropriate.

3. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 7 years. Current glycemic control cannot be assessed from the information available. Continued routine diabetes monitoring and risk-factor management are indicated.

4. Tobacco use. Smoking cessation is strongly advised, given its contribution to cardiovascular risk and potential adverse effects on chronic disease and overall health.

5. Overweight, BMI 27.5 kg/m². Lifestyle measures to support weight reduction and metabolic risk reduction were reviewed.

Plan

Repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to reassess the anemia and marrow response. Obtain vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate. Review the chronic infection for ongoing activity or inadequate control. Reassess iron studies in context; iron supplementation should not be started routinely based solely on the current results because ferritin is elevated and the pattern favors inflammation-related iron restriction.

Review for additional causes of persistent anemia, including occult blood loss or nutritional deficiency, guided by history and examination. Consider hematology evaluation if the anemia worsens, remains unexplained, or fails to improve with management of the underlying inflammatory condition.

Continue routine diabetes follow-up with appropriate glycemic monitoring and screening for diabetes-related complications. Encourage smoking cessation, regular physical activity as tolerated, balanced nutrition, and gradual weight reduction.

Address age-appropriate preventive care, including immunizations and routine cancer screening, based on prior results and current preventive care schedule. Arrange outpatient follow-up after repeat laboratory evaluation to review hemoglobin trend and determine further management.
