Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Sadie Hunter is an 81-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 25-year history of anemia and longstanding rheumatoid arthritis and chronic infection, both present for approximately 34 years, as well as inflammatory bowel disease for approximately 9 years. Her anemia has not been actively managed.

She currently denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or other reported anemia-related symptoms. No pallor has been reported. She is a current smoker. No medication list or treatment regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Height 151 cm. Weight 67.3 kg. BMI 29.5 kg/m².

Blood pressure 128/82 mmHg, pulse 85 bpm, respiratory rate 23/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results reviewed: hemoglobin 8.3 g/dL, hematocrit 25.0%, RBC count 2.66 million/µL, MCV 94 fL, serum iron 21 µg/dL, ferritin 367 ng/mL, TIBC 268 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 7.8%.

Assessment

1. Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation

Hemoglobin and hematocrit are significantly reduced, with a normocytic indices pattern. Low serum iron and markedly reduced transferrin saturation in the setting of elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC support impaired iron availability associated with chronic inflammation rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Her longstanding rheumatoid arthritis, chronic infection, and inflammatory bowel disease are likely contributing factors. Chronic kidney disease and other causes of reduced red cell production should also be assessed. She is currently clinically stable by reported symptoms and vital signs but has substantial anemia requiring active evaluation.

2. Rheumatoid arthritis, longstanding

Chronic inflammatory disease likely contributes to the anemia. Current disease activity and treatment status require review.

3. Chronic infection, longstanding

May be contributing to persistent inflammatory anemia. The underlying infection and current control should be clarified.

4. Inflammatory bowel disease, approximately 9 years

May contribute to chronic inflammation and potential occult gastrointestinal blood loss, even in the presence of an elevated ferritin.

5. Tobacco use

Current smoking increases cardiopulmonary and overall health risk. Smoking cessation counseling is indicated.

6. Overweight by BMI

BMI is 29.5 kg/m². Lifestyle counseling should be individualized to mobility, inflammatory disease burden, nutrition, and overall goals of care.

Plan

Repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to confirm the current pattern and assess marrow response. Check vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers. Review prior blood counts for trajectory and assess for evidence of occult blood loss, including gastrointestinal symptoms, bowel disease activity, and any visible bleeding.

The iron profile is compatible with functional iron restriction from chronic inflammation, but iron deficiency from gastrointestinal loss should not be excluded solely on the basis of ferritin. Consider gastroenterology evaluation and age-appropriate gastrointestinal assessment based on symptoms, inflammatory bowel disease history, prior screening history, and goals of care.

Review rheumatoid arthritis and chronic infection activity and coordinate management with the clinicians involved in those conditions. Consider hematology referral if anemia persists, worsens, the reticulocyte response is inadequate, or the initial evaluation does not identify a reversible contributor. Iron replacement, including oral or intravenous therapy, should be guided by the repeat evaluation and clinical context rather than started empirically without review. An erythropoiesis-stimulating agent would require assessment of the underlying cause, renal function, and specialist input.

Discuss urgent evaluation for new chest pain, shortness of breath at rest, syncope, marked weakness, palpitations, or evidence of active bleeding.

Provide smoking cessation counseling and offer evidence-based cessation support after reviewing readiness to quit. Review immunizations, cancer screening, fall risk, bone health, nutrition, and advance care preferences as part of preventive care, with recommendations individualized to age, comorbidities, prior screening, and patient goals.

Arrange prompt outpatient follow-up after repeat testing to reassess hemoglobin, symptoms, and the need for hematology or gastroenterology involvement.
