Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective
Spencer Lawson is a 97-year-old man, 190 cm tall and 82.7 kg (BMI 22.9 kg/m²), presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease reported over approximately 62 years. He reports persistent fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. His chronic medical history is notable for hypertension for approximately 60 years and coronary artery disease for 14 years. He currently smokes. There is no documented active management regimen reviewed today.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 170/93 mmHg, pulse 84/min, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Laboratory data:
Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL
BUN 95 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m²

No peripheral edema or other examination findings are documented today.

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced kidney function and substantially elevated BUN and creatinine. The patient has longstanding hypertension and coronary artery disease, with current blood pressure inadequately controlled. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction. Despite the severity of the laboratory abnormalities, he denies current nausea, pruritus, fluid-overload symptoms, dyspnea, or urinary changes. Given his age, prolonged disease duration, severe renal impairment, and lack of active management, this requires prompt renal evaluation and close monitoring.

Hypertension, currently uncontrolled.

Coronary artery disease.

Tobacco use.

Plan
Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of severe chronic kidney disease and discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate.

Obtain updated renal and metabolic assessment, including serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone; consider renal ultrasound if not previously performed.

Review and initiate an individualized blood-pressure management plan, taking renal function and coronary artery disease into account. Perform complete medication reconciliation before prescribing; avoid empiric nephrotoxic medications and avoid NSAIDs.

Counsel regarding prompt emergency evaluation for worsening shortness of breath, new edema, chest pain, persistent vomiting, confusion, profound weakness, or markedly reduced urine output.

Strongly encourage smoking cessation. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended after nephrology assessment and repeat laboratory evaluation.
