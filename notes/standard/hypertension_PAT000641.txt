Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Caroline Rivera is a 78-year-old woman with a 40-year history of essential hypertension and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 13 years ago. Blood pressure has not been actively managed recently. She reports intermittent dizziness but denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms are reported today.

She continues to smoke. Weight is 74.4 kg at 161 cm, with BMI 28.7 kg/m². Dietary sodium intake, physical activity, and alcohol use were not otherwise detailed today.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 172/93 mmHg; pulse 76 bpm; respiratory rate 20/min; temperature 36.8°C; oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Laboratory Data: Sodium 145 mmol/L; potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

General: Seen in the outpatient setting without reported acute distress.

Cardiopulmonary: No chest pain or shortness of breath reported. Pulse is regular by vital-sign assessment.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled. Blood pressure is markedly elevated at 172/93 mmHg, with associated dizziness but no reported symptoms suggestive of acute end-organ injury. Sodium and potassium are within acceptable ranges for consideration of antihypertensive therapy. Her age, tobacco use, overweight status, coronary artery disease, and likely prolonged uncontrolled blood pressure increase overall cardiovascular risk.

2. Coronary artery disease, longstanding, approximately 13 years. No current angina or heart failure symptoms reported. Significant blood pressure elevation warrants prompt risk reduction while avoiding excessive or overly rapid lowering given her age and dizziness.

3. Tobacco use. Continued smoking is an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

4. Overweight status, BMI 28.7 kg/m².

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement after a period of seated rest and confirm technique. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, including readings at different times of day and documentation of dizziness or other symptoms.

Given persistent marked elevation and coronary artery disease, initiate or promptly arrange clinician-directed antihypertensive treatment rather than continued observation alone. A regimen appropriate for an older adult with coronary disease should be selected after review of renal function, current medication list, allergies, orthostatic symptoms, and prior treatment tolerance. Reassess blood pressure and symptoms shortly after treatment initiation or adjustment.

Obtain an electrocardiogram, urinalysis, and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk and hypertension evaluation. Continue monitoring serum electrolytes and renal function after starting or intensifying therapy.

Counseled on reducing dietary sodium, maintaining regular low-intensity physical activity as tolerated, gradual weight reduction, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered cessation support.

Seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, new or worsening shortness of breath, severe headache, focal weakness, confusion, acute visual change, or persistent markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and management.
