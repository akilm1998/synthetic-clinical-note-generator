Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Charlotte Bailey is a 58-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 24 years. Disease has not been actively managed. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No tobacco use.

Relevant comorbidities include gout for 7 years and hyperlipidemia for 1 year. Current medication regimen was not provided and requires reconciliation.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 138/81 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 178 cm, weight 67.8 kg, BMI 21.4 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 4.1 mg/dL, estimated GFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 89 mg/dL.

No edema, respiratory, neurologic, or other examination findings were provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. She has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, oliguria, or dyspnea to suggest symptomatic fluid overload or overt uremic syndrome at this visit. Blood pressure is reasonably controlled today. The duration of kidney disease and current renal indices warrant expedited renal evaluation and close monitoring.

Gout, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current creatinine, BUN, and estimated GFR results. Arrange expedited nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation, renal replacement therapy education, and discussion of future dialysis access or transplant evaluation as clinically appropriate. Review goals of care and patient preferences during nephrology follow-up.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound to assess complications and underlying renal structure. Repeat renal function testing promptly and monitor for progression.

Complete medication reconciliation at the earliest opportunity. Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use, with avoidance of NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Ensure all medications are renally dosed. Dietary counseling should emphasize individualized sodium and protein guidance and avoidance of excessive dietary phosphorus or potassium if abnormalities are identified.

Monitor blood pressure and urinary output. Seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, progressive swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.

Continue outpatient follow-up after nephrology assessment and review of repeat laboratory testing.
