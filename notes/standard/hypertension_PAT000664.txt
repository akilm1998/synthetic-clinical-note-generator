Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Aiden Hawkins is a 37-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of hypertension, identified approximately 1 year ago and currently without active treatment. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Relevant comorbidity includes type 2 diabetes mellitus for 7 years. He currently smokes. No acute hypertensive symptoms reported.

Objective

Height 167 cm; weight 66.9 kg; BMI 24.0 kg/m². Blood pressure 141/89 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air. Serum sodium 138 mmol/L and potassium 4.0 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and no symptoms concerning for hypertensive emergency. Given coexisting type 2 diabetes mellitus and ongoing tobacco use, cardiovascular and renal risk reduction are important. Electrolytes are currently within the reported reference range.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management and accurate home blood pressure monitoring, including a written log for review. Counsel regarding sodium reduction, regular physical activity, avoidance of excess alcohol, smoking cessation, and maintenance of current healthy weight. Review need for initiation of antihypertensive therapy at follow-up, with selection guided by diabetes status, renal assessment, and repeated blood pressure measurements. Recommend routine evaluation with urinalysis, lipid profile, renal function assessment, and ECG as clinically appropriate. Continue diabetes care with attention to cardiovascular risk reduction. Seek emergency care for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure. Arrange close outpatient reassessment.
