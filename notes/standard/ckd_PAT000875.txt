She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. She does not smoke.

No specific current medication regimen is documented, so effectiveness and adverse effects cannot be assessed and no medication changes are made without verification. Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 33 years and coronary artery disease for 31 years.

Vital signs: BP 143/86 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 94 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and substantial azotemia, in the setting of longstanding type 2 diabetes and coronary artery disease. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction. She currently denies symptoms suggestive of fluid overload or overt uremic symptoms, including nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, or change in urination. Blood pressure is mildly elevated.

Medication review is incomplete pending verification of the active medication list. Renal dosing, adherence, effectiveness, and tolerance cannot be safely evaluated until all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use is confirmed.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation given creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 94 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m². Discuss renal replacement therapy planning and goals of care with the patient and family as appropriate through nephrology; no dialysis decision is made in this visit.

Complete medication reconciliation, including all prescriptions, over-the-counter medications, and supplements. Review adherence, therapeutic response, and adverse effects once the regimen is verified. Ensure all medications are appropriately adjusted for renal function and avoid nephrotoxic over-the-counter agents, particularly NSAIDs, unless specifically directed by a treating clinician.

Recommend serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as part of comprehensive CKD evaluation if not recently completed. Continue close monitoring of blood pressure and diabetes with attention to kidney and cardiovascular risk reduction.

Seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, chest pain, or substantially decreased urination. Follow up promptly after medication reconciliation and nephrology assessment.
