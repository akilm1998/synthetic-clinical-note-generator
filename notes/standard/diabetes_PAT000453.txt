Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Ivy Henderson is a 63-year-old woman presenting for outpatient medication review of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 3 years ago. Current glycemic control is poor, with recent HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 259 mg/dL. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance were reviewed. No active diabetes medication regimen is provided for review today; treatment reconciliation and clarification of current use are needed before determining whether an existing regimen is effective or tolerated. She has a history of dyslipidemia for approximately 33 years. She does not smoke.

Objective

Height 157 cm; weight 61.4 kg; BMI 24.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 129/76 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.9%; fasting glucose 259 mg/dL.

No abnormal physical examination findings or diabetes-related complications are reported today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently markedly above glycemic goal based on HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 259 mg/dL. Although she denies classic hyperglycemic symptoms, laboratory values indicate significant hyperglycemia. Medication effectiveness and tolerance cannot be assessed until her current medication use is reconciled. She is at risk for future microvascular and macrovascular complications if control remains poor.

Dyslipidemia, longstanding, approximately 33 years; current treatment and lipid values were not provided for review.

Plan

Reconcile all current prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use, including dose, frequency, adherence, adverse effects, and barriers to taking therapy. Discuss initiation or intensification of evidence-based glucose-lowering therapy, with metformin considered if no contraindication or intolerance is identified. Given the degree of hyperglycemia, treatment should not rely on lifestyle measures alone.

Provide counseling on a consistent carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, regular physical activity as tolerated, and home glucose monitoring if appropriate. Review recognition and management of hypo- and hyperglycemia.

Recommend repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response. Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before and during therapy, as well as fasting lipid evaluation to guide cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend routine diabetic foot and retinal evaluations.

Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management, including reassessment of longstanding dyslipidemia and confirmation of appropriate lipid-lowering therapy after medication reconciliation. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review glucose readings, adherence, treatment tolerance, and response to therapy.
