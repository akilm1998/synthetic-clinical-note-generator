Chief Complaint

Medication review for essential hypertension.

Subjective

Denise Wheeler is a 93-year-old woman seen for routine outpatient medication review of essential hypertension, diagnosed approximately 8 years ago. She reports no headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance were reviewed. There are no reported symptoms suggesting uncontrolled blood pressure or medication intolerance.

Her medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus of 34 years’ duration. She does not smoke. Height is 154 cm and weight is 58.1 kg, with BMI 24.5 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure is 139/85 mmHg. Pulse 73 bpm. Respiratory rate 16/min. Temperature 36.6°C. Oxygen saturation 97%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 4.2 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding, currently with a satisfactory office blood pressure and no associated symptoms. Available electrolyte values are within an acceptable range for ongoing antihypertensive management. Overall control appears stable on the current treatment approach, with no reported intolerance.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, noted as a relevant cardiovascular risk comorbidity.

Plan

Continue the current antihypertensive regimen as prescribed. Reinforce adherence and avoidance of missed doses. Continue monitoring for dizziness, symptomatic hypotension, edema, palpitations, or other medication-related adverse effects.

Maintain periodic blood pressure monitoring, with attention to both elevated readings and symptoms of overtreatment given advanced age. Continue lifestyle measures supportive of blood pressure control, including regular activity as tolerated and moderation of dietary sodium.

Reassess blood pressure control, adherence, and treatment tolerance at the next routine medication review.
