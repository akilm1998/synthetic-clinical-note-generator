Chief Complaint

Annual health review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Jesse Chapman is a 58-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 22-year history of anemia that has not been actively managed. He currently denies fatigue, weakness, reduced exercise tolerance, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, or awareness of pallor.

He has type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 6 years ago. No current diabetes-related symptoms are reported. He is a current smoker. No medication or allergy information was provided for review today.

Objective

Height 180 cm; weight 77.4 kg; BMI 23.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 118/82 mmHg, pulse 87 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin 9.0 g/dL, hematocrit 26.3%, RBC count 3.02 million/µL, and MCV 87 fL. Iron studies show serum iron 44 µg/dL, ferritin 302 ng/mL, TIBC 258 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 17.1%. This represents a normocytic anemia with low serum iron and transferrin saturation in the setting of preserved-to-elevated ferritin.

Assessment

1. Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic disease. Hemoglobin is significantly reduced at 9.0 g/dL, although he is currently asymptomatic and hemodynamically stable. The low serum iron and transferrin saturation with ferritin of 302 ng/mL support impaired iron availability rather than uncomplicated iron deficiency. Given the long duration, concurrent renal disease, inflammatory conditions, chronic infection, malignancy, nutritional deficiency, and occult blood loss should be considered as part of confirmation and evaluation.

2. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 6 years’ duration. Current glycemic control cannot be assessed from the information available today.

3. Current tobacco use. Smoking cessation is particularly important given the increased cardiovascular risk associated with diabetes and chronic anemia.

4. Preventive health maintenance review.

Plan

For the anemia, obtain an updated CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with renal function, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers. Review for evidence of chronic inflammatory or renal disease and assess for occult blood loss as clinically indicated. Repeat iron indices can help clarify the trend and response to any future treatment. Given the hemoglobin level and prolonged history, consider hematology consultation if the anemia persists, worsens, or remains unexplained. Seek urgent care for new chest pain, shortness of breath at rest, syncope, marked weakness, palpitations, or bleeding.

Iron supplementation should be guided by the completed evaluation, as the current ferritin is not typical of isolated iron deficiency. Treatment directed at an underlying chronic inflammatory or renal process may be more appropriate if identified.

For diabetes, arrange routine monitoring of glycemic control and diabetes complications, including renal assessment, foot examination, and recommended retinal screening. Continue nutrition, physical activity, and weight maintenance counseling; BMI is currently 23.9 kg/m².

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and offered support with a quit plan and pharmacologic options if desired. Review age-appropriate immunizations and cancer screening during preventive care follow-up.

Follow-up after the recommended laboratory evaluation to review anemia severity, clarify etiology, and establish an active management plan.
