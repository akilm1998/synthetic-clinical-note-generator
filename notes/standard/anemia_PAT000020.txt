Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Elliot Chandler is a 58-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 11 years. He reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or other active anemia-related symptoms. No smoking history. Relevant comorbidities include rheumatoid arthritis for 14 years and chronic kidney disease for 2 years. His anemia has not been actively managed to date. No medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Height 172 cm, weight 76.9 kg, BMI 26.0 kg/m². Blood pressure 139/84 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 9.2 g/dL
Hematocrit 27.3%
RBC 3.41 million/µL
MCV 80 fL
Serum iron 46 µg/dL
Ferritin 211 ng/mL
TIBC 204 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 22.5%

Assessment

Chronic anemia, currently asymptomatic, with hemoglobin 9.2 g/dL and hematocrit 27.3%. The red cell indices are low-normal, with low serum iron and TIBC and preserved ferritin, a pattern most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic disease in the setting of longstanding rheumatoid arthritis and chronic kidney disease. There is no current tachycardia, hypoxemia, or reported cardiopulmonary limitation. The condition has not been actively managed and warrants further characterization and coordinated treatment of contributing chronic disease.

Plan

Obtain or update a complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to further characterize the anemia. Recommend vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate. Continue evaluation for contribution from chronic kidney disease and rheumatoid arthritis, including review of current disease activity and treatment with the clinicians managing those conditions.

Do not assume iron deficiency based on the low serum iron alone; ferritin is preserved and TIBC is low. Consider iron replacement only after the overall iron profile and clinical context are reviewed. If anemia persists or worsens in the setting of chronic kidney disease, discuss specialist-directed management, including whether an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent or intravenous iron is appropriate.

Monitor for development of fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, headache, pallor, or reduced exercise tolerance. Seek urgent evaluation for chest pain, significant shortness of breath, syncope, or rapidly worsening symptoms. Arrange outpatient reassessment after the recommended evaluation to review results and establish an active management plan.
