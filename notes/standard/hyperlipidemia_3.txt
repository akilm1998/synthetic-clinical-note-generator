Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Julia Stone is a 34-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 6 years. Recent lipid results remain significantly elevated. She reports intermittent leg pain with walking and episodes of dizziness. She denies chest discomfort. She has not noticed fatty skin deposits or yellow plaques around the eyelids.

Relevant comorbidities include obesity for approximately 5 years, hypertension for 1 year, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 2 years. Current weight is 86.6 kg at 150 cm. She reports ongoing tobacco use. Medication regimen and adherence were not provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 134/87 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

BMI: 38.5 kg/m².

Recent lipid panel:
Total cholesterol 302 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 201 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 52 mg/dL
Triglycerides 246 mg/dL

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides. Lipid control appears suboptimal in the setting of multiple cardiovascular risk factors, including type 2 diabetes, hypertension, obesity, and active tobacco use. LDL cholesterol above 190 mg/dL warrants prompt evaluation and treatment, including consideration of a primary lipid disorder and secondary contributors.

Intermittent exertional leg pain is concerning for possible claudication and should be evaluated further, particularly given her substantial cardiovascular risk burden. Dizziness is nonspecific; no associated chest discomfort is reported.

Obesity, BMI 38.5 kg/m².

Type 2 diabetes mellitus and hypertension are important comorbid cardiovascular risk factors.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and overall cardiovascular risk profile. Recommend initiation or optimization of guideline-directed lipid-lowering therapy, with a high-intensity statin generally indicated at this LDL level, after confirming current medications, prior tolerance, pregnancy status or pregnancy plans, and shared decision-making. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, additional therapy such as ezetimibe may be considered.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary causes of severe dyslipidemia and measurement of additional cardiovascular risk markers as clinically appropriate. Given her age and LDL level, assess for possible inherited lipid disorder; family cardiovascular history should be reviewed at follow-up.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy diet emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, increased soluble fiber, avoidance of tobacco, weight reduction, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage diabetes and blood pressure optimization through ongoing primary care management.

Because of exertional leg pain, recommend focused vascular evaluation, including assessment of peripheral pulses and consideration of ankle-brachial index testing. Seek urgent care for new or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, sudden weakness, fainting, or severe persistent leg pain.

Follow up after medication and lifestyle review, or sooner for worsening exertional symptoms or dizziness.
