Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Pamela Donovan is a 38-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. Current disease control is poor, and she is not currently receiving documented diabetes management. Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin A1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 254 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has obesity with current weight 83.6 kg, height 158 cm, and BMI 33.5 kg/m², present for approximately 3 years. She reports current tobacco use. No medication regimen, allergies, or additional interval history is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 122/77 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 158 cm; weight 83.6 kg; BMI 33.5 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.2%. Fasting glucose 254 mg/dL.

No diabetes-related physical examination abnormalities are documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently significantly above glycemic goal based on A1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 254 mg/dL. She is presently asymptomatic despite marked hyperglycemia.

Obesity, BMI 33.5 kg/m².

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated A1c and fasting glucose, along with the long-term risks of untreated hyperglycemia. Recommend initiating active diabetes treatment, with metformin as a reasonable first-line option if no contraindication, following review of renal function and tolerability. Review medication risks, expected benefits, adherence, and gastrointestinal adverse effects before prescribing.

Counseled regarding reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, increased dietary fiber, and gradual progression toward at least 150 minutes of moderate physical activity weekly as tolerated. Weight reduction was encouraged.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with fasting readings and occasional postprandial readings during treatment initiation, with instructions to seek care for persistent marked hyperglycemia or symptoms such as vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, severe weakness, or dehydration.

Recommend obtaining renal function with estimated GFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, as well as a fasting lipid profile, to assess diabetes-related risk and guide preventive care. Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot examination and routine dilated retinal examination. Review immunizations and cardiovascular risk reduction at follow-up.

Smoking cessation counseling provided; encouraged a quit attempt and discussion of cessation aids at a subsequent visit.

Return for diabetes management follow-up in approximately 4 weeks, or sooner for new hyperglycemic symptoms.
