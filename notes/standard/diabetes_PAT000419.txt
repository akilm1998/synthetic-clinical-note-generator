Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Tyler Kelly is a 65-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 20 years ago. Glycemic control is currently suboptimal, with recent HbA1c of 9.6% and fasting glucose of 242 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections.

He reports numbness and tingling in the hands and feet. No acute neurologic complaints are reported. He continues to smoke. Relevant comorbidities include hypertension for approximately 15 years and chronic kidney disease for approximately 7 years.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 154/97 mmHg, pulse 76/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 184 cm, weight 75.2 kg, BMI 22.2 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.6%. Fasting glucose 242 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented acute complication, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. The patient is not achieving adequate disease control and is not reporting typical hyperglycemic symptoms. Numbness and tingling of the hands and feet raise concern for diabetes-associated peripheral sensory neuropathy and warrant further assessment.

Hypertension with elevated office blood pressure today.

Chronic kidney disease, duration approximately 7 years; renal status should be considered when selecting and dosing diabetes therapy.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the increased risk of microvascular and cardiovascular complications. Reviewed the importance of consistent nutrition, avoidance of concentrated sugars and refined carbohydrates, portion control, and regular physical activity as tolerated.

Recommend prompt initiation or intensification of glucose-lowering therapy after reviewing his current medication regimen and renal function. Renal function, estimated glomerular filtration rate, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio should be obtained or reviewed before selecting and dosing therapy because of his chronic kidney disease. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval to assess response.

Assess feet at follow-up, including skin integrity, pulses, and protective sensation. Counsel regarding daily foot inspection, appropriate footwear, and prompt evaluation of any wound or infection. Recommend a comprehensive eye examination for diabetes-related retinal disease.

Blood pressure is above goal today at 154/97 mmHg. Recheck blood pressure and review current antihypertensive therapy and adherence. Optimize blood pressure management with consideration of his chronic kidney disease.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and offered support for a cessation plan.

Follow up after medication and renal-function review, or sooner for worsening symptoms, marked hyperglycemia, new visual changes, foot wounds, or other acute concerns.
