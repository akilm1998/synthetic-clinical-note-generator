Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Simone Bishop is an 81-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, reportedly present for approximately 29 years. She reports persistent fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, lower-extremity swelling, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath.

Chronic kidney disease has not been actively managed recently. No current medication regimen was provided for review. She also has a history of hyperlipidemia for approximately 49 years. She currently smokes. No acute cardiopulmonary or volume-related symptoms are reported today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 148/92 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 164 cm, weight 79.3 kg, BMI 29.5 kg/m².

Laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL
BUN 80 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m²

Physical examination findings are limited to the documented vital signs; no additional examination findings were provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. The patient has a longstanding history of approximately 29 years and is currently not receiving documented active management. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction, although further evaluation is needed. She currently denies nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, edema, oliguria-type urinary changes, sleep disturbance, or dyspnea. No acute volume-overload symptoms are reported.

Blood pressure is elevated at 148/92 mmHg.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current creatinine, BUN, and estimated GFR results. Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management, assessment for complications of severe chronic kidney disease, and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if clinically indicated.

Recommend prompt repeat evaluation of renal function and serum electrolytes, along with CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone. Renal ultrasound may be considered as part of structural assessment if not previously completed.

Review medications and over-the-counter products at the next visit or with nephrology; avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a clinician. Counsel on limiting dietary sodium and maintaining an appropriate renal diet pending nephrology guidance. No new medication was initiated today because the current medication list and electrolyte status were not provided.

Monitor blood pressure closely and reassess treatment needs after medication reconciliation and renal evaluation.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation.

Reviewed emergency precautions, including new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, marked weakness, new swelling, or significant reduction in urination, for which immediate emergency evaluation is recommended.
