Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Nicole Harper is a 38-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 3 years ago. She is not currently reporting increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling. Recent glycemic values remain elevated, with HbA1c 9.4% and fasting glucose 246 mg/dL.

She has a 1-year history of chronic kidney disease and a 12-year history of anxiety. She does not smoke. No current diabetes medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 123/85 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respirations 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 152 cm, weight 58.2 kg, BMI 25.2 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.4%. Fasting glucose 246 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. She is clinically asymptomatic despite significant hyperglycemia. Diabetes has been present for approximately 3 years. BMI is 25.2 kg/m².

Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 1 year. Renal function will be important in selecting and dosing diabetes therapy.

Anxiety, longstanding history of approximately 12 years.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values are above target and that active treatment is needed to reduce the risk of long-term complications. Reviewed nutrition, portion control, reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sweetened beverages, and regular physical activity as tolerated.

Recommend initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering medication after medication reconciliation and review of renal function. Obtain serum creatinine with estimated GFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to guide treatment selection and assess kidney involvement. Consider metformin if renal function is appropriate; medication choice should be individualized given the history of chronic kidney disease.

Recommend fasting lipid profile and routine diabetes monitoring. Encourage home glucose monitoring with documentation of readings for review at follow-up.

Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot examination and retinal evaluation as part of routine diabetes care. Counsel regarding daily foot awareness and prompt evaluation of wounds or infection.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and renal status. Address anxiety symptoms at subsequent visits and provide treatment based on current severity and patient preference.

Follow up after completion of recommended laboratory assessment to review glycemic readings and establish an ongoing diabetes treatment regimen.
