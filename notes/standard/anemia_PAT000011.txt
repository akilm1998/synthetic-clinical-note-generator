Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for anemia and headache.

Subjective
Rachel Graham is an 85-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding anemia, present for approximately 12 years and not currently under active management. She reports headache and appears pale. She denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. She is a nonsmoker.

Relevant chronic conditions include rheumatoid arthritis for 31 years, inflammatory bowel disease for 45 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 32 years. Current weight is 66.7 kg and height is 171 cm, with BMI 22.8 kg/m². No medication regimen is provided.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 128/81 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Pallor is noted. Cardiovascular examination is normal. Respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin 9.0 g/dL, hematocrit 27.4%, RBC 3.08 million/µL, MCV 89 fL, serum iron 24 µg/dL, ferritin 393 ng/mL, TIBC 253 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 9.5%.

Assessment
Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic disease, longstanding and currently not actively managed. The anemia is normocytic with low serum iron and transferrin saturation and elevated ferritin, supporting inflammatory iron restriction in the setting of longstanding rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory bowel disease. Her headache and pallor may be related to the anemia, although she is hemodynamically stable and denies cardiopulmonary symptoms.

Plan
Discussed laboratory findings and the importance of addressing the anemia rather than continuing observation alone. Recommend repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers to further characterize contributors. Review for chronic gastrointestinal blood loss or active inflammatory bowel disease as clinically appropriate.

Recommend coordination with her treating clinicians regarding control of rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory bowel disease and consideration of hematology and/or gastroenterology input if anemia persists or worsens. Iron replacement should be considered only after reviewing the complete clinical picture and source of iron restriction or loss.

She should seek urgent evaluation for worsening or sudden headache, new neurologic symptoms, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, syncope, marked weakness, or evidence of bleeding. Arrange close outpatient reassessment after the recommended evaluation.
