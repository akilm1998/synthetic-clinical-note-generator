Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and abnormal renal function results.

Subjective

Christian Cook is a 34-year-old male presenting for focused follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 6 years and currently without documented active management. He reports fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath.

Relevant comorbidities include secondary hyperparathyroidism for 1 year, coronary artery disease for 1 year, and gout for 1 year. He currently smokes. No medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Height 165 cm; weight 58.3 kg; BMI 21.4 kg/m².

Vital signs: BP 135/84 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 4.42 mg/dL
BUN 81 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m²

No edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, or urinary frequency change reported. Physical examination otherwise limited to the information documented above.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. The patient is clinically stable by reported symptoms and vital signs but has fatigue and requires urgent renal evaluation. No current symptoms suggestive of acute fluid overload or overt uremic symptoms are reported. Comorbid secondary hyperparathyroidism, coronary artery disease, and gout increase overall medical complexity. Tobacco use noted.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation and review of renal replacement planning, given the markedly abnormal creatinine, BUN, and eGFR.

Recommend prompt repeat renal function and electrolyte assessment, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and evaluation of mineral-bone parameters including intact parathyroid hormone, as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously performed.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications at the next contact and avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potential nephrotoxins. Do not start or stop renal medications without clinician guidance.

Counseled regarding urgent/emergency evaluation for worsening fatigue, confusion, persistent nausea or vomiting, new shortness of breath, chest pain, swelling, markedly decreased urine output, or other acute deterioration.

Encouraged smoking cessation and continuation of close primary care follow-up in coordination with nephrology.
