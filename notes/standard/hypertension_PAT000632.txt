Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Bruce McKenzie is a 66-year-old man seen for follow-up of longstanding essential hypertension, present for approximately 19 years and currently without active management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. No interval hypertensive symptoms reported.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 13 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 19 years, and chronic kidney disease for 25 years. He continues to smoke. Weight is 89.5 kg at a height of 190 cm, with BMI 24.8 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 148/96 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Laboratory data: Serum sodium 144 mmol/L; potassium 4.9 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension with persistent elevation at today’s visit. Although asymptomatic, the current reading is above goal, particularly in the setting of coronary artery disease, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease. Long duration of hypertension and lack of active management increase his cardiovascular and renal risk.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Potassium is at the upper end of the reported range; renal function values were not provided.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management and the increased cardiovascular and renal risk associated with hypertension in the setting of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease. Recommend confirmation of blood pressure control with repeated office measurements and home blood pressure monitoring, with documentation of readings for review.

Recommend initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy after review of current medication history and renal function, with selection tailored to his chronic kidney disease, diabetes, and coronary artery disease. Monitor serum electrolytes and renal function after any treatment change, particularly given potassium of 4.9 mmol/L.

Recommend evaluation with renal function testing, urinalysis, and urine albumin assessment as clinically appropriate. An ECG and lipid profile are also appropriate as part of cardiovascular risk assessment.

Counseled regarding dietary sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, and smoking cessation. Continue management of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease in coordination with his treating clinicians.

Advise urgent evaluation for chest pain, new shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Schedule close outpatient follow-up to review home readings, medication reconciliation, and recommended laboratory results.
