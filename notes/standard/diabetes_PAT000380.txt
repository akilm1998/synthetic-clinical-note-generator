Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for long-standing type 2 diabetes mellitus.

History of Present Illness

Joseph Bailey is a 38-year-old man with a 15-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus presenting for initial outpatient evaluation. Diabetes has not been actively managed. Recent laboratory testing demonstrates marked hyperglycemia with HbA1c 9.5% and fasting glucose 247 mg/dL.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. He reports numbness and tingling involving the hands and/or feet. No additional distribution or precipitating factors are provided.

His comorbidities include depression of 14 years’ duration and obstructive sleep apnea of 8 years’ duration. He does not smoke.

Past Medical History

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 15 years ago.

Depression, 14 years.

Obstructive sleep apnea, 8 years.

Medications

No current diabetes or other medications are provided.

Allergies

No allergies are provided.

Social History

Never smoker. Height 184 cm, weight 81.3 kg, BMI 24.0 kg/m².

Review of Systems

Endocrine: Denies polydipsia, polyuria, polyphagia, or unintentional weight loss.

Constitutional: Denies fatigue.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Neurologic: Reports numbness and tingling of the hands and/or feet.

Skin/Infectious: Denies slow-healing wounds or frequent infections.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 115/78 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Anthropometrics: Height 184 cm, weight 81.3 kg, BMI 24.0 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: HbA1c 9.5%. Fasting glucose 247 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with significant persistent hyperglycemia. HbA1c of 9.5% and fasting glucose of 247 mg/dL indicate inadequate glycemic control despite the absence of classic hyperglycemic symptoms. He is not currently engaged in active diabetes management. Given the 15-year duration of disease, comprehensive evaluation for renal, retinal, vascular, and neurologic involvement is warranted.

2. Distal numbness and tingling. These symptoms may represent diabetic peripheral sensory neuropathy, although the available information is insufficient to establish etiology or severity. No foot examination findings are provided.

3. Depression, longstanding, with 14-year duration. Mood disorder may affect diabetes self-management and should be incorporated into the treatment plan.

4. Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding, with 8-year duration. This is relevant to cardiometabolic risk and overall diabetes management.

Plan

Type 2 diabetes: Discussed the significance of the current HbA1c and fasting glucose values and the need for active treatment. Recommend prompt initiation or re-establishment of a structured diabetes regimen, with metformin generally appropriate as first-line therapy if renal function and other contraindications permit. If glycemic response is inadequate or if baseline hyperglycemia persists, combination therapy should be considered based on renal function, cardiovascular and renal risk, weight considerations, cost, and patient preferences. Insulin therapy may be required if clinically indicated by subsequent assessment.

Recommend individualized nutrition counseling emphasizing consistent carbohydrate intake, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages and refined carbohydrates, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Although BMI is normal at 24.0 kg/m², lifestyle measures remain important for glycemic control. Begin or reinforce home glucose monitoring with documentation of fasting and selected postprandial values.

Obtain renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes complication screening. Recommend a comprehensive foot examination, including skin integrity, pulses, and sensory testing, as well as a dilated retinal examination given the 15-year disease duration. These evaluations are recommendations and are not represented as completed today.

Neurologic symptoms: Perform a focused neurologic and foot examination at follow-up and assess symptom pattern, progression, and functional impact. Optimize glycemic control. Further evaluation should be guided by examination findings and clinical course.

Depression and obstructive sleep apnea: Address these comorbidities as part of the diabetes care plan and assess their current impact on adherence, sleep quality, mood, and daily functioning.

Follow-up

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after completion of the recommended baseline evaluation to review glucose data, assess paresthesias, and finalize the diabetes treatment regimen. Earlier evaluation is appropriate for symptomatic hyperglycemia, progressive weakness or sensory loss, foot injury, or other acute concerns.
