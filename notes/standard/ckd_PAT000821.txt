Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Jonathan Gray is a 34-year-old man, height 163 cm, weight 72.3 kg, BMI 27.2 kg/m², with a 12-year history of chronic kidney disease. He also has type 2 diabetes mellitus and gout, each present for approximately 1 year. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath.

Medication review performed. No active medication regimen is documented in today’s record, so adherence and treatment tolerance cannot be confirmed. He is a current smoker.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, present for approximately 12 years. Current creatinine is 3.53 mg/dL, BUN 68 mg/dL, and eGFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m². He is presently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, but the degree of renal impairment warrants timely specialist evaluation and active management. Medication regimen, adherence, and tolerance require clarification.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus of approximately 1 year’s duration, a potential contributor to ongoing renal disease.

Gout of approximately 1 year’s duration; renal function is important in selection and dosing of gout therapy.

Tobacco use.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Reconcile all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplemental medications at the next contact and confirm actual use, adherence, adverse effects, and tolerance. Avoid assuming use of losartan, diuretics, phosphate binders, erythropoietin, calcium carbonate, or other renal-directed medications until confirmed.

Recommend prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive CKD management, discussion of renal replacement planning if appropriate, and medication dosing guidance given eGFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m².

Recommend monitoring with repeat renal function and electrolytes, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically indicated. Consider renal ultrasound as part of evaluation if not previously completed.

Optimize diabetes and blood pressure management while avoiding nephrotoxic medications, particularly NSAIDs. Review all gout therapies for renal appropriateness and adjust only after medication reconciliation and clinical review.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, avoidance of excess dietary sodium, and prompt evaluation for new nausea, vomiting, pruritus, progressive fatigue, reduced urine output, edema, dyspnea, confusion, or other worsening symptoms.

Continue close outpatient follow-up after medication reconciliation and nephrology evaluation.
