Chief Complaint

Initial outpatient consultation for management of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Joshua Ward is a 58-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. Current glycemic control is suboptimal, with hemoglobin A1c 9.6% and fasting glucose 234 mg/dL. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. He also denies numbness or tingling in the hands or feet.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 11 years’ duration and longstanding obesity of approximately 35 years’ duration. He is a current smoker. No diabetes medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: blood pressure 125/81 mmHg, pulse 80 beats/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 170 cm; weight 102.9 kg; BMI 35.6 kg/m².

Laboratory data: hemoglobin A1c 9.6%. Fasting glucose 234 mg/dL.

No acute hyperglycemic symptoms are reported. No diabetic neuropathic or infectious symptoms are reported.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, currently poorly controlled based on A1c of 9.6% and fasting glucose of 234 mg/dL. Although he is asymptomatic, the degree of hyperglycemia warrants prompt treatment intensification and comprehensive complication-risk assessment.

Chronic kidney disease, reported duration 11 years. Renal function and albuminuria status are not provided; these are important in selecting and dosing glucose-lowering therapy.

Obesity, BMI 35.6 kg/m², longstanding and contributing to insulin resistance and cardiometabolic risk.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of persistent hyperglycemia and the risks of microvascular and macrovascular complications. Recommend initiating an individualized diabetes treatment regimen promptly, with medication selection guided by renal function, albuminuria, cardiovascular risk, tolerability, cost, and patient preference. Metformin may be considered if renal function permits; an agent with demonstrated renal and cardiovascular benefit may also be appropriate depending on kidney function and comorbidity profile. Medication reconciliation and review of prior diabetes treatments are recommended before prescribing.

Recommend home glucose monitoring, including fasting readings and selected postprandial readings, with education regarding glucose targets, recognition of hypoglycemia, and sick-day precautions. Repeat hemoglobin A1c after approximately 3 months of active treatment adjustment.

Obtain renal assessment with serum creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and fasting lipid profile to guide risk reduction and medication decisions. These are recommended evaluations and are not available as completed results today.

Counseled on nutrition emphasizing portion control, reduced refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, increased dietary fiber, and avoidance of excessive sodium in view of chronic kidney disease. Encourage gradual, sustainable physical activity as tolerated and weight reduction; even modest weight loss may improve glycemic control.

Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot evaluation, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensory testing, and routine dilated retinal examination. Counsel regarding daily foot care and prompt evaluation of wounds or infection.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered discussion of cessation support and pharmacologic options at follow-up.

Follow up promptly after renal and metabolic evaluation to finalize therapy, review glucose data, and assess tolerance and adherence.
