Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Brooke Snyder is a 44-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 8 years ago. Current disease control is suboptimal, and she is not currently receiving active diabetes management. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She also has a history of depression for approximately 1 year. No medication regimen was provided for diabetes or depression. She does not smoke.

Objective

Height 151 cm, weight 46.3 kg, BMI 20.3 kg/m². Blood pressure 125/82 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: hemoglobin A1c 9.3%; fasting glucose 229 mg/dL.

No abnormal physical examination findings were provided. She is lean without documented symptoms of acute hyperglycemia or metabolic decompensation.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on hemoglobin A1c of 9.3% and fasting glucose of 229 mg/dL. Despite significant biochemical hyperglycemia, she denies osmotic symptoms, constitutional symptoms, visual complaints, neuropathic symptoms, or impaired wound healing. Her normal BMI and relatively young age at follow-up warrant careful review of the original diagnostic classification and consideration of other contributors to inadequate glycemic control while continuing to manage her established diabetes diagnosis.

Depression, present for approximately 1 year, which may affect self-care and diabetes management.

Plan

Discussed that current glycemic values are substantially above goal and require active treatment. Review medication history, prior diabetes therapies, adherence, dietary pattern, glucose monitoring practices, and barriers to care at the next medication-focused visit. Initiation of antihyperglycemic therapy should be addressed promptly; metformin would be a common first-line option if renal function and other contraindications permit. Given the degree of hyperglycemia and lean body habitus, consider whether combination therapy or insulin-based treatment is appropriate after medication reconciliation and clinical review.

Recommend diabetes-focused nutrition counseling with emphasis on consistent carbohydrate intake, adequate caloric and protein intake given BMI 20.3 kg/m², and avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated. Recommend home glucose monitoring, particularly while treatment is being established, with review of fasting and postprandial readings.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and repeat hemoglobin A1c to guide treatment and complication surveillance. Recommend routine retinal evaluation and comprehensive foot examination; no retinopathy, neuropathy, or vascular findings are documented today. Counsel regarding recognition of symptomatic hyperglycemia and the need for urgent evaluation if vomiting, abdominal pain, marked weakness, confusion, or inability to maintain oral intake develops.

Depression should be reassessed, including current mood, functional impact, and safety concerns, with treatment planning as clinically indicated.

Follow up promptly after medication and baseline complication-risk review, with repeat glycemic assessment after treatment has been initiated and stabilized.
