Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Ethan Griffin is a 98-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 60 years ago. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. There is no reported symptomatic interval change.

Diabetes control is currently inadequate based on recent laboratory values. No diabetes medications or other active treatment regimen are documented. He does not smoke. Relevant comorbidities include dyslipidemia of approximately 35 years’ duration, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of approximately 48 years’ duration, and anxiety of approximately 65 years’ duration.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 131/80 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 170 cm; weight 73.4 kg; BMI 25.4 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.3%. Fasting glucose 214 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, with persistent hyperglycemia. HbA1c of 9.3% and fasting glucose of 214 mg/dL indicate substantially above-target glycemic control despite the absence of reported polyuria, polydipsia, visual symptoms, constitutional symptoms, or peripheral neuropathic complaints. Given his very advanced age and prolonged diabetes duration, treatment decisions should prioritize avoidance of hypoglycemia, preservation of function, and alignment with his overall goals of care.

Dyslipidemia, longstanding.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Anxiety, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the available glycemic results and the importance of improving diabetes control while avoiding hypoglycemia. Confirm current medication use, adherence, dietary pattern, and ability to monitor glucose before initiating or intensifying therapy.

Recommend an individualized diabetes treatment plan with conservative titration appropriate for advanced age. Metformin, an SGLT2 inhibitor, DPP-4 inhibitor, sulfonylurea, or basal insulin may be considered based on renal and hepatic function, nutritional status, hypoglycemia risk, treatment goals, and patient preference; no medication is prescribed in this note because an active regimen and relevant safety laboratory data were not provided.

Recommend home glucose monitoring if feasible, with attention to symptoms or readings suggesting hypoglycemia or marked hyperglycemia. Counsel regarding balanced carbohydrate intake, adequate hydration, and avoidance of overly restrictive dietary measures.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin excretion, fasting lipid profile, and review of diabetes-related eye and foot care as clinically appropriate. Perform or arrange focused foot and neurologic assessment at a subsequent visit.

Continue clinical attention to dyslipidemia, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and anxiety in the context of overall goals of care. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review treatment preferences, glucose data, tolerability, and repeat glycemic assessment.
