Chief Complaint:
Urgent follow-up for anemia and headache.

Subjective:
Marcus Cross is a 94-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of longstanding anemia, present for approximately 36 years. He reports headache today. He denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor is noted. No current anemia-directed treatment is documented.

Relevant comorbidities include heart failure for 11 years, rheumatoid arthritis for 43 years, and malignancy for 28 years. He currently smokes. Height 173 cm, weight 62.9 kg, BMI 21.0 kg/m².

Objective:
Vital signs: BP 133/81 mmHg, pulse 92 bpm, respirations 23/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

General: Pallor present.
Cardiovascular: Normal examination.
Respiratory: Normal examination.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL
Hematocrit 25.6%
RBC 3.05 million/µL
MCV 84 fL
Serum iron 22 µg/dL
Ferritin 412 ng/mL
TIBC 245 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 9.0%

Assessment:
Anemia consistent with chronic disease/inflammation, with a normocytic pattern and low serum iron and transferrin saturation in the setting of elevated ferritin and reduced TIBC. His longstanding rheumatoid arthritis, heart failure, and malignancy are significant potential contributors. Hemoglobin is substantially reduced, and the anemia has not been actively managed. He is currently hemodynamically stable with normal oxygen saturation but has headache and borderline tachycardia with mildly increased respiratory rate.

Plan:
Recommend prompt repeat clinical evaluation and CBC with reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the anemia and assess for progression.

Recommend review of the underlying chronic inflammatory and malignant conditions and consideration of hematology involvement for treatment planning, including assessment for iron replacement or an erythropoietin-stimulating agent when clinically appropriate. No treatment is initiated in this note.

Because of his age, hemoglobin of 8.6 g/dL, and new headache, advised urgent escalation for worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, syncope, confusion, palpitations, bleeding, or marked weakness. Continue close outpatient follow-up after the above evaluation.
