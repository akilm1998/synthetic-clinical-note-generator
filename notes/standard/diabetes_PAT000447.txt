Chief Complaint

Annual review of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Spencer Lawson is a 56-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 21 years ago. Current glycemic control is suboptimal, with HbA1c 9.1% and fasting glucose 189 mg/dL. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. He denies numbness or tingling in the hands or feet.

He has a 14-year history of chronic kidney disease. Current diabetes treatment regimen was not provided. He continues to smoke. No medication or allergy history was provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 133/82 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 164 cm, weight 69.4 kg, BMI 25.8 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.1%. Fasting glucose 189 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, longstanding for approximately 21 years, currently above individualized glycemic goal based on HbA1c of 9.1% and fasting glucose of 189 mg/dL. Although he is not reporting hyperglycemic or neuropathic symptoms, biochemical control requires active management.

Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 14 years. Renal function parameters are not available in the information reviewed today; diabetes treatment and monitoring should be coordinated with kidney function.

Tobacco use. Continued smoking increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

BMI 25.8 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the importance of improving glycemic control through a structured diabetes management plan, including nutrition focused on consistent carbohydrate intake, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, regular physical activity as tolerated, and weight maintenance. Review and initiate or intensify pharmacologic therapy at follow-up, with medication selection and dosing guided by renal function. Confirm current medication use and adherence.

Recommend repeat HbA1c at an appropriate interval after treatment changes and continued home glucose monitoring if clinically indicated. Obtain renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, as well as a fasting lipid profile, to support cardiovascular and kidney risk assessment.

Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including inspection and sensory assessment, and routine dilated retinal examination. Review dental care and skin/foot protection. Counsel regarding recognition of hypoglycemia and marked hyperglycemia if glucose-lowering medication is started.

Strongly advised smoking cessation; discuss behavioral support and pharmacologic cessation options at follow-up.

Review age-appropriate preventive care, including immunizations and cancer screening based on preventive history and shared decision-making.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review renal function, current medications, glucose readings, and progress toward improved diabetes control.
