Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Kelsey Porter is a 50-year-old woman with an 11-year history of anemia in the setting of longstanding inflammatory bowel disease, chronic kidney disease for 4 years, and malignancy for 7 years. She is a nonsmoker. She reports headache and appears pale by history. She denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, and reduced exercise tolerance.

Medication review performed with focus on anemia treatment effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance. No active anemia-directed medication regimen is documented for review; therefore, there is no reported oral or intravenous iron, erythropoiesis-stimulating agent, or treatment-related intolerance to assess.

Objective

Height 162 cm, weight 62.2 kg, BMI 23.7 kg/m².

Blood pressure 125/81 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Patient-reported pallor is present. Cardiovascular and respiratory findings are otherwise consistent with the supplied clinical profile, without reported abnormality.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.4 g/dL
Hematocrit 25.0%
RBC count 2.78 million/µL
MCV 90 fL
Serum iron 48 µg/dL
Ferritin 431 ng/mL
TIBC 197 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 24.4%

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are substantially reduced, with a normocytic red cell profile. Low serum iron with reduced TIBC and elevated ferritin supports impaired iron availability in the setting of chronic inflammation rather than straightforward absolute iron depletion. Her inflammatory bowel disease, chronic kidney disease, and malignancy are all potential contributors. Headache and pallor may be related to the anemia, although she currently denies cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms.

The condition is not presently under an established anemia-directed treatment regimen based on the information reviewed today. Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance cannot be meaningfully assessed without a current treatment to evaluate.

Plan

Obtain repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to assess marrow response and confirm persistence and morphology of the anemia. Recommend vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as part of etiologic assessment. Continue evaluation of inflammatory bowel disease activity and malignancy-related inflammation with the treating specialists.

Review iron status in clinical context before initiating supplementation. Given elevated ferritin and reduced TIBC, empiric oral iron may have limited benefit if inflammation remains active. Consider intravenous iron or an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent only after review of renal function, inflammatory disease activity, malignancy status, and treatment goals, with specialist coordination.

Monitor for development of worsening fatigue, weakness, exertional dyspnea, dizziness, palpitations, chest discomfort, or reduced exercise tolerance. Arrange outpatient reassessment after the recommended laboratory evaluation to determine response and need for anemia-directed therapy.
