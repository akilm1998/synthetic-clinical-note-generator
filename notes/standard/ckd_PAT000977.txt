Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Kevin Peterson is a 75-year-old man seen for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 11 years ago. He reports ongoing fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. Chronic comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 35 years, coronary artery disease for 15 years, and hypertension for approximately 2 years. He continues to smoke. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

Objective

Height 165 cm; weight 76.0 kg; BMI 27.9 kg/m². Blood pressure 171/101 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 4.08 mg/dL, estimated glomerular filtration rate 15 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 81 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly impaired renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. The patient has significant risk factors including longstanding diabetes, hypertension, coronary artery disease, advanced age, and ongoing tobacco use. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction; no other reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms at this visit.

Severely elevated blood pressure in the setting of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease. Current antihypertensive medications and adherence are not documented.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease.

Tobacco use.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for evaluation and management of significant chronic renal impairment and discussion of renal-protective care and future planning. Obtain updated renal function testing, serum electrolytes, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously performed.

Repeat blood pressure after an appropriate period of rest and review home readings if available. Reconcile all prescription and over-the-counter medications, with particular attention to renal dosing and agents that may worsen kidney function. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications unless specifically advised by a clinician. Establish or intensify blood pressure management promptly in coordination with the treating clinician, given the markedly elevated reading and cardiovascular history.

Continue monitoring for worsening fatigue, nausea, vomiting, itching, confusion, reduced urine output, new swelling, chest pain, or shortness of breath. Seek emergency care for severe headache, neurologic symptoms, chest pain, acute shortness of breath, confusion, or persistently extreme blood pressure readings.

Address smoking cessation and reinforce diabetic and cardiovascular risk-factor control. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended after medication reconciliation and repeat evaluation.
