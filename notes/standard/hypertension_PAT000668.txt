Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Bruce McKenzie is a 39-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension, present for approximately 5 years and currently without active treatment documented. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive complications.

His medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 3 years and hyperlipidemia for 8 years. He currently smokes. Weight is 70.9 kg at a height of 162 cm, with BMI 27.0 kg/m². Current dietary and exercise habits were reviewed, with attention to sodium intake, physical activity, weight control, and tobacco use.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 154/89 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 138 mmol/L, potassium 4.4 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms are reported today. No additional examination findings are documented for this visit.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent elevation at today’s visit after approximately 5 years of disease and no current management documented. He is asymptomatic without features of hypertensive urgency or emergency. The elevated blood pressure is clinically important in the setting of type 2 diabetes, hyperlipidemia, tobacco use, and overweight BMI.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 3 years.

Hyperlipidemia, duration 8 years.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of consistent blood pressure management and reduction of long-term cardiovascular and renal risk. Recommend obtaining home blood pressure readings, including readings at different times of day, and bringing the log to follow-up. Pharmacologic treatment should be addressed promptly given the persistent elevation and coexisting diabetes; medication selection will take into account renal status, cardiovascular risk, tolerability, and laboratory monitoring needs.

Counseled on a lower-sodium eating pattern, moderation of processed foods, regular aerobic activity as tolerated, weight reduction toward a healthy range, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Strongly encouraged smoking cessation and offered support with a quit plan.

Recommend completion or review of an ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular and end-organ risk assessment. Continued monitoring of serum electrolytes and renal function is appropriate when antihypertensive therapy is initiated or adjusted. Diabetes and lipid control should be reviewed concurrently to reduce overall cardiovascular risk.

Follow up after home blood pressure monitoring and review of recommended testing, sooner for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure readings.
