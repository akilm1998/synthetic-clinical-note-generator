Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure with intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue.

Subjective

Eliza Chandler is a 60-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of longstanding hypertension, approximately 30 years in duration, which has not been actively managed. She reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or nosebleeds. She has type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 3 years ago. She does not smoke. Weight is 62.7 kg at a height of 159 cm, with BMI 24.8 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure is 149/93 mmHg. Pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, and oxygen saturation 98%. Serum sodium is 147 mmol/L and potassium is 4.3 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension with blood pressure above goal and no current documented management. She has no reported symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency today. Intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue warrant further outpatient evaluation, particularly given the prolonged history of hypertension and coexisting type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Plan

Discussed the importance of initiating consistent blood pressure management, including home blood pressure monitoring with a written log and reassessment after a period of regular readings. Pharmacologic therapy should be initiated or arranged promptly, with selection guided by diabetes status, renal function, contraindications, and repeat blood pressure measurements. Recommend a low-sodium, heart-healthy diet, regular physical activity as tolerated, and continued avoidance of tobacco.

Recommend ECG evaluation for palpitations and assessment of cardiovascular risk with lipid profile and urinalysis, with renal function testing as clinically appropriate before and after starting antihypertensive therapy. The mildly elevated sodium level was reviewed; encourage adequate fluid intake unless otherwise restricted.

She was advised to seek emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, fainting, or persistent/worsening palpitations or dizziness. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure review and treatment initiation.
