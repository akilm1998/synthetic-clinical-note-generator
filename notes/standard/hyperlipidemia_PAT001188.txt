Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for longstanding elevated cholesterol.

History of Present Illness

Teresa Cunningham is an 87-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of hyperlipidemia. She has a reported 34-year history of elevated lipids that is not currently being actively managed. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 371 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 265 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 41 mg/dL, and triglycerides 327 mg/dL. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She does not smoke.

Her medical history is also significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 5 years and obesity for approximately 23 years. She has a height of 168 cm, weight of 105.0 kg, and BMI of 37.2 kg/m². No lipid-lowering medication use was provided.

Review of Systems

Cardiovascular: Denies chest discomfort.

Neurologic: Denies dizziness.

Musculoskeletal/Vascular: Denies intermittent claudication or leg pain with walking.

Skin/Eyes: Denies fatty skin deposits or yellow plaques around the eyelids.

Respiratory: No respiratory symptoms reported.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 129/76 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 94%.

Anthropometrics: Height 168 cm, weight 105.0 kg, BMI 37.2 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Total cholesterol 371 mg/dL; LDL cholesterol 265 mg/dL; HDL cholesterol 41 mg/dL; triglycerides 327 mg/dL.

Physical examination findings provided for this visit are limited to the recorded vital signs and anthropometrics. She is currently without reported cardiovascular, neurologic, exertional leg, or cutaneous symptoms associated with her dyslipidemia.

Assessment

Longstanding hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol and concomitantly elevated triglycerides. The LDL level is substantially above usual treatment thresholds, and the overall cardiovascular risk is increased by advanced age, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and obesity. She is a nonsmoker and currently denies symptoms suggestive of angina, claudication, or other symptomatic atherosclerotic disease. Her blood pressure is within the recorded normal range. Lipid management has not been active, and the potential benefits and risks of treatment should be reviewed carefully in the context of her age, comorbidities, functional status, preferences, and goals of care.

Plan

Discussed the importance of addressing the significantly abnormal lipid profile with a focus on reducing cardiovascular risk. Recommend a shared decision-making discussion regarding initiation of lipid-lowering therapy, generally beginning with a statin if consistent with her goals and without contraindication. Given the degree of LDL elevation, additional therapy such as ezetimibe may be considered if an adequate response is not achieved or if statin therapy is not tolerated. Medication choice and intensity should be individualized for age, comorbid diabetes, potential drug interactions, and treatment preferences.

Recommend confirming the lipid profile with a fasting panel if the current specimen was not fasting and evaluating for potentially reversible secondary contributors before or at the time of treatment initiation, as clinically appropriate. Baseline safety monitoring and subsequent lipid monitoring should be arranged after therapy is selected. Further cardiovascular risk assessment, including consideration of additional testing, should be guided by the treatment decision and her overall goals rather than by the absence of current symptoms.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, lean protein, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess dietary cholesterol. Encourage gradual activity appropriate to her mobility and medical status, along with weight-focused lifestyle measures. Continue management of type 2 diabetes and obesity as part of overall cardiovascular risk reduction.

Return for review of treatment preferences, any recommended evaluation, and response to therapy. Advise prompt evaluation for new chest discomfort, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain.
