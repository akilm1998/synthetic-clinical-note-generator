Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Austin Dean is an 80-year-old man seen for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 46 years. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No interval uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 25 years and gout for 8 years. He continues to smoke. Current medication regimen was not provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 136/85 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 94%.

Height 177 cm, weight 69.2 kg, BMI 22.1 kg/m².

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 3.65 mg/dL, BUN 93 mg/dL, estimated GFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. Despite significant biochemical impairment, he currently denies symptoms suggestive of uremia or volume overload. Disease has not been actively managed recently and warrants prompt reassessment and renal-focused care.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Gout, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active chronic kidney disease management and close follow-up. Recommend nephrology referral for evaluation and guidance regarding advanced chronic kidney disease, renal-protective care, and future treatment planning.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, repeat renal function testing, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone as part of CKD evaluation. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously completed or if clinically indicated.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications at the next visit, with avoidance of NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a treating clinician. Encourage smoking cessation, avoidance of dehydration, and adherence to a kidney-conscious diet with individualized sodium and protein guidance.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and symptoms. Seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.

Follow up promptly after nephrology evaluation and repeat laboratory testing.
