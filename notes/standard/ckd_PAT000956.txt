Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Zoe Butler is a 64-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 25 years. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral swelling, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No tobacco use. She has secondary hyperparathyroidism, present for approximately 2 years. No current medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Height 164 cm, weight 71.3 kg, BMI 26.5 kg/m².

Blood pressure 137/85 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: creatinine 5.34 mg/dL, BUN 63 mg/dL, estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m².

No peripheral edema or respiratory symptoms reported. No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. She has had kidney disease for approximately 25 years and is currently symptomatic with fatigue, but without reported nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, urine changes, edema, or dyspnea. Current renal function warrants prompt specialist assessment and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning. Secondary hyperparathyroidism has been present for 2 years.

Blood pressure is acceptable today. Diabetes, medication list, and other contributing conditions are not provided.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation of advanced chronic kidney disease, assessment for indications for dialysis, and discussion of access and renal replacement therapy planning.

Obtain updated renal and metabolic assessment, including serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, repeat BUN and creatinine, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously performed to assess renal structure and chronicity.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications at the next visit and avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Medication dosing should be adjusted for current renal function. Do not begin or change renal-related medications without review of current laboratory results and nephrology input.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and volume status. Counsel regarding prompt evaluation for worsening fatigue, nausea or vomiting, confusion, new weakness, chest pain, shortness of breath, swelling, marked reduction in urination, or other acute change in condition.

Follow up promptly after nephrology evaluation and laboratory reassessment; emergency evaluation is appropriate if concerning uremic or fluid-overload symptoms develop.
