Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Alice Freeman is a 65-year-old woman with a 12-year history of anemia in the setting of a chronic infection of approximately 17 years’ duration. She is not currently receiving active anemia-directed treatment. She denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. She reports headache and appears to have pallor. No medication-related adverse effects are reported. Adherence to anemia therapy is not applicable because no active anemia medication regimen is documented for review.

She is a current smoker. Height is 174 cm, weight 68.1 kg, and BMI 22.5 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 128/75 mmHg, pulse 89 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

General: Pallor noted.

Cardiovascular: Normal cardiovascular examination; no tachycardia.

Respiratory: Normal respiratory examination.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.7 g/dL
Hematocrit 26.7%
RBC count 3.3 million/µL
MCV 81 fL
Serum iron 27 µg/dL
Ferritin 316 ng/mL
TIBC 214 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 12.6%

Assessment

Chronic anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/disease. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are significantly reduced, with a low RBC count and borderline microcytic indices. Low serum iron and transferrin saturation in the presence of elevated ferritin and reduced TIBC support impaired iron availability related to chronic inflammation rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. The longstanding chronic infection is a plausible contributing condition. She is currently clinically stable without cardiopulmonary symptoms, although headache and pallor may be anemia-related.

No active anemia-directed medication is being used; therefore medication effectiveness and tolerance cannot currently be assessed, and there is no regimen for adherence review.

Plan

Discussed the laboratory pattern and the importance of addressing the underlying chronic inflammatory process with the clinician managing the chronic infection.

Recommend further evaluation with repeat CBC, reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers to characterize the anemia and exclude additional reversible contributors.

Consider iron replacement only after clinical assessment of the inflammatory context and iron status; the elevated ferritin should be taken into account when deciding between oral and intravenous iron strategies. If anemia persists or worsens despite management of the underlying condition, consideration may be given to specialist evaluation and anemia-directed therapy, including an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent when clinically appropriate.

No medication changes made today. Counsel regarding smoking cessation, particularly given the potential cardiopulmonary consequences of chronic anemia and tobacco use.

Monitor for worsening fatigue, dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, or progressive headache, with prompt evaluation if these occur.
