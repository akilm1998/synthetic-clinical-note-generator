Chief Complaint

Medication review and evaluation of elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Anna Cook is a 67-year-old woman presenting for outpatient review of hypertension management. Hypertension was identified approximately 1 year ago and is currently not under active pharmacologic management. She reports no headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Medication adherence and treatment tolerance cannot be assessed because she is not currently engaged in an antihypertensive medication regimen.

Relevant comorbidities include hyperlipidemia of approximately 32 years’ duration and chronic kidney disease of approximately 25 years’ duration. She currently smokes. Weight is 77.5 kg at a height of 179 cm, with BMI 24.2 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure is 159/94 mmHg. Pulse 74 bpm. Respiratory rate 15/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 96%. Serum sodium is 136 mmol/L and potassium is 4.3 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with an elevated office blood pressure of 159/94 mmHg in an asymptomatic 67-year-old woman who is not currently receiving active treatment. The available electrolytes are within physiologically acceptable ranges, including a potassium of 4.3 mmol/L. Her longstanding chronic kidney disease is an important consideration in antihypertensive selection and requires attention to renal function and electrolyte monitoring when therapy is initiated or adjusted.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding, with no lipid values provided for review.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Current renal function parameters are not available for medication selection or dosing considerations.

Active tobacco use, which increases overall cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the importance of initiating consistent blood pressure management and obtaining reliable home blood pressure measurements, with proper seated technique, to assess the treatment response and identify sustained hypertension outside the clinic.

Recommend initiation of antihypertensive pharmacotherapy through shared decision-making, with selection guided by chronic kidney disease considerations, renal function, urine albumin assessment, comorbidities, and potential adverse effects. Reassess blood pressure and treatment tolerance after therapy is started; monitor renal function and serum electrolytes after initiation or dose changes.

Recommend urinalysis with assessment for albuminuria, renal function testing, lipid profile, and baseline electrocardiogram as clinically appropriate for cardiovascular risk assessment and treatment planning.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, avoidance of excess dietary sodium, moderation of alcohol intake, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Continue follow-up for hypertension management and review of home blood pressure readings and medication tolerance.
