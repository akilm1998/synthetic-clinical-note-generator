Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Violet Murphy is a 61-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 6 years and currently without active treatment. Recent lipid results remain markedly elevated. She denies chest discomfort, dizziness, or intermittent claudication. She reports fatty skin deposits consistent with xanthomas and denies xanthelasma.

She does not smoke. Relevant comorbidities include hypertension for approximately 12 years and obesity for approximately 1 year. Current weight is 105.1 kg at a height of 165 cm, with BMI 38.6 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 150/90 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results: Total cholesterol 381 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 279 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 45 mg/dL, triglycerides 283 mg/dL.

Xanthomas reported. No chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe elevation of LDL cholesterol and associated hypertriglyceridemia, currently untreated and inadequately controlled. LDL of 279 mg/dL places her at substantial lifetime risk for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. Reported xanthomas raise concern for a possible inherited lipid disorder, although no family history is available in today’s information.

Hypertension with elevated office blood pressure of 150/90 mmHg.

Obesity, BMI 38.6 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated cholesterol and LDL levels, including increased long-term cardiovascular risk despite the absence of current chest discomfort or claudication. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin 40–80 mg daily or rosuvastatin 20–40 mg daily, following shared decision-making and review of contraindications and potential adverse effects.

Recommend a heart-healthy, Mediterranean-style eating pattern with substantial reduction in saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and processed foods. Encourage gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated and continued weight-reduction efforts.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary causes of severe dyslipidemia and measurement of ApoB and lipoprotein(a); referral to a lipid specialist may be appropriate if LDL remains markedly elevated or if an inherited lipid disorder is suspected. Additional lipid-lowering therapy such as ezetimibe may be considered if treatment response is inadequate.

Discussed the elevated blood pressure; recommend continued monitoring and review of her antihypertensive management at follow-up. Seek urgent evaluation for new chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain.

Follow up after initiation of lipid-lowering therapy to review tolerance, adherence, blood pressure, and repeat lipid results.
