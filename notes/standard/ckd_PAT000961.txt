Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and fatigue.

Subjective
Mason Russell is a 65-year-old man, 179 cm, 74.3 kg, BMI 23.2 kg/m², with a 10-year history of chronic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 25 years. He reports ongoing fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath. He does not smoke. No current medication regimen is provided.

Objective
Blood pressure 137/86 mmHg, pulse 78/min, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%. Serum creatinine 3.5 mg/dL, BUN 77 mg/dL, estimated GFR 18 mL/min/1.73 m². No peripheral edema or other abnormal physical findings are reported.

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with marked reduction in renal filtration and significant azotemia, associated with fatigue. Longstanding type 2 diabetes is a major risk factor. He currently has no reported symptoms suggesting acute fluid overload or overt uremic syndrome, including no nausea, pruritus, cramps, altered urination, dyspnea, or edema. Blood pressure is acceptable at today’s visit. The degree of renal impairment and lack of active management warrant prompt evaluation and coordinated care.

Plan
Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management and discussion of renal replacement planning if clinically indicated.

Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone to assess complications of advanced kidney disease. Consider renal ultrasound as part of structural evaluation.

Review diabetes management and renal risk-factor control at follow-up; avoid nephrotoxic medications and over-the-counter NSAIDs. Maintain appropriate hydration without excessive fluid intake.

Seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, increasing edema, markedly reduced urine output, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness. Close outpatient follow-up after nephrology assessment and updated testing.
