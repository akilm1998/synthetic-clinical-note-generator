Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Christina Pierce is an 84-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 54 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 344 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 241 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 35 mg/dL, and triglycerides 342 mg/dL. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma.

Her medical history is also notable for hypertension for approximately 34 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She does not smoke. Height is 179 cm and weight is 82.3 kg, with BMI 25.7 kg/m². No medication list, allergy history, family history, or prior cardiovascular history was provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: blood pressure 148/84 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Available laboratory data:
Total cholesterol 344 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 241 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 35 mg/dL
Triglycerides 342 mg/dL

No abnormal physical examination findings are documented today. She reports no cutaneous lipid deposits or cardiovascular prodromal symptoms.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe elevation of LDL cholesterol and concurrent hypertriglyceridemia, longstanding and presently untreated. The LDL level of 241 mg/dL and low HDL are associated with substantially increased lifetime atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of advanced age, longstanding hypertension, and type 2 diabetes. Although she currently denies chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness, the absence of symptoms does not exclude subclinical atherosclerotic disease.

Triglycerides are elevated at 342 mg/dL but are below the range generally associated with the highest risk of pancreatitis. The markedly elevated LDL warrants prompt discussion of pharmacologic therapy, while the mixed pattern should also prompt assessment for secondary contributors and dietary factors. Treatment intensity should be individualized to her overall health, functional status, preferences, and tolerance.

Plan

Discussed the lipid results and cardiovascular risk in detail. Recommend initiation of evidence-based LDL-lowering therapy, typically a statin unless contraindicated or not tolerated, after review of current medications, allergies, prior statin exposure, renal and hepatic status, and patient preferences. Given the degree of LDL elevation, a substantial reduction in LDL should be the treatment goal; additional therapy such as ezetimibe may be considered if response to statin therapy is inadequate or if statin intolerance limits treatment.

Recommend confirming the lipid values with a fasting lipid panel and evaluating for potentially reversible secondary causes with clinically appropriate testing, including thyroid, hepatic, and renal assessment, as well as review of glycemic control. Consider one-time measurement of lipoprotein(a) or apolipoprotein B if it would help refine risk assessment and treatment decisions. Coronary artery calcium imaging may be considered only if uncertainty remains regarding preventive treatment after shared decision-making; a high LDL level and her comorbidities already provide a strong rationale for treatment.

Counseled regarding reduction of saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates and excess sugars to help lower triglycerides, increasing dietary fiber, and maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated. Continue avoidance of tobacco. Reviewed symptoms that should prompt urgent evaluation, including new chest pressure, shortness of breath, syncope, or sudden exertional leg symptoms.

Arrange follow-up after medication and laboratory review to assess tolerance, adherence, and lipid response.
