Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Brooklyn Barnes is a 67-year-old woman seen for routine follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 21 years. Blood pressure control has not been actively managed recently. She reports intermittent dizziness and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or nosebleeds. She does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 6 years and hyperlipidemia for 12 years. No current antihypertensive regimen is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 158/97 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 169 cm, weight 64.3 kg, BMI 22.5 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 139 mmol/L, potassium 4.5 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms or signs of hypertensive emergency reported today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and suboptimal control. She has associated intermittent dizziness and fatigue but no symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency. Electrolytes are within the provided reference ranges.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 6 years.

Hyperlipidemia, duration 12 years.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given the degree and longstanding nature of elevation, as well as her diabetes and hyperlipidemia. Recommend obtaining home blood pressure readings with a validated upper-arm cuff, seated after several minutes of rest, and recording readings for review.

Recommend initiation or re-establishment of an antihypertensive treatment plan at follow-up, with medication selection individualized after review of home readings, renal function, medication history, and contraindications. Recommend medication adherence counseling if therapy is started.

Counseled regarding a lower-sodium diet, regular physical activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Continue weight maintenance; current BMI is 22.5 kg/m². Reinforced cardiovascular risk-factor management in the setting of diabetes and hyperlipidemia.

Recommend ECG, lipid profile, urinalysis, and assessment of renal function as part of hypertension and cardiovascular risk evaluation. Continue monitoring serum electrolytes if pharmacologic therapy is initiated or adjusted.

Reviewed urgent precautions: seek immediate care for severe or worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, significant visual changes, or markedly elevated home blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up after home blood pressure monitoring to reassess control and finalize treatment.
