Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for anemia.

Subjective

Jesse Chapman is a 54-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 3 years. He has a 16-year history of inflammatory bowel disease. He reports headache but denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor. No current anemia-directed treatment is documented. He continues to smoke.

Objective

Height 183 cm; weight 86.7 kg; BMI 25.9 kg/m². Blood pressure 125/82 mmHg, pulse 89 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

He appears clinically stable. No pallor is noted. Cardiovascular examination is normal without tachycardia. Respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory results show hemoglobin 8.5 g/dL, hematocrit 26.3%, RBC count 2.96 million/µL, and MCV 89 fL. Serum iron is low at 37 µg/dL, with ferritin 364 ng/mL, TIBC 250 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 14.8%.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of longstanding inflammatory bowel disease. The anemia is normocytic with reduced serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and relatively low TIBC, supporting impaired iron availability associated with chronic inflammation rather than uncomplicated iron depletion. Hemoglobin remains significantly reduced, although he is currently hemodynamically stable and reports minimal symptoms aside from headache.

Longstanding inflammatory bowel disease may be contributing to ongoing inflammatory activity and/or gastrointestinal blood loss. Smoking history reviewed as an additional health risk factor.

Plan

Discussed the laboratory pattern and the importance of addressing the underlying inflammatory disease. Recommend repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the anemia. Recommend reassessment of inflammatory bowel disease activity and evaluation for occult gastrointestinal blood loss as clinically appropriate.

Review treatment options after the above evaluation, including consideration of oral or intravenous iron if iron-restricted erythropoiesis is confirmed and management of the underlying inflammatory condition. Erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy would require assessment of contributing causes and renal function before consideration. No medication regimen is initiated or presumed today.

Smoking cessation was encouraged. He was advised to seek urgent care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, palpitations, dizziness or syncope, marked weakness, or evidence of gastrointestinal bleeding. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review repeat testing and disease control.
