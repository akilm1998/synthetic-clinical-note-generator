Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Peter Greene is a 67-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 9 years ago. Diabetes has not been actively managed recently. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, unintentional weight loss, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He does not smoke. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 3 years’ duration, obstructive sleep apnea of 24 years’ duration, and depression of 20 years’ duration. No acute concerns reported today.

Objective

Height 179 cm. Weight 88.1 kg. BMI 27.5 kg/m².

Blood pressure 134/84 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Recent laboratory results: HbA1c 9.5%; fasting glucose 217 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c of 9.5% and fasting glucose of 217 mg/dL. Despite the degree of hyperglycemia, he is not reporting polyuria, polydipsia, visual symptoms, constitutional symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, or infectious complications.

Chronic kidney disease, duration approximately 3 years; renal function data are important for diabetes treatment selection and dosing.

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding.

Depression, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed that current glycemic control is above goal and carries increased risk for microvascular and cardiovascular complications. Reviewed nutrition, portion control, reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sweetened beverages, regular physical activity as tolerated, and weight management.

Recommend initiation of an active diabetes treatment plan, including discussion of appropriate pharmacologic therapy in the context of chronic kidney disease. Obtain serum creatinine with estimated GFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to guide medication selection and assess renal involvement. Recommend fasting lipid profile and routine diabetes monitoring.

Encourage home glucose monitoring if treatment is initiated, with review of readings at follow-up. Recommend routine comprehensive foot examination and dilated retinal examination as part of diabetes complication surveillance.

Continue assessment of depression and obstructive sleep apnea at subsequent visits and reinforce adherence to ongoing care for these chronic conditions.

Follow up promptly to establish treatment and review renal studies and glucose monitoring; otherwise return for diabetes reassessment in approximately 4 weeks.
