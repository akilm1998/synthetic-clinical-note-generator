Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Catherine Nichols is an 86-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 16 years ago. Recent glycemic control is significantly above goal, with hemoglobin A1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 229 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Her diabetes has not been actively managed recently. She has a history of chronic kidney disease for approximately 31 years, dyslipidemia for 11 years, and depression for 8 years. She does not smoke. No current diabetes medications were provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 142/86 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 172 cm, weight 64.8 kg, BMI 21.9 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.9%. Fasting glucose 229 mg/dL.

She reports no current hyperglycemic, neuropathic, or infectious symptoms.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently poorly controlled based on hemoglobin A1c of 9.9% and fasting glucose of 229 mg/dL. Although she is asymptomatic, the degree of hyperglycemia warrants re-engagement in active management. Her advanced age, normal BMI, and longstanding chronic kidney disease should be considered when selecting and dosing therapy, with attention to hypoglycemia risk and renal function.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, without stage specified.

Dyslipidemia.

Depression.

Mildly elevated blood pressure reading today.

Plan

Discussed the elevated A1c and fasting glucose and the importance of consistent diabetes care, nutrition, physical activity as tolerated, and monitoring for symptoms of hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia. Given her age and chronic kidney disease, treatment should be individualized with a conservative approach and avoidance of medications with substantial hypoglycemia risk when possible.

Recommend obtaining renal function, including serum creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate, before initiating or adjusting diabetes medication. Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and a fasting lipid profile are also appropriate for risk assessment and chronic disease management. Review home glucose monitoring practices and glucose log at the next visit if available.

Recommend medication reconciliation and discussion of initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy at the earliest opportunity, taking renal function, nutritional intake, functional status, and treatment preferences into account. Counsel regarding adequate hydration, regular meals, foot awareness, and prompt evaluation of wounds or infection. Recommend routine diabetic eye and foot care as clinically appropriate.

Continue follow-up for chronic kidney disease, dyslipidemia, depression, and blood pressure. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended to reassess glucose readings, treatment tolerance, and glycemic control.
