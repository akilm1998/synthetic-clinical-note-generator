Chief Complaint

Annual review and chronic kidney disease follow-up.

Subjective

Patrick Bell is a 90-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 25-year history of chronic kidney disease and a 35-year history of secondary hyperparathyroidism. Current renal disease is not under active documented management. He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. He does not smoke.

Height is 165 cm and weight is 64.8 kg, with BMI 23.8 kg/m². No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 146/83 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 3.67 mg/dL, BUN 82 mg/dL, estimated GFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m².

No peripheral edema or other physical examination abnormality is reported. Patient is currently asymptomatic from a uremic or volume-overload standpoint by review of symptoms.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding for approximately 25 years, with marked reduction in estimated GFR and elevated BUN and creatinine. Although he currently denies uremic symptoms, the degree of renal impairment warrants active evaluation and ongoing specialist-guided management. There is no evidence from today’s history of acute volume overload or symptomatic uremia.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, longstanding for approximately 35 years, requiring reassessment in the context of chronic kidney disease.

Blood pressure is above goal at today’s visit. No antihypertensive medications are documented.

Preventive and geriatric health maintenance review appropriate for a 90-year-old man, including immunizations, fall risk, functional status, cognition, nutrition, vision and hearing, and goals of care.

Plan

Recommend re-establishing active chronic kidney disease management, including nephrology consultation to review renal trajectory, prognosis, medication safety, blood pressure targets, and treatment preferences. Discuss renal replacement planning only as appropriate to his goals, functional status, and specialist assessment.

Recommend monitoring with serum electrolytes, repeat creatinine/eGFR and BUN, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously evaluated or if clinically indicated. These studies will help assess complications of chronic kidney disease and guide management of secondary hyperparathyroidism.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications at the next visit. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a treating clinician. Encourage appropriate hydration without excessive fluid intake; seek prompt assessment for new edema, dyspnea, substantially reduced urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, or worsening weakness.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring and review of readings at follow-up. Sodium restriction and an individualized renal diet should be discussed, with attention to maintaining adequate caloric and protein intake given advanced age.

Continue surveillance and management of secondary hyperparathyroidism through renal and mineral-bone laboratory assessment. Treatment should be guided by laboratory results and nephrology recommendations; no current medication is assumed.

Complete age-appropriate preventive care review, including immunizations, fall prevention, vision and hearing assessment, cancer screening based on life expectancy and patient preference, and discussion of advance care planning and health care goals.

Follow up after recommended renal evaluation and laboratory assessment, or sooner for any new symptoms suggestive of fluid overload or uremia.
