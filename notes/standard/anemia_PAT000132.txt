Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for longstanding anemia and headache.

Subjective

Valeria Ellis is a 78-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of anemia reportedly present for approximately 21 years. She is not currently receiving directed management for the anemia. She reports headache and appears pale. She denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance.

Relevant chronic conditions include rheumatoid arthritis for approximately 39 years, inflammatory bowel disease for approximately 48 years, and heart failure diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. These chronic inflammatory conditions may contribute to her anemia. She does not smoke. Height is 170 cm, weight 74.3 kg, BMI 25.7 kg/m².

Medication, allergy, bleeding, dietary, and prior anemia evaluation histories were not provided for this consultation.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 130/80 mmHg, pulse 89 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

General: Pallor noted. No acute cardiopulmonary distress described.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL
Hematocrit 27.1%
Red blood cell count 3.35 million/µL
MCV 81 fL
Serum iron 22 µg/dL
Ferritin 226 ng/mL
TIBC 258 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 8.5%

Assessment

Longstanding anemia with moderate reduction in hemoglobin and hematocrit. The low serum iron and transferrin saturation, together with a ferritin that is not low and a relatively low TIBC, are most consistent with iron-restricted erythropoiesis in the setting of chronic inflammation, or anemia of chronic disease. The low-normal MCV and very low transferrin saturation also warrant evaluation for a concurrent iron deficiency process, particularly given the history of inflammatory bowel disease. Her rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory bowel disease are significant potential contributors. Chronic kidney disease has not been documented but should be assessed, particularly in the setting of heart failure and advanced age.

She is hemodynamically stable today, with normal oxygen saturation and no reported dyspnea, chest pain, palpitations, dizziness, or exercise intolerance. Headache and pallor may be related to the anemia. Given the hemoglobin of 8.8 g/dL and the prolonged duration without directed management, further evaluation and treatment planning are indicated.

Plan

Obtain repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to confirm the current pattern and assess marrow response.

Evaluate for contributing nutritional and systemic causes with vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers. Review the activity and current treatment of rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory bowel disease, as ongoing inflammation may be contributing to impaired iron utilization.

Clarify history of gastrointestinal symptoms, visible or occult blood loss, dietary iron intake, prior transfusions, and prior anemia evaluations. If clinically indicated after history and examination, pursue evaluation for gastrointestinal blood loss, particularly because of her age and inflammatory bowel disease history.

Do not begin empiric iron replacement without reviewing the broader evaluation and gastrointestinal history. If iron-restricted anemia persists, consider oral or intravenous iron based on tolerance, absorption, inflammatory bowel disease activity, and renal function. Management of the underlying inflammatory conditions should be coordinated with the appropriate treating clinicians.

Given the severity and chronicity of the anemia, consider hematology consultation if the diagnosis remains uncertain, the hemoglobin declines, reticulocyte response is inadequate, or anemia does not improve with treatment of reversible contributors.

Advise urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, sustained palpitations, marked weakness, overt bleeding, or rapidly worsening headache. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after the recommended evaluation to review results and establish a treatment plan.
