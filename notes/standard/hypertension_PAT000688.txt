Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Christina Pierce is a 50-year-old woman with a 5-year history of essential hypertension that is currently not under active management. Blood pressure today is elevated at 154/92 mmHg. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 5 years, chronic kidney disease for 3 years, and hyperlipidemia for 20 years. She reports current tobacco use. Weight is 59.6 kg at 155 cm, BMI 24.8 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure 154/92 mmHg. Pulse 79 bpm. Respirations 15/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 98%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 138 mmol/L, potassium 4.3 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and no current management documented. She is asymptomatic without features concerning for hypertensive emergency today. Coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease increase cardiovascular and renal risk. Hyperlipidemia and ongoing smoking further increase risk.

Plan

Discussed the importance of prompt blood pressure management given her coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease. Recommend confirming control with repeated home or office measurements and arranging timely medication review and initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy by her treating clinician. Review renal function and cardiovascular risk factors as clinically appropriate before and during treatment. Counsel on sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and smoking cessation. Continue monitoring for headache, visual change, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly higher readings; seek emergency care if these occur. Close outpatient follow-up recommended for blood pressure reassessment.
