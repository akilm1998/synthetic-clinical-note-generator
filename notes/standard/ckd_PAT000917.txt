Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. The patient remains clinically stable by vital signs and symptom report but has persistent fatigue and laboratory findings concerning for advanced renal impairment. Disease control is currently suboptimal and requires prompt reassessment and specialist involvement.

Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding; fatigue may be related. No current blood count is available for review.

Gout, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation to review the severity and trajectory of renal dysfunction and discuss renal replacement therapy planning and overall goals of care.

Recommend timely repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone. Consider renal ultrasound as part of evaluation if not recently completed.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications at the next contact; avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a clinician. Encourage avoidance of dehydration and adherence to an individualized renal diet pending nephrology guidance.

Monitor blood pressure and symptoms closely. Seek emergency evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, severe weakness, persistent nausea or vomiting, markedly decreased urination, or new swelling.

Continue outpatient follow-up after nephrology assessment and updated laboratory evaluation.
