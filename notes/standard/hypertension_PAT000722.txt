Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Bruce McKenzie is a 66-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension, present for approximately 21 years and currently not under active management. Blood pressure today is elevated at 158/89 mmHg. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease of 4 years’ duration, hyperlipidemia of 2 years’ duration, and type 2 diabetes mellitus of 13 years’ duration. He is a nonsmoker. Weight is 68.8 kg at 173 cm, with BMI 23.0 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 158/89 mmHg; pulse 76 bpm; respiratory rate 16/min; temperature 36.7°C; oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 138 mmol/L; potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension

Persistent elevation to 158/89 mmHg in the setting of longstanding hypertension, coronary artery disease, diabetes mellitus, and hyperlipidemia. He is asymptomatic without features suggestive of acute hypertensive emergency. The duration of disease and associated cardiovascular comorbidities place him at increased risk for end-organ complications. Sodium and potassium are currently within the reported normal range.

2. Coronary artery disease

Established coronary artery disease, reported duration 4 years. No current chest pain, shortness of breath, or palpitations.

3. Type 2 diabetes mellitus

Established diabetes mellitus, reported duration 13 years. Glycemic data were not provided for review today.

4. Hyperlipidemia

Established hyperlipidemia, reported duration 2 years. Lipid values were not provided for review today.

Plan

Hypertension: Repeat blood pressure after a period of seated rest and begin home blood pressure monitoring with documented morning and evening readings. Given the persistent elevation, prolonged history, and concomitant coronary artery disease and diabetes, initiate a discussion regarding antihypertensive pharmacotherapy and establish an active treatment plan rather than continued observation. Reinforce a reduced-sodium eating pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, maintenance of healthy weight, and avoidance of excessive alcohol intake. Obtain or review an ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular risk and hypertension assessment. Reassess blood pressure and treatment response promptly, preferably within 2–4 weeks. Seek urgent care for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Coronary artery disease: Continue cardiovascular risk-factor modification and ensure blood pressure control is addressed as a priority. Monitor for recurrence of angina or exertional dyspnea.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus: Continue coordinated management with attention to cardiovascular risk reduction and confirmation of current glycemic control.

Hyperlipidemia: Review lipid profile when available and incorporate results into overall cardiovascular risk management.

Follow-up

Return for blood pressure review within 2–4 weeks, sooner for new or worsening cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms.
