Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Martin Shaw is a 68-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. He has not been receiving consistent active management for his kidney disease. No smoking history. Relevant comorbidities include hypertension for 8 years and gout for 18 years.

Objective

Height 168 cm; weight 74.5 kg; BMI 26.4 kg/m². Blood pressure 159/99 mmHg, pulse 70/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 4.03 mg/dL, BUN 95 mg/dL, estimated GFR 15 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function. Creatinine is 4.03 mg/dL with BUN 95 mg/dL and eGFR 15 mL/min/1.73 m². Despite the degree of renal impairment, he currently denies uremic or volume-overload symptoms. The condition has not been under active management and requires prompt structured evaluation and ongoing renal care.

2. Hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal at 159/99 mmHg. Poor blood pressure control may contribute to ongoing renal injury and increases cardiovascular risk.

3. Gout, longstanding for 18 years, in the setting of significant renal impairment. Renal function should be considered carefully in selection and dosing of any urate-lowering or flare treatment.

4. Overweight by BMI, 26.4 kg/m². No tobacco use.

Plan

Chronic kidney disease: Recommend prompt nephrology consultation for evaluation of advanced renal dysfunction, clarification of etiology, and development of a longitudinal management plan. Obtain repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, urinalysis and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Renal ultrasound is recommended to assess renal structure and exclude obstructive or structural disease. Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications for renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents, particularly NSAIDs. Counsel regarding avoidance of dehydration, monitoring for reduced urine output, and prompt evaluation for worsening nausea, vomiting, confusion, progressive weakness, swelling, or shortness of breath.

Hypertension: Reassess blood pressure promptly and initiate or adjust antihypertensive therapy based on renal function, potassium level, volume status, and comorbidities. Encourage dietary sodium reduction and home blood pressure monitoring. Persistent severe elevation, especially with neurologic symptoms, chest pain, or dyspnea, would warrant urgent evaluation.

Gout: Avoid empiric NSAID use given the degree of renal impairment. Review gout activity and prior treatment at the next medication-focused visit; any urate-lowering therapy should be selected and dosed for renal function.

Follow-up: Close outpatient follow-up after completion of the recommended renal evaluation, with earlier assessment for any new urinary changes, edema, shortness of breath, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, or marked fatigue.
