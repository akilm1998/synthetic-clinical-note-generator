Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Preston Oliver is an 84-year-old man, 186 cm tall and weighing 89.6 kg (BMI 25.9 kg/m²), presenting for comprehensive annual medical review. He has a 12-year history of chronic kidney disease. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No smoking history. 

Past medical history is also notable for gout for approximately 14 years. Current medication list was not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 144/91 mmHg, pulse 76/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory data:
Serum creatinine 3.81 mg/dL.
BUN 82 mg/dL.
Estimated GFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m².

No additional examination findings were provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of longstanding duration with significant reduction in renal function and marked azotemia. Despite substantial laboratory abnormalities, he is currently without reported uremic, volume-overload, or constitutional symptoms. His current renal disease management requires structured reassessment and close longitudinal follow-up.

Blood pressure is elevated at today’s visit at 144/91 mmHg, which may contribute to ongoing renal impairment and warrants confirmation with repeat measurements.

Gout, longstanding, with a 14-year history. Reduced renal function has important implications for urate-lowering and flare-treatment choices.

Older adult preventive care review.

Plan

Recommend prompt reassessment of chronic kidney disease, including repeat serum creatinine, eGFR, BUN, electrolytes, urinalysis, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Assess for anemia, electrolyte or acid-base abnormalities, proteinuria, and mineral-bone complications using the appropriate laboratory evaluation.

Recommend renal ultrasound if not previously performed or if clinically indicated to assess renal structure and exclude obstruction.

Arrange nephrology consultation or re-establish nephrology follow-up given the degree of renal impairment, elevated BUN, and longstanding disease. Review renal-protective strategies, medication dosing, avoidance of NSAIDs and other nephrotoxins, and planning for potential progression of kidney disease.

Repeat blood pressure measurements outside the office or at follow-up. Review dietary sodium intake and address blood pressure management in the context of kidney disease once the full medication and medical history are reconciled.

Review gout history and current urate-directed treatment, if any, with attention to renal dosing. Avoid empiric NSAID use because of chronic kidney disease.

Counseled to report new nausea, vomiting, confusion, marked fatigue, itching, worsening swelling, reduced urine output, chest discomfort, or shortness of breath promptly.

Complete age-appropriate preventive care review, including immunization status, fall-risk assessment, medication reconciliation, nutrition and exercise counseling, and individualized cancer screening based on overall health and prior screening history. Follow up after repeat renal evaluation and blood pressure assessment, or sooner for any new symptoms.
