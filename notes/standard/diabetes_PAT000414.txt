Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Kyle Sanders is a 46-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 7 years ago. Diabetes control is currently inadequate, with recent HbA1c 9.4% and fasting glucose 200 mg/dL. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has longstanding obesity, present for approximately 24 years, with current weight 100.9 kg and BMI 37.5 kg/m². He also has a history of depression for approximately 14 years. Smoking status is negative. No active diabetes medication regimen is documented for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 117/79 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 164 cm, weight 100.9 kg, BMI 37.5 kg/m².

Laboratory results:
HbA1c 9.4%.
Fasting glucose 200 mg/dL.

No acute hyperglycemic symptoms reported. No diabetes-related neurologic or infectious symptoms reported.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c 9.4% and fasting glucose 200 mg/dL. He is asymptomatic today without reported polyuria, polydipsia, visual symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, or impaired wound healing. The degree of hyperglycemia warrants active treatment and closer monitoring.

2. Severe obesity, BMI 37.5 kg/m², contributing to insulin resistance and difficulty achieving glycemic control.

3. Depression, longstanding, approximately 14 years. Mood symptoms and their impact on diabetes self-management should be reassessed.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of consistent diabetes management. Reviewed nutrition focused on reducing refined carbohydrates and sweetened beverages, portion control, increased fiber intake, and gradual weight reduction. Encouraged regular physical activity as tolerated, with a goal of progressively increasing moderate exercise.

Recommend initiation or optimization of evidence-based glucose-lowering therapy in conjunction with review of renal function and other baseline metabolic parameters. Consider metformin if no contraindication, with additional therapy based on glycemic response, comorbid risk, tolerability, and renal function. Medication reconciliation should be completed before finalizing the regimen.

Recommend home glucose monitoring, particularly fasting and periodically after meals, with review of readings at follow-up. Counsel regarding symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and the need to seek care for persistent vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, or inability to maintain hydration.

Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine/eGFR, and fasting lipid profile as part of diabetes risk assessment. Reinforce routine foot self-care and periodic comprehensive foot and eye evaluation.

Address obesity with structured dietary and activity counseling; discuss weight-focused treatment options if lifestyle measures are insufficient.

Screen for current depressive symptoms, adherence barriers, sleep, and dietary or activity changes, and coordinate appropriate mental health follow-up if clinically indicated.

Return for diabetes management follow-up in approximately 4–6 weeks, or sooner for worsening symptoms or persistently elevated home glucose readings.
