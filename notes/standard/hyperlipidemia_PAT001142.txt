Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Mason Russell is a 78-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 16 years. Current management has been limited, and he is not documented as taking lipid-lowering medication. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. He continues to smoke. Comorbidity includes type 2 diabetes mellitus of approximately 9 years’ duration. No medication or allergy information was provided.

Objective

Height 179 cm, weight 83.3 kg, BMI 26.0 kg/m². Blood pressure 133/82 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 313 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 230 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 32 mg/dL, triglycerides 253 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, longstanding and currently inadequately addressed. Low HDL is also present. His age, type 2 diabetes, and ongoing tobacco use substantially increase cardiovascular risk, although he currently reports no chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness. No xanthomas or xanthelasma reported.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for 9 years, contributing to overall cardiovascular risk.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significantly abnormal lipid profile and elevated risk for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. Recommend initiation of appropriate statin therapy, generally with a high-intensity statin if tolerated, after shared decision-making and review of contraindications and potential adverse effects. Emphasized that treatment should be consistent given the LDL cholesterol of 230 mg/dL and coexisting diabetes.

Counseled on a heart-healthy diet emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, increased fiber, avoidance of excess refined carbohydrates, portion control, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encouraged smoking cessation and offered cessation support.

Recommend evaluation for potentially reversible contributors to severe hyperlipidemia and review of diabetes management and cardiovascular risk factors. Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response and adherence. Consider additional cardiovascular risk assessment if treatment decisions remain uncertain.

Advised to seek urgent care for new chest pain, shortness of breath, sudden neurologic symptoms, or significant exertional leg pain. Continue routine outpatient follow-up to review treatment tolerance, lipid response, diabetes control, and smoking cessation progress.
