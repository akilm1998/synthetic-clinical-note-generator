Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Gabriella Holland is a 74-year-old woman, 180 cm tall and weighing 74.2 kg, with a 34-year history of chronic kidney disease. She presents for initial outpatient evaluation. Current laboratory results show creatinine 3.86 mg/dL, BUN 80 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m².

She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath. She has a history of secondary hyperparathyroidism for approximately 6 years. No current medication regimen is provided. She reports active tobacco use. No additional medication, allergy, family, or surgical history is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 144/87 mmHg, pulse 81/min, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 180 cm; weight 74.2 kg; BMI 22.9 kg/m².

Available laboratory data:
Serum creatinine 3.86 mg/dL
BUN 80 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m²

No edema, respiratory distress, or other examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia, present for approximately 34 years. She currently has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, change in urination, or dyspnea to suggest prominent symptomatic fluid overload or uremic prodrome at this visit. The condition appears to have had limited active management, and the degree of renal impairment warrants prompt comprehensive evaluation and nephrology involvement. Her elevated BUN and creatinine require assessment for complications and trajectory.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, reported for 6 years, likely related to chronic kidney disease. Biochemical status is not available today.

Elevated blood pressure reading at 144/87 mmHg in the setting of chronic kidney disease. Current antihypertensive therapy is not provided.

Active tobacco use, which increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease, discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if indicated, and coordination of longitudinal follow-up.

Obtain repeat renal function testing and serum electrolytes, along with urinalysis and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess current kidney injury burden and proteinuria. Recommend CBC to evaluate for anemia contributing to fatigue, and intact parathyroid hormone with calcium and phosphorus assessment to evaluate secondary hyperparathyroidism and mineral-bone complications. Renal ultrasound may be considered to evaluate renal structure and exclude potentially reversible obstruction or other structural disease.

Review all prescription medications, over-the-counter agents, and supplements at follow-up with emphasis on renal dosing and avoidance of NSAIDs and other nephrotoxic exposures. Medication reconciliation is needed before initiating or adjusting therapy.

Monitor blood pressure regularly and review home readings at follow-up. Dietary counseling should include individualized sodium and renal dietary guidance, with attention to hydration and avoidance of unmonitored potassium- or phosphorus-containing products until laboratory results are reviewed.

Discuss smoking cessation and offer appropriate cessation support.

Educate regarding urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent nausea or vomiting, marked weakness, progressive edema, or substantially decreased urine output.

Follow up promptly after nephrology evaluation and laboratory assessment, or sooner if symptoms develop.
