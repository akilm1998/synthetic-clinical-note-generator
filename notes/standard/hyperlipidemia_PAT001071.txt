Chief Complaint

Medication review for longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Simone Bishop is an 88-year-old woman seen for review of untreated hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 12 years. She is a nonsmoker. Current lipid-lowering medications are not documented, and there is no active regimen for adherence or tolerance review. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. Comorbidity includes chronic kidney disease of 16 years’ duration.

Objective

Height 168 cm; weight 82.7 kg; BMI 29.3 kg/m². Blood pressure 137/86 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 322 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 239 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 37 mg/dL, triglycerides 232 mg/dL.

Assessment

Severe longstanding hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, currently without an established treatment regimen. The lipid pattern confers substantially increased atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of advanced age and chronic kidney disease. She has no reported ischemic or claudication symptoms and no reported xanthomas or xanthelasma.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, relevant to medication selection, dosing, and monitoring.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated cholesterol levels and the expected cardiovascular risk reduction from lipid-lowering therapy. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of a statin, taking age, overall goals of care, chronic kidney disease, potential drug interactions, and prior medication tolerance into account. Medication reconciliation should specifically assess any prior statin exposure, intolerance, adverse effects, and reasons for lack of treatment.

If therapy is accepted, initiate an appropriately conservative, kidney-conscious lipid-lowering regimen with adjustment based on tolerance and renal function; consider additional therapy if LDL reduction is inadequate or statin intolerance develops. Review diet with emphasis on reducing saturated and trans fats and increasing fiber, while encouraging activity appropriate to her functional status.

Recommend follow-up lipid assessment after treatment initiation or adjustment to evaluate effectiveness and tolerance. Continue coordination of lipid management with monitoring of chronic kidney disease.
