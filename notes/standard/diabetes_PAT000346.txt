Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Brooklyn Barnes is a 34-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. Diabetes has not been actively managed and she is not taking a documented diabetes medication regimen. Recent laboratory testing shows HbA1c 9.8% and fasting glucose 232 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet.

She has a 5-year history of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. She does not smoke. No other interval concerns are reported.

Objective

Height 163 cm. Weight 65.4 kg. BMI 24.6 kg/m².

Blood pressure 125/79 mmHg. Pulse 73 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 36.6°C. Oxygen saturation 99%.

Recent laboratory results: HbA1c 9.8%. Fasting glucose 232 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently significantly above glycemic targets based on HbA1c 9.8% and fasting glucose 232 mg/dL. She is presently asymptomatic, without reported polyuria, polydipsia, visual symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, recurrent infections, or impaired wound healing. Despite a normal-range BMI of 24.6 kg/m², glycemic control requires prompt active management.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, present for approximately 5 years. Management should emphasize metabolic risk reduction and avoidance of excess alcohol; current liver-related laboratory data are not provided.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of initiating active diabetes treatment. Recommend prompt shared decision-making regarding pharmacotherapy, with metformin a reasonable initial option if renal function and other contraindications permit. Additional therapy may be needed depending on response and repeat glycemic assessment.

Recommend a structured nutrition plan emphasizing portion control, reduced refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, and adequate fiber, along with regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage home glucose monitoring if medication is initiated or clinically indicated, with review of readings at follow-up.

Recommend assessment of renal function with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and evaluation for diabetes-related eye and foot complications. A comprehensive foot examination and dilated retinal examination should be arranged as clinically appropriate.

Continue lifestyle measures addressing non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, including avoidance of excess alcohol, balanced nutrition, and regular physical activity. Coordinate monitoring of liver health with routine diabetes care.

Follow up promptly after treatment initiation or adjustment to review glucose readings, tolerance, and adherence, with repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval to assess response. Educated regarding symptoms of marked hyperglycemia and the need for urgent evaluation if vomiting, severe weakness, confusion, inability to maintain oral intake, or other acute symptoms develop.
