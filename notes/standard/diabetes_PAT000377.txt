Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Brianna Gibson is a 58-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 13 years ago. Diabetes has not been actively managed recently. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet. No acute concerns reported today.

She has a history of dyslipidemia for approximately 3 years and obstructive sleep apnea for approximately 22 years. She currently smokes. No current diabetes medications were provided for review.

Objective

Height 161 cm, weight 57.5 kg, BMI 22.2 kg/m². Blood pressure 125/82 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Hemoglobin A1c 9.4%. Fasting glucose 238 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently inadequately controlled based on HbA1c of 9.4% and fasting glucose of 238 mg/dL. Despite the degree of hyperglycemia, she denies osmotic symptoms, constitutional symptoms, visual symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, or evidence of impaired wound healing. Diabetes duration is approximately 13 years, so screening for renal, retinal, and peripheral nerve complications remains important.

Dyslipidemia, present for approximately 3 years, requiring cardiovascular risk assessment and management in the setting of diabetes and active tobacco use.

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding, approximately 22 years.

Active cigarette smoking.

Plan

Discussed that current glycemic control is above goal and warrants active treatment. Review and reconcile all current medications at the next visit; discuss initiation or intensification of diabetes pharmacotherapy, with selection based on medication history, renal function, cardiovascular risk, tolerability, and patient preference. Reinforced nutrition counseling emphasizing reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, regular balanced meals, and continuation of routine physical activity as tolerated.

Recommend home glucose monitoring if pharmacologic therapy is initiated, with education regarding recognition of symptomatic hypoglycemia and marked hyperglycemia. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response.

Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine with estimated GFR, and fasting lipid profile as part of diabetes complication and cardiovascular risk assessment. Recommend comprehensive dilated eye examination and annual diabetic foot examination; counsel patient to inspect feet regularly and report wounds, infection, or new sensory changes promptly.

Address dyslipidemia as part of overall cardiovascular risk reduction after medication reconciliation and review of lipid results. Continue blood pressure monitoring.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and offered discussion of behavioral and pharmacologic cessation support.

Review ongoing obstructive sleep apnea management and adherence to prescribed therapy if applicable.

Return for diabetes management follow-up after medication and screening laboratory review, sooner for polyuria, polydipsia, vomiting, weakness, confusion, significant visual change, or other acute concerns.
