Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Logan Stewart is a 78-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 22 years ago. Glycemic control is currently inadequate, with recent hemoglobin A1c of 9.8% and fasting glucose of 237 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has longstanding obesity, hypertension, and anxiety. He continues to smoke. No current diabetes medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Height 173 cm. Weight 95.8 kg. BMI 32.0 kg/m².

Blood pressure 138/91 mmHg. Pulse 84/min. Respiratory rate 18/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 94%.

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.8%. Fasting glucose 237 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications

Longstanding diabetes with significantly elevated A1c and fasting glucose, consistent with poor glycemic control despite absence of symptomatic hyperglycemia. He reports no neuropathic symptoms, recurrent infections, visual complaints, or impaired wound healing. Given his age and comorbidities, treatment should be intensified in a gradual, individualized manner while minimizing hypoglycemia risk.

2. Obesity

BMI 32.0 kg/m², a persistent risk factor for insulin resistance and difficulty achieving glycemic control.

3. Hypertension

Blood pressure is mildly elevated today at 138/91 mmHg in the setting of longstanding hypertension. Improved blood pressure control remains important for reduction of cardiovascular and renal risk.

4. Tobacco use

Current smoking increases cardiovascular, pulmonary, and vascular risk in the setting of diabetes and hypertension.

5. Anxiety

Longstanding anxiety, which may affect adherence to lifestyle changes and chronic disease management.

Plan

Discussed the elevated A1c and fasting glucose and the increased risk of microvascular and cardiovascular complications with persistent hyperglycemia. Recommend initiating or intensifying evidence-based glucose-lowering therapy after medication reconciliation and review of renal function, with preference for a regimen appropriate for an older adult and low risk of hypoglycemia. Avoid assuming a current medication regimen until confirmed.

Counseled on reducing refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, regular meals, and gradual increases in physical activity as tolerated. Weight reduction would likely improve insulin sensitivity and overall cardiometabolic risk.

Recommend monitoring glucose as clinically appropriate during treatment adjustment and repeating hemoglobin A1c after an appropriate interval to assess response.

Recommend assessment of renal status with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, along with a fasting lipid profile for comprehensive diabetes risk assessment. Recommend routine diabetic foot examination and dilated retinal evaluation, even in the absence of current symptoms.

Reinforced smoking cessation and offered counseling and pharmacologic support options as appropriate. Continue attention to blood pressure control and review current antihypertensive therapy at medication reconciliation.

Follow up after treatment review and monitoring to reassess glycemic control, blood pressure, adherence, adverse effects, and diabetes complications.
