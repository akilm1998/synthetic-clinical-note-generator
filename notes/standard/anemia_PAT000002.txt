Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for anemia.

Subjective

Logan Stewart is a 95-year-old man seen for outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 8 years. He has a 25-year history of inflammatory bowel disease. He reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or noted pallor. No interval anemia-related complaints are reported.

He continues to smoke. Current height is 169 cm and weight is 83.7 kg, with BMI 29.3 kg/m². No medication regimen for anemia is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 133/77 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Recent laboratory results: Hemoglobin 9.4 g/dL, hematocrit 27.4%, RBC count 3.38 million/µL, MCV 81 fL, serum iron 34 µg/dL, ferritin 448 ng/mL, TIBC 252 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 13.5%.

He is hemodynamically stable by current vital signs and is not reporting cardiopulmonary symptoms.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with chronic inflammation in the setting of longstanding inflammatory bowel disease. Hemoglobin and hematocrit remain reduced, with a low-normal MCV, low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and relatively low TIBC, a pattern supporting anemia of chronic disease with functional iron restriction. The low transferrin saturation also warrants consideration of a concomitant iron-restricted component. He is currently asymptomatic.

Plan

Review anemia and iron studies with the patient and emphasize monitoring despite the absence of symptoms.

Recommend repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to assess stability and marrow response. Recommend serum vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate to evaluate additional contributors and the inflammatory burden.

Continue assessment and management of the underlying inflammatory bowel disease, as ongoing inflammation may be contributing to the anemia. Consider gastroenterology coordination if anemia worsens, becomes symptomatic, or remains unexplained.

Discuss iron replacement only after review of the complete clinical picture and inflammatory bowel disease activity; oral versus intravenous iron should be individualized based on tolerance, absorption, and response. Erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy is not indicated from the information currently available and would require assessment of contributing conditions, particularly renal function.

Advise prompt evaluation for new shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, marked weakness, bleeding, or reduced exercise tolerance. Encourage smoking cessation. Follow up routinely with interval symptoms and repeat laboratory assessment.
