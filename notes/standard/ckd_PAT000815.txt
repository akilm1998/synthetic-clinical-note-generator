Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Chelsea Benson is a 60-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, identified approximately 5 years ago. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. She does not smoke.

Her relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 13 years and gout for approximately 20 years. No current medication regimen is provided. There have been no reported symptoms suggestive of acute fluid overload or overt uremic symptoms.

Objective

Height 163 cm, weight 68.5 kg, BMI 25.8 kg/m². Blood pressure 139/91 mmHg, pulse 70 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results show serum creatinine 3.0 mg/dL, estimated GFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 74 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with significant reduction in renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. The patient has persistent fatigue but no reported pruritus, nausea, cramping, sleep disturbance, edema, dyspnea, or change in urination. Longstanding diabetes and gout are important risk factors and may contribute to chronic renal impairment. Current renal status requires active evaluation and management; there is no evidence from the reported symptoms of acute volume overload.

Elevated blood pressure reading of 139/91 mmHg in the setting of chronic kidney disease. No antihypertensive regimen is documented.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for 13 years. Glycemic control cannot be assessed from the available information and is important to renal risk reduction.

History of gout for 20 years. Medication use and current disease activity are not provided.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current renal indices and the need for timely nephrology evaluation for comprehensive chronic kidney disease management and planning. Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Renal ultrasound may be considered to assess renal structure if not previously performed.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications with the patient and avoid nephrotoxic agents, particularly NSAIDs, unless specifically directed by a clinician. Encourage appropriate hydration without excessive fluid intake and advise the patient to report decreased urination, new swelling, shortness of breath, persistent nausea or vomiting, worsening weakness, confusion, or severe itching.

Monitor blood pressure closely and reinforce dietary sodium reduction. Diabetes management should be reviewed with attention to renal-appropriate medication selection and avoidance of hypoglycemia as kidney function declines. Gout therapy should also be reviewed for renal dosing and avoidance of potentially nephrotoxic treatments.

Follow up promptly after repeat laboratory evaluation and nephrology assessment, or sooner for any worsening symptoms.
