Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated diabetes laboratory values.

Subjective

Victoria Richardson is a 49-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 19 years ago. Recent testing shows HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 206 mg/dL, consistent with poorly controlled hyperglycemia. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has a 7-year history of dyslipidemia. She does not smoke. Current weight is 78.4 kg at a height of 175 cm, with BMI 25.6 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 128/78 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

General: No acute symptoms reported; clinically stable by vital signs.

Laboratory data:
HbA1c: 9.3%
Fasting glucose: 206 mg/dL

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently significantly above glycemic target based on HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 206 mg/dL. Despite the degree of biochemical hyperglycemia, she reports no osmotic, constitutional, visual, infectious, or neuropathic symptoms. Long disease duration and the absence of current management increase the importance of prompt treatment intensification and complication surveillance.

2. Dyslipidemia, longstanding, reported duration 7 years. No lipid values were provided today.

Plan

1. Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the need for prompt initiation or intensification of evidence-based diabetes therapy. Review prior treatment history, contraindications, renal function, preferences, and access before selecting a medication regimen.

2. Counsel regarding consistent carbohydrate intake, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage home glucose monitoring if a monitoring plan and equipment are established.

3. Obtain serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before or during treatment planning, and obtain a fasting lipid profile to support comprehensive cardiometabolic risk management.

4. Recommend routine diabetes complication surveillance, including a comprehensive foot examination and dilated retinal examination. Assess for neuropathic symptoms at subsequent visits even though none are reported today.

5. Continue follow-up promptly after treatment is initiated or adjusted to review glucose readings, tolerance, and adherence. Seek urgent care for vomiting, marked weakness, confusion, inability to maintain oral intake, or symptomatic worsening hyperglycemia.
