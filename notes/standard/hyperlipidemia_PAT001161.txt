Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Adrian Wells is a 61-year-old man with approximately 28 years of hyperlipidemia that is currently untreated, as well as coronary artery disease diagnosed 2 years ago, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 24 years, and longstanding obesity. He continues to smoke. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma.

Objective

Height 189 cm, weight 120.0 kg, BMI 33.6 kg/m². Blood pressure 132/75 mmHg, pulse 87 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Recent lipid profile: total cholesterol 361 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 249 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 34 mg/dL, and triglycerides 390 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Hyperlipidemia, unspecified. Severe longstanding dyslipidemia with markedly elevated LDL, elevated triglycerides, and low HDL. In the setting of established coronary artery disease and diabetes, this represents a very high cardiovascular-risk profile. No lipid-lowering treatment is currently documented.

2. Coronary artery disease, diagnosed 2 years ago. No current chest discomfort or other reported ischemic symptoms.

3. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding. This substantially increases cardiovascular risk and reinforces the need for aggressive lipid management.

4. Obesity, BMI 33.6 kg/m², longstanding.

5. Tobacco use. Ongoing smoking further increases cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the need for prompt risk-factor modification. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy for secondary cardiovascular prevention, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after reviewing contraindications, potential adverse effects, and patient preferences. Given the markedly elevated LDL and known coronary artery disease, addition of ezetimibe should be considered if LDL remains substantially elevated or if statin therapy is insufficient or not tolerated.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation and reassessment of response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and additional risk characterization with ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement as clinically appropriate. Triglycerides are elevated but below the range typically associated with pancreatitis; emphasize reduction of refined carbohydrates and alcohol if applicable.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy diet low in saturated and trans fats, weight reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, and smoking cessation. Continue close management of diabetes and coronary artery disease. He should seek immediate evaluation for new or worsening chest discomfort, dyspnea, syncope, or exertional leg symptoms.

Follow-up should be arranged promptly to confirm treatment initiation and review lipid response and tolerance.
