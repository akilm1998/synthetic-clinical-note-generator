Chief Complaint

Annual review and management of elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Maya Russell is a 43-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 4-year history of hyperlipidemia that has not been actively managed. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 370 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 263 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 47 mg/dL, and triglycerides 298 mg/dL. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma.

Past medical history is significant for hypertension for 12 years, obesity for 23 years, and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She does not smoke. No current lipid-lowering or other medications are documented.

Objective

Height 164 cm. Weight 96.6 kg. BMI 35.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 129/86 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 370 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 263 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 47 mg/dL, triglycerides 298 mg/dL.

Review of systems is negative for chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma.

Assessment

Severe hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia. This has been present for approximately 4 years without active management. The LDL level of 263 mg/dL is clinically significant, particularly in the setting of known coronary artery disease, hypertension, and obesity, and places her at substantially increased risk for further atherosclerotic cardiovascular events.

Coronary artery disease, present for approximately 1 year. She currently reports no chest discomfort or exertional leg symptoms.

Hypertension, longstanding, with current blood pressure 129/86 mmHg.

Obesity, BMI 35.9 kg/m², longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the importance of treatment given the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and history of coronary artery disease. Recommend prompt initiation of high-intensity lipid-lowering therapy, typically with atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after review of contraindications, allergies, pregnancy potential, medication interactions, and patient preferences. Because the LDL elevation is substantial, assess response after treatment and consider addition of ezetimibe or other nonstatin therapy if the LDL remains above an appropriate secondary-prevention target.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to document response, with consideration of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) testing for additional risk assessment. Evaluate for potentially reversible secondary contributors as clinically appropriate. Given the degree of LDL elevation and premature coronary artery disease, obtain a detailed family history of early cardiovascular disease and consider evaluation for an inherited lipid disorder.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, fish, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess dietary sugars. Encourage gradual increase in regular aerobic activity as tolerated, with attention to symptoms related to known coronary artery disease. Weight reduction is recommended through sustainable dietary and activity changes.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and reinforce adherence to cardiovascular risk-reduction strategies. Review current coronary artery disease management and determine whether cardiology follow-up is indicated based on her established disease and treatment status. Seek urgent evaluation for new or persistent chest discomfort, shortness of breath, syncope, or exertional symptoms.

Review routine preventive care, including immunizations and age-appropriate breast, cervical, and colorectal cancer screening, as well as diabetes and liver health assessment in the context of obesity and cardiometabolic risk. Follow up after lipid treatment initiation and repeat testing to reassess tolerance, adherence, and response.
