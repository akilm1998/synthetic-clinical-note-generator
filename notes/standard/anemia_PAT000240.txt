Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Nicole Harper is a 90-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of anemia present for approximately 17 years. She reports no current fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or subjective pallor. She is a nonsmoker. Her relevant comorbidity is inflammatory bowel disease, present for approximately 31 years. No current anemia-directed medication regimen is documented.

Objective

Height 156 cm; weight 52.3 kg; BMI 21.5 kg/m². Blood pressure 135/82 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Laboratory results: hemoglobin 9.0 g/dL, hematocrit 27.9%, RBC count 3.1 million/µL, MCV 90 fL, serum iron 29 µg/dL, ferritin 295 ng/mL, TIBC 212 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 13.7%.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, clinically stable and currently without reported anemia-related symptoms. The reduced hemoglobin, hematocrit, and RBC count confirm persistent anemia. Low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and low TIBC are most consistent with iron-restricted erythropoiesis in the setting of chronic inflammation, particularly her longstanding inflammatory bowel disease, rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Given the chronicity and absence of cardiopulmonary symptoms or tachycardia, there is no evidence of acute symptomatic decompensation at this visit.

Plan

Continue close outpatient monitoring. Review inflammatory bowel disease activity, including any recent change in gastrointestinal symptoms or evidence of occult blood loss, as ongoing intestinal inflammation may be contributing to the anemia.

Recommend repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to assess hematologic response and morphology. Recommend serum vitamin B12, folate, and renal function testing, along with inflammatory markers, to evaluate for additional contributors to the chronic anemia.

Given the low transferrin saturation despite elevated ferritin, assess for functional iron deficiency and consider iron replacement, with route and intensity guided by inflammatory bowel disease activity, gastrointestinal tolerance, and repeat laboratory assessment. Oral or intravenous iron should be selected only after clinical review rather than assumed to be currently in use.

Continue management of the underlying inflammatory bowel disease with the treating clinician. Consider specialist hematology and gastroenterology coordination if hemoglobin declines, symptoms develop, inflammatory disease is uncontrolled, or the anemia fails to respond to correction of reversible contributors. Seek prompt evaluation for new shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, marked weakness, overt gastrointestinal bleeding, or other evidence of acute blood loss.
