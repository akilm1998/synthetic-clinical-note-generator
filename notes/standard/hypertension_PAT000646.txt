Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Adrian Wells is an 80-year-old man presenting for follow-up of essential hypertension diagnosed approximately 7 years ago. Blood pressure has not been actively managed. He reports intermittent dizziness and palpitations. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, or epistaxis. No acute neurologic or cardiopulmonary symptoms are reported.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 19 years’ duration and hyperlipidemia of approximately 20 years’ duration. He currently smokes. Weight is 88.8 kg at 179 cm, with BMI 27.7 kg/m².

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 168/96 mmHg; pulse 78 bpm; respiratory rate 19/min; temperature 36.8°C; oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory Data: Sodium 138 mmol/L; potassium 4.7 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and associated intermittent dizziness and palpitations. Current reading is significantly above goal and warrants active treatment, particularly in the setting of advanced age and chronic kidney disease. He has no reported symptoms to suggest hypertensive emergency at this visit.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Confirm blood pressure with repeat measurements during the visit and recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, including pulse and symptom correlation.

Discussed the need to initiate a structured antihypertensive regimen rather than continued observation. Medication selection and dosing should account for his chronic kidney disease, potassium of 4.7 mmol/L, age, orthostatic risk, and serial blood pressure response.

Recommend monitoring renal function and electrolytes after initiation or adjustment of therapy. Obtain an ECG for assessment of palpitations and hypertensive cardiac effects; consider urinalysis and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular and renal risk assessment.

Counseled on sodium restriction, avoidance of excessive alcohol, regular low-impact physical activity as tolerated, weight maintenance, and smoking cessation. Advised to seek urgent medical attention for chest pain, new shortness of breath, severe headache, focal neurologic symptoms, acute visual change, or markedly worsening blood pressure.

Close outpatient reassessment is recommended after treatment initiation and review of home blood pressure readings.
