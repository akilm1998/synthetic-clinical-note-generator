Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Gavin Holland is a 90-year-old man seen for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 44 years ago. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No acute concerns reported today.

He has longstanding chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 29 years, and dyslipidemia diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. He does not smoke. Current weight is 63.6 kg at a height of 174 cm, with BMI 21.0 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 137/89 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Laboratory results:
HbA1c 9.4%.
Fasting glucose 214 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently above individualized glycemic goal based on HbA1c of 9.4% and fasting glucose of 214 mg/dL. Despite the absence of hyperglycemic, neuropathic, infectious, or constitutional symptoms, laboratory control is suboptimal. Given advanced age and longstanding disease, treatment decisions should balance glycemic benefit with avoidance of hypoglycemia and treatment burden.

Chronic kidney disease of approximately 29 years’ duration, requiring consideration in selection and dosing of any glucose-lowering therapy.

Dyslipidemia, approximately 1 year in duration.

Plan

Discussed laboratory evidence of inadequate diabetes control and the importance of a practical, individualized treatment approach. Review and reconcile all current medications and adherence at the next medication-focused visit; consider initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy after review of renal function and hypoglycemia risk. Avoid overly aggressive glycemic lowering.

Recommend checking serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to guide treatment selection and assess diabetic kidney involvement. Continue periodic HbA1c monitoring.

Reinforced regular meals, avoidance of concentrated sugars, balanced nutrition, and activity appropriate for strength and safety. Monitor for symptoms of hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia and seek care for confusion, marked weakness, vomiting, inability to maintain oral intake, or persistently very high glucose readings.

Recommend routine diabetic foot assessment and dilated eye examination, with attention to any new vision change, skin breakdown, or foot wound.

Continue follow-up for dyslipidemia and kidney disease, with treatment decisions coordinated with overall goals of care and renal function. Follow up after medication reconciliation and renal assessment, or sooner for new symptoms.
