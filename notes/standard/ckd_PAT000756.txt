Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Benjamin Murphy is an 80-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No interval uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported.

He is a nonsmoker. Relevant comorbidity includes longstanding secondary hyperparathyroidism, reported duration 35 years. Current medication use was not provided.

Objective

Height 170 cm; weight 78.6 kg; BMI 27.2 kg/m².

Blood pressure 138/91 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 5.33 mg/dL, estimated GFR 11 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 91 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and substantially elevated BUN and creatinine. Although he remains clinically asymptomatic by history, these results indicate advanced renal impairment and require prompt renal-focused evaluation. Disease control is currently suboptimal, with no established management plan or medication regimen documented.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, longstanding.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for comprehensive evaluation and management of advanced chronic kidney disease, including discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if clinically indicated.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, CBC, intact parathyroid hormone, urinalysis, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound to assess kidney structure and exclude obstructive disease.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications when available, with avoidance of NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Dose-adjust medications for renal function and avoid contrast exposure unless essential.

Monitor blood pressure and renal-related symptoms closely. Counsel regarding prompt emergency evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent nausea or vomiting, marked weakness, significant swelling, or substantially decreased urine output.

Continue close primary care follow-up while nephrology evaluation is arranged.
