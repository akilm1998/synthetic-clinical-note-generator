Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Colin Graham is a 64-year-old man with a 30-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of approximately 6 years’ duration. He is not currently reported to be taking a diabetes medication regimen. Medication effectiveness and adherence therefore cannot be assessed against an active regimen; treatment tolerance is likewise not applicable based on the information provided.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, and recurrent infections. Reports tingling in the hands or feet without reported numbness. He continues to smoke. No other interval concerns are reported.

Objective

Height 180 cm; weight 76.8 kg; BMI 23.7 kg/m².

Blood pressure 126/79 mmHg
Pulse 75 bpm
Respiratory rate 14/min
Temperature 36.7°C
Oxygen saturation 96%

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin A1c 9.9%
Fasting glucose 241 mg/dL

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently markedly above glycemic target based on HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 241 mg/dL. Despite the absence of classic hyperglycemic symptoms, these values indicate clinically significant persistent hyperglycemia. He reports distal tingling, which warrants assessment for possible peripheral neuropathic involvement, although no formal complication is established today.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, reported duration 6 years.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed that current glycemic control is inadequate and that lifestyle measures alone are unlikely to provide sufficient improvement at the present level of hyperglycemia. Recommend prompt initiation or re-initiation of evidence-based pharmacologic therapy after review of the complete medication history, contraindications, renal function, and patient preferences.

Reviewed adherence principles, including consistent medication use if prescribed, home glucose monitoring as clinically appropriate, nutrition emphasizing reduction of concentrated carbohydrates, and regular physical activity. Treatment tolerance should be reassessed after therapy is established.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes preventive care, including a comprehensive foot examination and dilated retinal evaluation. Counsel regarding daily foot care and prompt reporting of wounds or infection. Smoking cessation was strongly advised.

Follow up soon for treatment initiation or adjustment and review of glycemic response.
