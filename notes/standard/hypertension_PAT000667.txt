Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Ariana Lawson is a 49-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension, present for approximately 19 years. She reports intermittent dizziness and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or nosebleeds. Hypertension has not been actively managed recently.

Medical history is notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 8 years, hyperlipidemia for 15 years, and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She currently smokes. Weight is 73.9 kg at a height of 163 cm, with BMI 27.8 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 154/94 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Laboratory data: Sodium 136 mmol/L, potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms reported today.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and associated intermittent dizziness and fatigue. Long duration of disease and current lack of active management increase cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, coronary artery disease, overweight BMI, and ongoing tobacco use. No reported symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency today.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 8 years.

Hyperlipidemia, duration 15 years.

Coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of reestablishing consistent blood pressure management and reducing long-term cardiovascular and renal risk. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, including measurements taken at consistent times and during episodes of dizziness. Recommend initiating or adjusting antihypertensive therapy at a dedicated medication review, with selection guided by her diabetes and coronary artery disease and after review of her complete medication history and renal function.

Reviewed lifestyle measures, including reduction of dietary sodium, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, moderation of alcohol if applicable, and smoking cessation. Encouraged close attention to diabetes and lipid control as part of overall cardiovascular risk reduction.

Recommend obtaining a comprehensive medication reconciliation and clinically appropriate monitoring studies, including renal function and urine testing, before or during antihypertensive treatment planning. An ECG and updated lipid evaluation are also reasonable given her coronary artery disease and uncontrolled blood pressure.

She was advised to seek urgent care for severe or worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, marked visual changes, or substantially higher blood pressure readings with symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment initiation or adjustment.
