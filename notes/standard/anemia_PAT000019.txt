Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for anemia.

Subjective

Michelle Cross is a 35-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 7 years. She reports headache but denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor. No interval worsening of cardiopulmonary symptoms is reported.

Her medical history is notable for inflammatory bowel disease for 13 years, chronic infection for approximately 3 years, and malignancy diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She currently smokes. Weight is 72.2 kg at a height of 157 cm, with BMI 29.3 kg/m². No current anemia-directed treatment is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 115/78 mmHg, pulse 85 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.1 g/dL
Hematocrit 25.1%
RBC count 2.92 million/µL
MCV 86 fL
Serum iron 20 µg/dL
Ferritin 167 ng/mL
TIBC 226 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 8.8%

The patient is hemodynamically stable with normal oxygen saturation. Laboratory profile demonstrates significant normocytic anemia with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, low TIBC, and preserved/elevated ferritin, consistent with anemia associated with chronic inflammation. Her chronic inflammatory bowel disease, chronic infection, and malignancy are relevant potential contributors.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, longstanding, with hemoglobin 8.1 g/dL. Iron indices are more consistent with inflammation-mediated iron restriction than isolated iron deficiency, although concurrent iron deficiency cannot be excluded based on the available data. She is currently minimally symptomatic aside from headache and has no reported dyspnea, palpitations, dizziness, or reduced exercise tolerance. Underlying inflammatory bowel disease, chronic infection, and malignancy may all be contributing. No evidence of acute hemodynamic instability based on today’s vital signs.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed laboratory findings and the likely contribution of chronic inflammatory conditions to her anemia. Recommend completion or review of a CBC with reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the anemia. Continue assessment for active inflammation, blood loss, and nutritional deficiency in the setting of inflammatory bowel disease and her other chronic conditions.

Recommend coordination with the clinicians managing her inflammatory bowel disease, chronic infection, and malignancy. Hematology consultation should be considered given the severity and prolonged duration of the anemia. Iron replacement, including oral or intravenous therapy, and erythropoiesis-stimulating treatment should be considered only after review of the complete evaluation and underlying disease activity.

Reviewed precautions to seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, rapid or irregular heartbeat, significant dizziness, bleeding, or marked worsening of weakness. Encourage smoking cessation. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to reassess symptoms and review additional evaluation.
