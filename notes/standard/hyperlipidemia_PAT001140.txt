Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated cholesterol levels.

Subjective

Edward Holland is an 87-year-old man seen for focused follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 33 years and currently without documented lipid-lowering management. Recent laboratory results show substantially elevated cholesterol and triglycerides. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. He is a nonsmoker. Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease of approximately 9 years’ duration and longstanding obesity.

Objective

Vital signs: blood pressure 127/77 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 172 cm, weight 92.3 kg, BMI 31.2 kg/m².

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 367 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 277 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 37 mg/dL, triglycerides 263 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia, longstanding and presently untreated. In the setting of established coronary artery disease, this represents a significant modifiable cardiovascular risk requiring prompt management. He is currently free of reported anginal or claudication symptoms. Obesity is an additional cardiometabolic risk factor.

Plan

Discussed the severity and cardiovascular implications of the lipid abnormalities, particularly the LDL cholesterol of 277 mg/dL in the setting of coronary artery disease. Recommend prompt initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy, typically a maximally tolerated statin for secondary cardiovascular prevention, with treatment selection and intensity individualized to age, frailty, comorbidities, potential interactions, and patient preference.

If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, recommend consideration of adjunctive nonstatin therapy such as ezetimibe and, if clinically appropriate, further specialist-directed therapy. Review for possible secondary contributors to dyslipidemia with appropriate laboratory evaluation, including thyroid, hepatic, and renal assessment, before or during treatment. A fasting lipid panel should be reassessed after therapy is initiated to document response; ApoB and lipoprotein(a) may be considered for additional risk characterization.

Counseled regarding reduction of saturated and trans fats, limitation of refined carbohydrates and alcohol, weight reduction, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Continue monitoring for new chest discomfort, dyspnea, dizziness, or exertional leg symptoms; urgent evaluation is advised if acute cardiovascular symptoms develop. Close follow-up is recommended to finalize and monitor lipid-lowering treatment.
