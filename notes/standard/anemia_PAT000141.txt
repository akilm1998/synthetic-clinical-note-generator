Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for longstanding anemia.

Subjective

Anthony Cooper is a 93-year-old man seen for outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 59 years in the setting of a chronic infection reported to have been present for 53 years. He reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, dyspnea, palpitations, or other cardiopulmonary symptoms. No interval anemia-related concerns are reported. His anemia has not been under active treatment.

He is a current smoker. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Height 167 cm; weight 60.8 kg; BMI 21.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 134/75 mmHg, pulse 84 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Laboratory results: hemoglobin 8.1 g/dL, hematocrit 23.8%, RBC count 2.94 million/µL, MCV 81 fL, serum iron 26 µg/dL, ferritin 388 ng/mL, TIBC 261 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 10.0%.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with chronic inflammation/chronic disease. Hemoglobin and hematocrit remain substantially reduced, with a low RBC count and near-normocytic indices. Low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC support impaired iron availability in the setting of chronic inflammation rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. The patient is currently clinically stable and denies anemia-related symptoms, with normal resting pulse and acceptable oxygen saturation.

Chronic infection, longstanding, and likely contributing to inflammatory iron sequestration and impaired erythropoiesis. The underlying infectious condition should remain a central focus of anemia management.

Tobacco use. Ongoing smoking may further compromise cardiopulmonary reserve in the setting of significant anemia.

Plan

Obtain interval complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to assess hematologic response and red-cell morphology. Check vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate to evaluate additional contributors to the anemia.

Continue evaluation and management of the chronic infection with the treating clinician, as control of the underlying inflammatory process is important for correction of anemia of chronic disease.

Given the transferrin saturation of 10% and ferritin of 388 ng/mL, assess for functional iron restriction before initiating iron replacement. Iron therapy, including intravenous iron, should be considered only after clinical review of the inflammatory state, renal function, and overall goals of care. An erythropoiesis-stimulating agent would require careful specialist consideration and should not be initiated empirically.

Review for occult blood loss and other reversible contributors if clinically indicated, despite the current laboratory pattern being most compatible with inflammation-associated anemia. Monitor for development of dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, worsening weakness, or reduced exercise tolerance, with urgent evaluation if these occur.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of continued tobacco exposure. Arrange routine hematology follow-up after repeat laboratory assessment.
