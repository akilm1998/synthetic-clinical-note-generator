Chief Complaint

Annual review of diabetes and chronic medical conditions.

Subjective

Kimberly Franklin is an 83-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 19-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and has not been actively managing the condition. Recent laboratory testing shows HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 234 mg/dL, consistent with persistent hyperglycemia.

She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No diabetes-related complications are reported.

She has a 51-year history of hypertension and a 5-year history of depression. She does not smoke. Current medication use, allergies, and home glucose readings are not provided.

Objective

Height 171 cm. Weight 84.2 kg. BMI 28.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 143/84 mmHg. Pulse 76 bpm. Respiratory rate 15/min. Temperature 36.8°C. Oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.9%. Fasting glucose 234 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 234 mg/dL. Although she is clinically asymptomatic, the degree and duration of hyperglycemia confer increased risk for microvascular and cardiovascular complications. No neuropathic, infectious, constitutional, or visual symptoms are reported.

Hypertension with blood pressure above typical outpatient treatment targets today. Long duration of hypertension increases overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

Overweight status with BMI 28.8 kg/m².

Depression, present for approximately 5 years; mood symptoms and current treatment require routine review.

Advanced age and prolonged diabetes duration warrant individualized treatment goals, attention to hypoglycemia risk, and assessment of functional status, cognition, nutrition, and ability to participate in self-management.

Plan

Discuss diabetes results and the importance of active management with the patient. Review dietary pattern, carbohydrate intake, physical activity, glucose monitoring ability, treatment preferences, functional status, and risk of hypoglycemia. Develop an individualized glycemic target appropriate for her age and overall health. Medication therapy should be reviewed and initiated or adjusted as clinically appropriate; no current diabetes regimen is documented.

Recommend home glucose monitoring if feasible, with education regarding symptoms of hypo- and hyperglycemia and when to seek medical attention. Reinforce hydration, balanced nutrition, portion control, and activity appropriate to her mobility and fall risk.

Obtain follow-up HbA1c after a clinically appropriate interval to assess response to treatment. Recommend renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes monitoring. Arrange a comprehensive dilated eye examination and complete diabetic foot examination, including skin assessment, pulses, and sensation. Encourage prompt evaluation of any new visual change, wound, infection, or sensory loss.

Reassess blood pressure with repeat measurements and review current antihypertensive treatment, adherence, home readings, dietary sodium intake, orthostasis, and fall risk. Coordinate diabetes and blood pressure management with attention to renal protection and avoidance of treatment-related adverse effects.

Review depression symptoms, including mood, sleep, appetite, energy, cognition, and safety, and assess current treatment and need for additional support.

Address preventive care appropriate for age and chronic disease, including immunization review, cardiovascular risk reduction, fall prevention, dental care, and age-appropriate cancer screening based on overall health and patient preferences. Encourage continued avoidance of tobacco.

Follow up promptly to establish a diabetes and blood pressure management plan, with ongoing monitoring rather than waiting for annual review.
