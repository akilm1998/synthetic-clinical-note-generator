Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Sarah Griffin is an 80-year-old woman seen for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 39 years ago. Diabetes control is currently suboptimal. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet.

She continues to smoke. She also has a 22-year history of obstructive sleep apnea. No medication regimen or recent treatment changes are provided.

Objective

Height 172 cm. Weight 71.9 kg. BMI 24.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 129/79 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

HbA1c 9.4%. Fasting glucose 193 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without complications

Long-standing type 2 diabetes mellitus with inadequate glycemic control, reflected by HbA1c of 9.4% and fasting glucose of 193 mg/dL. She is not reporting current hyperglycemic, neuropathic, infectious, or constitutional symptoms. Despite a normal BMI, her prolonged disease duration and current laboratory values place her at risk for future microvascular and cardiovascular complications. No complications are documented today.

2. Obstructive sleep apnea

History of obstructive sleep apnea for approximately 22 years. No current treatment details or related symptoms are provided.

3. Tobacco use

Continues to smoke. Smoking cessation is particularly important given her diabetes and associated vascular risk.

Plan

1. Discussed that current glycemic control is above goal and requires active management. Review diabetes treatment history, adherence, dietary pattern, home glucose monitoring, and barriers to care at the next medication-focused discussion. Consider initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy, such as metformin or another agent appropriate for her renal function, comorbidities, and overall goals of care; no medication is prescribed or assumed today because an active regimen is not provided.

2. Counsel regarding carbohydrate-conscious nutrition, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, regular physical activity as tolerated, and prevention of hypoglycemia if medication therapy is initiated. Given her age, treatment goals should be individualized with attention to functional status, safety, and quality of life.

3. Recommend assessment of renal function with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, along with a fasting lipid profile, to evaluate diabetes-related risk and guide preventive management. Recommend routine retinal evaluation and comprehensive foot examination, including assessment of sensation and pulses, given the 39-year duration of diabetes.

4. Encourage smoking cessation and offer cessation support at follow-up.

5. Review management and adherence to therapy for obstructive sleep apnea at a future visit.

6. Continue outpatient follow-up after treatment review and reassessment of glycemic control.
