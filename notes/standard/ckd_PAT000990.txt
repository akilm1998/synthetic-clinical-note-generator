Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Colin Graham is a 55-year-old male presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 6 years. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported today.

Medical history is significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 5 years, hypertension for 23 years, and coronary artery disease for 2 years. He does not smoke. Current medication regimen and allergies were not provided.

Objective

Height 165 cm, weight 66.2 kg, BMI 24.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 146/99 mmHg, pulse 80/min, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 4.06 mg/dL, estimated GFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 77 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. He is currently asymptomatic by history, without reported edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, or urinary change. The degree of renal impairment warrants prompt reassessment and specialist involvement. No electrolyte, acid-base, urine protein, blood count, or mineral-bone data are available for review today.

Hypertension, currently above goal at 146/99 mmHg, in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 5-year duration.

Coronary artery disease, 2-year duration.

Plan

Discussed the importance of prompt nephrology evaluation given creatinine 4.06 mg/dL, BUN 77 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m². Recommend expedited referral for comprehensive kidney disease management, assessment for complications, and discussion of future renal replacement planning if clinically indicated.

Obtain repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes and additional evaluation including urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound. Review for anemia, electrolyte or acid-base abnormalities, proteinuria, and mineral-bone complications when results are available.

Address blood pressure control at the next medication reconciliation and treatment review, with emphasis on kidney- and cardiovascular-protective management. Review diabetes control and cardiovascular risk reduction in coordination with the renal plan. Avoid nephrotoxic medications and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs; review all over-the-counter medications and supplements.

Counseled to seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, markedly reduced urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness. Arrange close primary care follow-up after laboratory reassessment and nephrology review.
