Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Alexandra Boyd is an 81-year-old woman with a 39-year history of chronic kidney disease, presenting for outpatient medication review. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. Smoking status is negative.

Her relevant comorbidities include anemia of chronic disease for 26 years, hyperlipidemia for 22 years, and coronary artery disease for 1 year. Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance were reviewed as priorities for today’s visit; however, no specific medication regimen is documented in the information available for reconciliation, and no medication-related adverse effects are reported.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 150/85 mmHg, pulse 88 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Height 154 cm, weight 57.9 kg, BMI 24.4 kg/m².

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, estimated GFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 78 mg/dL.

No peripheral edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, or urinary frequency change reported. No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly impaired renal function and significant azotemia. The patient has longstanding disease and currently reports fatigue but no other reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms. Current renal function warrants prompt specialty assessment and close monitoring. Medication effectiveness and adherence cannot be fully assessed without a complete medication reconciliation.

Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding, which may contribute to fatigue.

Blood pressure is elevated at today’s visit. No current antihypertensive regimen is documented for review.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation to review the severity and trajectory of renal dysfunction, discuss renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate, and establish ongoing CKD management. Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically indicated; renal ultrasound may also be considered.

Complete medication reconciliation with the patient and pharmacy, including all prescription medications, over-the-counter products, and supplements. Assess adherence, therapeutic response, renal dosing, and tolerance once the full regimen is available. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications unless specifically approved by a clinician. Any medication changes should account for the markedly reduced renal function and coronary artery disease.

Monitor blood pressure closely and review home readings if available. Dietary and fluid recommendations should be individualized based on renal and cardiovascular status.

Discussed urgent evaluation for worsening shortness of breath, new or progressive swelling, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, profound weakness, chest pain, or a marked decrease in urine output. Follow up promptly after nephrology review or sooner for any change in symptoms.
