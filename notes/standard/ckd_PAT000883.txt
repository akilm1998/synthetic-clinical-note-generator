Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Isabella Parker is a 39-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 6-year history of chronic kidney disease in the setting of type 2 diabetes mellitus for 7 years and gout for 1 year. She is not currently reporting fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, changes in urination, or shortness of breath. She does not smoke.

No medication regimen or allergies were provided for review today. Preventive health and chronic disease management were addressed.

Objective

Height 177 cm, weight 91.5 kg, BMI 29.2 kg/m².

Blood pressure 139/86 mmHg, pulse 75/min, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Serum creatinine 3.86 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m². BUN 83 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN and creatinine. Although currently asymptomatic and without reported edema or dyspnea, the renal indices are concerning for advanced disease and warrant prompt specialist assessment and close monitoring. Underlying risk factors include longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus and borderline-elevated blood pressure. Current chronic kidney disease management appears insufficiently established.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 7-year duration, requiring ongoing renal-protective management and monitoring.

Gout, 1-year duration, with need to avoid therapies that may worsen renal function and to coordinate urate management with kidney function.

Overweight, BMI 29.2 kg/m².

Preventive health review completed.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology referral for evaluation of markedly reduced estimated GFR, assessment of renal prognosis, optimization of chronic kidney disease care, and discussion of future renal replacement planning if clinically indicated.

Obtain repeat renal function testing and serum electrolytes, along with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate. Monitor for anemia, mineral and bone complications, electrolyte abnormalities, and progression of kidney disease.

Review and optimize diabetes control with renal-appropriate treatment choices. Continue close blood pressure monitoring, with an individualized renal-protective blood pressure goal. Perform medication reconciliation at the earliest opportunity and avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, unnecessary contrast exposure, and other nephrotoxic agents. Dosing of all medications should be adjusted for current renal function.

Encourage a kidney-conscious eating pattern with sodium reduction, avoidance of excessive protein or processed foods, attention to fluid recommendations from nephrology, and gradual weight reduction through safe dietary and physical activity measures.

For gout, avoid unsupervised over-the-counter anti-inflammatory use and coordinate urate-lowering treatment with renal function and nephrology or rheumatology guidance.

Seek urgent medical care for new shortness of breath, chest pain, progressive swelling, markedly reduced urine output, persistent vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, or other acute deterioration.

Continue annual preventive care, including age- and risk-appropriate immunizations and screening, diabetes-related preventive care, cardiovascular risk assessment, and routine health maintenance. Follow up promptly after nephrology evaluation and repeat laboratory testing, with closer monitoring rather than routine annual follow-up alone.
