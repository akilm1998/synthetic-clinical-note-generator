Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Nora Kelly is a 68-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 4 years and not currently actively managed. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. Relevant cardiovascular risk factors include active tobacco use, obesity, hypertension, and known coronary artery disease. Obesity has been present for approximately 21 years, hypertension for 33 years, and coronary artery disease for 17 years. No lipid-lowering medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 147/86 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 162 cm, weight 103.9 kg, BMI 39.6 kg/m².

Recent lipid panel: total cholesterol 326 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 226 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 36 mg/dL, triglycerides 321 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, currently not actively managed. The lipid pattern confers very high atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of known coronary artery disease, hypertension, active smoking, obesity, low HDL cholesterol, and advanced age. She reports no current chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness.

Hypertension with blood pressure above goal today.

Obesity, BMI 39.6 kg/m².

Active tobacco use.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and triglycerides and the need for active lipid-lowering treatment given her established coronary artery disease. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy unless contraindicated, with treatment selection and dosing reviewed with the patient. If LDL reduction remains inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, consider addition of ezetimibe and/or referral for further lipid management.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy diet emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, limitation of refined carbohydrates and alcohol, increased fiber intake, weight reduction, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Smoking cessation strongly advised.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, along with monitoring for medication adverse effects as clinically indicated. Consider assessment for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and measurement of additional cardiovascular risk markers when appropriate.

Continue close monitoring of blood pressure and coronary artery disease. Seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, syncope, or exertional leg symptoms.

Follow up after treatment initiation and repeat lipid testing to reassess lipid control and cardiovascular risk reduction.
