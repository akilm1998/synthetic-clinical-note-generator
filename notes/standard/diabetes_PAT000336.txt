Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Trevor Warren is a 38-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 3 years ago. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. Current diabetes management is limited, and no active diabetes medications are documented.

He has a 17-year history of obesity and a 7-year history of hypertension. He also reports a 16-year history of anxiety. He currently smokes. No interval diabetes-related complications are reported.

Objective

Height 167 cm. Weight 101.8 kg. BMI 36.5 kg/m².

Blood pressure 135/89 mmHg. Pulse 84 bpm. Respiratory rate 19/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 98%.

Recent laboratory results: HbA1c 9.9%. Fasting glucose 215 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications. Glycemic control is above goal, with HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 215 mg/dL. He is currently asymptomatic, without reported neuropathic, visual, infectious, or constitutional symptoms. His obesity and physical risk profile likely contribute to poor glycemic control.

Obesity, BMI 36.5 kg/m².

Hypertension, with blood pressure mildly elevated today at 135/89 mmHg.

Anxiety, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the increased risk of future microvascular and cardiovascular complications. Reviewed dietary strategies emphasizing portion control, reduced refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, and increased intake of vegetables, lean protein, and high-fiber foods. Encouraged gradual, sustainable physical activity as tolerated and weight reduction.

Recommend initiation of evidence-based pharmacologic therapy for diabetes, with metformin an appropriate initial consideration if no contraindication, after review of renal function and medication history. Medication selection and dosing should be finalized after confirming current medications, allergies, and renal status. If glycemic targets are not achieved, additional therapy should be considered at follow-up.

Recommend home glucose monitoring during treatment initiation and recording values for review. Obtain repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval, along with renal assessment including serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and a fasting lipid profile. Recommend routine comprehensive foot examination and dilated retinal examination for diabetes-related screening.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and reinforce dietary sodium reduction, weight management, physical activity, and smoking cessation. Counseling provided regarding the benefits of stopping tobacco use; cessation support and pharmacotherapy can be discussed based on readiness.

Follow up in approximately 4 weeks to review glucose readings, treatment tolerance, blood pressure, and diabetes management, or sooner for symptomatic hyperglycemia or other concerns.
