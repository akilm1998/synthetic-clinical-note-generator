Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Margaret Walsh is a 31-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 2 years and currently untreated. Recent lipid results show markedly elevated total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, and triglycerides with low-normal HDL cholesterol. She reports xanthomas and xanthelasma. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or claudication, and dizziness.

She has type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She currently smokes tobacco. No lipid-lowering medications are documented.

Objective

Height 159 cm; weight 53.1 kg; BMI 21.0 kg/m².

Blood pressure 124/76 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 313 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 199 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 40 mg/dL, triglycerides 368 mg/dL.

Xanthomas and xanthelasma are reported. No chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and triglycerides, currently untreated. The LDL level of 199 mg/dL and presence of xanthomas/xanthelasma raise concern for a significant underlying lipid disorder, including possible familial contribution. Her type 2 diabetes and ongoing tobacco use further increase long-term cardiovascular risk. No current cardiovascular symptoms are reported.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago; degree of glycemic control is not addressed by the available information.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the lipid results and the importance of prompt treatment. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after reviewing risks, benefits, contraindications, pregnancy plans, and potential adverse effects. If LDL reduction remains inadequate or therapy is not tolerated, consider addition of ezetimibe and/or referral for further lipid management.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation to assess response, along with evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia as clinically appropriate. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement given the markedly elevated LDL and cutaneous lipid deposits. Assess for a family history of premature cardiovascular disease and consider evaluation for familial hypercholesterolemia.

Counseled on a heart-healthy diet emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, increased soluble fiber, and avoidance of excess refined carbohydrates and alcohol given the elevated triglycerides. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated. Her BMI is currently 21.0 kg/m².

Strongly advised smoking cessation; discussed benefits for cardiovascular risk reduction and offered cessation support and pharmacotherapy options.

Continue routine diabetes follow-up and ensure diabetes management is coordinated with cardiovascular risk reduction. Advised to seek urgent care for new chest pain, significant shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain. Follow up after repeat lipid testing to review response and finalize ongoing management.
