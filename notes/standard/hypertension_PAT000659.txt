Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated blood pressure.

Subjective
Lily Peterson is an 82-year-old woman with a 47-year history of essential hypertension that has not been actively managed. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and nosebleeds. She is a nonsmoker. Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for 27 years, chronic kidney disease for 36 years, and hyperlipidemia for 22 years.

Objective
Height 163 cm, weight 57.9 kg, BMI 21.8 kg/m². Blood pressure 170/93 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%. Serum sodium 146 mmol/L and potassium 4.2 mmol/L. No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with significantly elevated blood pressure in the setting of longstanding untreated disease. She currently has no symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency, but her age and history of coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease increase cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan
Arrange prompt clinician-directed initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy, with selection and dosing guided by renal function, coronary artery disease, and overall tolerance. Repeat blood pressure measurement after a period of rest and begin home blood pressure monitoring with a written log.

Recommend reduced dietary sodium intake, avoidance of excess alcohol, and continued physical activity as tolerated. Review cardiovascular risk management, including hyperlipidemia, at follow-up.

Recommend assessment of renal function and electrolytes, urinalysis, ECG, and lipid profile as clinically appropriate before or during treatment initiation. Seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, vision changes, or other acute deterioration. Close follow-up is recommended within days for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning.
