Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Savannah Bell is a 77-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 38 years. Current glycemic control is poor, with recent hemoglobin A1c of 9.8% and fasting glucose of 230 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections.

She reports numbness in the hands or feet without associated tingling. No medication regimen is provided for review today. She does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 27 years, depression for 12 years, and obstructive sleep apnea for 17 years.

Objective

Height 157 cm. Weight 63.8 kg. BMI 25.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 133/78 mmHg. Pulse 72 bpm. Respiratory rate 15/min. Temperature 36.6°C. Oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results: hemoglobin A1c 9.8%. Fasting glucose 230 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently inadequately controlled based on A1c and fasting glucose. She is not reporting symptomatic hyperglycemia, but the degree and duration of elevation increase her risk for microvascular and macrovascular complications.

Peripheral numbness, concerning for possible diabetic neuropathic symptoms. No wounds or recurrent infections reported.

Overweight by BMI, with BMI 25.9 kg/m².

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Depression, longstanding.

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the elevated A1c and fasting glucose and the importance of improving glycemic control. Review diabetes self-management, nutrition, portion control, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Given her age and significantly elevated A1c, recommend prompt medication review and initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy based on renal function, hepatic status, hypoglycemia risk, comorbidities, and overall treatment goals. No specific current diabetes medications are documented today.

Recommend home glucose monitoring if feasible, with a record of fasting and occasional postprandial readings for review. Reviewed symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia and advised her to seek urgent care for persistent vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, inability to maintain hydration, or markedly elevated glucose readings.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and updated diabetes complication screening. Arrange a comprehensive foot examination, particularly given the reported numbness, and ensure routine retinal evaluation. Counsel on daily foot inspection, protective footwear, and prompt evaluation of any skin breakdown or nonhealing lesion.

Continue attention to weight management and physical activity as tolerated. Consider the impact of diabetes treatment choices on her longstanding fatty liver disease, depression, and obstructive sleep apnea. Follow up after medication and laboratory review to reassess glycemic control and treatment tolerance.
