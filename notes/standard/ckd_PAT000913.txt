Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Shannon Douglas is a 56-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 24 months ago. She reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No interval symptoms suggestive of fluid overload or uremic illness are reported.

She has a 15-year history of gout. She does not smoke. Current medication regimen and home blood pressure readings were not provided.

Objective

Height 171 cm; weight 75.7 kg; BMI 25.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 147/83 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: creatinine 3.91 mg/dL, estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 67 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. She is currently clinically asymptomatic without reported edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, or urinary changes. Blood pressure is mildly elevated today. Longstanding gout may be a relevant contributor and requires renal-adjusted management. The degree of renal impairment warrants expedited renal evaluation and close monitoring, even in the absence of symptoms.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current kidney function and the importance of nephrology involvement. Recommend prompt nephrology referral for evaluation, renal-protective management, and discussion of future renal replacement planning if appropriate.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Consider renal ultrasound as part of evaluation of chronic kidney disease.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications for renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxins. Avoid NSAIDs unless specifically directed by a clinician. Gout treatment should be reviewed with renal function taken into account.

Monitor blood pressure closely and address hypertension with a kidney-protective regimen as clinically appropriate. Encourage a renal-conscious diet with attention to sodium intake and avoidance of dehydration. Review warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including new shortness of breath, progressive swelling, markedly decreased urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, or chest pain.

Follow up promptly after nephrology evaluation and repeat laboratory testing.
