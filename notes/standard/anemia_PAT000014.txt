Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for longstanding anemia.

Subjective

Christina Pierce is an 84-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 32 years. She reports headache but denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor has been noted. There is no reported interval change in functional status.

Her medical history is notable for longstanding rheumatoid arthritis, present for approximately 49 years, and chronic infection, present for approximately 53 years. She is a current smoker. No anemia-directed medication regimen is documented.

Objective

Height 157 cm, weight 62.4 kg, BMI 25.3 kg/m². Blood pressure 135/79 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Patient appears clinically stable. Pallor is present. Cardiovascular examination is without tachycardia, and respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory results show hemoglobin 9.6 g/dL, hematocrit 28.4%, RBC count 3.42 million/µL, and MCV 83 fL. Serum iron is 44 µg/dL, ferritin 314 ng/mL, TIBC 217 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 20.3%.

Assessment

Longstanding anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic disease in the setting of rheumatoid arthritis and chronic infection. The anemia is moderate and normocytic, with low serum iron and TIBC and elevated ferritin, supporting impaired iron utilization rather than straightforward iron depletion. She is hemodynamically stable and has no cardiopulmonary symptoms, although pallor and headache persist.

Rheumatoid arthritis and chronic infection are significant ongoing inflammatory comorbidities likely contributing to the anemia. Current tobacco use may further affect overall cardiopulmonary risk.

Plan

Review the chronicity and stability of the anemia with repeat complete blood count and reticulocyte count. Recommend peripheral blood smear, vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers to further characterize contributors and exclude additional reversible causes. Continue evaluation and management of the underlying rheumatoid arthritis and chronic infection, as control of inflammation is central to improving anemia.

Iron studies should be interpreted in the context of inflammation; routine empiric iron therapy is not clearly indicated from the current pattern without further assessment. Consider hematology input if hemoglobin declines, symptoms develop, the cause remains uncertain, or anemia fails to respond to management of the underlying inflammatory conditions. Erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy or intravenous iron would require individualized consideration based on the subsequent evaluation and comorbidities.

Counseled to report new or worsening shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, marked weakness, or reduced exercise tolerance. Smoking cessation was encouraged. Reassess after updated laboratory evaluation and clinical review of the chronic inflammatory conditions.
