Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Connor Walters is a 47-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 16 years and currently without an established treatment regimen documented. He denies chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, and dizziness. He reports xanthomas and xanthelasma. No lipid-lowering medications are provided in the current history.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease present for approximately 2 years, chronic kidney disease present for approximately 2 years, and longstanding obesity. He currently smokes. His weight is 118.4 kg at a height of 188 cm, with BMI 33.5 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 133/89 mmHg, pulse 85/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent lipid profile: total cholesterol 346 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 227 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 42 mg/dL, triglycerides 383 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled. Lipid values demonstrate marked elevation of total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with concomitant hypertriglyceridemia and low-normal HDL. The presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma supports significant chronic lipid burden. Given known coronary artery disease, chronic kidney disease, active tobacco use, and obesity, he is at high cardiovascular risk despite the absence of current anginal or claudication symptoms.

Coronary artery disease, established approximately 2 years ago, without reported chest discomfort today. The markedly elevated LDL cholesterol is particularly concerning in the setting of known atherosclerotic disease.

Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 2 years. This increases cardiovascular risk and should be considered when selecting and monitoring lipid-lowering therapy.

Obesity, BMI 33.5 kg/m².

Active tobacco use, an important modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

Plan

Discussed the severity of the lipid abnormalities and the importance of active treatment, particularly in the setting of coronary artery disease. Medication reconciliation should be completed, and initiation of a high-intensity statin should be strongly considered if not contraindicated. If LDL cholesterol remains substantially above goal despite maximally tolerated statin therapy, addition of ezetimibe and, if clinically indicated, further lipid-lowering therapy should be considered.

Recommend evaluation for contributing secondary causes of severe dyslipidemia, with attention to renal disease, metabolic factors, diet, and other potentially reversible causes. A fasting lipid panel should be repeated after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider measurement of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a) for further cardiovascular risk assessment; coronary calcium scoring is less likely to change management given the established coronary artery disease.

Counseled regarding reduction of saturated and trans fats, avoidance of excess refined carbohydrates and alcohol, increased dietary fiber, weight reduction, and gradual regular physical activity as tolerated. Smoking cessation was strongly encouraged.

Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management in coordination with care for coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease. Seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening chest discomfort, exertional shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or significant leg pain with walking.

Follow-up after medication reconciliation and repeat lipid assessment to update treatment intensity and response.
