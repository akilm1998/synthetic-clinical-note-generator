Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Riley Benson is a 61-year-old man, height 183 cm, weight 72.3 kg, BMI 21.6 kg/m², with a 2-year history of chronic kidney disease in the setting of longstanding hypertension for 31 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 15 years. He also has secondary hyperparathyroidism of approximately 15 years’ duration. He currently smokes.

He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. No current medication regimen is identified for review today; consequently, medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be confirmed. He reports no symptoms suggestive of current uremic prodrome or fluid overload.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 161/94 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Laboratory data: Serum creatinine 3.7 mg/dL, estimated GFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 67 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with significantly reduced renal function. Creatinine is 3.7 mg/dL, eGFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 67 mg/dL. The longstanding diabetes and hypertension are important likely contributors. He is presently without reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms, but the degree of renal impairment warrants close follow-up and active management. The condition does not appear to be under effective treatment at this time based on the medication history available for review.

2. Hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal at 161/94 mmHg. Poor blood pressure control may accelerate further renal decline. A medication regimen and adherence pattern could not be verified during this review.

3. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for 15 years. Glycemic control data are not provided today. Diabetes remains a major risk factor for progression of chronic kidney disease.

4. Secondary hyperparathyroidism, longstanding in the setting of chronic kidney disease. No current treatment or recent mineral-bone laboratory data are available for medication effectiveness or tolerance assessment.

5. Tobacco use. Continued smoking increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Complete a thorough medication reconciliation at the next contact and verify all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use, including dose, adherence, perceived benefit, and adverse effects. Avoid empiric medication changes until the active regimen is confirmed. Review all medications for renal dosing and avoid nephrotoxic agents when possible, particularly nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.

Because of the markedly elevated blood pressure and reduced renal function, arrange prompt outpatient renal and blood pressure management, with consideration of nephrology referral. Recommend monitoring renal function, electrolytes, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Renal ultrasound may also be considered to assess renal structure.

Address blood pressure and diabetes control with the goal of slowing further kidney disease progression. Counsel regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of dehydration and non-prescribed nephrotoxic medications. He should seek urgent evaluation for new shortness of breath, progressive swelling, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, or substantially decreased urine output.
