Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of longstanding anemia.

Subjective

Spencer Lawson is a 55-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 20 years. He has a 12-year history of malignancy, which is a relevant chronic disease context for his anemia. He is not currently described as receiving treatment or active management for the anemia.

He denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or awareness of pallor. No smoking history. Weight is 67.8 kg at a height of 166 cm, with BMI 24.6 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 123/78 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: Hemoglobin 8.4 g/dL, hematocrit 24.5%, RBC count 2.88 million/µL, MCV 85 fL, serum iron 34 µg/dL, ferritin 260 ng/mL, TIBC 239 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 14.2%.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with chronic disease/inflammation. The patient has significant, longstanding, normocytic anemia with hemoglobin 8.4 g/dL and hematocrit 24.5%. Low serum iron and transferrin saturation with ferritin 260 ng/mL and relatively low TIBC support an iron-restricted inflammatory pattern rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. His chronic malignancy is a plausible contributing inflammatory condition. He is currently clinically stable by vital signs and denies cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms, but the degree of anemia warrants further evaluation and active management.

Plan

Obtain a repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to confirm persistence and assess marrow response and red-cell morphology.

Complete evaluation for potentially reversible contributors with vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers. Reassess iron indices in conjunction with the repeat blood count and clinical context. Consider evaluation for occult blood loss if clinically indicated.

Review the malignancy history and current disease activity as part of determining the driver of chronic inflammation. Coordination with the treating oncology team and/or hematology is appropriate given the severity and long duration of the anemia.

Treatment options, including iron replacement when iron-restricted erythropoiesis is confirmed and an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent in an appropriate malignancy-related setting, should be considered based on the underlying disease, renal function, reticulocyte response, and specialist assessment. No current anemia medication regimen is documented.

He should seek urgent evaluation for new shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, syncope, marked dizziness, worsening weakness, or evidence of bleeding. Follow up after the recommended evaluation to review results and establish an active anemia management plan.
