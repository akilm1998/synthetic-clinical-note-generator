Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Richard Lawson is a 72-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 23 years and currently without active management. He denies chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, and dizziness. He reports fatty skin deposits consistent with xanthomas; no xanthelasma is reported.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for approximately 18 years, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 35 years, and longstanding obesity. He continues to smoke. No current lipid-lowering medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 128/79 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 175 cm, weight 120.4 kg, BMI 39.3 kg/m².

Lipid panel:
Total cholesterol 341 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 229 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 36 mg/dL
Triglycerides 378 mg/dL

Reported physical finding: xanthomas. No xanthelasma reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, in the setting of longstanding disease, obesity, active tobacco use, diabetes, and established coronary artery disease. The lipid pattern represents substantially increased atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk. Lack of active lipid management is concerning given his comorbidities and presence of xanthomas.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, without current chest discomfort or exertional claudication reported.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Severe obesity with BMI 39.3 kg/m².

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current lipid values and the increased cardiovascular risk associated with his coronary artery disease, diabetes, smoking, and obesity.

Recommend initiation of guideline-directed intensive LDL-lowering therapy, typically with a high-intensity statin if no contraindication, after medication reconciliation and review of prior intolerance or contraindications. If LDL remains substantially elevated despite maximally tolerated statin therapy, recommend consideration of additional therapy such as ezetimibe and/or a PCSK9-directed agent based on clinical response and coverage.

Recommend a fasting repeat lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, with consideration of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk assessment. Evaluate for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia as clinically appropriate.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy eating pattern low in saturated and trans fats, reduction of refined carbohydrates and alcohol if applicable, gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated, and weight reduction.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered cessation support. Reviewed emergency precautions, including seeking immediate care for new or persistent chest pain, dyspnea, or other symptoms concerning for acute coronary syndrome.

Continue close follow-up for lipid response and coordination of management of coronary artery disease and diabetes.
