Chief Complaint

Annual review and evaluation of longstanding elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Ellie Wood is a 54-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 21-year history of hyperlipidemia that is currently not under active treatment. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 348 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 219 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 49 mg/dL, and triglycerides 400 mg/dL. She reports fatty skin deposits consistent with xanthomas. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, or xanthelasma.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 6 years and obesity for 11 years. She does not smoke. No current lipid-lowering medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Height 163 cm. Weight 80.5 kg. BMI 30.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 127/82 mmHg. Pulse 79 bpm. Respiratory rate 18/min. Temperature 36.6°C. Oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 348 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 219 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 49 mg/dL, triglycerides 400 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and triglycerides, longstanding for approximately 21 years and not currently managed. The degree of LDL elevation is associated with increased lifetime atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk. Triglycerides are also significantly elevated, requiring attention to dietary factors, glycemic control, weight, and secondary contributors. Xanthomas further support prolonged lipid elevation. She has no reported anginal symptoms, claudication, or dizziness.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 6-year duration, an important cardiovascular risk enhancer in the setting of severe dyslipidemia.

Obesity, BMI 30.3 kg/m², present for approximately 11 years and contributing to cardiometabolic risk.

Plan

Discussed the importance of initiating active lipid management given the LDL cholesterol of 219 mg/dL, elevated triglycerides, diabetes, and presence of xanthomas. Recommend shared decision-making regarding high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after review of contraindications, potential adverse effects, and patient preferences. If LDL cholesterol remains substantially elevated despite adherence and tolerated statin therapy, addition of ezetimibe and consideration of specialist-directed therapy may be appropriate.

Recommend a fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, along with evaluation for potentially reversible contributors to dyslipidemia, including glycemic status, thyroid function, renal function, and liver-related causes. Consider one-time measurement of lipoprotein(a) and apolipoprotein B for additional cardiovascular risk assessment. Coronary artery calcium imaging may be considered if treatment decisions remain uncertain, though the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and diabetes already support active risk reduction.

Counseled on reducing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and added sugars; increasing dietary fiber; emphasizing vegetables, legumes, whole grains, and unsaturated fats; and avoiding excess alcohol because of the triglyceride elevation. Encouraged gradual, sustainable weight reduction and at least 150 minutes of moderate physical activity weekly as tolerated.

Continue comprehensive diabetes care with attention to diet, physical activity, and routine monitoring. Preventive care reviewed, including age-appropriate cancer screening, immunizations, blood pressure monitoring, and assessment of diabetes-related eye, kidney, and foot health.

Follow up after initiation or adjustment of lipid therapy to review tolerance and repeat lipid results, with ongoing chronic disease follow-up.
