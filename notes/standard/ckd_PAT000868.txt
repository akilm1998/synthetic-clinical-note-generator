Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Teresa Cunningham is a 63-year-old woman with a 28-year history of chronic kidney disease, presenting for outpatient follow-up. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. No interval symptoms suggestive of overt fluid overload or a symptomatic uremic prodrome are reported.

Her other medical problems include hyperlipidemia for approximately 18 years and gout for approximately 1 year. She currently smokes. Medication use was not provided for review today.

Objective

Height is 169 cm and weight is 61.7 kg, with BMI 21.6 kg/m². Blood pressure is 144/89 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.6°C, and oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results show serum creatinine 5.79 mg/dL, BUN 78 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m². No additional laboratory results or imaging findings are available from this encounter.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. The chronicity is longstanding at approximately 28 years, but the current creatinine, BUN, and eGFR indicate advanced renal impairment requiring prompt specialist assessment. She has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, dyspnea, or urinary change. There is no reported evidence of acute fluid overload based on symptoms, although blood pressure remains above goal.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Gout, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago; any treatment will require careful consideration of renal function.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Given the degree of renal impairment, arrange urgent nephrology evaluation to assess overall kidney function, complications of chronic kidney disease, and preparation for renal replacement therapy if clinically indicated. This should not be deferred despite the absence of prominent uremic or volume-overload symptoms.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound to assess renal structure and chronicity if not previously completed. Results should be reviewed promptly, with particular attention to electrolyte or acid-base abnormalities, anemia, mineral and bone complications, and progression from baseline.

Perform a complete medication reconciliation when available. Avoid nephrotoxic medications, particularly nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, and ensure all prescriptions are renally dosed. Gout management should be coordinated with renal function in mind.

Continue close blood pressure monitoring and reassess antihypertensive management after medication reconciliation. Address cardiovascular risk related to longstanding hyperlipidemia. Provide smoking-cessation counseling and support.

She was advised to seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, marked weakness, confusion, persistent vomiting, new edema, substantially reduced urine output, or other acute deterioration. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended after nephrology assessment and review of repeat studies.
