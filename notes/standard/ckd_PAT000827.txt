Chief Complaint

Medication review for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Bella Dean is a 58-year-old woman, height 176 cm, weight 79.3 kg, BMI 25.6 kg/m², with a 6-year history of chronic kidney disease and secondary hyperparathyroidism for approximately 10 years. She reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, change in urination, peripheral edema, or shortness of breath. She is an active smoker.

Today’s visit focused on medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerability. A medication regimen was not documented for review. No medication-related adverse effects are reported.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 139/84 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory data: Serum creatinine 3.45 mg/dL, BUN 71 mg/dL, eGFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m².

No edema or other examination findings were provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced kidney function, longstanding for approximately 6 years. Current laboratory values indicate substantial azotemia. She is clinically without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms today. Blood pressure is acceptable but requires continued monitoring in the context of renal disease.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, longstanding.

Medication review: Effectiveness, adherence, renal dosing, drug interactions, and tolerability require structured reconciliation, particularly given significantly impaired renal clearance. Avoidance of nephrotoxic and over-the-counter agents is important.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Complete a comprehensive medication reconciliation, including prescription medications, over-the-counter agents, supplements, and NSAID use. Confirm adherence, indication, response, and adverse effects for each medication, and adjust all renally cleared medications to current kidney function. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a treating clinician.

Continue close blood pressure monitoring and reinforce adherence to the prescribed renal-protective regimen once reconciled. Counsel regarding sodium restriction, avoidance of dehydration, and prompt evaluation for new edema, dyspnea, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, marked reduction in urine output, or worsening pruritus.

Given eGFR of 13 mL/min/1.73 m², arrange timely nephrology management for advanced kidney disease care, including discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if kidney function remains at this level or declines. Recommend monitoring of serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and mineral-bone parameters including intact PTH as clinically indicated.

Smoking cessation counseling provided; cessation is strongly recommended to reduce cardiovascular and renal risk.
