Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Kayla Pierce is a 42-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 14-year history of chronic kidney disease and reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, changes in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath.

She continues to smoke. No current medication regimen is documented for review today. She has a history of coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. No acute cardiopulmonary or uremic symptoms are reported.

Objective

Height 180 cm; weight 71.9 kg; BMI 22.2 kg/m².

Blood pressure 145/90 mmHg, pulse 81/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results: creatinine 2.99 mg/dL, estimated GFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 71 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings documented.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of 14 years’ duration with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN and creatinine. Fatigue may be related to chronic kidney disease. She currently denies symptoms suggesting clinically evident fluid overload or significant uremic symptom progression. Blood pressure is above goal in the setting of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease.

Coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago.

Current tobacco use.

Annual preventive health review.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation given the markedly reduced estimated GFR and longstanding, currently inadequately addressed kidney disease. Obtain or update urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum electrolytes, complete blood count, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate to assess kidney complications and disease etiology. Repeat renal function testing should be arranged with close outpatient follow-up.

Review blood pressure monitoring and establish an individualized blood pressure treatment plan, including medication reconciliation before initiating or adjusting therapy. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications; review all over-the-counter and herbal products. Counsel regarding appropriate hydration, avoidance of excess dietary sodium, and prompt evaluation for worsening renal symptoms.

Discuss kidney disease complications, including anemia, mineral and bone disorder, electrolyte abnormalities, and progression of renal impairment. Counsel to seek urgent care for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, markedly reduced urine output, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.

Strongly recommend smoking cessation, with discussion of behavioral support and pharmacologic options after review of renal function and cardiovascular history.

Continue comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction, including blood pressure control, tobacco cessation, heart-healthy nutrition, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Confirm appropriate preventive care for age, including immunizations and recommended cervical, breast, and colorectal cancer screening based on prior screening history and risk factors.

Arrange close primary care follow-up after repeat testing and nephrology assessment.
