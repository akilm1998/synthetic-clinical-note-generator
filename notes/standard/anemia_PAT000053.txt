Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of longstanding anemia.

Subjective

Leah Coleman is a 34-year-old woman, height 155 cm, weight 52.1 kg, BMI 21.7 kg/m², presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has an 8-year history of anemia in the setting of chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, and chronic infection present for approximately 3 years. She reports headache and pallor but denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance.

She currently smokes. No current treatment regimen for anemia is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 127/82 mmHg, pulse 88 bpm, respirations 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Patient appears clinically stable. Pallor is noted. Cardiovascular examination is normal without documented abnormality. Respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin 9.3 g/dL, hematocrit 27.8%, RBC 3.35 million/µL, MCV 83 fL, serum iron 38 µg/dL, ferritin 286 ng/mL, TIBC 222 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 17.1%.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic disease/chronic inflammation. This is a longstanding, moderate normocytic anemia with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and reduced TIBC, in the context of chronic kidney disease and chronic infection. She is hemodynamically stable without cardiopulmonary symptoms, although headache and pallor are present.

Chronic kidney disease, approximately 1 year since diagnosis. Renal function data are not provided for review today.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 1 year since diagnosis.

Chronic infection, approximately 3 years in duration.

Tobacco use.

Annual health maintenance review.

Plan

Obtain or review an updated CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to assess the current anemia and marrow response. Recommend renal function testing and inflammatory markers as clinically indicated, along with vitamin B12 and folate levels to evaluate for additional reversible contributors. Continue evaluation and management of the underlying chronic kidney disease and chronic infection, as control of these conditions is central to improving the anemia.

Review iron studies in clinical context before initiating supplementation. Consider oral or intravenous iron only if subsequent evaluation supports iron deficiency or inadequate iron availability. If anemia persists or worsens, consider specialist input regarding further evaluation and possible erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Advise prompt reassessment for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, marked weakness, or reduced exercise tolerance. Monitor headache and reassess if persistent or progressive.

Review diabetes care, renal protection, nutrition, physical activity, and routine preventive health measures at follow-up. Smoking cessation counseling provided; recommend cessation support and discussion of available treatment options. Schedule follow-up after repeat laboratory evaluation and sooner for worsening symptoms.
