Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and abnormal renal function.

Subjective

Rebecca Burke is a 56-year-old woman with a 16-year history of chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease for 8 years, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 4 years. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. She does not smoke. No current medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Height 173 cm, weight 76.9 kg, BMI 25.7 kg/m². Blood pressure 132/84 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respirations 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: creatinine 3.81 mg/dL, eGFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 91 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. Fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction. Although she currently denies nausea, pruritus, edema, dyspnea, or urinary changes, the degree of renal impairment requires prompt evaluation and active management. History is also notable for coronary artery disease and secondary hyperparathyroidism.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation to assess current renal function, complications, and renal replacement therapy planning as clinically indicated. Obtain prompt repeat renal and metabolic assessment, including serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone; consider renal ultrasound as part of the evaluation. Medication reconciliation and review for nephrotoxic agents, dosing adjustments, blood pressure control, and renal-protective management are needed.

Proceed to the emergency department for worsening fatigue, nausea or vomiting, confusion, new shortness of breath, chest pain, progressive swelling, markedly decreased urination, or other acute deterioration. Continue close outpatient follow-up after urgent renal evaluation.
