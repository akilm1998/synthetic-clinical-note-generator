Chief Complaint

Annual review of longstanding hypertension and chronic disease management.

Subjective

Christina Pierce is an 88-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 31-year history of essential hypertension that is currently untreated. She reports intermittent dizziness and palpitations. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, or epistaxis. She does not smoke. She was diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus approximately one year ago.

Her height is 152 cm and weight is 54.3 kg, with a BMI of 23.5 kg/m². No current antihypertensive or other medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: blood pressure 168/86 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 136 mmol/L, potassium 4.4 mmol/L.

The elevated blood pressure is consistent with poorly controlled longstanding hypertension. She is not reporting symptoms suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency at this visit.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension, longstanding for approximately 31 years, currently inadequately controlled and untreated. Blood pressure is significantly elevated at 168/86 mmHg. Intermittent dizziness and palpitations warrant confirmation of blood pressure pattern and assessment for cardiovascular effects, particularly given her advanced age and coexisting type 2 diabetes.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately one year ago. Diabetes increases her overall cardiovascular and renal risk and should be incorporated into blood pressure treatment goals and monitoring.

Normal BMI. Nonsmoker.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management and the increased cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, and renal risks associated with persistent hypertension, particularly in the setting of diabetes. Blood pressure should be rechecked carefully after a period of rest and confirmed with home or ambulatory readings if feasible. Given the degree of elevation and long duration of untreated disease, initiation of antihypertensive therapy should be considered promptly, using a cautious age-appropriate approach with monitoring for orthostatic symptoms, dizziness, renal effects, and electrolyte disturbance.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, including seated readings and symptoms. Review the log at close follow-up and reassess for orthostatic blood pressure changes. She should seek urgent medical attention for new chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, focal neurologic symptoms, acute visual change, or persistent markedly elevated blood pressure.

Recommend ECG to evaluate palpitations and assess for evidence of hypertensive cardiac effects. Lipid profile and urinalysis are appropriate cardiovascular and renal risk assessments. Continue periodic serum electrolyte monitoring if antihypertensive therapy is initiated or adjusted. Diabetes monitoring and preventive care should be reviewed alongside blood pressure management.

Counseled on limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity appropriate to her functional status, avoiding excess alcohol, and rising slowly from sitting or lying positions because of her dizziness. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning rather than waiting until the next annual visit.
