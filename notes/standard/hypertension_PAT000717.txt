Chief Complaint
Medication review and follow-up of hypertension.

Subjective
Bruce McKenzie is a 46-year-old man with a 10-year history of essential hypertension, coronary artery disease, and chronic kidney disease of approximately 3 years’ duration, presenting for outpatient medication review. He is not reporting headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Current antihypertensive use, adherence, and treatment tolerance were not established during today’s review. He continues to smoke. Weight is 80.3 kg at 170 cm, with BMI 27.8 kg/m².

Objective
Vital signs: BP 160/98 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory data: Sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 4.7 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported today. No additional examination findings documented.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure. He is asymptomatic without reported features of hypertensive emergency, but blood pressure remains above goal in the setting of known coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease. Long-term blood pressure control appears inadequate, and medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance require clarification.

Chronic kidney disease, approximately 3 years’ duration.

Coronary artery disease, approximately 1 year’s duration.

Tobacco use.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan
Complete medication reconciliation, including confirmation of any antihypertensive agents currently being taken, dosing, missed doses, adverse effects, and barriers to adherence. Discussed the importance of consistent daily therapy and home blood pressure monitoring with a written log.

Recommend initiating or re-establishing a guideline-directed antihypertensive regimen, selected with consideration of his coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease. Review renal function and potassium before and after starting or adjusting an ACE inhibitor, ARB, diuretic, or other appropriate agent; current potassium is 4.7 mmol/L.

Recommend low-sodium dietary pattern, moderation of alcohol if applicable, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and smoking cessation. Encourage avoidance of nonprescription agents that may raise blood pressure, particularly NSAIDs and decongestants.

Recommend ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular and hypertension risk assessment. Continue coordination of care for coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease.

Arrange close outpatient blood pressure follow-up after medication reconciliation and treatment adjustment. Seek urgent medical attention for chest pain, severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, shortness of breath, vision loss, or markedly higher blood pressure readings.
