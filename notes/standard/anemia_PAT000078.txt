Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Robert Collins is a 97-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding anemia. He reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. He does not report pallor. The anemia has been present for approximately 54 years. His significant comorbidity is longstanding malignancy, reported for approximately 55 years. He is a nonsmoker. No current anemia-directed medications are documented.

Objective

Height 160 cm, weight 72.7 kg, BMI 28.4 kg/m². Blood pressure 128/81 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Patient is clinically stable without reported pallor. Cardiovascular examination is normal with no tachycardia. Respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory results: hemoglobin 8.7 g/dL, hematocrit 26.8%, RBC count 3.35 million/µL, MCV 80 fL, serum iron 37 µg/dL, ferritin 494 ng/mL, TIBC 208 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 17.8%.

Assessment

Chronic anemia consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation in the setting of longstanding malignancy. Hemoglobin remains significantly reduced, with a low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and low TIBC, supporting impaired iron availability associated with chronic disease. MCV is at the lower end of normal. He is currently asymptomatic and hemodynamically stable, with normal oxygen saturation and no tachycardia.

Plan

Continue outpatient monitoring and clinical observation given the absence of current cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms.

Recommend repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to reassess severity and marrow response. Consider vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate to evaluate for additional contributors to the chronic anemia. Review for any evidence of occult blood loss or other changes in his underlying malignancy.

Iron studies suggest limited circulating iron but do not support routine empiric iron treatment based on ferritin alone. Consider oral or intravenous iron only after clinical review of the overall cause of anemia and iron availability. Hematology involvement and consideration of anemia-directed therapy may be appropriate if hemoglobin declines, symptoms develop, or the anemia is not adequately explained by chronic disease.

Seek urgent evaluation for new shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, syncope, significant dizziness, bleeding, or marked decline in exercise tolerance. Continue routine follow-up.
