Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Diana Prince is an 88-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 47 years ago. She is not currently engaged in active diabetes management. Recent glycemic measures show suboptimal control, although she denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has a 20-year history of obstructive sleep apnea. She reports current tobacco use. No diabetes medications or other medication regimen are provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 128/77 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 175 cm; weight 68.6 kg; BMI 22.4 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Hemoglobin A1c 9.2%. Fasting glucose 212 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, present for approximately 47 years, with current marked hyperglycemia. HbA1c of 9.2% and fasting glucose of 212 mg/dL indicate inadequate glycemic control despite the absence of reported hyperglycemic, constitutional, neuropathic, or infectious symptoms. Her normal BMI and advanced age warrant individualized treatment planning with attention to hypoglycemia risk, functional status, treatment burden, and overall goals of care.

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding, by history.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of re-engaging in structured diabetes care. Review treatment goals and establish an individualized glycemic target appropriate for her age and overall health, prioritizing avoidance of symptomatic hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia.

Perform a complete medication reconciliation and discuss initiation or adjustment of evidence-based glucose-lowering therapy based on renal function, comorbidities, dietary pattern, treatment preferences, and ability to safely administer and monitor therapy. Renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio should be obtained before selecting or adjusting therapy. Consider fasting lipid assessment as part of comprehensive diabetes risk evaluation.

Counseled regarding a consistent, nutrient-dense meal pattern, avoidance of concentrated sugars, appropriate physical activity as safely tolerated, and home glucose monitoring if pharmacologic therapy is initiated. Reviewed symptoms requiring prompt medical attention, including worsening thirst or urination, vomiting, confusion, profound weakness, or inability to maintain oral intake.

Recommend routine diabetic eye, foot, and kidney complication surveillance, with examination findings and testing guided by current goals of care and patient preference.

Counseled regarding tobacco cessation and offered assistance with cessation planning. Continue attention to management of longstanding obstructive sleep apnea.

Follow up after treatment planning and repeat glycemic assessment to evaluate response and tolerability.
