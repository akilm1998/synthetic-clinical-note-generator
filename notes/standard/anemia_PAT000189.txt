Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Lily Peterson is a 50-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of anemia, present for approximately 19 years. She reports intermittent headache but denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor has been noted. No smoking history.

Her medical history is notable for chronic kidney disease for 8 years, inflammatory bowel disease for 11 years, and chronic infection for 7 years. Anemia has not been under an active treatment plan. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

Height 154 cm; weight 58.8 kg; BMI 24.8 kg/m².

Patient appears clinically stable. Pallor is present. Cardiovascular examination is normal without tachycardia. Respiratory examination is normal.

Assessment

1. Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation and chronic kidney disease. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are significantly reduced, with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and reduced TIBC. This pattern favors impaired iron utilization in the setting of chronic disease rather than straightforward iron depletion. Her chronic kidney disease, inflammatory bowel disease, and chronic infection are all potential contributors. She is hemodynamically stable without cardiopulmonary symptoms, although the persistent headache and pallor may be related to the anemia.

2. Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 8 years, likely contributing to impaired erythropoiesis. Current renal function values were not provided.

3. Inflammatory bowel disease, present for approximately 11 years. Ongoing inflammation and possible gastrointestinal blood loss or impaired iron absorption may contribute to the anemia.

4. Chronic infection, present for approximately 7 years, representing an additional potential source of inflammatory suppression of erythropoiesis.

Plan

Obtain or review a repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to further characterize the chronic anemia. Check vitamin B12 and folate levels and obtain renal function testing and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate. Review for evidence of active inflammatory bowel disease, chronic infection, or occult gastrointestinal blood loss.

Given the hemoglobin of 8.9 g/dL and prolonged duration of anemia, coordinate management with the clinician managing her chronic kidney disease and inflammatory bowel disease. Consider hematology or nephrology input regarding additional evaluation and whether an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent or iron replacement is appropriate after review of renal function, inflammatory activity, and iron status. Oral or intravenous iron should be selected based on the overall clinical assessment; the elevated ferritin suggests that empiric iron therapy should not be initiated without further review.

Monitor for worsening fatigue, weakness, dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, or increasing headache. Seek urgent evaluation for new cardiopulmonary symptoms, syncope, or evidence of significant bleeding. Continue outpatient follow-up after the recommended evaluation to reassess symptoms and hemoglobin response.
