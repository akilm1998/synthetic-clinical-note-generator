Initial consultation for chronic kidney disease and markedly reduced renal function.

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal filtration

Seven-year history of chronic kidney disease with creatinine 4.06 mg/dL, BUN 67 mg/dL, and eGFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m². Although he is currently without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, these values indicate advanced chronic renal impairment requiring prompt nephrology-directed evaluation and longitudinal management. The absence of symptoms does not lessen the risk for electrolyte, acid-base, volume, anemia, mineral-bone, and medication-related complications.

2. Hypertension, incompletely controlled by today’s measurement

Blood pressure is 152/88 mmHg. In the setting of significant chronic kidney disease, confirmation with repeat measurements and establishment of an individualized blood pressure target are important. Current antihypertensive regimen was not provided and requires medication reconciliation.

3. Anemia of chronic disease

Longstanding anemia of chronic disease, reported for approximately 29 years. No current hemoglobin or iron studies are available in the provided data. Further characterization is important in the context of advanced kidney disease.

4. Secondary hyperparathyroidism

Longstanding secondary hyperparathyroidism, reported for approximately 26 years, consistent with chronic kidney disease–mineral and bone disorder. Current calcium, phosphorus, alkaline phosphatase, and intact parathyroid hormone values are not provided.

5. Hyperlipidemia

Longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 43 years, contributing to overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

6. Tobacco use

Current smoking, which increases cardiovascular and renal disease risk.

Plan

Prompt nephrology follow-up is recommended for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if kidney function remains at this level or declines further. This should include review of the trajectory of renal function, symptom surveillance, dietary counseling, medication safety, and advance planning appropriate to the patient’s goals and overall health.

Obtain or review a serum electrolyte and renal function panel, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and mineral-bone assessment including calcium, phosphorus, and intact parathyroid hormone. A renal ultrasound is also appropriate if prior structural evaluation is not available.

Perform complete medication reconciliation before initiating or adjusting therapy. Avoid nephrotoxic medications when possible, adjust renally cleared medications, and avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. Management of blood pressure should be individualized after confirming the measurement and reviewing current treatment.

Continue clinical monitoring for nausea, anorexia, pruritus, progressive fatigue, sleep disturbance, muscle cramps, confusion, reduced urine output, edema, or shortness of breath. Urgent evaluation is warranted for severe dyspnea, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, rapidly progressive edema, or substantially decreased urine output.

Address smoking cessation at the next available opportunity and continue cardiovascular risk reduction, including management of hyperlipidemia. Further treatment of anemia and secondary hyperparathyroidism should be guided by updated CBC, iron indices, mineral studies, and parathyroid hormone results.
