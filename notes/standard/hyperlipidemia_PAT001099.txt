Chief Complaint

Annual review and evaluation of markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Autumn West is a 46-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease follow-up. She has a 16-year history of hyperlipidemia that has not been actively managed. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 345 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 233 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 47 mg/dL, and triglycerides 324 mg/dL. She reports no chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, or other cardiovascular prodromal symptoms. Xanthelasma is present; she denies other reported fatty skin deposits.

She has type 2 diabetes mellitus of 8 years’ duration and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of 7 years’ duration. She does not smoke. No current lipid-lowering medication regimen is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 125/78 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 172 cm, weight 74.8 kg, BMI 25.3 kg/m².

Reported physical finding: xanthelasma. No chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness reported.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 345 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 233 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 47 mg/dL, triglycerides 324 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Hyperlipidemia, unspecified, longstanding and currently untreated. Lipid profile demonstrates severe elevation of LDL cholesterol with concomitant hypertriglyceridemia. The presence of xanthelasma further supports prolonged lipid exposure. In the setting of diabetes, this represents a substantially increased long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk despite currently normal blood pressure and absence of anginal or claudication symptoms.

2. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 8-year history. Diabetes significantly increases cardiovascular risk and reinforces the need for aggressive lipid management. Glycemic control should be reviewed as part of chronic disease follow-up.

3. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, 7-year history. This commonly coexists with insulin resistance and dyslipidemia. It is not, by itself, a contraindication to statin therapy, but liver status should be considered when initiating treatment.

4. Overweight by BMI, 25.3 kg/m². Nonsmoker.

Plan

Discussed the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and triglycerides and the associated cardiovascular risk. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after shared decision-making, with medication selection and dosing reviewed at the treatment visit. Given the LDL cholesterol above 190 mg/dL, pharmacologic therapy is indicated independent of calculated risk. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe and further lipid specialist-directed therapy may be considered.

Recommend confirmation with a fasting lipid panel and assessment of treatment response after therapy is initiated. Consider evaluation for contributing secondary factors and measurement of lipoprotein(a) or apolipoprotein B to further characterize risk. Coronary artery calcium scanning is not required before initiating treatment but could be discussed if uncertainty about long-term risk remains after clinician-patient discussion.

Counseled on a Mediterranean-style eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, legumes, whole grains, fish, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and alcohol intake. Encourage regular aerobic and resistance activity as tolerated, with a goal of at least 150 minutes of moderate activity weekly. Maintain weight and avoid further weight gain.

Continue comprehensive diabetes management with attention to nutrition, physical activity, routine glycemic monitoring, kidney risk assessment, and preventive cardiovascular care. Review liver-related monitoring in the context of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and lipid treatment.

Blood pressure is currently well controlled. Continue avoidance of tobacco exposure.

At the annual preventive visit, review immunizations and age-appropriate breast, cervical, and colorectal cancer screening based on prior screening history and current recommendations. Follow up after initiation or adjustment of lipid therapy to review tolerance and repeat lipid results, with ongoing chronic disease follow-up thereafter.
