Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Julia Stone is a 71-year-old woman, 170 cm tall and 72.2 kg, with a 10-year history of chronic kidney disease. She presents for routine outpatient follow-up and reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported today.

Her medical history is also notable for hypertension for 28 years, gout for 16 years, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 10 years. She continues to smoke. Current medication list and adherence were not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 151/94 mmHg, pulse 74/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 170 cm; weight 72.2 kg; BMI 25.0 kg/m².

Recent laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 5.31 mg/dL.
Estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m².
BUN 65 mg/dL.

Review of systems is negative for fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, edema, urinary frequency change, and shortness of breath.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, with markedly reduced renal function on available laboratory testing. Creatinine is 5.31 mg/dL, eGFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 65 mg/dL. Although she is currently asymptomatic, this degree of renal impairment requires prompt specialist assessment and close monitoring. Disease control appears suboptimal, and no active management regimen is documented.

Hypertension, longstanding, with elevated blood pressure today at 151/94 mmHg.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, longstanding.

Gout, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation of advanced chronic kidney disease and discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically indicated. Advise urgent evaluation for new or worsening nausea, vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, progressive itching, muscle cramps, reduced urine output, swelling, chest pain, or shortness of breath.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level to assess complications of chronic kidney disease. Consider renal ultrasound as part of the renal evaluation.

Review and reconcile all current medications at the next contact, with attention to renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications such as NSAIDs. Blood pressure should be monitored closely and treatment adjusted based on the complete medication history and renal evaluation.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and the importance of attending nephrology follow-up. Continue outpatient monitoring with close follow-up after laboratory reassessment or sooner for any concerning symptoms.
