Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Autumn West is a 97-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of longstanding essential hypertension, present for approximately 48 years. She has not had an active blood-pressure management regimen recently. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. She reports intermittent palpitations and fatigue. She does not smoke. Weight is stable at 62.2 kg with BMI 24.9 kg/m².

Relevant medical history includes type 2 diabetes mellitus for 49 years, coronary artery disease for 10 years, and hyperlipidemia for 14 years.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 153/91 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 158 cm; weight 62.2 kg; BMI 24.9 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 143 mmol/L, potassium 4.1 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with elevated office blood pressure at 153/91 mmHg in the setting of longstanding disease and no current active management. She has no symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Given her advanced age, coronary artery disease, diabetes, and reported palpitations and fatigue, blood-pressure reduction should be individualized and undertaken cautiously, with attention to orthostatic symptoms and overall functional status.

Palpitations and fatigue, without associated chest pain, dyspnea, dizziness, or syncope reported. These symptoms warrant further assessment, particularly given her coronary artery disease history.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurements after seated rest and obtain home or assisted blood-pressure readings if feasible to assess the persistent pattern and avoid overtreatment based on a single reading. Review for orthostatic symptoms and monitor closely with any treatment changes.

Discuss initiation of an antihypertensive regimen appropriate for a very elderly patient with coronary artery disease and diabetes, using a low starting dose and gradual adjustment based on repeated readings, tolerance, renal status, and orthostatic blood pressure. Review dietary sodium intake, hydration, and fall-risk precautions.

Recommend electrocardiogram for evaluation of palpitations and screening for ischemic or rhythm-related abnormalities. Advise prompt evaluation for new chest pain, shortness of breath, syncope, focal neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly increased blood pressure.

Continue management of diabetes, coronary artery disease, and hyperlipidemia in coordination with her established care plan. Consider lipid profile, urinalysis, and renal function assessment as part of cardiovascular risk and antihypertensive treatment planning. Sodium and potassium are currently within acceptable ranges.

Follow up after blood-pressure reassessment and cardiac evaluation, sooner for worsening symptoms.
