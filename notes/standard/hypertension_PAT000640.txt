Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Pamela Donovan is a 54-year-old woman with an 18-year history of essential hypertension, currently not under active pharmacologic management. She presents for review of blood pressure control and treatment options. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. No medication regimen is documented for review; consequently, medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be assessed.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease diagnosed 1 year ago, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 14 years, and hyperlipidemia for 16 years. She does not smoke. Weight is stable by provided measurements, with a BMI of 21.8 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure is 152/90 mmHg. Pulse 73 bpm. Respiratory rate 16/min. Temperature 36.8°C. Oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Height 165 cm; weight 59.4 kg; BMI 21.8 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 141 mmol/L, potassium 4.7 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding and presently above goal at 152/90 mmHg. She is asymptomatic without features suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency. The absence of active management is concerning given her concomitant coronary artery disease, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia. Current electrolytes are within expected ranges and do not identify a contraindication to standard antihypertensive therapy.

Coronary artery disease, diagnosed 1 year ago, clinically stable by today’s symptom review.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding for 14 years.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding for 16 years.

Plan

Discussed the importance of consistent blood pressure treatment and risk-factor reduction, particularly in the setting of coronary artery disease and diabetes. Recommend initiating or re-establishing a guideline-directed antihypertensive regimen after review of the complete medication list, prior treatment response, contraindications, and home blood pressure readings. Medication selection should account for her diabetes and coronary artery disease. Review for adverse effects, adherence barriers, and ability to obtain medications at the next visit.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with seated readings recorded consistently and brought to follow-up. Reinforced a low-sodium dietary pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and continued smoking avoidance.

Recommend follow-up blood pressure assessment after treatment is initiated or adjusted, with repeat serum electrolytes as clinically appropriate. Continue coordinated management of coronary artery disease, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia. She was advised to seek urgent evaluation for chest pain, severe headache, acute neurologic symptoms, or shortness of breath.
