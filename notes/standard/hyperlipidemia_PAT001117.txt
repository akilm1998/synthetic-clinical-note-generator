Chief Complaint

Annual review and chronic disease follow-up.

Subjective

Ruby Marshall is a 71-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 41-year history of hyperlipidemia, currently without active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid results show markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with elevated triglycerides and low HDL cholesterol. She reports xanthomas. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness.

She continues to smoke. Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus of 36 years’ duration and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of 12 years’ duration. No medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Height 165 cm, weight 54.7 kg, BMI 20.1 kg/m². Blood pressure 132/81 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Laboratory results: total cholesterol 364 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 277 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 38 mg/dL, triglycerides 246 mg/dL.

Xanthomas are reported. No chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness reported.

Assessment

Severe longstanding hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, elevated triglycerides, and low HDL cholesterol. The presence of xanthomas and 41 years of disease duration indicate a substantial cumulative atherosclerotic risk. Hyperlipidemia is not currently being actively managed.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, increasing overall cardiovascular risk.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Active tobacco use, an additional modifiable cardiovascular risk factor.

Plan

Discussed the substantially elevated cardiovascular risk associated with the current lipid profile, diabetes, smoking, and age. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy unless contraindicated, with shared decision-making regarding benefits, potential adverse effects, and adherence. If LDL cholesterol remains significantly elevated despite tolerated statin therapy, consider addition of ezetimibe and further lipid specialist assessment.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for additional risk assessment, particularly given the severity and longstanding nature of the dyslipidemia. Review for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and ensure ongoing monitoring of liver disease and diabetes with the clinicians managing those conditions.

Counseled on a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fiber, legumes, whole grains, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated.

Strongly advised smoking cessation; offer cessation counseling and pharmacologic support at follow-up.

Continue routine diabetes and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease follow-up. Review immunizations and age-appropriate preventive screening as part of ongoing annual care. Follow up after lipid management is initiated or adjusted to review tolerance, adherence, and response.
