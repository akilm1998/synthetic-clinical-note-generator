Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Audrey Jenkins is a 62-year-old woman seen for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 29 years ago. Diabetes control is currently suboptimal, and she is not receiving an active management regimen per the available history. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, and frequent infections. She reports tingling in the hands or feet, without reported numbness.

She has a 14-year history of dyslipidemia. She does not smoke. Current weight is 73.4 kg at a height of 170 cm, with BMI 25.4 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 125/79 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory data: Hemoglobin A1c 9.3%. Fasting glucose 220 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with markedly elevated fasting glucose and HbA1c indicating inadequate glycemic control. She is not reporting current hyperglycemic or constitutional symptoms, but the degree of biochemical elevation warrants prompt treatment intensification and close follow-up. Tingling in the hands or feet is noted and should be evaluated further for possible peripheral neuropathic involvement, although no formal diabetic complication is established today.

Dyslipidemia, longstanding for approximately 14 years, requiring continued cardiovascular risk assessment and management in the context of diabetes.

BMI 25.4 kg/m², mildly above the healthy range.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of consistent diabetes management. Recommend initiation or re-establishment of a structured diabetes treatment plan, including nutrition counseling focused on portion control, reduction of concentrated carbohydrates, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Pharmacologic therapy should be addressed promptly, with selection based on renal function, cardiovascular risk, tolerability, and patient preference.

Recommend monitoring home glucose readings if a glucometer is available and reviewing results at follow-up. Counsel regarding symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and the need to seek care for persistent worsening symptoms, vomiting, dehydration, confusion, or inability to maintain oral intake.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and a comprehensive diabetes complication evaluation, including foot and retinal assessment. Tingling should be reassessed at follow-up, with attention to sensation and vascular status of the feet. Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management, including review of dyslipidemia treatment and lifestyle measures.

Return for close diabetes follow-up after treatment is initiated or adjusted, with repeat HbA1c monitoring at an appropriate interval.
