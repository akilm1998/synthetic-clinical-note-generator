Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Stella Gray is a 79-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of longstanding essential hypertension, present for approximately 49 years. Blood pressure control has not been actively managed. She reports dizziness but denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. She does not smoke.

Medical history is significant for chronic kidney disease for approximately 22 years, coronary artery disease for 21 years, and hyperlipidemia for 15 years. Current medication regimen and adherence were not provided for review today.

Objective

Height 155 cm; weight 63.2 kg; BMI 26.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 173/96 mmHg. Pulse 73 bpm. Respiratory rate 15/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 98%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 145 mmol/L, potassium 4.9 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported during today’s visit.

Assessment

Longstanding essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure and associated dizziness. No reported chest pain, dyspnea, visual change, severe headache, or other symptoms suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency. Her chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease increase the importance of achieving reliable blood pressure control. Sodium is at the upper end of the provided range and potassium is 4.9 mmol/L.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure after seated rest and confirm readings with home blood pressure monitoring using an appropriately sized cuff. Record readings and symptoms for review.

Arrange prompt clinician follow-up to reconcile current medications and initiate or adjust a consistent antihypertensive treatment plan, taking chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease, dizziness, and potassium level into account. Avoid unsupervised use of over-the-counter decongestants or other agents that may raise blood pressure.

Recommend a lower-sodium eating pattern, regular activity as tolerated, weight maintenance, and continued avoidance of tobacco. Her BMI is 26.3 kg/m².

Recommend evaluation with ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as clinically appropriate for longstanding hypertension and cardiovascular and renal comorbidity. Continue monitoring serum electrolytes and renal function with treatment changes.

Seek urgent medical attention for severe or worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, marked visual change, or persistently very high blood pressure with concerning symptoms.
