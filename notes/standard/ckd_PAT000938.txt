Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Damian Boyd is a 73-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 30 years. He reports ongoing fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. Chronic kidney disease is not currently under active management. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Relevant comorbidities include anemia of chronic disease for approximately 19 years and secondary hyperparathyroidism for approximately 1 year. He does not smoke. Height is 189 cm, weight 102.9 kg, BMI 28.8 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 144/87 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, estimated GFR 8 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 80 mg/dL.

No edema or respiratory symptoms are reported. No additional examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia, in the setting of approximately 30 years of disease. He currently reports fatigue but no nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, fluid retention, urinary change, dyspnea, or sleep disturbance. The current renal indices warrant prompt specialist assessment and clarification of renal replacement planning. Blood pressure is above goal at today’s visit.

Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding, likely contributing to fatigue; current blood counts are not available for review.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago; current mineral and bone parameters are not available.

Overweight by BMI, 28.8 kg/m².

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease, including discussion of renal replacement options and timing, as clinically appropriate.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, intact parathyroid hormone, complete blood count, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and repeat renal function testing. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously completed or if clinically indicated.

Review and address blood pressure control with renal-protective management, using current medication reconciliation before initiating or adjusting therapy. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents, and review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications for renal dosing.

Further evaluate fatigue and chronic anemia with a complete blood count and iron studies; treatment decisions should be guided by results and nephrology input.

Continue monitoring for symptoms of uremia or fluid overload. Seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, marked weakness, rapidly progressive swelling, or substantially reduced urine output.

Return promptly after nephrology assessment and updated laboratory testing to review results and establish an active CKD management plan.
