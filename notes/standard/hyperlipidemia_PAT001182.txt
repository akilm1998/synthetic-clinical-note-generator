Chief Complaint

Annual review and evaluation of longstanding elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Liam Matthews is a 78-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 29-year history of hyperlipidemia that is currently not being actively managed. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or claudication, and dizziness. He reports fatty deposits involving the skin and eyelids, consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma.

Relevant chronic conditions include type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 45 years and obesity for approximately 10 years. He does not smoke. No current lipid-lowering medications were provided for review.

Objective

Height 169 cm; weight 94.8 kg; BMI 33.2 kg/m².

Blood pressure 133/76 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Lipid panel:
Total cholesterol 320 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 232 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 36 mg/dL
Triglycerides 261 mg/dL

Xanthomas and xanthelasma are reported. No chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness reported today.

Assessment

1. Hyperlipidemia, longstanding and substantially uncontrolled. Current results show markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol, with low HDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides. The presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma supports a prolonged burden of dyslipidemia. Although he does not report current cardiovascular symptoms, his age, longstanding diabetes, obesity, and lipid levels place him at high risk for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.

2. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding for approximately 45 years. Chronic diabetes materially increases his cardiovascular risk and is an important consideration in lipid treatment planning. No diabetes laboratory results or medication list were provided today.

3. Obesity, BMI 33.2 kg/m², present for approximately 10 years. This likely contributes to his elevated triglycerides and overall cardiometabolic risk.

4. Preventive health maintenance. Annual review completed with attention to cardiovascular risk reduction and chronic disease care.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the importance of initiating evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy. Recommend prompt shared decision-making regarding a high-intensity statin, taking into account age, diabetes history, overall goals of care, potential adverse effects, and patient preferences. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, consider additional lipid-lowering therapy such as ezetimibe and, if clinically appropriate, specialist-directed therapy.

Recommend confirming the lipid profile with a fasting panel if the current testing was not fasting and assessing for potentially reversible contributors to severe dyslipidemia, including thyroid, renal, hepatic, and glycemic abnormalities. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for additional cardiovascular risk assessment. Coronary artery calcium imaging may be considered if treatment decisions remain uncertain, although the markedly elevated LDL level and long-standing diabetes already provide strong rationale for active therapy.

Counseled on reducing saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates and excess alcohol if applicable, increasing soluble fiber and vegetables, and pursuing gradual weight reduction. Encourage physical activity appropriate to functional status and safety, with gradual progression toward regular moderate exercise.

Recommend ongoing diabetes monitoring and coordination of comprehensive diabetes care, including review of current medications and routine complication screening. Continue blood pressure monitoring.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including new chest pressure, shortness of breath, sudden neurologic symptoms, or leg pain with exertion.

Review immunizations and age-appropriate preventive screening at the next visit, individualized to life expectancy, functional status, and patient preferences.

Follow-up after initiation or adjustment of lipid therapy to assess tolerance and response, with repeat lipid testing at an appropriate interval. Earlier follow-up is recommended to address the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and establish a treatment plan.
