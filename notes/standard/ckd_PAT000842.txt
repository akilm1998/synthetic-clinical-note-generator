Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and abnormal renal function.

Subjective
Frank Montgomery is a 94-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 25 years. He reports fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. He has a 9-year history of hypertension and longstanding anemia of chronic disease. He does not smoke. Weight is 75 kg, height 175 cm, BMI 24.5 kg/m². No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective
Blood pressure 166/91 mmHg, pulse 76/min, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: creatinine 3.76 mg/dL, eGFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 88 mg/dL.

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. The patient has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, fluid overload symptoms, dyspnea, or change in urination. Given the prolonged history and current degree of renal impairment, disease requires prompt reassessment and active management.

Hypertension, currently above goal at 166/91 mmHg, in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Chronic anemia of disease by history, present for approximately 47 years; current degree of anemia is not provided.

Plan
Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive chronic kidney disease management and discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate.

Obtain or review a complete blood count, serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, albumin, intact parathyroid hormone, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and repeat renal function testing. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously performed.

Address blood pressure promptly with medication reconciliation and individualized antihypertensive management; avoid assuming or initiating a regimen without review of current medications, renal function, and orthostatic status.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications for renal dosing and avoid nephrotoxic agents, particularly NSAIDs. Counsel regarding monitoring of fluid intake and dietary sodium without imposing dietary restrictions that could compromise nutrition.

Monitor fatigue and assess anemia with CBC and iron studies; treatment should be guided by results and overall goals of care.

Seek urgent evaluation for new shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, marked weakness, rapidly worsening edema, or substantially decreased urine output.
