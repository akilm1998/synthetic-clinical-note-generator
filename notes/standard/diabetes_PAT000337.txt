Chief Complaint

Medication review for type 2 diabetes.

Subjective

Henry Walsh is a 32-year-old man presenting for follow-up and review of diabetes treatment. He has had type 2 diabetes for approximately 9 years and is not currently actively managing the condition. Current HbA1c is 9.9% and fasting glucose is 228 mg/dL, consistent with above-goal glycemic control. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, unintentional weight loss, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance were reviewed. No active diabetes regimen was identified for assessment of effectiveness or adverse effects. He also has longstanding obesity, obstructive sleep apnea for 2 years, and depression for 2 years. He currently smokes.

Objective

Height 167 cm. Weight 98.4 kg. BMI 35.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 117/79 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.9%. Fasting glucose 228 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. He is clinically asymptomatic today without reported symptoms suggestive of acute hyperglycemia, neuropathy, or infection.

Severe obesity.

Obstructive sleep apnea.

Depression.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed that current glycemic control is significantly above goal and requires active treatment. Reviewed the importance of consistent medication use, nutrition changes, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and home glucose monitoring if available.

Recommend prompt initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy, with medication selection based on renal function, comorbidities, cost, and tolerance. Reviewed potential medication adverse effects and the importance of reporting intolerance rather than discontinuing therapy without follow-up.

Recommend diabetes monitoring with repeat HbA1c after treatment has been established, along with routine assessment of renal function, urine albumin, lipids, and diabetic eye and foot health as clinically appropriate.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and its impact on cardiovascular and diabetes-related risk. Continue management of obesity, sleep apnea, and depression. Follow up promptly to establish an active diabetes treatment plan and reassess response and tolerance.
