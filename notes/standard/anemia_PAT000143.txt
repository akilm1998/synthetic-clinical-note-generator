Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for longstanding anemia.

Subjective

Madelyn Stewart is a 71-year-old woman, 169 cm tall and weighing 72.5 kg, with BMI 25.4 kg/m², presenting for routine follow-up of anemia present for approximately 37 years. She has a history of malignancy of approximately 27 years’ duration. She does not smoke. She currently denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. She has not noticed symptomatic decline since the prior assessment. Pallor remains present.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 124/78 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

She appears clinically stable. Pallor is noted. Cardiovascular examination is normal without tachycardia. Respiratory examination is normal, with no reported respiratory distress.

Laboratory studies show hemoglobin 8.4 g/dL, hematocrit 24.9%, RBC count 2.65 million/µL, and MCV 94 fL, consistent with a significant normocytic anemia. Serum iron is 32 µg/dL, ferritin 264 ng/mL, TIBC 244 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 13.1%. The combination of low serum iron and transferrin saturation with preserved-to-elevated ferritin and reduced TIBC is most consistent with iron-restricted erythropoiesis in the setting of chronic inflammation rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency.

Assessment

Longstanding normocytic anemia consistent with anemia of chronic disease/chronic inflammation. Current hemoglobin remains substantially reduced at 8.4 g/dL, although she is hemodynamically stable and denies cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms. Iron indices demonstrate restricted iron availability with ferritin 264 ng/mL and low TIBC, supporting the clinical impression. Her history of longstanding malignancy is a relevant potential contributor to chronic inflammatory anemia. There is no current evidence of symptomatic tachycardia, hypoxemia, or respiratory compromise.

Plan

Continue outpatient evaluation and management of the underlying chronic inflammatory process, with particular attention to the history of malignancy. Recommend repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to assess marrow response and red-cell morphology. Recommend assessment of vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate to evaluate additional reversible or contributory causes of persistent normocytic anemia.

Given the low transferrin saturation, review for a superimposed component of absolute or functional iron deficiency should be considered before initiating therapy. Iron supplementation, including oral or intravenous iron, should be guided by the broader clinical context and response to evaluation rather than ferritin alone. Erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy may be considered only if clinically appropriate after evaluation of the underlying disease and renal status.

She should seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, marked weakness, or evidence of bleeding. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended to reassess symptoms and hemoglobin trend.
