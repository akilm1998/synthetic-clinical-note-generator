Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Zachary Barnes is a 54-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 18 years ago. Blood pressure control has not been adequately managed. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. No acute hypertensive symptoms reported.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease for approximately 7 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 14 years. He does not smoke. Current weight is 73.4 kg at 168 cm, with BMI 26.0 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 159/92 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Laboratory data: Sodium 136 mmol/L, potassium 3.5 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure at 159/92 mmHg. Patient is asymptomatic without findings suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency. Long duration of hypertension and coexisting coronary artery disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus increase overall cardiovascular risk. Current control is inadequate and requires active outpatient management.

Coronary artery disease, approximately 7-year history, without current chest pain or dyspnea.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 14-year history.

Potassium is at the low end of the normal range; sodium is within the expected range.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement during the visit if feasible and begin home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, including timing and associated readings. Review technique and confirm persistent elevation with multiple readings rather than relying on a single measurement.

Discuss prompt initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy, taking his coronary artery disease and diabetes into account. Medication selection and dosing should be reviewed at follow-up, with monitoring of renal function and electrolytes after treatment changes. Do not self-adjust or discontinue any prescribed therapy without medical guidance.

Counseled on limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, moderating alcohol intake if applicable, and continuing weight control. Continue smoking avoidance.

Recommend routine cardiovascular risk-factor management in the setting of coronary artery disease and diabetes, including review of glycemic control and lipid management at subsequent visits. Consider ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of ongoing hypertension and cardiovascular risk assessment if not recently completed.

Seek urgent medical attention for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to reassess blood pressure control and review the home log.
