Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Sadie Hunter is a 49-year-old woman with a 12-year history of anemia, inflammatory bowel disease for 18 years, chronic kidney disease for 5 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 14 years. She has not been under active treatment for the anemia. She denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. She reports headache. She does not report pallor.

Medication review focused on anemia-directed therapy. No active oral iron, intravenous iron, or erythropoiesis-stimulating agent regimen is identified for effectiveness, adherence, or tolerance assessment. She continues to smoke. Height is 150 cm, weight 57.8 kg, BMI 25.7 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 129/80 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.5 g/dL
Hematocrit 26.2%
RBC 3.01 million/µL
MCV 87 fL
Serum iron 32 µg/dL
Ferritin 447 ng/mL
TIBC 266 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 12.0%

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic disease, with likely contributions from chronic kidney disease and inflammatory bowel disease. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are significantly reduced, with a normal MCV. Low serum iron and transferrin saturation in the setting of elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC support inflammatory iron sequestration or functional iron deficiency rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Her chronic kidney disease may also be contributing through impaired erythropoietin response.

She is currently hemodynamically stable and denies cardiopulmonary symptoms. Headache may occur in the setting of significant anemia, although no acute neurologic or cardiopulmonary features are reported. There is no current evidence from the provided history of symptomatic decompensation.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic inflammatory bowel disease are relevant comorbid conditions that may affect inflammatory burden and anemia management. Ongoing tobacco use is an additional modifiable health risk.

Plan

Discussed that the current laboratory pattern warrants active evaluation and treatment rather than observation alone. Clarify all current prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use, including any oral iron, intravenous iron, or erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy, with specific review of adherence, gastrointestinal tolerance, constipation, nausea, and perceived benefit.

Recommend repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the anemia. Assess inflammatory bowel disease activity and potential chronic gastrointestinal blood loss as clinically indicated. Review renal contribution to anemia and consider coordination with the clinician managing her chronic kidney disease regarding appropriateness of iron replacement and possible erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy. Iron treatment should be individualized because ferritin is elevated despite low transferrin saturation.

Monitor for worsening fatigue, weakness, dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, or other signs of reduced oxygen delivery; urgent evaluation is warranted if these develop. Encourage smoking cessation. Further management should be guided by the medication reconciliation, repeat hematologic assessment, renal function, and inflammatory disease activity.
