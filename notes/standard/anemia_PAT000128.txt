Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Julia Stone is a 56-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 21 years. She reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor. There is no reported interval worsening of symptoms. Her anemia has not been under active management.

Her medical history is notable for chronic kidney disease for 14 years, malignancy for 16 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 4 years. She does not smoke. Current weight is 70.6 kg and height is 173 cm, with BMI 23.6 kg/m². No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 128/88 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Laboratory results reviewed:
Hemoglobin 8.9 g/dL
Hematocrit 26.2%
RBC 3.23 million/µL
MCV 81 fL
Serum iron 32 µg/dL
Ferritin 192 ng/mL
TIBC 227 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 14.1%

The results demonstrate persistent anemia with a low-normal MCV, reduced serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated iron storage relative to the serum iron level, and low TIBC. This pattern is most consistent with anemia associated with chronic inflammation and chronic kidney disease, with possible functional iron restriction.

Assessment

Chronic anemia, clinically stable and currently asymptomatic. Laboratory findings are compatible with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation in the setting of longstanding chronic kidney disease and malignancy. The hemoglobin remains significantly reduced at 8.9 g/dL. Low transferrin saturation and low serum iron suggest limited iron availability for erythropoiesis, while the ferritin and TIBC pattern is less typical of isolated absolute iron deficiency. There is no evidence from the provided vital signs or symptom review of current cardiopulmonary compromise.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, without diabetes-related laboratory data provided today.

Plan

Discussed the laboratory pattern and the likely contribution of chronic kidney disease and malignancy-related inflammation to the anemia. Given the degree and chronicity of anemia, recommend active evaluation and management rather than continued observation alone.

Repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear is recommended to assess ongoing production and stability. Renal function testing and inflammatory markers should be reviewed or obtained as clinically appropriate. Vitamin B12 and folate testing may be considered to exclude additional reversible contributors. Given the low transferrin saturation, assess for functional or absolute iron deficiency before selecting oral or intravenous iron therapy.

Recommend coordination of anemia management with the clinicians managing her chronic kidney disease and malignancy. An erythropoiesis-stimulating agent or iron replacement should be considered only after review of renal function, malignancy status, iron studies, and overall treatment goals. No medication is initiated today based on the information available.

She should seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, marked weakness, or bleeding. Routine follow-up is recommended after repeat laboratory assessment to review hemoglobin trend and establish an appropriate treatment plan.
