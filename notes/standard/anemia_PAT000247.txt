Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for anemia.

Subjective

Sophia Turner is an 81-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of anemia identified approximately 3 years ago. She denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, pallor, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms reported today. Her history is significant for rheumatoid arthritis for 16 years and heart failure for 10 years. She currently smokes. Anemia has not been under active management.

Objective

Height 175 cm; weight 86.7 kg; BMI 28.3 kg/m².
Blood pressure 138/81 mmHg, pulse 90 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 9.4 g/dL, hematocrit 28.2%, RBC 3.17 million/µL, MCV 89 fL. Serum iron 36 µg/dL, ferritin 219 ng/mL, TIBC 192 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 18.8%.

Cardiovascular examination is without abnormality provided in the available assessment. Respiratory examination is without abnormality provided.

Assessment

Anemia with moderate reduction in hemoglobin and hematocrit, currently asymptomatic and normocytic. Low serum iron and TIBC with preserved/elevated ferritin are most consistent with anemia associated with chronic inflammation, in the setting of longstanding rheumatoid arthritis and heart failure. Iron-restricted erythropoiesis may coexist; iron studies should be interpreted in the context of inflammation and renal function. No evidence from today’s history of symptomatic cardiopulmonary compromise.

Plan

Obtain or review a repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to assess persistence and marrow response. Recommend evaluation of vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers, as clinically appropriate, to further characterize the anemia and identify reversible contributors.

Continue assessment and treatment of underlying rheumatoid arthritis and heart failure with the clinicians managing those conditions. Review for occult blood loss and other sources of iron deficiency if anemia worsens or fails to improve; consider gastrointestinal evaluation based on clinical judgment and overall goals of care.

No empiric iron, erythropoietin-stimulating agent, or intravenous iron is initiated from the information available today. Encourage smoking cessation. Advise urgent evaluation for new shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, sustained palpitations, marked weakness, or rapid clinical decline. Arrange close outpatient reassessment after repeat laboratory evaluation.
