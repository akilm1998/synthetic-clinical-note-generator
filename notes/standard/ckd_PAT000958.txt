Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Jonathan Gray is a 57-year-old man with an approximately 18-year history of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. He presents for outpatient medication review. He reports fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. He does not smoke. Weight is 63.1 kg at 169 cm, with BMI 22.1 kg/m².

No specific medication regimen is identified for review today. Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance should be assessed against a complete current medication list, with particular attention to renal dosing and cardiovascular risk reduction.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 137/90 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 3.29 mg/dL, estimated GFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 94 mg/dL.

No peripheral edema or respiratory symptoms are reported. No additional examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine, present for approximately 18 years. The patient has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, change in urination, edema, or dyspnea to suggest symptomatic fluid overload or prominent uremic manifestations at this visit. Current management requires structured medication reconciliation, renal dosing review, monitoring for complications, and specialty coordination.

Coronary artery disease, present for approximately 2 years. Cardiovascular risk remains clinically important in the setting of advanced renal impairment.

Blood pressure is 137/90 mmHg, with mildly elevated diastolic pressure. Medication effectiveness cannot be determined without confirmation of the active regimen and home blood pressure pattern.

Plan

Complete medication reconciliation, including prescription medications, over-the-counter agents, and supplements. Assess adherence, therapeutic response, and adverse effects for each medication before making adjustments. Confirm that all medications are dosed appropriately for the current renal function and avoid nephrotoxic agents, particularly nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, unless specifically directed by a treating clinician.

Arrange nephrology follow-up for management of significantly reduced kidney function and planning of ongoing renal care. Recommend monitoring with a serum electrolytes panel, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone; consider renal ultrasound if not previously evaluated. Review for anemia, mineral and bone complications, electrolyte abnormalities, and progression based on serial results.

Continue cardiovascular risk management in coordination with the treating clinician, including individualized blood pressure control and review of indicated coronary artery disease therapies. Encourage a low-sodium diet, avoidance of dehydration, and avoidance of unapproved supplements. Counsel the patient to seek prompt care for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, persistent vomiting, confusion, marked reduction in urine output, or chest pain.

Follow up after medication reconciliation and repeat kidney-related monitoring to reassess fatigue, blood pressure, medication tolerance, and overall renal management.
