Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Ava Mitchell is a 60-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 17 years and currently without active management. Recent lipid results remain markedly elevated. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness. She reports fatty deposits on the skin consistent with xanthomas. No xanthelasma is reported.

Her history is also significant for coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 14 years ago and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 4 years. She continues to smoke. No current lipid-lowering medication regimen is provided. Discussion today focused on the significance of her lipid levels in the setting of known coronary artery disease and tobacco use.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 133/81 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 163 cm, weight 75.7 kg, BMI 28.5 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Total cholesterol 343 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 240 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 44 mg/dL, triglycerides 296 mg/dL.

Xanthomas are reported. No chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness reported. No xanthelasma reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severely elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, currently inadequately controlled after approximately 17 years. The lipid pattern is concerning for a significant atherogenic burden, particularly in the setting of established coronary artery disease and ongoing tobacco use. Reported xanthomas further support longstanding lipid elevation.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding by history, without current chest discomfort or other reported ischemic symptoms.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, present for approximately 4 years.

Overweight, BMI 28.5 kg/m².

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Strongly recommend initiation of evidence-based lipid-lowering therapy, with a high-intensity statin discussed as first-line treatment given the LDL level and known coronary artery disease. Review of prior medication exposure, tolerance, contraindications, and patient preferences is needed before prescribing. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe and consideration of specialist-directed therapy may be appropriate.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, lean proteins, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol. Encourage gradual increase in regular physical activity as tolerated and weight reduction toward a healthier range.

Recommend smoking cessation; counseling and pharmacologic support options should be offered.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment is initiated or adjusted to assess response. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for additional cardiovascular risk characterization. Given the history of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and anticipated lipid therapy, obtain appropriate baseline and follow-up liver assessment as clinically indicated.

Continue monitoring for symptoms of ischemia. Seek urgent medical attention for new or persistent chest pain, shortness of breath, diaphoresis, syncope, or other concerning cardiovascular symptoms. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended to establish and monitor an active lipid-management plan.
