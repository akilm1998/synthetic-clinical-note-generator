Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for diabetes and abnormal blood glucose results.

Subjective

Robert Collins is a 71-year-old man with a 39-year history of type 2 diabetes presenting for focused follow-up of markedly elevated glucose values. Diabetes has not been actively managed. Recent HbA1c is 9.2% and fasting glucose is 228 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Relevant history includes dyslipidemia for 1 year, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 32 years, and anxiety for 20 years. He currently smokes. No diabetes medications are documented.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 124/80 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respirations 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 172 cm, weight 65.4 kg, BMI 22.1 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.2%; fasting glucose 228 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently significantly above goal based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. Patient is asymptomatic without reported polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, wounds, or recurrent infections. No evidence of acute metabolic decompensation based on current history and vital signs.

2. Dyslipidemia, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago.

3. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

4. Anxiety, longstanding.

5. Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the need for prompt re-engagement in diabetes care. Review treatment options and initiate an individualized glucose-lowering regimen at the earliest opportunity, taking renal and hepatic status into account. Encourage consistent meal planning, reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, and regular activity as tolerated.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with documentation of fasting and occasional postprandial readings for review. Obtain renal function, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and fasting lipid profile as part of diabetes risk assessment; arrange routine retinal and comprehensive foot evaluations. Reinforced daily foot inspection and prompt reporting of wounds or infection.

Discussed smoking cessation and cardiovascular risk reduction. Continue follow-up for dyslipidemia, fatty liver disease, and anxiety. Close outpatient follow-up recommended to review glucose readings and treatment response; seek urgent care for vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, severe weakness, inability to maintain oral intake, or persistently very high glucose readings.
