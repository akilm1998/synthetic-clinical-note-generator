Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Erica Chapman is a 44-year-old woman with a 10-year history of essential hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus for 6 years, and chronic kidney disease for 3 years. She presents for review of blood pressure control, adherence, and treatment tolerance. Her current blood pressure regimen and recent medication-taking pattern require clarification during reconciliation. She reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis.

She currently smokes. Weight is 82.1 kg at a height of 177 cm, with BMI 26.2 kg/m². Her hypertension has not been under active management. Dietary sodium intake, physical activity, and alcohol exposure should be reviewed as modifiable contributors to poor control.

Objective

Blood pressure is 159/93 mmHg with pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, and oxygen saturation 97% on room air. Serum sodium is 138 mmol/L and potassium is 5.1 mmol/L. No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms are reported today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and inadequate current control in the setting of longstanding disease, type 2 diabetes, and chronic kidney disease. Her dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue are nonspecific and occur without symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency. The potassium of 5.1 mmol/L is mildly elevated and is clinically relevant when selecting or adjusting antihypertensive therapy, particularly agents that may further increase potassium.

Medication effectiveness cannot be determined until the complete current medication list, dosing, adherence, and tolerance are confirmed. There is no evidence from today’s history of hypertensive crisis.

Plan

Complete medication reconciliation, including confirmation of any antihypertensive agents being taken, dosing schedule, missed doses, adverse effects, and barriers to adherence. Reinforce consistent daily administration if therapy is prescribed and review home blood pressure technique and readings. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log for review.

Given the current reading and comorbid diabetes and chronic kidney disease, antihypertensive therapy should be actively re-established or adjusted after medication reconciliation. Review renal function and repeat serum electrolytes before selecting or intensifying therapy, with particular caution regarding agents that can raise serum potassium. Obtain an ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as clinically appropriate for cardiovascular and renal risk assessment.

Counsel regarding sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight maintenance, and smoking cessation. Advise urgent evaluation for severe or worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with acute symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to reassess blood pressure, symptoms, adherence, tolerance, potassium, and response to treatment.
