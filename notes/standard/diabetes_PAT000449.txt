Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Mason Russell is an 88-year-old male presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 15 years ago. Diabetes control has not been actively managed recently. Most recent HbA1c is 9.8% and fasting glucose is 225 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has longstanding obesity of approximately 57 years, hypertension for approximately 30 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 33 years. He continues to smoke. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 153/91 mmHg, pulse 84/min, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 172 cm. Weight 107.4 kg. BMI 36.3 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.8%. Fasting glucose 225 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently significantly above goal based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. Despite marked hyperglycemia, he reports no polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, or infectious complications.

Hypertension with elevated office blood pressure today at 153/91 mmHg.

Severe obesity, BMI 36.3 kg/m², contributing to diabetes and cardiovascular risk.

Longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of re-establishing active diabetes management. Review and initiate or intensify glucose-lowering therapy at the next medication reconciliation, taking into account his advanced age, renal function, nutritional status, hypoglycemia risk, and treatment preferences. Reinforced consistent meal planning, reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, and gradual physical activity as tolerated.

Recommend monitoring home glucose readings if feasible, particularly during medication changes, and review readings at follow-up. Obtain renal assessment, including serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, before selecting or adjusting therapy. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response. Recommend routine diabetic eye evaluation and comprehensive foot examination; advised prompt evaluation for new vision changes, foot wounds, numbness, or symptoms of marked hyperglycemia.

Blood pressure remains elevated. Recommend repeat measurements outside the office if possible and medication reconciliation with consideration of adjustment based on confirmed readings, renal function, orthostatic symptoms, and overall goals of care.

Counseled regarding weight reduction through a sustainable calorie-controlled diet and activity appropriate for his functional capacity. Smoking cessation was strongly encouraged, with discussion that cessation would reduce cardiovascular and diabetes-related risk.

Follow-up after medication and glucose review, sooner for worsening hyperglycemic symptoms, inability to maintain oral intake, confusion, chest pain, or shortness of breath.
