Chief Complaint

Annual review of chronic medical conditions, with focus on longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Blake Hunter is an 82-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 34-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and is not currently documented as receiving active diabetes management. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet.

Relevant comorbidities include dyslipidemia for 21 years, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 10 years, and hypertension for 3 years. He does not smoke.

Objective

Height 177 cm. Weight 89.9 kg. BMI 28.7 kg/m².

Blood pressure 141/86 mmHg. Pulse 71 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 36.8°C. Oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.5%. Fasting glucose 214 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications. Glycemic control is above goal based on HbA1c of 9.5% and fasting glucose of 214 mg/dL, despite the absence of reported hyperglycemic symptoms. Given the 34-year duration of diabetes and current degree of hyperglycemia, he is at increased risk for microvascular and cardiovascular complications, although no complications are documented today.

Hypertension with mildly elevated office blood pressure at 141/86 mmHg.

Longstanding dyslipidemia.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Overweight, BMI 28.7 kg/m².

Advanced age, requiring individualized treatment goals and attention to hypoglycemia risk, renal function, functional status, and overall treatment burden.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of re-establishing active diabetes management. Review dietary pattern, carbohydrate intake, physical activity, home glucose monitoring if appropriate, barriers to care, and patient preferences. Pharmacologic treatment should be considered and selected after review of renal function, hepatic status, current medications, contraindications, and hypoglycemia risk. Avoid overly aggressive glucose lowering in view of age, with individualized targets and emphasis on safety.

Obtain renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before selecting or adjusting diabetes therapy. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response. Review for symptoms of hypoglycemia at each visit.

Recommend comprehensive foot assessment, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensory testing, and arrange a dilated retinal examination if not recently completed. Counsel regarding daily foot care, appropriate footwear, and prompt evaluation of wounds or infection. Encourage routine dental care and vaccination review as part of preventive care.

Continue cardiovascular risk reduction through nutrition counseling, regular activity appropriate to his abilities, weight management, and review of lipid and blood pressure treatment needs. A fasting lipid profile and medication reconciliation are appropriate to guide management of longstanding dyslipidemia and hypertension.

Monitor blood pressure on follow-up and reinforce a lower-sodium, heart-healthy dietary pattern. Review the history and current management of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease when assessing medication choices.

Follow up after completion of the recommended evaluation to establish a practical diabetes treatment plan and reassess glycemic control, blood pressure, tolerability, and functional status.
