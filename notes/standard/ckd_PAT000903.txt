Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Christopher Perry is a 59-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, reported duration approximately 23 years. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. No smoking history. Weight is 74.6 kg at 185 cm, with BMI 21.8 kg/m².

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease of approximately 14 years’ duration and hyperlipidemia of approximately 13 years’ duration. No interval disease-related concerns are reported today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 134/83 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: Serum creatinine 3.22 mg/dL, BUN 60 mg/dL, estimated GFR 20 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with significantly reduced renal function and elevated creatinine and BUN. He is currently clinically without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms. Blood pressure is acceptable at today’s visit. Given the long duration of kidney disease and current renal indices, he requires structured renal monitoring and active risk-reduction management.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding. No chest pain or shortness of breath reported today.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the importance of ongoing kidney-focused care despite the absence of symptoms. Recommend nephrology referral for evaluation and longitudinal management, including review of renal trajectory and preparation for potential future complications of advanced renal dysfunction.

Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum electrolyte panel, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone level to assess for common complications of chronic kidney disease. Renal ultrasound is also recommended if not previously performed to evaluate renal structure and exclude obstructive or other structural disease.

Monitor blood pressure regularly and continue dietary measures appropriate for kidney disease, including avoidance of excess sodium. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications unless specifically approved by a clinician. Review all prescribed and over-the-counter medications for renal dosing at the next visit.

Counseled to seek prompt care for new or worsening shortness of breath, leg swelling, marked reduction in urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.

Continue follow-up for coronary artery disease and hyperlipidemia with attention to cardiovascular risk reduction. Return for outpatient follow-up after recommended renal evaluation and laboratory monitoring.
