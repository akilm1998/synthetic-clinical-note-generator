Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Rachel Graham is a 98-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of longstanding anemia, present for approximately 68 years. She reports no current fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, headache, palpitations, shortness of breath, or reduced exercise tolerance. She does not note pallor. No smoking history. Her chronic medical history is notable for inflammatory bowel disease for 28 years and heart failure for 7 years. Current anemia management is not established.

Objective

Height 162 cm, weight 73.5 kg, BMI 28.0 kg/m². Blood pressure 135/75 mmHg, pulse 85 bpm, respiratory rate 24/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent laboratory results: hemoglobin 8.1 g/dL, hematocrit 24.8%, RBC count 2.67 million/µL, MCV 93 fL, serum iron 38 µg/dL, ferritin 295 ng/mL, TIBC 217 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 17.5%.

Assessment

Chronic significant normocytic anemia, asymptomatic by current history. The low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and reduced TIBC are most consistent with anemia associated with chronic inflammation, particularly in the setting of longstanding inflammatory bowel disease. Heart failure and advanced age may further reduce physiologic reserve. Hemoglobin is substantially reduced despite the absence of reported cardiopulmonary symptoms. Respiratory rate is mildly elevated at 24/min, with normal oxygen saturation.

Plan

Discussed laboratory pattern and the importance of evaluating the persistent anemia despite minimal symptoms. Recommend repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, along with vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers to further characterize contributing factors. Recommend review for ongoing gastrointestinal inflammation or occult blood loss in coordination with the clinician managing her inflammatory bowel disease.

Given hemoglobin of 8.1 g/dL and her history of heart failure, recommend timely evaluation and consideration of hematology consultation for treatment options, including assessment of whether iron replacement or an erythropoiesis-stimulating approach is appropriate. Any iron therapy should be guided by the overall inflammatory and renal assessment rather than ferritin alone. Continue monitoring for new fatigue, weakness, dizziness, chest discomfort, palpitations, dyspnea, syncope, or declining exercise tolerance; seek urgent evaluation if these occur. Arrange close outpatient reassessment after the recommended evaluation.
