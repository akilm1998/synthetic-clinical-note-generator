Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease, assessment of renal replacement therapy needs, and discussion of advance planning if appropriate. This should not be delayed given the creatinine of 5.1 mg/dL, BUN of 78 mg/dL, and eGFR of 12 mL/min/1.73 m².

Obtain updated renal and metabolic assessment, including serum electrolytes, repeat renal function testing, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Consider renal ultrasound to evaluate renal structure and exclude potentially reversible obstructive or structural disease.

Review and optimize blood pressure and diabetes management with attention to renal safety. Medication reconciliation is needed before initiating or adjusting therapy; avoid nephrotoxic over-the-counter medications, particularly nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, unless specifically directed by a clinician.

Evaluate and manage the known anemia of chronic disease after obtaining a current complete blood count and iron-related assessment as clinically indicated. Fatigue, worsening weakness, chest discomfort, increasing shortness of breath, confusion, persistent vomiting, new swelling, or markedly reduced urine output should prompt urgent or emergency evaluation.

Counseled regarding a kidney-conscious diet with attention to sodium restriction and individualized potassium, phosphorus, and protein recommendations after review of electrolytes and renal nutrition needs. Continue monitoring blood pressure and symptoms closely.

Follow up promptly after the recommended renal evaluation and laboratory testing, or sooner for any worsening symptoms.
