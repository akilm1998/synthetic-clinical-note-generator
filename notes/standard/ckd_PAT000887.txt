Chief Complaint

Annual review and chronic kidney disease follow-up.

Subjective

Maxwell Knight is a 78-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 30-year history of chronic kidney disease and has not been receiving active management. He reports persistent fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath.

History is also notable for anemia of chronic disease for 29 years and gout for 21 years. He does not smoke. Current medication list and allergies were not provided and should be reviewed carefully, particularly for nephrotoxic medications and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug use.

Objective

Height 165 cm; weight 68.3 kg; BMI 25.1 kg/m².

Blood pressure 150/85 mmHg, pulse 78/min, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m²
BUN 93 mg/dL

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of approximately 30 years’ duration with markedly reduced renal function. Creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 93 mg/dL, and eGFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m² indicate significant renal impairment. Fatigue may reflect chronic kidney disease and his longstanding anemia of chronic disease. He currently reports no nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, urinary change, or dyspnea to suggest symptomatic fluid overload or prominent uremic prodrome.

Blood pressure is elevated at 150/85 mmHg, an important modifiable contributor to renal and cardiovascular risk. No current antihypertensive or renal-specific medication regimen is documented.

Longstanding gout, with attention to renal-safe management and avoidance of nephrotoxic therapies.

Preventive care and general health maintenance reviewed in the context of age and chronic kidney disease.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease, assessment for complications, and discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if clinically appropriate. This should not be delayed given the eGFR of 10 mL/min/1.73 m² and BUN of 93 mg/dL.

Obtain updated renal and complication assessment as clinically indicated, including serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound. Results should guide management of anemia, mineral-bone disorder, electrolyte abnormalities, and renal structural disease.

Review and reconcile all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a treating clinician. Review renal dosing for all medications, including any treatment used for gout.

Address blood pressure promptly with confirmation of readings and institution or adjustment of kidney-appropriate therapy by the treating clinician. Encourage moderate sodium restriction and avoidance of dehydration.

Monitor for urgent symptoms, including worsening shortness of breath, new edema, markedly reduced urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness; seek urgent medical attention if these occur.

Continue surveillance and management of longstanding anemia of chronic disease through nephrology/primary care, with treatment decisions based on updated CBC and iron assessment.

Review age-appropriate immunizations, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, and shingles vaccination as appropriate, and ensure preventive cancer screening is individualized to overall health and life expectancy.

Follow up promptly after nephrology assessment and updated laboratory testing; maintain routine primary care follow-up for blood pressure, gout, preventive care, and coordination of chronic disease management.
