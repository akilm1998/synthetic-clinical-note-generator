Chief Complaint

Annual review of diabetes and preventive health.

Subjective

Nicholas Powell is a 31-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 7-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and has not been actively managing the condition. Recent laboratory testing shows HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 233 mg/dL.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No current diabetic complications are reported.

Comorbidities include dyslipidemia diagnosed approximately 1 year ago and depression with a 13-year history. Smoking status: never smoker. Current medication use, allergies, and family history were not provided.

Objective

Height 180 cm; weight 79.4 kg; BMI 24.5 kg/m².

Blood pressure 120/78 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.9%; fasting glucose 233 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently significantly above glycemic target based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. Patient is asymptomatic despite marked hyperglycemia. Seven-year disease duration warrants assessment for renal, retinal, neurologic, and cardiovascular risk complications.

Dyslipidemia, diagnosed 1 year ago; current lipid values are not available for review.

Depression, longstanding. No mood symptoms were reported during today’s review.

Preventive health maintenance review.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the increased risk of microvascular and cardiovascular complications. Recommend prompt initiation or re-establishment of structured diabetes management, including nutrition counseling, regular physical activity, home glucose monitoring as clinically appropriate, and review of pharmacologic therapy. Metformin would generally be a reasonable first-line option if renal function and other contraindications permit; treatment should be finalized after medication reconciliation and review of kidney function.

Obtain renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. Recommend fasting lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment and treatment planning. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval to assess response to treatment.

Recommend comprehensive dilated retinal examination and routine diabetic foot examination, including assessment of skin integrity, sensation, and peripheral pulses. Counsel regarding daily foot inspection, appropriate footwear, and prompt evaluation of wounds or infection.

Encouraged balanced carbohydrate-conscious nutrition, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, regular aerobic and resistance activity, and maintenance of his current healthy BMI. Continue to avoid tobacco.

Review dyslipidemia management after lipid results and overall cardiovascular risk assessment.

Screen depression symptoms and suicide risk at follow-up or sooner if mood has worsened; offer mental health support as clinically indicated.

Review immunization status, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, hepatitis B, and routine adult vaccinations, and update as appropriate. Encourage routine dental care and age-appropriate preventive screening.

Follow up promptly after laboratory evaluation to establish a specific diabetes treatment plan; earlier assessment advised for polyuria, polydipsia, vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, or other symptoms of significant hyperglycemia.
