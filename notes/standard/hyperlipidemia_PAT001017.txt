Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Emma Carter is an 81-year-old woman seen for follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 30 years and currently untreated. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She continues to smoke. She has longstanding obesity, present for approximately 41 years. No medication regimen for lipid management is reported.

Objective

Height 178 cm; weight 120.4 kg; BMI 38.0 kg/m². Blood pressure 136/75 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Recent lipid panel: total cholesterol 352 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 259 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 38 mg/dL, triglycerides 273 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, longstanding and currently without pharmacologic treatment. The degree of LDL elevation confers substantial atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk despite the absence of current chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness.

Obesity, BMI 38.0 kg/m², longstanding.

Active tobacco use, contributing to overall cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the lipid results and the importance of treatment. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of a high-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, taking into account her age, overall health, medication preferences, and potential contraindications. If LDL remains significantly elevated despite maximally tolerated statin therapy, addition of ezetimibe or referral for further lipid management may be considered.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern with reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess calories; increased intake of vegetables, legumes, whole grains, and fiber; and gradual activity as tolerated. Weight reduction was encouraged.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered assistance with cessation planning.

Recommend evaluation for secondary contributors to severe dyslipidemia and consideration of additional risk assessment, including ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement when clinically appropriate. Repeat fasting lipid testing after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response and adherence.

Follow up in approximately 6–12 weeks after treatment discussion or sooner for new chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, or other concerning symptoms.
