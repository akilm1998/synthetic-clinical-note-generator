Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for management of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Nicholas Powell is a 38-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 5 years ago and currently without an established treatment regimen. Recent laboratory testing shows HbA1c 9.5% and fasting glucose 232 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet.

He has a 7-year history of anxiety. He currently smokes. No diabetes-related complications are reported. There is no reported history of hypoglycemic symptoms.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 121/75 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 182 cm, weight 76.5 kg, BMI 23.1 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.5%. Fasting glucose 232 mg/dL.

General: Well-nourished adult male by anthropometric measurements, without reported acute distress.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, diagnosed approximately 5 years ago, currently significantly above glycemic goal based on HbA1c of 9.5% and fasting glucose of 232 mg/dL. Despite the degree of hyperglycemia, he denies osmotic, constitutional, visual, infectious, or neuropathic symptoms. He is hemodynamically stable, with normal blood pressure and BMI in the normal range. There is no current clinical information suggesting an acute metabolic emergency.

Anxiety, longstanding, present for approximately 7 years. This may affect diabetes self-management and will be incorporated into ongoing care.

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose, including the risk of developing renal, retinal, neurologic, and cardiovascular complications over time. Reviewed nutrition, portion control, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, and regular aerobic and resistance activity as tolerated. Because glycemic control is substantially above goal, lifestyle measures alone are unlikely to be sufficient; pharmacologic treatment should be initiated after review of renal function and contraindications. Metformin would be a reasonable first-line option if appropriate, with escalation based on response and tolerability.

Recommend serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and repeat HbA1c for treatment planning and complication risk assessment. Recommend baseline comprehensive foot examination, including skin inspection, pulses, and monofilament sensation, as well as a dilated retinal examination. These evaluations are recommended as part of routine diabetes care.

Reviewed symptoms that should prompt urgent evaluation, including persistent vomiting, abdominal pain, marked weakness, confusion, inability to maintain hydration, or rapidly worsening polyuria and polydipsia.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and offered discussion of cessation strategies at follow-up. Anxiety should be reassessed during ongoing care, with attention to its impact on adherence, sleep, diet, and activity.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after completion of recommended baseline testing to review results, finalize medication therapy, and establish individualized glycemic targets.
