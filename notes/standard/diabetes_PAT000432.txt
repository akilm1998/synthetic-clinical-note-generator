Chief Complaint
Initial outpatient consultation for management of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective
Rachel Graham is an 87-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 3 years ago. Current glycemic control is poor, with HbA1c 9.4% and fasting glucose 251 mg/dL. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Her diabetes has not been actively managed. No current diabetes medications are provided in the available history. She has chronic kidney disease of 21 years’ duration and depression of 37 years’ duration. She does not smoke. No additional medication, allergy, family, or surgical history is provided.

Objective
Vital signs: Blood pressure 138/78 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Height 158 cm, weight 60.4 kg, BMI 24.2 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.4%. Fasting glucose 251 mg/dL.

No acute hyperglycemic symptoms are reported. No diabetic foot or neurologic examination findings are provided today.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently markedly above individualized glycemic target based on HbA1c 9.4% and fasting glucose 251 mg/dL. Although she denies classic hyperglycemic symptoms, the laboratory values indicate significant hyperglycemia. She is 87 years old with longstanding chronic kidney disease, so treatment intensity and medication selection should be individualized with attention to renal function, hypoglycemia risk, functional status, nutritional intake, and overall goals of care.

Chronic kidney disease, reported duration 21 years. The degree of renal impairment is not defined in the available information and will be important in selecting and dosing diabetes therapy.

Depression, reported duration 37 years. Current symptom burden and treatment status were not established during this diabetes-focused visit.

Plan
Obtain a complete medication reconciliation, including any current or recently discontinued diabetes therapies, over-the-counter medications, and agents that may affect renal function or glucose control.

Review individualized glycemic goals with the patient, balancing reduction of symptomatic hyperglycemia and diabetes-related complications against hypoglycemia risk in advanced age and chronic kidney disease.

Recommend prompt initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy after review of renal function, current medications, oral intake, cognitive and functional status, and treatment preferences. Metformin, sulfonylureas, SGLT2 inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors, or insulin should not be selected or dosed without confirming kidney function and assessing hypoglycemia risk. Avoid overly aggressive glucose lowering.

Recommend home glucose monitoring as clinically appropriate, with education regarding symptoms of hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia and instructions to seek urgent care for confusion, vomiting, inability to maintain oral intake, severe weakness, or persistently extreme glucose readings.

Recommend renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before finalizing the diabetes regimen. Recommend fasting lipid profile and review of cardiovascular risk factors as part of comprehensive diabetes care.

Perform or arrange a comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including skin integrity, pulses, and sensory testing, and recommend a dilated retinal examination because of the duration and current degree of hyperglycemia.

Counsel regarding consistent carbohydrate intake, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, adequate hydration, and physical activity appropriate to her mobility and safety. Nutrition and activity recommendations should be individualized given her age and chronic kidney disease.

Continue attention to blood pressure and renal-protective care. Coordinate assessment of chronic kidney disease with the clinician managing her renal condition.

Assess current depressive symptoms, functional status, medication adherence, and safety at follow-up, as depression may affect diabetes self-management.

Follow up soon after medication reconciliation and renal laboratory evaluation to establish a safe treatment plan and review home glucose readings.
