Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of hypertension.

Subjective

Hayden Johnston is a 41-year-old man presenting for review of longstanding hypertension, reported for approximately 8 years. He is not currently documented as being on an antihypertensive regimen. Medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance therefore cannot be assessed against an active regimen.

He reports dizziness but denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. He does not smoke. Medical history is also notable for coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease, both present for approximately 1 year.

Objective

Height 178 cm, weight 88.1 kg, BMI 27.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 158/105 mmHg. Pulse 78 bpm. Respiratory rate 17/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 98%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 138 mmol/L, potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, currently inadequately controlled based on today’s markedly elevated office blood pressure. The patient has had hypertension for approximately 8 years and is not documented to have active treatment. Dizziness is present without reported chest pain, dyspnea, visual change, severe headache, or other symptoms suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency.

Coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease increase the importance of prompt blood pressure control and careful selection and monitoring of therapy.

Overweight, BMI 27.8 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the elevated blood pressure and associated cardiovascular and renal risk. Recommend prompt initiation or re-establishment of clinician-directed antihypertensive therapy, with medication selection tailored to his coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease. Review medication adherence and tolerance at the next visit; he should bring all current medications and home blood pressure readings.

Recommend repeat blood pressure measurement after a period of rest and regular home monitoring, with documentation of readings. Recommend assessment of renal function and urine testing before or soon after treatment initiation, as well as an ECG and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk assessment.

Counseled on reducing dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, and pursuing gradual weight reduction. Avoid over-the-counter agents that may raise blood pressure unless reviewed with a clinician.

He was advised to seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe or new headache, neurologic symptoms, vision loss, or persistently worsening blood pressure. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning.
