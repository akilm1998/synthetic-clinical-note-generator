Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Marcus Cross is a 45-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 2 years and currently without an established treatment regimen. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, and dizziness. He reports fatty skin deposits and yellowish plaques around the eyelids consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma.

Medical history is significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 9 years, chronic kidney disease for 5 years, and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. He does not smoke. Current weight is 97.1 kg at a height of 183 cm, with BMI 29.0 kg/m².

Recent lipid results reviewed: total cholesterol 300 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 221 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 34 mg/dL, and triglycerides 224 mg/dL.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 129/84 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

General: No acute distress documented.

Skin/eyes: Xanthomas and xanthelasma reported/present.

Cardiopulmonary: No cardiopulmonary symptoms reported today.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, low HDL cholesterol, and elevated triglycerides. The degree of LDL elevation, presence of xanthomas/xanthelasma, and established coronary artery disease place him at high cardiovascular risk. Additional risk is conferred by type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, and overweight status. Lipid disorder has not been actively managed over the approximately 2-year duration. No current chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the importance of active risk-factor management given his coronary artery disease, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease.

Recommend initiation of a high-intensity statin unless contraindicated, with medication selection and dosing reviewed in the context of renal function and comorbidities. If LDL reduction remains inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, consider addition of ezetimibe and further lipid-specialist-directed therapy.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, lean proteins, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated fats, trans fats, processed foods, and refined carbohydrates. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated and continued avoidance of tobacco. Weight reduction from current BMI of 29.0 kg/m² would be beneficial.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and measurement of additional cardiovascular risk markers, including lipoprotein(a) or apolipoprotein B, as clinically appropriate. Coronary artery calcium scanning is not necessary to establish treatment need in the setting of known coronary artery disease.

Continue coordination of cardiovascular, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease care. Return for follow-up after treatment initiation and repeat lipid assessment, or sooner for new chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or other concerning cardiovascular symptoms.
