Chief Complaint

Annual health review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease and hypertension.

Subjective

Maya Russell is a 40-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 3-year history of chronic kidney disease in the setting of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 8 years. She is not currently reporting fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, changes in urination, peripheral edema, or shortness of breath. No acute uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported.

She does not smoke. Height is 155 cm, weight 61.5 kg, and BMI 25.6 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 152/97 mmHg, pulse 69/min, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: Serum creatinine 3.0 mg/dL, BUN 73 mg/dL, estimated GFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m².

No peripheral edema or other examination findings are reported today.

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, documented for approximately 3 years. Creatinine is 3.0 mg/dL, BUN 73 mg/dL, and eGFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m². She is currently asymptomatic without reported pruritus, nausea, cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, dyspnea, or urinary changes. The degree of renal impairment requires prompt reassessment and active renal care, even in the absence of symptoms.

2. Hypertension, longstanding for 8 years, currently above goal at 152/97 mmHg. Poor blood pressure control may contribute to ongoing renal decline. Current antihypertensive regimen was not provided and requires medication reconciliation.

3. Preventive health maintenance. Nonsmoker with BMI in the overweight range. Annual cardiovascular risk reduction and age-appropriate preventive care reviewed.

Plan

Chronic kidney disease: Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation given eGFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m² and elevated BUN. Repeat renal function testing and obtain serum electrolytes, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically indicated. Renal ultrasound is recommended to assess renal structure and exclude obstructive or other structural contributors. Review renal trajectory, urine findings, anemia and mineral-bone parameters with the renal specialist.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications for renal dosing and avoid NSAIDs unless specifically directed. Avoid unnecessary iodinated contrast exposure and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Counsel regarding individualized sodium restriction, avoidance of high-salt processed foods, appropriate hydration, and maintenance of a healthy weight. Seek urgent care for new or worsening shortness of breath, rapidly increasing swelling, markedly reduced urine output, persistent vomiting, confusion, or chest pain.

Hypertension: Confirm blood pressure with repeat office or home measurements and complete medication reconciliation before selecting or adjusting therapy. Recommend close blood pressure follow-up and renal-protective management coordinated with nephrology. Continue lifestyle measures including reduced sodium intake, regular activity as tolerated, and weight maintenance.

Preventive care: Review vaccination status, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, and hepatitis B vaccination as appropriate for advanced kidney disease. Continue age-appropriate cervical, breast, colorectal, and other preventive screening based on prior results and current guidelines. Discuss cardiovascular risk reduction, regular physical activity, balanced nutrition, dental care, and avoidance of tobacco.

Follow-up

Follow up promptly after repeat renal assessment and nephrology consultation, with earlier evaluation for any new uremic or fluid-overload symptoms.
