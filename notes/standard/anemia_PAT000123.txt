Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for anemia.

Subjective

Paige Palmer is a 38-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of anemia present for approximately 10 years and not currently under active management. She denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or awareness of pallor. No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms are reported.

Relevant history includes type 2 diabetes mellitus for 4 years. She currently smokes. Height is 152 cm, weight 55.9 kg, BMI 24.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 121/82 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.2 g/dL
Hematocrit 24.5%
RBC count 3.02 million/µL
MCV 81 fL
Serum iron 23 µg/dL
Ferritin 469 ng/mL
TIBC 227 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 10.1%

Assessment

Anemia of chronic disease/inflammation with significant anemia and an essentially normocytic pattern. Low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and low TIBC support iron-restricted erythropoiesis in the setting of chronic inflammation rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. She is currently hemodynamically stable and denies symptoms of tissue hypoxia, but the hemoglobin of 8.2 g/dL warrants prompt evaluation and active management. The underlying inflammatory or chronic disease process has not been established in the available information. Type 2 diabetes mellitus and tobacco use are relevant comorbid risk factors.

Plan

Arrange prompt repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to confirm persistence and characterize the anemia. Obtain vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as clinically indicated. Evaluate for potential sources of chronic blood loss and for an underlying inflammatory, renal, infectious, autoimmune, or other chronic disease process.

Discuss hematology or primary care referral for expedited evaluation given the degree and duration of anemia. Iron replacement, including oral or intravenous therapy, should be guided by the clinical evaluation and response; elevated ferritin makes empiric iron treatment alone insufficient without clarifying the underlying process. Review emergency precautions, including new shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, palpitations, or rapidly worsening weakness. Encourage smoking cessation and continued diabetes care.
