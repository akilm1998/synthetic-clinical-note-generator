Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Paige Palmer is a 79-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 14 years. She reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No interval symptoms of fluid overload or uremic illness are reported. She does not smoke.

Medical history is also notable for hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 23 years. Current medication list and treatment adherence were not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 139/84 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Height 158 cm; weight 70.6 kg; BMI 28.3 kg/m².

Laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL
BUN 81 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m²

No abnormal physical examination findings were provided. She is currently without reported edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, or other symptoms suggestive of overt volume overload or uremia.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. Despite the absence of reported symptoms, creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 81 mg/dL, and eGFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m² indicate advanced renal impairment requiring prompt specialist assessment and active management. Electrolyte and acid-base status, anemia status, mineral-bone parameters, urine protein burden, and renal structural findings have not been provided.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding for approximately 23 years; current management is not documented.

Overweight by BMI, 28.3 kg/m².

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for advanced chronic kidney disease, assessment of renal replacement therapy needs, and discussion of treatment planning. This should not be deferred solely because she is currently asymptomatic.

Obtain/review a comprehensive metabolic panel including electrolytes and bicarbonate, repeat renal function testing, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate.

Monitor closely for development of nausea, vomiting, worsening fatigue, confusion, pruritus, muscle cramps, reduced urine output, new edema, or shortness of breath. Proceed to the emergency department for acute dyspnea, chest pain, confusion, severe weakness, persistent vomiting, or markedly decreased urine output.

Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents when possible. Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and contrast exposures at the next medication reconciliation. Renal-dose medications and dietary sodium and fluid recommendations should be individualized after review of volume status and laboratory results.

Continue blood pressure monitoring; current reading is 139/84 mmHg. Review cardiovascular risk reduction and lipid management, including current therapy and adherence, at follow-up.

Close follow-up after nephrology assessment and repeat laboratory evaluation.
