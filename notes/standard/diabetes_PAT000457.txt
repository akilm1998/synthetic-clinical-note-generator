Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Abigail Reed is a 65-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 35 years ago. She is not currently reporting increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. Recent glycemic markers remain significantly elevated, with HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 258 mg/dL.

Comorbidities include dyslipidemia for approximately 15 years, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 14 years, and obstructive sleep apnea for approximately 16 years. She currently smokes. No diabetes treatment regimen or medication list was provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 121/82 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Height 164 cm; weight 64.8 kg; BMI 24.1 kg/m².

Patient is clinically asymptomatic by review of the supplied history. No additional examination findings documented.

Laboratory results:
HbA1c: 9.9%
Fasting glucose: 258 mg/dL

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, with suboptimal glycemic control based on HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 258 mg/dL. Despite marked hyperglycemia, she denies polyuria, polydipsia, visual changes, constitutional symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, wounds, or recurrent infections. Long duration of diabetes increases the importance of comprehensive complication surveillance.

Dyslipidemia, longstanding.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values indicate inadequate diabetes control and reviewed the importance of active treatment, regular glucose monitoring as clinically appropriate, nutrition, and physical activity. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation or adjustment of pharmacologic therapy, with consideration of a diabetes regimen appropriate to her overall health, renal function, hepatic history, treatment preferences, and risk of hypoglycemia. Medication reconciliation should be completed before prescribing.

Recommend diabetes-focused nutrition counseling, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened foods, and regular moderate physical activity as tolerated. Encourage daily attention to foot care and prompt evaluation of any skin breakdown, infection, or change in sensation.

Recommend assessment of diabetes complications and risk factors, including urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine/eGFR, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive foot examination, and dilated retinal examination if not recently completed. Continue management of dyslipidemia and monitor liver-related comorbidity in the context of diabetes care.

Smoking cessation counseling provided; discussed the cardiovascular and microvascular benefits of stopping tobacco use and offered assistance with a cessation plan.

Follow up after treatment planning and repeat glycemic assessment to evaluate response, with earlier evaluation for polyuria, polydipsia, vomiting, marked weakness, confusion, or other symptoms of significant hyperglycemia.
