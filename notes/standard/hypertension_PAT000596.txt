Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Gavin Holland is an 87-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension, present for approximately 28 years and currently without active management documented. He reports no headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. There are no reported interval symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive complications.

His medical history is also significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 45 years, hyperlipidemia for 56 years, and coronary artery disease for 14 years. He does not smoke. He is 173 cm tall and weighs 67.9 kg, with a BMI of 22.7 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 156/93 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 138 mmol/L, potassium 4.9 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings are documented for today’s visit.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent elevation of office blood pressure at 156/93 mmHg in an asymptomatic 87-year-old man. Given the longstanding history and absence of active management, blood pressure control requires reassessment and establishment of a structured treatment plan. His advanced age, coronary artery disease, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia increase his overall cardiovascular risk. Electrolytes are currently within an acceptable range, with potassium at the upper end of normal.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement after a period of rest and obtain home blood pressure readings if feasible, including seated and standing measurements given his age and the risk of orthostatic hypotension. Review blood pressure trends at follow-up rather than relying on a single office value.

Discuss initiation or re-establishment of antihypertensive therapy, selected with consideration of his coronary artery disease, diabetes, current potassium level, renal function, orthostatic symptoms, and overall goals of care. Medication reconciliation should be completed before prescribing, as no current regimen is documented.

Recommend dietary sodium moderation, regular activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excessive alcohol intake. He should seek urgent care for new chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or acute visual change.

Obtain clinically appropriate baseline assessment, including renal function and repeat electrolytes after any antihypertensive therapy is initiated or adjusted. An ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis are also reasonable in the cardiovascular risk assessment and hypertension evaluation.

Continue coordinated management of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and coronary artery disease. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment adjustment.
