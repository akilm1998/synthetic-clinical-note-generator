Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure and intermittent dizziness.

Subjective

Kevin Peterson is a 50-year-old man with a 12-year history of hypertension that has not been actively managed. He reports dizziness and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or nosebleeds. He does not smoke. Comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 17 years, chronic kidney disease for 7 years, and hyperlipidemia for 2 years.

Objective

Blood pressure 164/101 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air. Height 188 cm, weight 79.5 kg, BMI 22.5 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 4.4 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, persistently elevated and currently untreated, with reading of 164/101 mmHg. Dizziness and fatigue are present, but there are no reported symptoms suggesting an acute hypertensive emergency. His longstanding hypertension, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and hyperlipidemia place him at increased cardiovascular and renal risk.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Chronic kidney disease, 7-year history.

Hyperlipidemia, 2-year history.

Plan

Prompt initiation or re-initiation of clinician-directed antihypertensive therapy is recommended, with medication selection and dosing tailored to his diabetes and chronic kidney disease. Review adherence and barriers to care at the next contact.

Begin home blood pressure monitoring with a validated upper-arm cuff and record readings for review. Reinforce a low-sodium diet, regular physical activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol.

Recommend evaluation with renal function testing, urinalysis, lipid profile, and ECG as clinically appropriate, given prolonged hypertension and associated comorbidities. Continue coordinated management of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and hyperlipidemia.

Seek emergency care immediately for severe or worsening headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, marked visual changes, or persistent substantially elevated blood pressure. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and treatment adjustment.
