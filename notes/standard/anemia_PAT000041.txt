Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for anemia.

Subjective
Lydia Ford is a 39-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of anemia in the setting of rheumatoid arthritis diagnosed 7 years ago. She reports intermittent headache but denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or noted pallor. She does not smoke. No current anemia-directed medication regimen is documented.

Objective
Height 152 cm, weight 53.6 kg, BMI 23.2 kg/m². Blood pressure 123/77 mmHg, pulse 85 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin 8.2 g/dL, hematocrit 24.3%, RBC count 2.79 million/µL, and MCV 87 fL. Serum iron is 40 µg/dL, ferritin 357 ng/mL, TIBC 264 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 15.2%.

Assessment
Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation associated with longstanding rheumatoid arthritis. The anemia is normocytic, with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and relatively low TIBC, supporting impaired iron availability related to inflammation rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Hemoglobin is significantly reduced at 8.2 g/dL, although she is currently hemodynamically stable and reports only headache without cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms.

Rheumatoid arthritis, present for approximately 7 years, is the likely chronic inflammatory contributor. Current inflammatory disease activity and treatment status are not established in the available history.

Plan
Repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to further characterize the anemia and assess marrow response.

Recommend serum vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers as part of the evaluation for other contributors and to assess the impact of chronic inflammation.

Review rheumatoid arthritis disease activity and coordinate management with the clinician overseeing her inflammatory disease, as improved control of chronic inflammation may improve the anemia.

Do not begin empiric iron replacement solely on the basis of the low serum iron; the elevated ferritin and low TIBC favor inflammation-related iron sequestration. Consider iron therapy only after assessment for a concurrent absolute iron deficiency and overall clinical context.

Given hemoglobin of 8.2 g/dL, arrange timely reassessment and consider hematology consultation if the anemia persists, worsens, or remains unexplained. Advise urgent evaluation for new shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, sustained palpitations, significant dizziness, bleeding, or rapidly worsening weakness.
