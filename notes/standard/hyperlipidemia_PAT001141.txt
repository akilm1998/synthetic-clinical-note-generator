Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Marcus Cross is a 45-year-old man presenting for follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 5 years and currently not actively managed. Recent lipid profile shows total cholesterol 285 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 202 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 33 mg/dL, and triglycerides 249 mg/dL. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 3 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 15 years. He currently smokes. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Height 185 cm; weight 95.1 kg; BMI 27.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 125/81 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results:
Total cholesterol 285 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 202 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 33 mg/dL
Triglycerides 249 mg/dL

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, low HDL cholesterol, and elevated triglycerides. The degree and duration of LDL elevation confer substantially increased long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of diabetes and active tobacco use. He has no reported anginal symptoms, claudication, or other current cardiovascular symptoms.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 3-year history, increasing overall cardiovascular risk.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Overweight by BMI.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid profile and the importance of active treatment. Recommend initiation of high-intensity lipid-lowering therapy after shared decision-making, with medication selection and dosing reviewed at the visit. If LDL reduction is inadequate or therapy is not tolerated, consider addition of non-statin therapy.

Emphasized a heart-healthy, Mediterranean-style dietary pattern with reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol; increase soluble fiber and physical activity as tolerated. Encourage gradual weight reduction.

Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered cessation support.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response and adherence. Monitor liver enzymes in the context of treatment and known non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, using clinical judgment. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to severe LDL elevation if not previously performed.

Continue comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction in coordination with diabetes care. Instructed to seek urgent evaluation for new chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain.

Follow up after repeat lipid testing to reassess control and treatment tolerance.
