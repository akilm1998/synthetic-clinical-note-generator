Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Logan Stewart is a 40-year-old man seen for outpatient review of hypertension diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. He is not currently documented as taking an antihypertensive medication. There is therefore no active regimen to assess for blood pressure effectiveness, adherence, or medication tolerance. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds.

Relevant history includes type 2 diabetes mellitus for 8 years, hyperlipidemia for 7 years, and coronary artery disease for 1 year. He does not smoke. Current weight is 65.6 kg at 165 cm, with BMI 24.1 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure 145/95 mmHg. Pulse 82 bpm. Respiratory rate 15/min. Temperature 36.9°C. Oxygen saturation 98%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 137 mmol/L, potassium 4.3 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with blood pressure above the desired outpatient range and no documented active antihypertensive therapy. He is asymptomatic today, without reported features of acute hypertensive injury. Hypertension requires treatment optimization given the concurrent history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and coronary artery disease.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, and coronary artery disease are established comorbidities relevant to cardiovascular risk management.

Plan

Review and reconcile all current prescription, over-the-counter, and supplemental medications at the next medication review. Discuss initiation of an antihypertensive regimen and select therapy in the context of his diabetes, coronary artery disease, renal status, contraindications, and tolerance. Emphasize adherence once therapy is prescribed and review common adverse effects at initiation.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with standardized technique and documentation of readings for review. Counsel on limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity, moderating alcohol intake if applicable, and continuing tobacco avoidance.

Recommend follow-up evaluation for treatment response and tolerance after medication initiation or adjustment. Consider renal function and urine assessment, lipid profile review, and electrocardiogram as part of cardiovascular risk and hypertension evaluation; no additional results are available for review today. Continue coordinated management of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and coronary artery disease. Advise urgent evaluation for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.
