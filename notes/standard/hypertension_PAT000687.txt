Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Amelia Foster is an 89-year-old woman with a 38-year history of essential hypertension, currently with poor disease control and no documented active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. No acute hypertensive symptoms reported today.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 50 years, chronic kidney disease for 21 years, and hyperlipidemia for 1 year. She is a current smoker. Weight is 63.3 kg at 157 cm, with BMI 25.7 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 171/99 mmHg; pulse 71 bpm; respiratory rate 17/min; temperature 36.9°C; oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory data: Sodium 146 mmol/L; potassium 3.6 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings provided.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently markedly elevated office blood pressure, asymptomatic today and without reported features of hypertensive emergency. Long duration of disease and comorbid chronic kidney disease and diabetes mellitus increase cardiovascular and renal risk. Mildly elevated sodium and low-normal potassium warrant consideration when selecting therapy and monitoring electrolytes. Current tobacco use is an additional cardiovascular risk factor.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure after seated rest using an appropriately sized cuff and confirm with home blood pressure monitoring if feasible. Review adherence, access to care, dietary sodium intake, alcohol use, and prior antihypertensive history before establishing or intensifying treatment.

Given the degree of elevation and her diabetes and chronic kidney disease, initiate or adjust antihypertensive therapy cautiously with close follow-up, accounting for age, renal function, orthostatic symptoms, and fall risk. Obtain renal function and repeat serum electrolytes after treatment changes; only sodium and potassium values are available today.

Recommend urinalysis, lipid profile, and ECG as part of cardiovascular and end-organ risk assessment. Continue coordinated management of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and hyperlipidemia.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, reduced dietary sodium, and regular activity as tolerated.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring immediate emergency evaluation, including chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, vision change, or epistaxis. Arrange close outpatient reassessment for blood pressure response and treatment tolerance.
