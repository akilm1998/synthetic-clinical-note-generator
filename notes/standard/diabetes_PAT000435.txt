Annual Review Note

Patient: Sarah Griffin, 94-year-old female

Chief Complaint

Annual review of chronic medical conditions, with focus on longstanding type 2 diabetes.

Subjective

Sarah Griffin has a 39-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet. Diabetes is currently not actively managed. No diabetes treatment regimen is documented today.

She has a longstanding history of anxiety, present for approximately 37 years. No acute anxiety-related concerns are reported at this visit.

She does not smoke.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 132/76 mmHg, pulse 68 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 160 cm; weight 70.9 kg; BMI 27.7 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.6%. Fasting glucose 220 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, longstanding for 39 years, currently inadequately controlled based on HbA1c of 9.6% and fasting glucose of 220 mg/dL. Although she denies osmotic, visual, constitutional, infectious, or neuropathic symptoms, the degree and duration of hyperglycemia increase her risk for microvascular and cardiovascular complications. At age 94, glycemic targets and treatment intensity should be individualized according to functional status, preferences, comorbidity burden, and risk of hypoglycemia.

Overweight, BMI 27.7 kg/m².

Chronic anxiety, approximately 37 years’ duration; no acute concern reported.

Plan

Discussed the importance of re-engaging with diabetes care, including individualized glycemic goals and treatment options. Review current medications, adherence, dietary pattern, home glucose monitoring practices, functional status, and hypoglycemia risk before initiating or intensifying therapy. Avoid overly aggressive glucose lowering given advanced age and the potential consequences of hypoglycemia.

Recommend nutrition counseling focused on regular balanced meals, reduction of concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, and activity appropriate to mobility and safety. Encourage hydration and routine home glucose monitoring if clinically appropriate.

Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval to assess response to treatment. Recommend assessment of renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and consideration of cardiovascular risk reduction based on overall goals of care. Recommend annual dilated retinal evaluation and comprehensive foot examination, including assessment of sensation and pulses, given the duration of diabetes. Review immunizations appropriate for age and diabetes, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, and herpes zoster vaccination.

Continue to monitor anxiety symptoms and their effect on sleep, appetite, function, and diabetes self-management. Use a supportive, low-burden care plan with shared decision-making and involvement of family or caregivers as appropriate.

Follow up promptly to establish an individualized diabetes management plan and routinely thereafter based on glycemic control and treatment tolerance.
