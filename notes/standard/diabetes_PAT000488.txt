Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Deborah Lawson is a 33-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 4 years ago. She is not currently reporting increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. Diabetes control has not been actively managed recently. She does not smoke. Her comorbidity is obstructive sleep apnea, present for approximately 1 year.

Objective

Height 162 cm; weight 73.2 kg; BMI 27.9 kg/m². Blood pressure 122/78 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Hemoglobin A1c 9.5%. Fasting glucose 248 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c of 9.5% and fasting glucose of 248 mg/dL. Despite the degree of hyperglycemia, she is presently asymptomatic. There is no reported neuropathic symptomatology, recurrent infection, visual complaint, or unintentional weight loss. BMI is in the overweight range. The absence of symptoms does not lessen the risk of progressive microvascular and macrovascular complications.

Obstructive sleep apnea, reported duration approximately 1 year.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose, along with the importance of active diabetes management to reduce long-term complications. Recommend initiation of guideline-directed glucose-lowering pharmacotherapy, with medication selection based on renal function, contraindications, tolerability, cost, and shared decision-making. Obtain renal assessment, including serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, before and during treatment planning. Recommend fasting lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment.

Counseled regarding reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, increased dietary fiber, and gradual progression toward at least 150 minutes of moderate physical activity weekly as tolerated. Discussed home glucose monitoring and recognition of symptoms requiring prompt evaluation, including polyuria, polydipsia, vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, or marked weakness.

Recommend comprehensive diabetes preventive care, including an annual dilated retinal examination, complete foot examination with assessment of sensation and pulses, and ongoing monitoring for nephropathy. Continue attention to weight management and cardiovascular risk reduction. Follow up after initiation or adjustment of therapy to review glucose readings and tolerance, with repeat HbA1c in approximately 3 months.
