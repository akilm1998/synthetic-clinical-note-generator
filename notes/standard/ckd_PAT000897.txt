Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Eliana Matthews is a 60-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 17 years. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, or shortness of breath.

She has a history of coronary artery disease for 2 years and hyperlipidemia for 17 years. She currently smokes. No medication regimen is documented for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 144/84 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 176 cm, weight 73.1 kg, BMI 23.6 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 2.52 mg/dL, estimated GFR 20 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 60 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with significant reduction in renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. She has fatigue but no current symptoms suggestive of fluid overload or prominent uremic symptoms. Blood pressure is above goal in the setting of chronic kidney disease. Longstanding tobacco use and coexisting coronary artery disease increase overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active ongoing management of her chronic kidney disease and cardiovascular risk factors. Recommend nephrology referral for evaluation and longitudinal renal care given the current degree of renal impairment and long disease duration.

Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, BUN, and creatinine, along with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess renal disease burden. Recommend CBC to evaluate fatigue and screen for anemia of chronic disease, and intact parathyroid hormone testing to assess for chronic kidney disease–related mineral and bone disorder. Renal ultrasound may be obtained as part of structural evaluation if not previously completed.

Blood pressure should be monitored closely at home and reviewed at follow-up. Review and optimize antihypertensive therapy based on renal function, albuminuria, potassium level, and cardiovascular history; no specific medication regimen is documented today. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter products, and review all prescription medications and supplements for renal dosing.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, cardiovascular risk reduction, and adherence to a kidney-conscious diet with attention to sodium intake. Continue monitoring for edema, worsening shortness of breath, nausea, confusion, marked reduction in urine output, or other acute changes, with urgent evaluation if these occur.

Follow up after recommended laboratory testing and nephrology evaluation, or sooner for worsening symptoms.
