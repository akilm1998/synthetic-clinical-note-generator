Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated diabetes laboratory values.

Subjective

Colin Graham is a 90-year-old man with a 36-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. Recent testing showed HbA1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 256 mg/dL. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling. No acute symptoms reported. Diabetes is currently not actively managed. He does not smoke. History is also notable for non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 38 years.

Objective

Height 180 cm, weight 94.0 kg, BMI 29.0 kg/m². Blood pressure 138/79 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%. HbA1c 9.9%. Fasting glucose 256 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with significant hyperglycemia based on HbA1c and fasting glucose. Despite marked laboratory elevation, he currently denies osmotic, constitutional, visual, infectious, or neuropathic symptoms. Advanced age and coexisting non-alcoholic fatty liver disease should be considered when selecting and intensifying therapy.

Plan

Discussed the elevated glucose and HbA1c results and the importance of re-establishing active diabetes management. Arrange prompt outpatient medication and treatment review, with an individualized glycemic target given his age and overall health. Review dietary intake, hydration, physical activity appropriate for safety, home glucose monitoring, and ability to administer and monitor any prescribed therapy. Medication options, including metformin or other glucose-lowering therapy, should be considered after review of renal function, liver status, hypoglycemia risk, medication tolerance, and patient preferences; no current regimen is documented.

Recommend renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, along with a fasting lipid profile as part of diabetes risk evaluation. Review foot care and arrange routine diabetic eye and foot assessments as clinically appropriate. Seek urgent care for vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, confusion, marked weakness, severe abdominal pain, or development of frequent urination with worsening thirst. Close follow-up is recommended to reassess glucose control and treatment response.
