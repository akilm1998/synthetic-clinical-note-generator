Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Chelsea Benson is a 51-year-old woman, 171 cm tall and weighing 61.7 kg, with a BMI of 21.1 kg/m², presenting for routine outpatient follow-up. Hyperlipidemia has been present for approximately 9 years and is currently not under active management. Recent lipid testing shows total cholesterol 303 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 195 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 37 mg/dL, and triglycerides 353 mg/dL.

She reports no chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, or fatty skin deposits. Xanthelasma is present. She continues to smoke. Her cardiovascular risk is further increased by known coronary artery disease of approximately 3 years’ duration, hypertension for 19 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 17 years. No current lipid-lowering medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 133/85 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

General: 51-year-old woman with normal BMI, seen for follow-up.

Laboratory data:
Total cholesterol 303 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 195 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 37 mg/dL
Triglycerides 353 mg/dL

Xanthelasma is noted. No reported chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness.

Assessment

1. Hyperlipidemia, longstanding and currently untreated. Lipid profile demonstrates marked elevation of total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with elevated triglycerides and low HDL cholesterol. In the setting of established coronary artery disease, diabetes, hypertension, and active tobacco use, this represents a very high atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk profile. Xanthelasma further supports longstanding dyslipidemia. There are no current symptoms suggestive of active angina or claudication.

2. Coronary artery disease, present for approximately 3 years. No current chest discomfort or dizziness reported. Substantial lipid-related residual cardiovascular risk is present.

3. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 17 years. This significantly increases cardiovascular risk and reinforces the need for aggressive risk-factor modification.

4. Hypertension, present for approximately 19 years. Blood pressure today is 133/85 mmHg.

5. Tobacco use. Continued smoking substantially compounds her cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the markedly abnormal lipid profile and the importance of treatment, particularly given established coronary artery disease and diabetes. Recommend initiation of guideline-directed high-intensity statin therapy after review of contraindications, prior intolerance, and patient preferences. If LDL cholesterol remains above goal despite maximally tolerated statin therapy, addition of ezetimibe and consideration of further lipid-lowering therapy should be discussed.

Recommend a fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, with consideration of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a) measurement for additional risk assessment. Review for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia should be performed clinically.

Counseled on reducing saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates and alcohol because of the elevated triglycerides, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, and continuing a heart-healthy dietary pattern. Strongly advised complete smoking cessation; cessation support and pharmacologic options should be offered.

Continue coordinated management of coronary artery disease, diabetes, and hypertension, with attention to comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction. Follow up after lipid management is initiated to review tolerance, adherence, and repeat lipid results.
