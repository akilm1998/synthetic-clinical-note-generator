Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and markedly elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Julia Holland is an 89-year-old woman, nonsmoker, with a 32-year history of chronic kidney disease, longstanding hypertension for 42 years, anemia of chronic disease for 24 years, and coronary artery disease for 7 years. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. Current medication regimen and allergies are not documented.

Objective

Height 169 cm, weight 83.7 kg, BMI 29.3 kg/m². Blood pressure 172/94 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: creatinine 3.9 mg/dL, BUN 76 mg/dL, eGFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m². No additional examination findings provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with significant renal impairment and elevated azotemia. The prolonged disease duration, creatinine 3.9 mg/dL, BUN 76 mg/dL, and eGFR 16 are concerning for advanced chronic renal dysfunction. She currently reports fatigue but no symptoms suggestive of acute fluid overload or prominent uremic prodrome.

Severely uncontrolled hypertension in the setting of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease. Blood pressure requires prompt reassessment and active management to reduce risk of further renal and cardiovascular injury.

Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding; current degree of anemia cannot be assessed from the available results.

Coronary artery disease, chronic.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure promptly with confirmation of technique and assess for headache, chest pain, neurologic symptoms, acute dyspnea, or visual changes. If any of these develop, or if blood pressure rises further, proceed to emergency evaluation.

Arrange urgent primary care/nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management and review of the current medication regimen. Review for renally appropriate antihypertensive and cardiovascular therapy without assuming current medications.

Recommend serum electrolyte panel, repeat renal function testing, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate to characterize complications and progression of kidney disease.

Counsel on avoidance of NSAIDs and other potential nephrotoxins, monitoring for reduced urine output, edema, nausea, confusion, or shortness of breath, and seeking urgent care if these occur. Close follow-up is warranted given the markedly elevated blood pressure and degree of renal impairment.
