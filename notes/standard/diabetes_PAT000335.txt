Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Harper Bennett is a 59-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 7 years ago. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. Current diabetes management has been limited, and no active medication regimen is documented.

Recent laboratory results show HbA1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 207 mg/dL, consistent with persistent hyperglycemia. She also has a history of hypertension for 18 years and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 9 years. She currently smokes. No medication or allergy information was provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 135/80 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 161 cm, weight 75.4 kg, BMI 29.1 kg/m².

General: Alert, comfortable-appearing woman in no acute distress.

Laboratory data: HbA1c 9.2%. Fasting glucose 207 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, present for approximately 7 years, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 207 mg/dL. She denies symptoms of hyperglycemia, neuropathy, infection, or constitutional complications.

Overweight, BMI 29.1 kg/m².

Hypertension, longstanding, with blood pressure 135/80 mmHg today.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed that the current glucose values indicate inadequate diabetes control despite the absence of symptoms. Reviewed the importance of regular meals, reducing concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, and gradual weight loss. Encouraged routine physical activity as tolerated, with a practical goal of consistent moderate exercise most days of the week.

Because no active diabetes medication regimen is documented, recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of pharmacologic therapy, including consideration of first-line metformin if clinically appropriate and renal/hepatic status permit. Medication selection should account for her comorbid hypertension, overweight status, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Recommend repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval to assess response to treatment and ongoing monitoring with fasting glucose or home glucose checks if a monitoring plan is established. Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine/eGFR, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes eye and foot assessments as part of complication screening.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and reinforce dietary sodium reduction, physical activity, and weight management. Strongly encouraged smoking cessation; discussed that cessation would reduce cardiovascular risk in the setting of diabetes and hypertension.

Return for diabetes management follow-up after treatment planning and interval monitoring, sooner for marked hyperglycemia or development of excessive thirst, frequent urination, vomiting, confusion, weakness, or other concerning symptoms.
