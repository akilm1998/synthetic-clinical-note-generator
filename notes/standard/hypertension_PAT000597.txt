Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Alexander Fisher is a 38-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension, present for approximately 13 years and currently without documented active management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency.

His relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 4 years, coronary artery disease for 1 year, and chronic kidney disease for 1 year. He does not smoke. His current weight is 61.4 kg at a height of 163 cm, with BMI 23.1 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 150/99 mmHg; pulse 79 bpm; respiratory rate 18/min; temperature 37.0°C; oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 147 mmol/L; potassium 4.3 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension remains above the desired outpatient range at 150/99 mmHg after a prolonged history of disease. He is asymptomatic, with no current features of hypertensive crisis. The coexistence of diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease places him at increased cardiovascular and renal risk and warrants timely reassessment and active treatment planning. Potassium is within the reported normal range; sodium is mildly elevated and should be interpreted in clinical context.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease are important comorbid conditions that should be incorporated into antihypertensive selection and overall cardiovascular risk reduction.

Plan

Confirm blood pressure control with repeat standardized office measurements and a structured home blood pressure log, including measurement technique and timing. Given persistent elevation and the patient’s diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease, initiate or promptly review an evidence-based antihypertensive treatment strategy rather than continuing observation alone. Medication reconciliation and assessment of adherence, contraindications, and prior treatment response should precede selection of therapy.

Recommend dietary sodium reduction, regular physical activity as clinically tolerated, maintenance of a healthy body weight, and avoidance of excessive alcohol intake. He should continue smoking avoidance.

Obtain or review renal function and urine protein assessment, along with diabetes and cardiovascular risk monitoring, to guide treatment intensity and evaluate target-organ involvement. A lipid profile, urinalysis, and electrocardiogram are appropriate components of his cardiovascular and hypertension evaluation.

He was advised to seek urgent medical attention for severe or sudden headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, acute visual change, or markedly elevated blood pressure accompanied by symptoms. Arrange close outpatient reassessment after blood pressure monitoring and treatment review.
