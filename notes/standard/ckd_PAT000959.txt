Chief Complaint

Initial outpatient consultation for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Scott Douglas is a 76-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of chronic kidney disease, reported for approximately 2 years. He has a history of hypertension for 8 years and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 18 years. He currently smokes. Weight is stable at 75.0 kg with BMI 24.2 kg/m².

He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, shortness of breath, or change in urinary frequency. No uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported at this visit. Medication use, allergies, prior renal imaging, and prior nephrology treatment are not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 166/99 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 176 cm; weight 75.0 kg; BMI 24.2 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Serum creatinine 5.41 mg/dL, BUN 87 mg/dL, estimated GFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m².

No additional examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly impaired renal filtration based on creatinine 5.41 mg/dL, BUN 87 mg/dL, and eGFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m². Despite the degree of laboratory abnormality, he is currently without reported uremic symptoms, edema, dyspnea, or urinary changes. The duration of approximately 2 years and associated secondary hyperparathyroidism support an established chronic process. The available information does not establish the underlying etiology.

Hypertension, currently significantly elevated at 166/99 mmHg, representing an important modifiable risk factor for progression of kidney disease. Current antihypertensive regimen is not provided.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism by history.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation given the substantially reduced eGFR and elevated nitrogenous waste markers. Discussed the need for close monitoring and early education regarding kidney replacement therapy options should renal function, symptoms, or metabolic complications warrant it; no dialysis indication is established from the currently provided information alone.

Recommend repeat renal function assessment with serum electrolytes, creatinine/eGFR, and BUN, along with urinalysis and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to further characterize kidney disease and assess proteinuria. Recommend CBC to evaluate for anemia associated with advanced chronic kidney disease and intact parathyroid hormone assessment for mineral-bone disorder management. Renal ultrasound is recommended to evaluate renal structure and exclude obstructive or other structural disease.

Blood pressure requires prompt reassessment and treatment optimization with a kidney-protective regimen selected after review of current medications, potassium, volume status, and overall clinical context. Medication reconciliation is required before prescribing. Avoidance of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents should be reinforced, with renal dosing of all medications.

Continue monitoring for new nausea, vomiting, worsening fatigue, confusion, pruritus, muscle cramps, reduced urine output, progressive edema, chest discomfort, or shortness of breath. He should seek urgent medical attention if these symptoms develop or if blood pressure becomes severely elevated with neurologic or cardiopulmonary symptoms. Smoking cessation was advised.
