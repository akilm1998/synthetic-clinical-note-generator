Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Preston Oliver is a 61-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 25 years. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No interval uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported.

Relevant comorbidities include anemia of chronic disease for 4 years, gout for 5 years, and hyperlipidemia for 4 years. He currently smokes. No medication regimen is documented for review today.

Objective

Height 173 cm; weight 68.2 kg; BMI 22.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 142/86 mmHg, pulse 84 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 4.27 mg/dL, BUN 61 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m².

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of approximately 25 years’ duration with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN and creatinine. Although he is currently asymptomatic, the degree of renal impairment warrants prompt nephrology-directed management and preparation for potential renal replacement therapy if clinically indicated. Blood pressure is above goal. Anemia of chronic disease, gout, and hyperlipidemia are relevant comorbidities. Ongoing tobacco use increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management, renal replacement therapy education, and assessment of candidacy and timing for access planning if appropriate.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as part of CKD complication assessment. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously performed or if clinically indicated.

Review blood pressure management and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents, including NSAIDs. Medication reconciliation is needed before initiating or adjusting therapy, with renal dosing of all medications.

Monitor for new nausea, pruritus, progressive fatigue, muscle cramps, worsening edema, dyspnea, or changes in urine output; seek urgent evaluation for significant deterioration or acute symptoms.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and the importance of ongoing renal and cardiovascular risk-factor management. Follow up after nephrology assessment or sooner for any change in symptoms.
