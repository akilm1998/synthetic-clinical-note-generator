Chief Complaint

Annual review of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Nathan Jenkins is a 54-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review of type 2 diabetes, diagnosed approximately 17 years ago. Current laboratory results show HbA1c 9.4% and fasting glucose 199 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimal glycemic control. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. He does report numbness in the hands or feet without associated tingling.

He has a 22-year history of obstructive sleep apnea. He currently smokes. No diabetes medications are listed in the available history.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 118/75 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 163 cm, weight 76.0 kg, BMI 28.6 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.4%. Fasting glucose 199 mg/dL.

Review of systems is notable for distal numbness as above. No reported polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, weight loss, fatigue, visual disturbance, impaired wound healing, or recurrent infection.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus with significant persistent hyperglycemia based on HbA1c 9.4% and fasting glucose 199 mg/dL. He is currently not receiving documented diabetes management. There are no reported symptoms of acute metabolic decompensation or infection. Distal numbness raises concern for possible early peripheral sensory neuropathy, although no formal complication has been established from the available examination.

Overweight, BMI 28.6 kg/m².

Tobacco use, active.

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding, approximately 22 years.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the increased risk of microvascular and cardiovascular complications with persistent hyperglycemia. Recommend prompt initiation of a structured diabetes treatment program, including nutrition counseling, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, and gradual progression toward at least 150 minutes of moderate physical activity weekly as tolerated.

Recommend discussion of first-line pharmacologic therapy, typically metformin when clinically appropriate, with escalation of treatment if glycemic response is inadequate. Medication selection and dosing should be guided by renal function, tolerance, contraindications, and shared decision-making.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with review of fasting and periodic postprandial values to guide treatment. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response.

Recommend diabetes complication surveillance, including urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive foot examination with sensory and pulse assessment, and dilated retinal examination. The reported numbness should be reassessed at follow-up, with counseling on daily foot inspection, appropriate footwear, and prompt evaluation of any skin breakdown or nonhealing lesion.

Provide smoking-cessation counseling and offer evidence-based cessation support. Encourage weight reduction through dietary modification and regular activity.

Review preventive care as part of the annual visit, including age-appropriate immunizations and cardiovascular risk assessment. Continue attention to obstructive sleep apnea management and reinforce adherence to prescribed therapy if applicable.

Follow up promptly after initiation of diabetes management for glucose review and treatment adjustment, with ongoing diabetes follow-up at regular intervals.
