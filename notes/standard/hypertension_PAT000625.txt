Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Hazel Fisher is a 74-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension, present for approximately 19 years and currently without an active treatment regimen documented. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. No symptoms suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency are reported.

Her medical history is also notable for hyperlipidemia for approximately 24 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 2 years. She continues to smoke. Weight is stable by current measurements, with BMI in the normal range. Medication use, allergies, and home blood pressure readings were not provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 149/89 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 95% on room air.

Height 170 cm, weight 63.6 kg, BMI 22.0 kg/m².

Recent serum electrolytes: sodium 143 mmol/L, potassium 4.5 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding, with blood pressure above goal at today’s visit. She is asymptomatic without findings to suggest hypertensive crisis. Control appears suboptimal in the setting of no documented active management. Electrolytes are within the reported reference ranges.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration approximately 2 years.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding, approximately 24 years.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given her age, longstanding hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and ongoing tobacco use. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, including readings taken at consistent times and proper seated technique.

Reviewed lifestyle measures, including limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, avoiding excessive alcohol intake, and continuing a healthy weight. Strongly encouraged smoking cessation and offered cessation counseling.

Recommend follow-up evaluation for initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy based on home readings and shared decision-making. Appropriate medication selection should account for her diabetes and renal status. Recommend periodic monitoring of renal function and electrolytes after treatment is initiated or adjusted.

Recommend routine cardiovascular risk assessment, including lipid evaluation, diabetes monitoring, urinalysis, and consideration of an ECG as clinically appropriate. Continue follow-up of hyperlipidemia and diabetes as part of overall cardiovascular risk reduction.

Reviewed emergency precautions: seek immediate care for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Return for blood pressure reassessment after a short interval with home readings, sooner for new or worsening symptoms.
