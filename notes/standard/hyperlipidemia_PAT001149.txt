Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Jasmine Spencer is a 79-year-old woman seen for follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 22 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid panel shows markedly elevated total cholesterol at 314 mg/dL, LDL-C 222 mg/dL, HDL-C 44 mg/dL, and triglycerides 238 mg/dL. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness. Xanthelasma is present.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease of approximately 2 years’ duration, chronic kidney disease of approximately 32 years’ duration, and obesity of approximately 29 years’ duration. She continues to smoke. Weight is 89.8 kg at 166 cm, with BMI 32.6 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: BP 139/87 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respirations 20/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 93%.

Lipid panel: Total cholesterol 314 mg/dL; LDL-C 222 mg/dL; HDL-C 44 mg/dL; triglycerides 238 mg/dL.

Xanthelasma noted by history. No reported chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia, uncontrolled, with markedly elevated LDL-C and mixed elevation of triglycerides. Long duration without active management, with additional atherosclerotic risk from known coronary artery disease, tobacco use, obesity, advanced age, and chronic kidney disease.

Obesity, BMI 32.6 kg/m².

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the substantially elevated cholesterol levels and increased cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of known coronary artery disease. Recommend initiation of evidence-based statin therapy after reviewing treatment goals, potential adverse effects, renal considerations, and patient preferences. Consider addition of nonstatin therapy if LDL-C remains significantly elevated despite maximally tolerated statin treatment.

Counseled on reducing saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates and alcohol, increasing fiber-rich foods, and pursuing gradual weight reduction as tolerated. Encouraged smoking cessation and offered cessation support.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel and appropriate safety laboratory monitoring after treatment initiation or adjustment. Consider assessment for additional cardiovascular risk factors, including lipoprotein(a) or coronary artery calcium evaluation, if results would change management.

Follow up after treatment initiation and repeat lipid testing, or sooner for new chest discomfort, shortness of breath, or exertional leg symptoms.
