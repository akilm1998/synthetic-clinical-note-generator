Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for management of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

History of Present Illness

Damian Boyd is a 72-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 5 years ago. Current glycemic control is poor, with hemoglobin A1c 9.9% and fasting glucose 219 mg/dL. He reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No diabetic complications are reported or documented at this visit. He is not currently described as having an established diabetes treatment regimen.

He has a longstanding history of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, reported duration 43 years. He does not smoke.

Past Medical History

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 5 years ago.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Medications

No current medication regimen provided.

Allergies

Not documented.

Social History

72-year-old male. Denies tobacco use. Other social history was not provided.

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fatigue and unintentional weight loss.

Endocrine: Denies increased thirst, frequent urination, and increased hunger.

Eyes: Denies blurred vision.

Skin/Infectious: Denies slow-healing wounds and frequent infections.

Neurologic: Denies numbness or tingling in the hands or feet.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 124/78 mmHg, pulse 73 beats/minute, respiratory rate 14/minute, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 164 cm. Weight 78.3 kg. BMI 29.1 kg/m².

Laboratory Results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.9%. Fasting glucose 219 mg/dL.

Physical examination findings beyond the recorded vital signs and anthropometric measurements were not provided.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications

Glycemic control is substantially above goal based on HbA1c of 9.9% and fasting glucose of 219 mg/dL. The patient is currently asymptomatic, without reported polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, visual symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, recurrent infections, or impaired wound healing. There is no documented evidence of nephropathy, retinopathy, neuropathy, or other diabetes-related complication at this time. His overweight status, with BMI 29.1 kg/m², is an additional modifiable risk factor. Given the degree of hyperglycemia and lack of an established management regimen, active treatment initiation and close follow-up are indicated.

2. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease

Longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, reported duration 43 years. The coexistence of diabetes and excess weight increases metabolic risk. No liver-related laboratory results or imaging findings were provided today.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the need for consistent diabetes management despite the absence of symptoms.

Review dietary pattern with emphasis on reducing concentrated sugars and refined carbohydrates, moderating portion sizes, increasing fiber-rich foods, and avoiding sugar-sweetened beverages. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated, with gradual progression and attention to safety.

Discuss initiation of glucose-lowering pharmacotherapy, with selection based on renal function, hepatic status, tolerability, cost, and overall health. Metformin may be considered if renal function and other clinical factors permit; additional therapy may be needed given the degree of hyperglycemia. Medication reconciliation and review of contraindications are recommended before prescribing.

Recommend assessment of renal status with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and appropriate diabetes monitoring. Recommend a comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including assessment of skin integrity, pulses, and protective sensation, as well as a dilated eye examination for diabetic retinal disease.

Review home glucose monitoring and hypoglycemia precautions if medication therapy is initiated. Advise prompt evaluation for marked hyperglycemic symptoms, vomiting, dehydration, confusion, or inability to maintain oral intake.

For fatty liver disease, recommend continued weight management, avoidance of excess alcohol if applicable, optimization of glycemic control, and review of liver-related laboratory monitoring as clinically appropriate.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after treatment initiation and repeat HbA1c monitoring to assess response and guide intensification.
