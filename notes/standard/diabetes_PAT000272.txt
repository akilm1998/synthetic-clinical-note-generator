Chief Complaint

Medication review and diabetes follow-up.

Subjective

Eliza Chandler is a 32-year-old woman with an 11-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, presenting for medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance review. She is not currently reporting use of an active diabetes medication regimen and is not engaged in regular pharmacologic management. No medication-related adverse effects are reported. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She continues to smoke. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease of approximately 1 year and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease of approximately 4 years’ duration. No acute concerns reported today.

Objective

Height 168 cm. Weight 66.9 kg. BMI 23.7 kg/m².

Blood pressure 135/87 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Recent laboratory results: HbA1c 9.6%; fasting glucose 224 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently inadequately controlled based on HbA1c 9.6% and fasting glucose 224 mg/dL. Despite marked hyperglycemia, she is asymptomatic today. Medication effectiveness and tolerance cannot be demonstrated because she is not reporting an active diabetes medication regimen. Her normal-range BMI does not lessen the need for treatment optimization. Chronic kidney disease and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease are important considerations when selecting therapy.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed that current glycemic values are above goal and that lifestyle measures alone are unlikely to provide adequate control. Reviewed the importance of beginning or re-establishing consistent diabetes pharmacotherapy. Metformin would be a reasonable consideration if renal function and hepatic status are appropriate; medication selection and dosing should be guided by review of kidney and liver function and shared decision-making. Discussed adherence strategies, anticipated benefits, and common gastrointestinal tolerability issues. She will notify the office promptly for medication intolerance or difficulty obtaining or taking therapy.

Recommend monitoring fasting and postprandial glucose when possible, along with nutrition focused on limiting concentrated carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, regular physical activity, and continued avoidance of excess alcohol given fatty liver disease. Encourage smoking cessation and offer cessation support.

Recommend renal and diabetes monitoring, including serum creatinine/eGFR, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and repeat HbA1c after treatment is established. Review for diabetic eye, foot, and cardiovascular risk assessment at follow-up. Close outpatient follow-up is recommended to reassess adherence, treatment tolerance, home glucose readings, and glycemic response.
