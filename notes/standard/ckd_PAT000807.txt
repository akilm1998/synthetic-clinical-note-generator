Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease and hypertension.

Subjective

Emily Cooper is a 46-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 7 years ago, with longstanding hypertension for 16 years and anemia of chronic disease for 2 years. She reports persistent fatigue. She denies itching, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. She does not smoke. No current medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 154/93 mmHg, pulse 76/min, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 156 cm, weight 70.8 kg, BMI 29.1 kg/m².

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 3.84 mg/dL, estimated GFR 11 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 86 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. She has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, cramping, fluid overload, respiratory symptoms, or urinary changes. The degree of renal impairment requires prompt specialist assessment and close monitoring for complications.

Hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal at 154/93 mmHg, contributing to ongoing renal risk.

Anemia of chronic disease, present for approximately 2 years; likely contributing to fatigue, though current blood count is not available.

Overweight by BMI, 29.1 kg/m².

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for advanced chronic kidney disease, discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate, and guidance regarding blood pressure and kidney-protective management.

Obtain updated renal function and serum electrolytes, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as part of CKD complication assessment. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously completed or if clinically indicated.

Review and reconcile all prescribed, over-the-counter, and herbal medications at the next contact. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a clinician. Medication dosing should be reviewed for renal adjustment.

Address elevated blood pressure promptly with home blood pressure monitoring and individualized antihypertensive management, coordinated with nephrology. Counsel regarding a lower-sodium diet, avoidance of dehydration, and moderation of processed foods.

Monitor for worsening fatigue, nausea or vomiting, itching, confusion, reduced urine output, new swelling, chest discomfort, or shortness of breath. She should seek urgent evaluation for these symptoms or for significant clinical deterioration.

Continue close primary care follow-up after nephrology assessment and updated laboratory testing.
