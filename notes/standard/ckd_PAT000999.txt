Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Bob Hill is a 32-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No interval symptoms suggestive of fluid overload or uremic illness are reported.

He is a nonsmoker. Current weight is 85.2 kg at a height of 190 cm, with BMI 23.6 kg/m². Comorbid conditions include anemia of chronic disease and hyperlipidemia, each present for approximately 1 year. No medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 132/91 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 3.12 mg/dL, BUN 81 mg/dL, estimated GFR 20 mL/min/1.73 m².

The patient is clinically stable by reported symptoms and vital signs. No physical examination findings are provided for today’s visit.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with significantly reduced renal function and elevated nitrogenous waste markers. The condition has been present for approximately 1 year and is not currently under active management. He is presently asymptomatic without reported edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, or urinary changes. Blood pressure is mildly elevated, particularly the diastolic reading.

Anemia of chronic disease, present for approximately 1 year; current blood counts are not available for review.

Hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 1 year.

Plan

Discussed the importance of prompt, structured follow-up for his chronic kidney disease given the creatinine of 3.12 mg/dL, BUN of 81 mg/dL, and eGFR of 20 mL/min/1.73 m². Recommend nephrology referral for evaluation and ongoing renal management.

Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Renal ultrasound is also recommended to assess renal structure and exclude obstructive or structural causes.

Review and reconcile all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use at the next visit. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a clinician. Encourage appropriate hydration while monitoring for swelling, reduced urination, or shortness of breath. Discussed limiting excess dietary sodium and avoiding unmonitored supplements.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring and continued primary care follow-up. Further treatment of blood pressure, anemia, mineral-bone abnormalities, and hyperlipidemia should be guided by updated laboratory evaluation and renal consultation.

Seek urgent medical attention for new shortness of breath, progressive swelling, markedly decreased urine output, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.
