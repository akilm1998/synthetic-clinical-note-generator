Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Ivy Henderson is an 82-year-old woman, 162 cm tall and weighing 53.0 kg (BMI 20.2 kg/m²), presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 46 years. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, and shortness of breath. There are no reported symptoms suggestive of current fluid overload or overt uremic prodrome.

Her chronic kidney disease has not been actively managed. She also has secondary hyperparathyroidism of approximately 22 years’ duration. She is a current smoker. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 146/88 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 95% on room air.

Laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL.
Estimated glomerular filtration rate 6 mL/min/1.73 m².
Blood urea nitrogen 86 mg/dL.

No peripheral edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, or urinary frequency change is reported.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia, evidenced by creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 86 mg/dL, and eGFR 6 mL/min/1.73 m². She currently reports fatigue but no nausea, pruritus, cramps, sleep disturbance, oliguria-type urinary change, edema, or shortness of breath. Despite limited symptoms, the degree of renal impairment is clinically significant and requires prompt specialist assessment and active management.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, longstanding for approximately 22 years, likely related to chronic kidney disease.

Elevated blood pressure at today’s visit, 146/88 mmHg, in the setting of advanced chronic kidney disease.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of severe chronic kidney disease, assessment for complications, and discussion of renal replacement therapy options and goals of care. Given the degree of azotemia, she was advised to seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening nausea, vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, progressive decrease in urination, chest pain, worsening shortness of breath, or swelling.

Obtain updated renal and complication assessment, including serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, albumin, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone. Renal ultrasound should be considered as part of structural evaluation if not previously completed.

Review and address blood pressure control with attention to kidney-protective management. Medication reconciliation is needed before initiating or adjusting therapy, with avoidance of nephrotoxic medications and appropriate renal dosing of all prescriptions.

Further management of secondary hyperparathyroidism should be guided by updated calcium, phosphorus, and parathyroid hormone results in conjunction with nephrology.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and the importance of close follow-up.
