Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Grace Powell is an 88-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 28 years. Blood pressure control has not been actively managed. She reports intermittent dizziness and palpitations. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, or epistaxis. She does not smoke. Weight is 67.3 kg at 160 cm, with BMI 26.3 kg/m².

Her medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 12 years. No current antihypertensive regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 151/99 mmHg; pulse 75 bpm; respiratory rate 17/min; temperature 37.0°C; oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory data: Sodium 138 mmol/L; potassium 5.0 mmol/L.

General: No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms reported at today’s visit. Further examination findings are not documented.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent elevation at today’s visit in the setting of longstanding disease that has not been actively managed. She has associated intermittent dizziness and palpitations but no symptoms currently suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Potassium is at the upper end of the provided reference context, which is relevant to antihypertensive selection, particularly agents that may further increase potassium.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration approximately 12 years, increasing overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Discussed the importance of initiating structured blood pressure management given persistent elevation, advanced age, diabetes, and prolonged duration of hypertension. Before selecting therapy, review renal function, medication history, contraindications, and orthostatic blood pressures. Given potassium of 5.0 mmol/L, avoid empiric initiation of an agent likely to increase potassium until renal function and repeat electrolytes are reviewed.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with seated readings recorded at consistent times, along with documentation of dizziness or palpitations. Assess for orthostatic blood pressure changes at follow-up.

Recommend a low-sodium dietary pattern, regular activity as tolerated, moderation of alcohol if applicable, and maintenance of current nonsmoking status. Counsel regarding gradual position changes and fall precautions because of intermittent dizziness.

Recommend ECG evaluation for palpitations and assessment for hypertensive cardiac effects. Consider urinalysis, lipid profile, and renal function testing as part of cardiovascular and diabetes risk assessment; repeat serum electrolytes after treatment is initiated or adjusted.

Reviewed urgent precautions: seek emergency care for severe or persistent headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, syncope, or acute visual change. Arrange close outpatient reassessment after home readings and medication review.
