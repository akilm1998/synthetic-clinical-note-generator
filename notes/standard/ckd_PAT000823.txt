Chief Complaint
Annual review and chronic kidney disease follow-up.

Subjective
Blake Hunter is a 79-year-old man, 164 cm tall and 66.4 kg, with BMI 24.7 kg/m², presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 38-year history of chronic kidney disease in the setting of longstanding hypertension and coronary artery disease, each present for approximately 26 and 28 years, respectively. Chronic kidney disease is currently not under active management.

He reports fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath. He continues to smoke. No medication list is provided for review.

Objective
Vital signs: Blood pressure 164/96 mmHg, pulse 77/min, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Recent laboratory results: Serum creatinine 3.26 mg/dL, estimated GFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 91 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment
Chronic kidney disease with significant reduction in renal function and markedly elevated BUN and creatinine. The prolonged disease history and current lack of active management warrant prompt reassessment and renal-focused care. He does not currently report symptoms suggestive of fluid overload or prominent uremic prodrome, although fatigue may be related to chronic kidney disease or other comorbidity.

Hypertension, longstanding and poorly controlled today at 164/96 mmHg. This is an important modifiable risk factor for further renal and cardiovascular deterioration.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Fatigue, without associated dyspnea, edema, nausea, pruritus, sleep disturbance, or urinary frequency change reported today.

Plan
Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of chronic kidney disease, assessment of progression, medication review, renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate, and evaluation for potentially reversible contributors.

Obtain repeat renal function and serum electrolyte testing, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, complete blood count, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Consider renal ultrasound as part of structural assessment if not previously completed.

Address blood pressure urgently with confirmation of readings and initiation or adjustment of an appropriate antihypertensive regimen after review of current medications, renal function, potassium, and cardiovascular history. Encourage avoidance of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Review dietary sodium and renal dietary needs with appropriate nutritional guidance.

Assess fatigue in the context of chronic kidney disease and screen for anemia and mineral-bone complications with the planned laboratory evaluation.

Continue cardiovascular risk reduction, including blood pressure optimization, smoking cessation counseling, and review of lipid and antiplatelet management as clinically appropriate for known coronary artery disease. Smoking cessation was strongly advised.

Review preventive care, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, shingles, and other age-appropriate immunizations, along with individualized cancer screening and fall-risk assessment.

Seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, markedly reduced urine output, or new swelling. Follow up promptly after laboratory testing and nephrology assessment.
