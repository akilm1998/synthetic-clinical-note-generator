Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Olivia Brooks is a 95-year-old woman with a 56-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and longstanding dyslipidemia. She presents for review of diabetes treatment effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance. Current glycemic management is not established in the provided history, and no specific glucose-lowering medication regimen is documented for review today.

She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. No medication-related adverse effects are reported. She continues to smoke.

Objective

Height 180 cm. Weight 72.9 kg. BMI 22.5 kg/m².

Blood pressure 130/78 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Recent laboratory results: hemoglobin A1c 9.4%; fasting glucose 220 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled based on hemoglobin A1c of 9.4% and fasting glucose of 220 mg/dL. Despite significant biochemical hyperglycemia, she is currently asymptomatic and has no reported neuropathic or infectious manifestations. Given her age and 56-year disease duration, treatment intensification should be individualized with attention to functional status, hypoglycemia risk, treatment burden, and goals of care.

Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance cannot be adequately assessed without confirmation of the complete current medication list and actual use pattern. Longstanding dyslipidemia is also noted. Active tobacco use increases overall vascular risk.

Plan

Complete medication reconciliation with the patient and caregiver, including all prescription medications, over-the-counter agents, and supplements. Confirm whether any glucose-lowering therapy is being taken, dosing accuracy, missed doses, access barriers, and prior intolerance or hypoglycemia.

Discuss initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy using a conservative, individualized approach. Metformin may be considered if renal function, gastrointestinal tolerance, nutritional status, and overall treatment goals are appropriate. Renal function should be assessed before selecting or dosing medication. Avoid treatment escalation that creates unnecessary hypoglycemia risk, particularly with insulin or insulin secretagogues, unless clinically indicated and closely monitored.

Provide individualized dietary counseling emphasizing consistent carbohydrate intake and avoidance of concentrated sugars. Encourage physical activity appropriate to mobility and fall-risk status.

Recommend home glucose monitoring if medication is initiated or intensified, with education regarding symptoms and management of hypoglycemia and marked hyperglycemia. Repeat hemoglobin A1c after an appropriate interval to assess response to the confirmed treatment plan.

Continue cardiovascular risk assessment and address dyslipidemia and tobacco use through shared decision-making, taking age, comorbidity burden, life expectancy, and patient preferences into account. Reassess promptly after medication reconciliation or sooner for polyuria, polydipsia, vomiting, confusion, weakness, or hypoglycemic symptoms.
