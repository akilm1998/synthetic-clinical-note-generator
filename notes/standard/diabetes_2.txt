Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for type 2 diabetes with increased urinary frequency, increased hunger, and intermittent blurred vision.

Subjective

Damian Boyd is a 45-year-old man with a 4-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus presenting for focused follow-up. He reports frequent urination, increased hunger, and blurred vision. He denies increased thirst, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. He does not smoke. He has longstanding obesity, present for approximately 20 years.

Objective

Height 164 cm; weight 97.6 kg; BMI 36.3 kg/m². Blood pressure 124/82 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 19/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98%. Recent HbA1c is 7.7% and fasting glucose is 147 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with glycemic control above goal and symptoms consistent with intermittent hyperglycemia. Current symptoms include urinary frequency, increased hunger, and blurred vision. No reported neuropathic symptoms, abnormal wound healing, or recurrent infections.

Class II obesity, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed that the HbA1c and fasting glucose indicate suboptimal glycemic control and may be contributing to his urinary frequency, hunger, and blurred vision. Reinforced nutrition changes emphasizing portion control, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, and increased dietary fiber. Encouraged gradual, sustainable physical activity as tolerated and weight reduction given BMI of 36.3 kg/m².

Reviewed the importance of monitoring glucose as directed and maintaining hydration. Medication therapy and possible adjustment or initiation should be reviewed based on his current regimen and home glucose pattern; no current diabetes medications were provided in the available history.

Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine/eGFR, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes eye and foot evaluations as part of ongoing care. He should seek prompt evaluation for worsening blurred vision, vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, marked weakness, or persistently very high glucose readings.

Follow up closely for reassessment of symptoms and glycemic control.
