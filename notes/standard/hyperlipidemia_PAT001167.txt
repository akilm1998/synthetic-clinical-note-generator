Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective
Bob Hill is a 54-year-old man with a 21-year history of hyperlipidemia and known coronary artery disease for approximately 8 years. He is not currently receiving documented lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid testing showed total cholesterol 272 mg/dL, LDL-C 194 mg/dL, HDL-C 30 mg/dL, and triglycerides 239 mg/dL. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness. Reports fatty deposits involving the skin and eyelids, consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma. He does not smoke. Weight is 77.4 kg at 177 cm (BMI 24.7 kg/m²).

Objective
Vital signs: BP 122/82 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respirations 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory data: Total cholesterol 272 mg/dL; LDL-C 194 mg/dL; HDL-C 30 mg/dL; triglycerides 239 mg/dL.

Assessment
Severe mixed dyslipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C, low HDL-C, and moderately elevated triglycerides. In the setting of established coronary artery disease, this represents very high atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk and requires prompt secondary-prevention management. Cutaneous xanthomas and xanthelasma support longstanding, clinically significant lipid elevation. Currently without reported anginal, claudication, or neurologic symptoms. Blood pressure and BMI are within acceptable range.

Plan
Discussed the significance of the lipid profile and the increased cardiovascular risk associated with untreated LDL-C of 194 mg/dL in the setting of coronary artery disease.

Recommend prompt initiation of guideline-directed high-intensity statin therapy after medication reconciliation and review of contraindications, with treatment intensity directed toward a substantial LDL-C reduction. If LDL-C remains above goal despite maximally tolerated statin therapy, recommend addition of ezetimibe and consideration of a PCSK9 inhibitor based on response and cardiovascular risk.

Recommend dietary reduction of saturated and trans fats, increased soluble fiber, regular aerobic activity as tolerated, and continued tobacco avoidance.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response and adherence. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and measurement of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) for further risk characterization. Coronary artery calcium scoring is not expected to change management given established coronary artery disease.

Advised urgent evaluation for new or worsening chest discomfort, dyspnea, syncope, or exertional leg symptoms. Close follow-up is recommended to establish lipid-lowering therapy and review response.
