Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Margaret Walsh is a 72-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes, diagnosed approximately 14 years ago. Diabetes control is currently suboptimal, with recent HbA1c of 9.9% and fasting glucose of 233 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has chronic kidney disease of approximately 8 years’ duration, obesity of approximately 30 years’ duration, and dyslipidemia of approximately 35 years’ duration. She does not smoke. No current diabetes medications or other medication regimen are provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 133/81 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 165 cm, weight 87.7 kg, BMI 32.2 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.9%. Fasting glucose 233 mg/dL.

No acute distress or diabetes-related symptoms reported today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, with markedly elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose indicating poor glycemic control. Despite the degree of hyperglycemia, she currently denies polyuria, polydipsia, visual changes, constitutional symptoms, neuropathic symptoms, or infectious complications.

Chronic kidney disease, duration approximately 8 years; renal function values were not provided and will be important in selecting diabetes therapy.

Obesity, BMI 32.2 kg/m².

Dyslipidemia.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the increased risk of microvascular and cardiovascular complications. Recommend initiation or intensification of pharmacologic diabetes treatment after reviewing current medications, allergies, renal function, and treatment preferences. Any medication choice and dosing should be adjusted for chronic kidney disease; options may include metformin if renal function permits, with consideration of agents offering renal and cardiovascular benefit when clinically appropriate.

Order or obtain serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess kidney status and guide treatment. Recommend repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response; no additional result is available today.

Reviewed nutrition counseling focused on reducing concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, and gradual weight reduction. Encourage regular physical activity as tolerated and appropriate for age and comorbid kidney disease.

Recommend routine diabetic foot examination and annual dilated retinal evaluation. Counsel regarding daily foot inspection and prompt evaluation of wounds, redness, or infection. Continue monitoring for symptoms of hyperglycemia, hypoglycemia once treatment is initiated, neuropathy, and visual change.

Continue outpatient follow-up for diabetes management after medication and renal assessment, with earlier evaluation for worsening symptoms or persistently elevated glucose.
