Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for longstanding elevated cholesterol and triglycerides.

Subjective

Benjamin Murphy is a 61-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 17 years and not currently managed with documented lipid-lowering therapy. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 302 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 196 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 42 mg/dL, and triglycerides 320 mg/dL.

He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain consistent with claudication, and dizziness. He reports fatty skin deposits involving the xanthelasma region and xanthomas. He is a nonsmoker. Relevant comorbidity includes non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, present for approximately 5 years.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 126/75 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 176 cm; weight 78.1 kg; BMI 25.2 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Total cholesterol 302 mg/dL; LDL cholesterol 196 mg/dL; HDL cholesterol 42 mg/dL; triglycerides 320 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Hyperlipidemia with marked LDL elevation and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia

The lipid profile demonstrates severe untreated atherogenic dyslipidemia, with LDL cholesterol 196 mg/dL and triglycerides 320 mg/dL. The duration of approximately 17 years and presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma raise concern for a significant underlying lipid disorder, including possible inherited susceptibility, although family history is not documented. He has no reported anginal or claudication symptoms at this visit. His blood pressure, BMI, and nonsmoking status are favorable, but his lipid burden places him at increased lifetime atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk.

2. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease

Established comorbidity of approximately 5 years. This is clinically relevant to metabolic risk assessment and should be considered when monitoring lipid therapy. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease alone is not a contraindication to statin treatment.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and triglycerides and the importance of active lipid-lowering therapy. Recommend initiation of a high-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after shared decision-making and review of medication history, allergies, and baseline hepatic safety testing. The treatment goal is a substantial reduction in LDL cholesterol; if the response is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe and consideration of specialist-directed therapy would be appropriate.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation and dose adjustment to assess response. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for additional risk characterization, particularly given the prolonged elevation and cutaneous lipid deposits. Evaluation for secondary contributors should include clinically appropriate metabolic and hepatic testing, with interpretation in the context of his known non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Counseled regarding reduction of saturated and trans fats, limitation of refined carbohydrates and excess alcohol, increased soluble fiber, consumption of vegetables and unsaturated fats, and regular aerobic and resistance exercise. Continue tobacco avoidance. Coronary artery calcium imaging may be considered if treatment decisions remain uncertain, although the LDL level and physical findings already support active pharmacologic management.

Follow-up is recommended after initiation or adjustment of therapy to review tolerance, adherence, lipid response, and ongoing cardiovascular risk reduction.
