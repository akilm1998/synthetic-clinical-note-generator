Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Nora Kelly is a 66-year-old woman, 176 cm tall and weighing 79.3 kg (BMI 25.6 kg/m²), presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 32 years. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, increased or decreased urinary frequency, and shortness of breath. She does not smoke.

Her medical history is also notable for gout for 6 years, coronary artery disease for 7 years, and anemia of chronic disease for 5 years. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 152/84 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respirations 19/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Recent laboratory results show serum creatinine 3.25 mg/dL, BUN 71 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 17 mL/min/1.73 m². No peripheral edema or respiratory symptoms are reported. Other physical examination findings are not documented for this visit.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated creatinine and BUN. The patient has persistent fatigue but no current symptoms suggestive of fluid overload or significant uremic prodrome. Blood pressure is above goal today. Overall disease control requires closer outpatient management and monitoring.

Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding, likely contributing to fatigue.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

Gout, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed the importance of re-establishing consistent kidney disease management given the creatinine of 3.25 mg/dL, BUN of 71 mg/dL, and estimated GFR of 17 mL/min/1.73 m². Recommend nephrology evaluation for comprehensive CKD management and planning based on renal function trajectory.

Repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, creatinine, BUN, and estimated GFR is recommended, along with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. Obtain CBC to reassess anemia and intact parathyroid hormone as part of CKD-related complication monitoring. Renal ultrasound may be considered if not recently performed.

Blood pressure should be rechecked and addressed promptly, with review of home readings if available. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications, and review all prescription and over-the-counter medications for renal dosing. Encourage appropriate hydration without excessive fluid or sodium intake.

Reviewed symptoms that should prompt urgent evaluation, including new or worsening shortness of breath, rapidly developing swelling, markedly reduced urination, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness. Follow up after updated laboratory testing, or sooner for worsening symptoms.
