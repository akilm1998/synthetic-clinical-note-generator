Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Jasmine Spencer is a 49-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension, present for approximately 11 years and not currently under active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. No interval hypertensive symptoms reported.

She does not smoke. Weight is 59.2 kg at 153 cm, with BMI 25.3 kg/m². She also has a 4-year history of hyperlipidemia.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 146/93 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

General: Appears clinically stable based on vital signs and absence of acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms.

Laboratory Data: Sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 3.9 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure today. She is asymptomatic, without features suggesting hypertensive emergency. Hypertension has been present for approximately 11 years and is not currently being actively managed.

Hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 4 years.

BMI 25.3 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the importance of consistent blood pressure management and the long-term risks of untreated hypertension, including cardiovascular and renal disease.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a validated upper-arm cuff, recording readings at different times of day, and bringing the log to follow-up.

Recommend lifestyle measures including limiting dietary sodium, following a heart-healthy eating pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, maintaining a healthy weight, and avoiding excess alcohol.

Given the persistent elevation and prolonged history of hypertension, recommend initiating antihypertensive pharmacologic therapy after shared decision-making and review of baseline clinical factors. Medication selection and dosing to be addressed at this visit or promptly at follow-up.

Recommend repeat serum electrolytes after initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy. Consider ECG, urinalysis, and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk assessment and hypertension evaluation.

Return for blood pressure reassessment in approximately 2–4 weeks, sooner for persistently higher home readings. Seek urgent care for severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.
