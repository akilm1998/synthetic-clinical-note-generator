Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for chronic kidney disease and markedly reduced renal function.

Subjective

Andrew Morris is a 31-year-old man with approximately 10 years of chronic kidney disease presenting for focused follow-up. His renal disease has not been actively managed. Recent laboratory results show creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 74 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m². He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, and shortness of breath.

Relevant comorbidities include hypertension, gout, and hyperlipidemia, each present for approximately 1 year. He currently smokes. No medication regimen is provided; medication reconciliation and renal-dose review are important at urgent evaluation.

Objective

Height 173 cm; weight 76.6 kg; BMI 25.6 kg/m².

Blood pressure 139/97 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory results: serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 74 mg/dL, estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m².

No uremic or volume-overload symptoms are reported today. No additional examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with severely reduced kidney function and significant azotemia, present for approximately 10 years and currently without active management. Although he denies current uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, the degree of renal impairment warrants urgent specialist assessment and evaluation for complications, renal replacement planning, and potentially reversible contributors.

Hypertension, with elevated diastolic pressure.

Gout and hyperlipidemia.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Recommend same-day urgent nephrology evaluation or emergency department assessment given creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 74 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m², even in the absence of symptoms. Immediate assessment should include electrolytes, acid-base status, volume status, CBC, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and consideration of renal ultrasound as clinically indicated.

Perform complete medication reconciliation and avoid nephrotoxic agents, particularly NSAIDs; ensure all medications are appropriately renally dosed. Blood pressure management should be reviewed urgently in the context of advanced renal dysfunction. Counsel regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of over-the-counter supplements or medications without clinician approval.

Proceed immediately to emergency care for nausea or vomiting, worsening fatigue, confusion, muscle weakness, chest pain, shortness of breath, progressive edema, or a significant change in urine output.
