Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Isaac Henderson is a 53-year-old man with a 21-year history of hypertension presenting for focused follow-up after an elevated blood pressure reading. He reports no headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Hypertension has not been actively managed. Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for 7 years, hyperlipidemia for 11 years, and coronary artery disease diagnosed 1 year ago. He does not smoke.

Objective

Height 171 cm, weight 82.2 kg, BMI 28.1 kg/m². Blood pressure 163/95 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%. Serum sodium 145 mmol/L and potassium 4.4 mmol/L. No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported.

Assessment

Persistent uncontrolled essential hypertension in an asymptomatic 53-year-old man with chronic kidney disease and known coronary artery disease. Current reading is significantly elevated but without reported features of hypertensive emergency. Long duration of hypertension and associated cardiovascular and renal comorbidity increase overall risk. Overweight status may also contribute.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement with appropriate cuff and confirm readings outside the office if feasible. Arrange prompt primary care follow-up to initiate or intensify antihypertensive therapy, with medication selection guided by chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease, renal function, and repeat electrolytes. Review adherence and barriers to care at the next visit.

Recommend reduction of dietary sodium, weight reduction, regular activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Continue tobacco avoidance. Recommend evaluation with renal function testing, urinalysis, lipid profile, and ECG as clinically appropriate; only sodium and potassium results are available today.

Seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, acute visual change, or other evidence of acute end-organ symptoms. Close follow-up is recommended for blood pressure reassessment.
