Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Gavin Holland is a 46-year-old male, height 186 cm, weight 81.3 kg, BMI 23.5 kg/m², presenting for routine follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 14 years. He reports persistent fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No interval uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported.

His chronic kidney disease has not been under active management. He has a 7-year history of hypertension. He currently smokes. Medication list and adherence were not provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 151/92 mmHg, pulse 78/min, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory data:
Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL
BUN 93 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m²

No peripheral edema or other examination findings were provided. Patient denies shortness of breath and is oxygenating normally at rest.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of approximately 14 years’ duration with markedly reduced estimated GFR, creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, and BUN 93 mg/dL. The patient reports fatigue but no current nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, change in urination, edema, or dyspnea. Given the degree of renal impairment and limited prior management, this requires prompt nephrology evaluation and active renal replacement therapy planning.

Hypertension, currently above goal at 151/92 mmHg, in the setting of advanced chronic kidney disease.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for assessment of advanced kidney disease, determination of renal replacement therapy needs, and discussion of dialysis access and transplant evaluation as clinically appropriate.

Recommend prompt repeat assessment with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, CBC, intact parathyroid hormone, urinalysis, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. Recommend renal ultrasound to assess kidney structure and exclude obstruction.

Review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal medications at the earliest opportunity. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a clinician. Medication dosing should be adjusted for the markedly reduced kidney function.

Address blood pressure promptly with an individualized renal-protective regimen after medication reconciliation and review of potassium and volume status. Encourage sodium restriction and avoidance of excess dietary sodium.

Discuss smoking cessation, as continued smoking increases cardiovascular and kidney-related risk.

Seek emergency evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, marked weakness, progressive swelling, inability to urinate, or other acute deterioration.

Close follow-up is required after urgent renal evaluation and laboratory reassessment.
