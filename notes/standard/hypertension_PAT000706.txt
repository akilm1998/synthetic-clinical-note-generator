Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Hayden Johnston is a 78-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension, present for approximately 26 years. He is not currently under active treatment for blood pressure control. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 37 years, chronic kidney disease for 2 years, and hyperlipidemia for 6 years. He continues to smoke. No medication regimen is provided today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 165/99 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Height 189 cm, weight 88.2 kg, BMI 24.7 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 136 mmol/L, potassium 4.5 mmol/L.

Patient is currently asymptomatic from a cardiovascular and neurologic standpoint based on today’s review of systems. No additional examination findings are documented.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated blood pressure and inadequate current control. He has no symptoms or findings provided to suggest an acute hypertensive emergency today, but the degree of elevation is clinically significant, particularly in the setting of long-standing diabetes and chronic kidney disease.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Chronic kidney disease.

Hyperlipidemia.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of re-establishing active blood pressure management to reduce cardiovascular and renal risk. Recommend confirming the elevation with repeat measurements and home blood pressure monitoring, with a written log for review. Given the persistent elevation and comorbid diabetes and chronic kidney disease, initiation of antihypertensive therapy should be discussed promptly; an agent with renal and diabetic benefit, such as an ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker, may be appropriate if clinically suitable. Renal function and electrolytes should be monitored after initiation or adjustment; current sodium and potassium are within acceptable ranges.

Counseled on a lower-sodium diet, regular physical activity as tolerated, moderation of alcohol if applicable, and continued weight maintenance. Strongly encouraged smoking cessation and offered outpatient cessation support.

Recommend review of diabetes and lipid management as part of overall cardiovascular risk reduction. Consider routine evaluation with urinalysis, lipid profile, and electrocardiogram as clinically appropriate.

Advised immediate emergency evaluation for new chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, focal neurologic symptoms, acute visual changes, confusion, or other concerning symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to reassess blood pressure and finalize treatment.
