Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Valeria Ellis is a 64-year-old woman with a 23-year history of hypertension, currently without active blood pressure management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. She does not smoke. She has longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus for 24 years and chronic kidney disease for 19 years. Weight is 74.0 kg at 179 cm, with BMI 23.1 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure 170/100 mmHg. Pulse 69 bpm. Respiratory rate 18/min. Temperature 36.8°C. Oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Serum sodium 141 mmol/L. Serum potassium 3.5 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with significantly elevated blood pressure today in the setting of prolonged inadequate management. She is currently asymptomatic, with no reported features suggestive of acute hypertensive end-organ symptoms. Her chronic kidney disease and diabetes increase her cardiovascular and renal risk and make timely treatment particularly important.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, increasing overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, requiring consideration in antihypertensive selection and monitoring.

Plan

Confirm the elevated reading with repeat seated measurements using appropriate technique and arrange prompt same-day clinical reassessment. Initiation or re-initiation of antihypertensive therapy should occur promptly, with selection guided by chronic kidney disease, diabetes, renal function, potassium level, and contraindications. The current potassium is 3.5 mmol/L; renal function and electrolytes should be monitored after therapy is started or adjusted.

Recommend dietary sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excessive alcohol intake. Continue attention to weight maintenance; BMI is currently 23.1 kg/m².

Obtain or review kidney function, urinalysis, lipid profile, and electrocardiogram as part of cardiovascular and hypertensive risk assessment. Emphasized adherence to follow-up and blood pressure monitoring.

Proceed to emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, vision loss, confusion, or recurrent epistaxis. Close outpatient follow-up is required after treatment initiation or adjustment.
