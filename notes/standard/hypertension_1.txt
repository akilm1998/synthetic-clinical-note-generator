Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of hypertension.

Subjective

Victoria Richardson is a 33-year-old woman with a 7-year history of essential hypertension, currently without active treatment management documented. She reports dizziness, chest pain, shortness of breath, and intermittent nosebleeds. She denies headache, blurred vision, palpitations, and fatigue. Medication effectiveness and tolerance cannot be assessed because no current antihypertensive regimen is provided for review. She is a current smoker.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, and coronary artery disease, each present for approximately 1 year.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 147/91 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Height 164 cm, weight 53.8 kg, BMI 20.0 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 146 mmol/L, potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent elevation above goal in the setting of a 7-year history without documented active pharmacologic management. Current chest pain and shortness of breath are concerning symptoms, particularly with known coronary artery disease, and require prompt assessment rather than routine medication adjustment. Dizziness and epistaxis may also occur with blood pressure elevation, although the current reading is not in the severe range.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, and coronary artery disease increase overall cardiovascular risk.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Because of active chest pain and shortness of breath with known coronary artery disease, advised immediate emergency evaluation for assessment of acute cardiopulmonary disease. She should not drive herself if symptoms are ongoing or worsening.

Defer outpatient antihypertensive initiation or adjustment until urgent evaluation is completed and an accurate medication reconciliation is performed. If no contraindication is identified, establish a consistent antihypertensive regimen with close monitoring of response and tolerance. Review adherence, dosing schedule, adverse effects, and home blood pressure readings at follow-up.

Recommend low-sodium dietary habits, regular physical activity as medically tolerated after acute evaluation, and smoking cessation. Cardiovascular risk reduction should be coordinated with management of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and coronary artery disease.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after urgent evaluation for blood pressure reassessment and medication management. Seek emergency care immediately for worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, or persistent nosebleeding.
