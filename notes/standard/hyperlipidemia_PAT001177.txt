Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Madelyn Stewart is a 70-year-old woman presenting for evaluation and management of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 16 years and not currently under active treatment. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 296 mg/dL, LDL-C 195 mg/dL, HDL-C 39 mg/dL, and triglycerides 309 mg/dL.

She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. She currently smokes tobacco. She has longstanding obesity, reported for approximately 47 years. No medication regimen, family history, or prior cardiovascular events were provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 130/82 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 170 cm, weight 113.9 kg, BMI 39.4 kg/m².

Lipid profile:
Total cholesterol 296 mg/dL
LDL-C 195 mg/dL
HDL-C 39 mg/dL
Triglycerides 309 mg/dL

No xanthomas or xanthelasma reported. No cardiovascular or claudication symptoms reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with severe LDL-C elevation and concomitant moderate hypertriglyceridemia. LDL-C of 195 mg/dL represents a markedly elevated atherogenic burden and warrants pharmacologic lipid-lowering therapy independent of calculated 10-year risk. The low HDL-C and ongoing tobacco use further increase cardiovascular risk. The 16-year duration of untreated or undertreated dyslipidemia, combined with LDL-C near 200 mg/dL, raises consideration of an underlying genetic predisposition, although no family history is available in the current history.

Obesity, BMI 39.4 kg/m², longstanding.

Active tobacco use.

Currently without reported anginal symptoms, claudication, dizziness, or visible lipid deposits.

Plan

Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after shared decision-making, with a goal of substantially reducing LDL-C. Atorvastatin or rosuvastatin would be appropriate first-line options. Review potential adverse effects, drug interactions, and the importance of adherence before prescribing.

Repeat a fasting lipid panel approximately 4–12 weeks after treatment initiation or dose adjustment, followed by periodic monitoring. If LDL-C remains significantly elevated despite adherence to maximally tolerated statin therapy, consider addition of ezetimibe and subsequent specialist-directed therapies as clinically indicated.

Recommend evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia, including thyroid, hepatic, renal, and glycemic disorders, with testing guided by the broader clinical history and examination. Consider one-time lipoprotein(a) measurement and apolipoprotein B assessment for further risk stratification. Given the LDL-C level, treatment should not be deferred while awaiting coronary artery calcium testing; CAC imaging may be considered only if there is uncertainty regarding treatment decisions.

Counseled regarding a Mediterranean-style eating pattern, reduction of saturated and trans fats, limitation of refined carbohydrates and alcohol to address triglyceride elevation, increased dietary fiber, and gradual physical activity as tolerated. Weight reduction was encouraged, with attention to sustainable nutrition and activity goals.

Strongly advised complete smoking cessation; offer structured cessation support and pharmacotherapy at follow-up if acceptable.

Follow up after initiation of therapy and repeat lipid testing to assess response, tolerance, and need for intensification. Obtain a more detailed family history of premature cardiovascular disease and markedly elevated cholesterol at the next visit.
