Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Patrick Harper is a 73-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 37-year history of chronic kidney disease, with management currently not established. He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, changes in urination, or shortness of breath. No uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported today.

His medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 14 years, anemia of chronic disease for 8 years, and gout for 11 years. He continues to smoke. Current medication list and adherence were not provided and should be reviewed carefully, including any over-the-counter medications or NSAID use.

Objective

Height 174 cm. Weight 81.4 kg. BMI 26.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 143/91 mmHg, pulse 83/min, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent renal indices: serum creatinine 3.34 mg/dL, BUN 65 mg/dL, estimated GFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m².

No abnormal physical examination findings were provided. He is not reporting edema, dyspnea, or other symptoms suggestive of current volume overload.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine, present for approximately 37 years. Although he is currently asymptomatic, the degree of renal impairment warrants prompt structured evaluation and renal care planning. No formal disease stage is assigned today.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 14-year duration, with renal disease requiring ongoing risk-factor assessment.

Anemia of chronic disease, 8-year duration; current hemoglobin is not available for review.

Gout, 11-year duration, requiring attention to renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic medications.

Elevated blood pressure at today’s visit, 143/91 mmHg. A single reading is insufficient to establish a new diagnosis, but blood pressure control is important for preservation of renal function.

Active tobacco use.

BMI 26.9 kg/m².

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for comprehensive CKD evaluation, renal-protective management, assessment of complications, and discussion of future renal replacement planning if clinically indicated.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, creatinine/eGFR and BUN, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone. Consider renal ultrasound as part of structural evaluation. Results should be reviewed promptly, particularly for hyperkalemia, worsening azotemia, metabolic complications, or progression of anemia.

Review all prescribed, over-the-counter, and herbal medications. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a clinician. Ensure all medications are appropriately renally dosed, including any treatment used for gout or diabetes. Medication reconciliation should include confirmation of allergies and adherence.

Monitor blood pressure with repeat office or home readings. Reinforce a low-sodium diet, avoidance of excess processed foods, appropriate hydration, and avoidance of tobacco. Diabetes control should be reviewed with updated glycemic monitoring, with attention to individualized targets given his age and renal function.

Discuss kidney-protective nutrition, including moderation of sodium and avoidance of excessive protein intake. Dietary potassium and phosphorus recommendations should be guided by laboratory results and renal dietitian input.

Continue surveillance for anemia, mineral and bone complications, worsening edema, reduced urine output, nausea, pruritus, confusion, chest discomfort, or shortness of breath. Seek urgent medical attention for severe breathlessness, chest pain, confusion, markedly reduced urination, or rapidly progressive swelling.

Smoking cessation was strongly advised; offer counseling and pharmacologic support after medication and contraindication review.

Complete age-appropriate preventive care, including immunization review, influenza and COVID vaccination as appropriate, pneumococcal vaccination, shingles vaccination, fall-risk assessment, dental and vision care, and recommended colorectal and prostate cancer screening based on prior screening history and patient preference. Because he is a male smoker, assess eligibility for abdominal aortic aneurysm screening and lung cancer screening according to smoking exposure history.

Follow up after the above evaluation, or sooner for any new renal or volume-related symptoms.
