Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Aria Patterson is a 94-year-old woman, 163 cm and 66.4 kg (BMI 25.0 kg/m²), presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension present for approximately 25 years. Blood pressure has not been actively managed recently. She reports intermittent palpitations and fatigue. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. She does not smoke.

She also has a history of hyperlipidemia for approximately 20 years. No current medication regimen is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 157/93 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 4.9 mmol/L.

General and cardiopulmonary examination findings are not otherwise documented today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently above the desired range on today’s reading. She has no symptoms to suggest hypertensive emergency, including chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or visual change. Given her age and prolonged period without active management, treatment should be initiated cautiously with attention to orthostatic symptoms, falls, renal function, and tolerability.

Palpitations and fatigue. Pulse is regular in the recorded vital signs at 72 bpm, but intermittent palpitations warrant rhythm assessment, particularly in the setting of hypertension and advanced age.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding for approximately 20 years. Cardiovascular risk reduction should be reviewed in the context of overall health status, functional goals, and patient preference.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure after seated rest and obtain home blood pressure readings if feasible, including attention to symptoms with standing. Recommend gradual blood pressure reduction rather than aggressive lowering. Discuss initiation of a low-dose antihypertensive agent, such as amlodipine or an ACE inhibitor/ARB, after confirming treatment goals and reviewing renal function, orthostatic symptoms, and contraindications. Reassess blood pressure and tolerance within a short interval after treatment initiation.

Obtain an ECG to evaluate the reported palpitations. Advise urgent evaluation for persistent or worsening palpitations associated with chest pain, shortness of breath, syncope, new neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure.

Recommend urinalysis and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular and hypertension risk assessment. Review dietary sodium intake, physical activity appropriate to her functional capacity, and avoidance of excess alcohol if applicable. Continue smoking abstinence.

Review management options for hyperlipidemia, balancing potential benefit with age, comorbidity burden, life expectancy, and patient preferences.

Follow up for blood pressure review and medication decision/titration after home readings and recommended evaluation.
