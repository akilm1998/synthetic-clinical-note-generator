Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Connor Walters is a 59-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension, present for approximately 9 years. Blood pressure management has not been active recently. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds.

Medical history is also notable for coronary artery disease for 7 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 8 years. He currently smokes. Weight is 92.4 kg at 186 cm, with BMI 26.7 kg/m². No current antihypertensive medications were provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 143/94 mmHg, pulse 87 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 144 mmol/L, potassium 3.8 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently above the desired outpatient range at 143/94 mmHg. No symptoms suggesting hypertensive emergency. Control is suboptimal in the setting of known coronary artery disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Coronary artery disease, present for 7 years. No current chest pain, dyspnea, or palpitations.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for 8 years. No diabetes-related laboratory data were provided for review today.

Tobacco use, ongoing.

Plan

Discussed the importance of re-establishing active blood pressure management given the duration of hypertension and comorbid coronary artery disease and diabetes. Recommend confirming readings with appropriately sized cuff and keeping a home blood pressure log, including seated readings taken after several minutes of rest.

Recommend follow-up visit to review home readings and discuss initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy. Medication selection should account for his coronary artery disease and diabetes, with renal function and repeat electrolytes considered when clinically appropriate. Continue lifestyle measures including limiting dietary sodium, regular physical activity as tolerated, maintaining a healthy weight, and moderating alcohol intake.

Strongly encouraged smoking cessation and discussed its importance for blood pressure and cardiovascular risk reduction.

Recommend routine cardiovascular risk assessment, including lipid profile, urinalysis, and ECG if not recently completed. Continue monitoring for chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly elevated blood pressure; seek urgent care if these occur.

Return for blood pressure reassessment and review of management plan.
