Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Sophia Turner is a 39-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of hypertension, present for approximately 3 years and not currently under active treatment. She reports fatigue but denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or epistaxis. No symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive crisis are reported.

She has a history of coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. Smoking status: never smoker. Current weight is 91.2 kg at a height of 177 cm, with BMI 29.1 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 148/93 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 143 mmol/L, potassium 4.0 mmol/L.

Blood pressure remains elevated above goal on today’s reading. Electrolytes are within expected range.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, persistent elevation noted today. She is asymptomatic without findings concerning for hypertensive emergency, but her blood pressure is inadequately controlled and she has not been receiving active management. Coexisting coronary artery disease increases the importance of consistent blood pressure control.

Overweight by BMI, which may contribute to cardiovascular risk.

Fatigue, nonspecific; no associated cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported today.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active treatment and regular monitoring of blood pressure. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with measurements taken at consistent times and a written log for review. Reviewed low-sodium, heart-healthy dietary choices, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and limiting alcohol intake.

Recommend initiation of antihypertensive therapy after discussion of risks, benefits, and patient preferences, with medication selection guided by her history of coronary artery disease and baseline renal function/electrolyte assessment. No current medication regimen was provided today. Recheck blood pressure and tolerance after treatment initiation or adjustment.

Recommend completing cardiovascular risk assessment, including lipid profile, urinalysis, and ECG, if not recently performed. Continue coordination of care for her coronary artery disease.

Seek urgent medical attention for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic deficits, vision changes, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Follow up in the outpatient setting within several weeks for blood pressure review and management update.
