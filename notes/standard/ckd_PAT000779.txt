Chief Complaint

Medication review for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Nicole Harper is a 36-year-old woman with approximately 10 years of chronic kidney disease, hypertension for 3 years, coronary artery disease for 1 year, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 1 year. She presents for review of treatment effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance. She reports fatigue but denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. Current medication names, doses, adherence, and treatment tolerance require verification before medication effectiveness can be assessed. She is a current smoker.

Objective

Height 164 cm; weight 71.5 kg; BMI 26.6 kg/m².

Blood pressure 152/94 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m². BUN 61 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and persistent fatigue. The available renal indices indicate advanced kidney dysfunction in the setting of a 10-year history; formal staging is not assigned here because it was not provided. She currently denies symptoms suggestive of overt volume overload or prominent uremic manifestations, but the severity of renal impairment warrants urgent specialist assessment and active management.

Hypertension, currently above goal at 152/94 mmHg, with chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism associated with chronic kidney disease.

Medication review is incomplete without a verified active medication list. Therefore, medication efficacy, adherence, renal dosing, and adverse effects cannot be reliably determined today.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of severe chronic kidney disease, assessment of renal replacement therapy needs, and complication management. She should seek emergency care promptly for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, progressive edema, persistent vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, or substantially decreased urine output.

Obtain and reconcile the complete medication list, including prescription medications, over-the-counter agents, and supplements. Assess adherence, tolerability, blood-pressure response, renal dosing, and avoidance of nephrotoxic medications once the regimen is verified. No medication changes are made today without an accurate current list.

Reassess blood pressure promptly and address hypertension in the context of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease.

Recommend renal evaluation including urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum electrolytes, complete blood count, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound if not recently completed. Continue monitoring renal function and CKD-related complications through nephrology.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.
