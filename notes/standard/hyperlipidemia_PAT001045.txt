Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for longstanding elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Ariana Lawson is a 97-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of hyperlipidemia, reportedly present for approximately 31 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 293 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 206 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 38 mg/dL, and triglycerides 244 mg/dL. She reports xanthelasma. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain consistent with claudication, and dizziness.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 10 years and chronic kidney disease for approximately 22 years. She currently smokes tobacco. Weight is 83.7 kg at a height of 172 cm. No medication or allergy information was provided for this encounter.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 136/81 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 172 cm, weight 83.7 kg, BMI 28.3 kg/m².

Laboratory results:
Total cholesterol 293 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 206 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 38 mg/dL
Triglycerides 244 mg/dL

Xanthelasma is reported. No chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia and low HDL cholesterol. The degree and longstanding nature of LDL elevation, together with xanthelasma, indicate substantial cumulative atherosclerotic risk. Risk is further increased by advanced age, active tobacco use, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, and overweight status. Although she denies current cardiovascular or claudication symptoms, absence of symptoms does not exclude subclinical atherosclerotic disease.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for approximately 10 years, representing an important cardiovascular risk-enhancing comorbidity.

Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 22 years, which is relevant to overall cardiovascular risk and to selection and monitoring of lipid-lowering therapy.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid profile and the patient’s elevated cardiovascular risk. Recommend shared decision-making regarding initiation of lipid-lowering therapy, taking into account her age, functional status, goals of care, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, potential benefits, and medication-related risks. A statin would generally be considered first-line therapy for this degree of LDL elevation, with dose selection and monitoring individualized to renal function and tolerability. If adequate LDL reduction is not achieved or statin therapy is not tolerated, addition of a nonstatin agent such as ezetimibe may be considered.

Recommend confirming current medication use and obtaining baseline safety assessments appropriate for treatment, including renal and hepatic function, before therapy is initiated. Repeat a fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider measurement of lipoprotein(a) or apolipoprotein B if results would meaningfully assist risk stratification or treatment decisions. Additional cardiovascular risk assessment, including consideration of coronary artery calcium imaging, should be individualized given her age, chronic kidney disease, diabetes, and likely impact on management.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess dietary cholesterol; increased intake of vegetables, fiber, and unsaturated fats; and weight maintenance or modest weight reduction as clinically appropriate. Encourage physical activity appropriate to her mobility and overall health.

Strongly advised tobacco cessation and offered cessation counseling. Continued monitoring and coordinated management of diabetes and chronic kidney disease are recommended because both materially affect cardiovascular risk.

Seek urgent medical attention for new or persistent chest discomfort, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or acute limb pain. Follow up after review of current medications and recommended baseline assessments to finalize lipid-lowering treatment.
