Chief Complaint

Initial evaluation of hypertension.

Subjective

Autumn West is a 32-year-old woman presenting for initial outpatient consultation regarding hypertension, present for approximately 6 years and not actively managed. She reports intermittent palpitations. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, and nosebleeds. She does not smoke. Current weight is 61.5 kg at 152 cm, with BMI 26.6 kg/m².

Medical history is notable for coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease, each present for approximately 1 year. No current antihypertensive regimen is provided. No medication allergies or additional medication history are documented.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 167/99 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Laboratory data: Sodium 147 mmol/L. Potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure and prolonged period without active management. She is currently without symptoms suggestive of acute hypertensive end-organ injury, including no chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, visual changes, or severe headache. Palpitations are reported and warrant cardiac evaluation, particularly in the setting of known coronary artery disease.

Chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease are important comorbidities that increase cardiovascular risk and should be considered when selecting antihypertensive therapy. Mildly elevated sodium may support attention to dietary sodium intake; potassium is within an acceptable range but should be monitored if a renin-angiotensin system agent is initiated.

Overweight, BMI 26.6 kg/m².

Plan

Repeat blood pressure during the visit after seated rest, using appropriate cuff size and measurement technique. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log, including morning and evening readings, for review at close follow-up.

Given persistent significant elevation and comorbid coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease, initiate timely pharmacologic management after review of renal function and medication contraindications. An ACE inhibitor or ARB may be considered if clinically appropriate, with repeat renal function and potassium monitoring after initiation; alternative or additional therapy may include a calcium channel blocker or thiazide-type diuretic based on kidney function and response. Medication selection should be individualized after reconciliation of current medications and confirmation of kidney status.

Recommend ECG to evaluate palpitations and assess for hypertensive or ischemic changes. Recommend urinalysis and assessment of renal function, including serum creatinine and estimated GFR, as well as urine albumin evaluation. Obtain lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment.

Counseled on a low-sodium, heart-healthy eating pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction toward a healthy BMI, and limiting alcohol intake. Continue avoidance of tobacco.

Discussed emergency precautions. She should seek immediate emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe or unusual headache, new neurologic symptoms, acute visual change, confusion, or markedly worsening blood pressure.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up within 1–2 weeks for blood pressure review, symptom reassessment, and treatment adjustment, sooner for worsening palpitations or any concerning symptoms.
