Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Lydia Ford is a 51-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of anemia present for approximately 18 years. She reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or other anemia-related symptoms. She has not noted pallor. Anemia has not been under active management.

Relevant chronic conditions include rheumatoid arthritis for 21 years and heart failure for 2 years. She is a current smoker. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

Objective

Height 160 cm; weight 69.1 kg; BMI 27.0 kg/m².

Blood pressure 126/77 mmHg, pulse 87 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory results: hemoglobin 8.7 g/dL, hematocrit 26.7%, RBC count 3.0 million/µL, MCV 89 fL, serum iron 37 µg/dL, ferritin 218 ng/mL, TIBC 250 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 14.8%.

Assessment

Chronic, currently asymptomatic normocytic anemia with significant reduction in hemoglobin and hematocrit. The pattern of low serum iron and transferrin saturation with relatively preserved ferritin and low-normal TIBC is most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation, in the setting of longstanding rheumatoid arthritis. Heart failure may also contribute to chronic inflammatory burden. Iron-restricted erythropoiesis remains possible despite the ferritin level, and alternative or contributory causes have not been fully characterized from the available data.

She is hemodynamically stable without reported cardiopulmonary symptoms, although the respiratory rate is mildly elevated at 22/min. No formal disease stage is assigned.

Plan

Recommend active evaluation rather than continued observation alone. Obtain a repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to confirm persistence and assess marrow response. Recommend serum vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers to evaluate for additional reversible or contributory causes.

Review the anemia and iron profile with her rheumatology and heart failure care teams, with emphasis on control of the underlying inflammatory disease. Consider assessment for occult blood loss or other sources of iron deficiency if clinically indicated, particularly given the low transferrin saturation.

Iron replacement should be guided by the complete evaluation and clinical context; empiric long-term oral iron should not be assumed to be beneficial given the elevated ferritin and inflammatory pattern. If anemia persists after clarification of nutritional, renal, inflammatory, and bleeding contributors, specialty consideration of intravenous iron or an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent may be appropriate, particularly if renal dysfunction or other qualifying indications are identified.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, as continued tobacco use may adversely affect cardiovascular and overall health. Advise prompt evaluation for new dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, or marked decline in exercise tolerance.
