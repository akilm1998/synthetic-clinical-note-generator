Chief Complaint

Annual review of longstanding hypertension and chronic disease management.

Subjective

Valeria Ellis is a 74-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 41-year history of essential hypertension that is not currently under active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis.

Medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 1 year ago and hyperlipidemia diagnosed approximately 2 years ago. She continues to smoke. Current weight is 57.5 kg at a height of 151 cm. No medication regimen, allergies, or additional social history are provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 141/89 mmHg; pulse 73 bpm; respiratory rate 14/min; temperature 37.0°C; oxygen saturation 96%.

BMI: 25.2 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 140 mmol/L; potassium 4.9 mmol/L.

She is asymptomatic at today’s visit. No additional examination findings are provided.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding, with current blood pressure 141/89 mmHg. She has no symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency or acute end-organ injury. Given the prolonged duration and lack of active management, cardiovascular and renal risk reduction should be addressed systematically, particularly in the context of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and ongoing tobacco use.

2. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago.

3. Hyperlipidemia, diagnosed approximately 2 years ago.

4. Tobacco use.

5. Preventive health maintenance in an older adult.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management and cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend confirmation of blood pressure control with properly performed home readings and review of a blood pressure log at follow-up. Recommend dietary sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, maintenance of current healthy weight, moderation of alcohol intake if applicable, and smoking cessation. Medication therapy for hypertension should be reviewed and initiated or adjusted based on repeated measurements, overall cardiovascular risk, diabetes status, renal assessment, and shared decision-making.

Recommend routine monitoring of renal function and electrolytes when clinically indicated, with attention to the potassium level of 4.9 mmol/L before and during antihypertensive therapy that may affect potassium balance. Recommend completion or review of diabetes monitoring and lipid management, including appropriate laboratory assessment, as part of comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction.

Recommend standard hypertension evaluation, including urinalysis, lipid profile, and electrocardiogram if not recently performed. Continue age-appropriate preventive care, including review of immunizations, cancer screening, fall-risk assessment, vision and hearing care, and osteoporosis prevention according to current guidelines and prior screening history.

Seek urgent medical attention for severe headache, chest pain, acute shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms. Arrange outpatient follow-up after home blood pressure monitoring to reassess control and establish a sustained management plan.
