Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated cholesterol.

Subjective
Jane Watson is a 43-year-old woman presenting for focused follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 4 years and currently untreated. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 327 mg/dL, LDL-C 225 mg/dL, HDL-C 46 mg/dL, and triglycerides 279 mg/dL. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. She continues to smoke. History is also notable for non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 10 years. No current lipid-lowering medications are reported.

Objective
Height 164 cm, weight 65.4 kg, BMI 24.3 kg/m². Blood pressure 115/78 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%. No reported cutaneous lipid deposits or cardiovascular prodromal symptoms.

Assessment
Hyperlipidemia with severe LDL elevation and concomitant hypertriglyceridemia, untreated. LDL-C of 225 mg/dL and total cholesterol of 327 mg/dL confer substantially increased long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk despite normal BMI and currently normal blood pressure. No current symptoms suggestive of an acute cardiovascular event. Active tobacco use further increases risk.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding. This does not by itself preclude statin therapy, but hepatic status should be considered when initiating treatment.

Plan
Discussed the significance of the lipid abnormalities and the importance of prompt risk-factor modification. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after shared decision-making, with medication selection and dosing reviewed at this visit. Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel and hepatic monitoring after treatment initiation to assess response and tolerance.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy diet low in saturated and trans fats, increased soluble fiber, avoidance of excess refined carbohydrates and alcohol, and regular aerobic activity. Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered cessation support.

Consider assessment for additional risk-enhancing factors, including ApoB and lipoprotein(a), and further cardiovascular risk stratification if treatment decisions remain uncertain. Return for close outpatient follow-up after therapy initiation or sooner for chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dyspnea, or other concerning symptoms.
