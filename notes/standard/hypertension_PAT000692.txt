Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Mason Russell is a 72-year-old man seen for outpatient follow-up of hypertension, present for approximately 17 years and currently without active treatment documented. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. There is no reported interval hypertensive symptomatology.

His medical history is also notable for coronary artery disease of approximately 25 years’ duration, chronic kidney disease for 19 years, and hyperlipidemia for 27 years. He does not smoke. Current weight is 69.0 kg at 171 cm, with BMI 23.6 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 180/101 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported today.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension with markedly elevated blood pressure, currently inadequately controlled and not under an established treatment regimen. Although asymptomatic today, the degree of elevation is concerning, particularly in the setting of longstanding coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease.

Chronic kidney disease.

Coronary artery disease.

Hyperlipidemia.

Plan

Given the blood pressure of 180/101 mmHg and his significant cardiovascular and renal comorbidity, recommend prompt same-day clinical reassessment rather than routine observation, with confirmation of the reading and evaluation for acute end-organ injury. He was advised to seek emergency care immediately for chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic changes, confusion, vision loss, or epistaxis.

A clinician-directed antihypertensive regimen should be initiated or re-established promptly, with selection and dosing tailored to his coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease. Review of renal function and additional baseline monitoring should accompany treatment planning. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a written log and close follow-up after treatment initiation or adjustment.

Continue risk-factor management for hyperlipidemia, coronary artery disease, and chronic kidney disease. Consider ECG, urinalysis, lipid profile, and appropriate renal monitoring as part of cardiovascular and hypertension assessment.
