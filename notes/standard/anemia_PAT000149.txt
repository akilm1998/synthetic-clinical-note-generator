Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Cody Warren is a 55-year-old man, 188 cm, 76.7 kg, BMI 21.7 kg/m², presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 12-year history of anemia in the setting of chronic kidney disease for approximately 10 years and a chronic infection for approximately 1 year. He denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, pallor, reduced exercise tolerance, dyspnea, palpitations, or other reported anemia-related symptoms. He does not smoke. No active treatment for the anemia is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 137/81 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respirations 20/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL
Hematocrit 26.7%
RBC 3.34 million/µL
MCV 80 fL
Serum iron 34 µg/dL
Ferritin 395 ng/mL
TIBC 206 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 16.5%

Assessment

Chronic anemia, currently asymptomatic, with hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL and hematocrit 26.7%. Indices are low-normal normocytic with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated ferritin, and low TIBC, a pattern most consistent with anemia associated with chronic inflammation and chronic kidney disease. The prolonged duration and absence of active management warrant reassessment and clarification of contributing factors. No current cardiopulmonary instability by vital signs or reported symptoms.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, relevant contributor to anemia.

Chronic infection, approximately 1 year in duration, may be contributing to ongoing inflammation.

Preventive health review performed.

Plan

Repeat CBC with differential, reticulocyte count, and peripheral smear to assess current severity and marrow response.

Reassess renal function and inflammatory markers, and repeat iron studies as clinically indicated. Check vitamin B12 and folate levels to exclude additional nutritional contributors.

Review the chronic infection and kidney disease management, including coordination with the clinicians managing these conditions. Consider nephrology and/or hematology input if anemia persists, worsens, or the initial evaluation is unrevealing.

Do not start empiric iron therapy without reviewing the complete evaluation, given the elevated ferritin and inflammatory pattern. If iron-restricted erythropoiesis related to chronic kidney disease or inflammation is confirmed, discuss oral or intravenous iron and possible erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy with the appropriate specialist based on renal function and overall risk assessment.

Advise prompt evaluation for new shortness of breath, chest pain, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, bleeding, or marked decline in exercise tolerance.

Continue routine preventive care, including age-appropriate cancer screening, immunization review, blood pressure and cardiovascular risk assessment, and routine dental and eye care. Encourage regular activity as tolerated and a balanced diet.

Follow up after repeat laboratory evaluation, or sooner for any new symptoms.
