Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Leo Davidson is a 49-year-old man, height 178 cm, weight 89.7 kg, BMI 28.3 kg/m², presenting for follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 6 years. He reports persistent fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No interval uremic or volume-overload symptoms are reported.

Comorbidities include gout for approximately 6 years and secondary hyperparathyroidism for approximately 2 years. He currently smokes. Chronic kidney disease care has not been actively managed, and no medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 144/82 mmHg, pulse 80/min, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 4.14 mg/dL
BUN 66 mg/dL
eGFR 12 mL/min/1.73 m²

No additional examination findings or laboratory results are provided.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of approximately 6 years’ duration with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. Current fatigue may be related to advanced renal dysfunction. He does not report nausea, pruritus, cramping, altered urination, edema, or dyspnea to suggest active symptomatic uremia or fluid overload at this visit. Renal disease is not currently under an established active management plan.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism, reported for 2 years, in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Gout, reported for 6 years.

Blood pressure is above goal at today’s visit.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology evaluation to re-establish coordinated CKD care and assess the need for renal replacement therapy education and forward planning. The eGFR and BUN warrant close clinical surveillance even in the absence of overt uremic symptoms.

Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, and CBC, along with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to support CKD risk assessment and management. Recommend assessment of intact parathyroid hormone and ongoing evaluation of the secondary hyperparathyroidism.

Recommend renal ultrasound if not previously completed to evaluate renal structure and exclude an obstructive component.

Review blood pressure management at the next visit and avoid nephrotoxic medications, including NSAIDs when possible. Medication reconciliation is needed before initiating or adjusting therapy, particularly in view of the degree of renal impairment and history of gout.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, moderation of dietary sodium, and avoidance of over-the-counter supplements or medications without renal review. Reviewed symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including worsening shortness of breath, new edema, marked reduction in urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, or progressive weakness.

Close follow-up is recommended after nephrology assessment and updated laboratory testing.
