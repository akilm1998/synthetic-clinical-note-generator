Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Cody Warren is a 73-year-old male presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 16-year history of chronic kidney disease and reports ongoing fatigue. He denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No smoking history. He has a 21-year history of gout. No current medication list or treatment regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 141/83 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respirations 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 162 cm, weight 69.0 kg, BMI 26.3 kg/m².

Recent laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 3.3 mg/dL
BUN 67 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m²

No peripheral edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, urinary frequency change, muscle cramps, or sleep disturbance reported.

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with significantly reduced renal function, present for approximately 16 years. Current creatinine is 3.3 mg/dL, BUN 67 mg/dL, and eGFR 19 mL/min/1.73 m². Fatigue may be related to chronic kidney disease. No current symptoms suggesting clinically significant fluid overload or prominent uremic symptoms. Disease requires active reassessment and coordinated management.

2. Gout, longstanding, approximately 21 years. Current flare symptoms were not reported.

3. Elevated blood pressure reading at today’s visit, 141/83 mmHg, in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

4. Preventive health maintenance review.

Plan

Discussed the importance of re-establishing active chronic kidney disease management and monitoring renal function closely. Recommend nephrology consultation for evaluation of substantially reduced kidney function, assessment of progression, medication and renal-dose review, and discussion of future planning if renal function declines further.

Recommend repeat renal function and serum electrolyte testing, along with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone as part of chronic kidney disease assessment. Renal ultrasound may be considered if not previously evaluated or if clinically indicated.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications at the next visit, with avoidance of NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically advised. Encourage appropriate hydration without excessive fluid intake, avoidance of high-sodium foods, and a kidney-appropriate diet guided by renal function and specialist recommendations. Monitor for worsening fatigue, nausea, itching, confusion, reduced urine output, new swelling, or shortness of breath; seek urgent evaluation for severe or rapidly progressive symptoms.

Recheck blood pressure and review home readings if available. Management should account for renal function and gout history; no medication changes made today because an active medication list was not provided.

For gout, review flare frequency, urate-lowering treatment history, dietary triggers, and renal dosing at follow-up. Avoid unsupervised NSAID use.

Preventive care reviewed in general terms, including age-appropriate immunizations, colorectal cancer screening, prostate health discussion, vision and hearing care, fall-risk assessment, dental care, physical activity as tolerated, and routine depression and cognitive screening. Continue tobacco avoidance.

Follow up promptly after recommended laboratory reassessment and nephrology evaluation, with routine primary care follow-up for annual health maintenance and chronic disease review.
