Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Isaac Henderson is a 76-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes, diagnosed approximately 26 years ago. Current glycemic control is suboptimal, with recent hemoglobin A1c of 9.2% and fasting glucose of 229 mg/dL. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, unintentional weight loss, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections. He reports numbness in the hands or feet without associated tingling.

He has longstanding obesity, dyslipidemia, and anxiety. He does not smoke. No diabetes medications or other current medications were provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: blood pressure 135/81 mmHg, pulse 76 beats/min, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 163 cm, weight 80.0 kg, BMI 30.1 kg/m².

Laboratory results: hemoglobin A1c 9.2%; fasting glucose 229 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented acute complication, currently poorly controlled based on elevated A1c and fasting glucose. Although he denies osmotic or constitutional symptoms, his reported distal numbness raises concern for possible diabetic peripheral neuropathic symptoms. He has no reported wounds or recurrent infections.

Obesity with BMI 30.1 kg/m².

Longstanding dyslipidemia.

Anxiety, present for approximately one year.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values are above goal and that improved glycemic control is important to reduce the risk of neuropathy, kidney disease, visual complications, and cardiovascular disease. Reviewed nutrition focused on reducing concentrated sugars and refined carbohydrates, appropriate portion control, and gradual weight reduction. Encouraged regular physical activity as tolerated and continued avoidance of tobacco.

Medication reconciliation and initiation or adjustment of diabetes therapy should be addressed promptly, with selection individualized to renal function, hypoglycemia risk, comorbidities, cost, and patient preferences. Recommend monitoring home glucose readings if a monitoring plan is established and bringing the log to follow-up.

Recommend evaluation for diabetes-related complications, including urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine with estimated renal function, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive foot assessment, and diabetic retinal evaluation. He was advised to inspect his feet regularly and report any skin breakdown, wounds, increasing numbness, or signs of infection.

Continue attention to cardiovascular risk reduction and management of dyslipidemia. Anxiety and its effect on self-care were acknowledged and can be reassessed at subsequent visits.

Follow up after treatment is initiated or adjusted to review glucose readings and tolerance, with repeat hemoglobin A1c at an appropriate interval.
