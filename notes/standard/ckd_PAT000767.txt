Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Ruby Marshall is an 83-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 6 years. She reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. She has a 15-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, 36-year history of hypertension, and secondary hyperparathyroidism diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She continues to smoke. No current medication list is provided.

Objective

Height 161 cm, weight 61.2 kg, BMI 23.6 kg/m².

Blood pressure 164/94 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Serum creatinine 3.12 mg/dL. Estimated GFR 16 mL/min/1.73 m². BUN 66 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and elevated creatinine and BUN. She is currently clinically without reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, but the degree of renal impairment warrants close renal follow-up and active complication surveillance. Management appears suboptimal, particularly given persistent hypertension and longstanding diabetes.

Hypertension, currently above goal at 164/94 mmHg, in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus for 15 years, an important contributor to renal disease risk.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism associated with chronic kidney disease.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange close nephrology follow-up for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease and discussion of renal replacement planning if renal function declines or complications develop.

Repeat renal function assessment and obtain serum electrolytes, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound to assess renal structure.

Review and intensify blood pressure management with attention to renal protection. Medication reconciliation is needed before selecting or adjusting therapy; no current medications are documented today. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring and review of readings at follow-up.

Continue diabetes management with emphasis on individualized glycemic control and avoidance of hypoglycemia given advanced age and renal impairment.

Assess and manage CKD-related mineral and bone disorder and anemia once recommended laboratory studies are available.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of nephrotoxins, including nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. Advise prompt evaluation for new shortness of breath, progressive swelling, markedly reduced urine output, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, or chest discomfort.

Follow up promptly for blood pressure and kidney disease reassessment.
