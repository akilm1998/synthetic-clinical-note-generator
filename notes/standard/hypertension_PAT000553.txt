Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Ruby Marshall is a 75-year-old woman seen for follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 15 years. Her blood pressure is currently not under active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 44 years, coronary artery disease for 6 years, and chronic kidney disease for 2 years. She currently smokes. Height is 164 cm and weight is 59.2 kg, with BMI 22.0 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 160/100 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 142 mmol/L; potassium 4.0 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent elevation at 160/100 mmHg, currently without active management. She is asymptomatic, with no reported features suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Her long duration of hypertension and coexisting diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease place her at elevated cardiovascular and renal risk.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 2 years.

Coronary artery disease, present for approximately 6 years.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed that the current blood pressure is above goal and warrants prompt outpatient treatment planning, particularly given her diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease. Recommend confirming blood pressure with repeat office measurement and/or home blood pressure monitoring, with proper seated technique and a written log.

Recommend clinician-guided initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy, with selection coordinated to her renal disease, diabetes, and coronary artery disease. Review renal function and urine testing should be considered before and after treatment decisions; serum sodium and potassium are currently 142 and 4.0 mmol/L, respectively.

Recommend cardiovascular risk assessment including lipid profile and ECG, and urinalysis as clinically appropriate. Reinforce a lower-sodium diet, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and continued weight maintenance. Smoking cessation counseling provided.

She should seek urgent medical attention for new severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, confusion, or vision loss. Follow up promptly for blood pressure reassessment and management initiation.
