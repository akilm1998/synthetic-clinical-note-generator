Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective
Hunter Ford is a 73-year-old male seen for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 39 years. He is not currently receiving documented treatment for blood pressure. He reports no headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. No interval hypertensive symptoms reported.

He has type 2 diabetes mellitus of approximately 27 years’ duration. He continues to smoke. No medication regimen or allergies were provided for review.

Objective
Vital signs: Blood pressure 146/95 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Height 170 cm, weight 66.2 kg, BMI 22.9 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 143 mmol/L, potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

Assessment
Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure, currently without reported symptoms or documented active treatment. Long duration of disease and coexisting type 2 diabetes increase cardiovascular and renal risk.

Tobacco use.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Plan
Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given the elevated reading, long duration of hypertension, age, and diabetes. Recommend obtaining home blood pressure measurements with a validated upper-arm cuff, including readings at different times of day, and bringing the log to follow-up.

Recommend initiation of antihypertensive therapy after medication reconciliation and shared decision-making. Review renal function and repeat electrolytes before and after starting or adjusting therapy; current sodium is 143 mmol/L and potassium is 4.8 mmol/L.

Counseled on limiting dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, moderating alcohol intake if applicable, and continuing a heart-healthy diet. Encouraged smoking cessation and offered cessation counseling.

Recommend cardiovascular risk assessment, including lipid profile, urinalysis, and ECG, as clinically appropriate, with attention to diabetic and hypertensive end-organ risk.

Return for blood pressure reassessment within 2–4 weeks, sooner for markedly elevated readings or development of severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or visual changes.
