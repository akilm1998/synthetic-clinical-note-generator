Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective
Ruby Marshall is a 33-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 3 years ago. Current disease control is suboptimal, with no established management regimen documented. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. She does not smoke.

She has obesity of approximately 3 years’ duration. Current weight is 100.0 kg at a height of 173 cm, with BMI 33.4 kg/m².

Objective
Vital signs: Blood pressure 122/76 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.6%. Fasting glucose 232 mg/dL.

No acute symptoms or diabetes-related complications are reported today.

Assessment
Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently inadequately controlled based on HbA1c of 9.6% and fasting glucose of 232 mg/dL. Despite significant biochemical hyperglycemia, she is presently asymptomatic. There are no reported neuropathic, infectious, constitutional, or visual symptoms.

Obesity, BMI 33.4 kg/m², likely contributing to insulin resistance and impaired glycemic control.

Plan
Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the risks associated with persistent hyperglycemia. Recommend initiation of structured diabetes management, including evidence-based pharmacologic therapy such as metformin if no contraindication, with treatment selection individualized after review of renal function, tolerability, reproductive considerations, and patient preference.

Counseled regarding nutrition, portion control, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, weight reduction, and gradual progression toward regular physical activity as tolerated. Recommend home glucose monitoring with documentation of readings to guide treatment adjustment.

Obtain renal assessment including serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, along with a fasting lipid profile for cardiovascular risk assessment. Recommend routine comprehensive foot examination and dilated retinal examination as part of diabetes complication screening.

Return for reassessment of glycemic response and treatment tolerance after initiation of management, with earlier evaluation for polyuria, polydipsia, vomiting, marked weakness, visual changes, or other acute concerns.
