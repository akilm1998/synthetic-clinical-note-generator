Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of hypertension.

Subjective

Grace Powell is an 80-year-old woman seen for outpatient review of long-standing essential hypertension, present for approximately 35 years. Hypertension is currently not actively managed. No antihypertensive medication regimen is identified for review today, so medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be assessed. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and nosebleeds.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 23 years and chronic kidney disease for 17 years. She does not smoke. Weight is 75.3 kg at 162 cm, with BMI 28.7 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure 157/94 mmHg. Pulse 76 bpm. Respiratory rate 18/min. Temperature 36.6°C. Oxygen saturation 95%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 137 mmol/L, potassium 5.1 mmol/L.

No acute hypertensive symptoms reported.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and no active treatment regimen identified for medication review. She is asymptomatic today without features suggesting hypertensive emergency. Long duration of hypertension, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease increase her cardiovascular and renal risk. Potassium is mildly elevated at 5.1 mmol/L, which is relevant when selecting or adjusting medications that may further increase potassium.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Confirm the complete current medication list, including any medications obtained outside this practice, and reconcile adherence, dosing, and prior intolerance before making treatment changes.

Recommend initiation or re-establishment of a structured antihypertensive treatment plan with consideration of her chronic kidney disease, diabetes, age, and potassium level. Avoid empiric escalation of medications that may raise potassium until renal function and repeat electrolytes are reviewed. Obtain repeat serum electrolytes and renal function as clinically appropriate before and after medication initiation or adjustment.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with seated readings and a written log for review. Counsel on sodium reduction, avoidance of excessive alcohol, regular activity as tolerated, and gradual weight reduction.

Review symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, vision loss, or markedly elevated blood pressure.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure reassessment and medication review.
