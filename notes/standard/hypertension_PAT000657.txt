Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Noah Hamilton is a 74-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding essential hypertension, reported duration 24. Hypertension has not been actively managed. He reports intermittent dizziness and fatigue. He denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or nosebleeds. He does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include coronary artery disease of 1 year and type 2 diabetes mellitus of 2 years. No current antihypertensive regimen is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 146/92 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 169 cm; weight 68.8 kg; BMI 24.1 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 140 mmol/L, potassium 5.1 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with blood pressure above goal at today’s visit and associated intermittent dizziness and fatigue. No symptoms suggesting an acute hypertensive emergency. Hypertension is currently untreated/not actively managed.

Mildly elevated potassium at 5.1 mmol/L. This is clinically relevant when selecting or initiating renin-angiotensin system therapy, particularly in the setting of diabetes and coronary artery disease.

Coronary artery disease, known history.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, known history.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given his age, diabetes, and coronary artery disease. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a validated upper-arm cuff, recording readings at different times of day, and bringing the log to follow-up.

Recommend a low-sodium, heart-healthy diet, regular activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Continue tobacco avoidance.

Recommend repeat serum electrolytes and assessment of renal function before initiating or titrating an ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker, given potassium of 5.1 mmol/L. Antihypertensive therapy should be initiated or adjusted after review of home readings and laboratory safety parameters; a non–potassium-raising option may be considered if clinically appropriate.

Recommend ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular risk and hypertension assessment if not recently completed. Continue coordinated management of coronary artery disease and diabetes.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including severe or persistent headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up in approximately 2–4 weeks for blood pressure review and treatment planning, sooner for worsening dizziness or development of cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms.
