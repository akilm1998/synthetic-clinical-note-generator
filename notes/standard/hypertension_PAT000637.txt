Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for persistently elevated blood pressure.

Subjective

Scarlett Hughes is a 73-year-old woman with a 38-year history of essential hypertension and coronary artery disease of approximately 14 years’ duration. Hypertension is currently not being actively managed. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. She continues to smoke. Weight is 68.8 kg at 169 cm, with BMI 24.1 kg/m².

Objective

Blood pressure is 153/93 mmHg with pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, and oxygen saturation 99%. Serum sodium is 147 mmol/L and potassium is 5.3 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure in an asymptomatic 73-year-old woman and established coronary artery disease. There is no reported symptom complex suggesting an acute hypertensive emergency today. Mild hyperkalemia is present and should be confirmed before selecting antihypertensive therapy, particularly agents that may further increase potassium.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement after seated rest and begin home blood pressure monitoring with a written log. Initiate structured antihypertensive management after confirmation of the blood pressure pattern, with treatment selection individualized to her coronary artery disease and potassium level. Repeat serum electrolytes and assess renal function before starting or titrating therapy; avoid empiric initiation of a potassium-raising agent until the potassium result is reassessed. Obtain an ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as part of cardiovascular risk and hypertension evaluation. Counsel regarding smoking cessation, sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, and moderation of alcohol intake. Seek emergency care for chest pain, new shortness of breath, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, visual changes, or markedly worsening blood pressure. Arrange close outpatient reassessment.
