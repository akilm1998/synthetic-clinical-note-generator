Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Damian Boyd is a 76-year-old man, height 177 cm, weight 78.0 kg, BMI 24.9 kg/m², presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease present for approximately 25 years. His renal disease has occurred in the setting of longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus for 33 years and hypertension for 19 years. He also has anemia of chronic disease of approximately 2 years’ duration. He continues to smoke.

He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, and shortness of breath. No interval uremic or volume-overload symptoms are reported.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 168/97 mmHg, pulse 85 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Laboratory results: Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 80 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function and substantial azotemia. Despite the absence of reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms, the creatinine of 6.0 mg/dL, BUN of 80 mg/dL, and eGFR of 7 mL/min/1.73 m² represent severe renal impairment requiring prompt specialist assessment. Current disease control is inadequate, with no active renal management documented.

Hypertension, longstanding and currently poorly controlled at 168/97 mmHg, increasing risk for further renal and cardiovascular injury.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Anemia of chronic disease, present for approximately 2 years; current hemoglobin is not available for assessment.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Given the degree of renal impairment and uncontrolled blood pressure, arrange urgent nephrology evaluation and same-day clinical reassessment to determine whether emergency evaluation is indicated. Assess for dialysis indications, including evolving uremic symptoms, volume overload, acid-base or electrolyte abnormalities, and inability to control blood pressure.

Obtain repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, and BUN/creatinine, along with a complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and urinalysis as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound to evaluate renal structure and chronicity if not previously completed.

Perform medication reconciliation urgently and review all prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal products. Avoid nephrotoxic agents, particularly NSAIDs, and adjust all renally cleared medications. Renal-appropriate dietary counseling, including sodium and individualized protein and fluid guidance, is recommended.

Address hypertension promptly with a renal- and cardiovascular-appropriate treatment plan after medication reconciliation and review of electrolytes and volume status. Continue close monitoring of blood pressure and symptoms.

Monitor for new nausea, vomiting, pruritus, confusion, progressive weakness, reduced urine output, swelling, or shortness of breath; these symptoms should prompt emergency evaluation. Strongly counsel smoking cessation.

Follow up urgently with nephrology rather than routine interval follow-up alone.
