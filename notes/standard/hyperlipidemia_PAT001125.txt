Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective
Madeline Warren is a 90-year-old woman seen for outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 51 years and currently without active lipid-lowering management. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, and dizziness. She reports fatty skin deposits consistent with xanthomas; no xanthelasma is reported. She does not smoke.

Her medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 7 years and hypertension for 18 years. Current weight is 66.1 kg at 152 cm, with BMI 28.6 kg/m².

Objective
Vital signs: blood pressure 147/90 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Recent lipid panel shows total cholesterol 375 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 271 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 37 mg/dL, and triglycerides 337 mg/dL. Xanthomas are reported. No chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness is present.

Assessment
Longstanding untreated hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol, low HDL cholesterol, and elevated triglycerides. The presence of xanthomas supports a prolonged significant lipid burden. She has additional cardiovascular risk factors including advanced age, type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and overweight status, although she is a nonsmoker and currently reports no symptoms suggestive of angina or peripheral arterial disease.

Blood pressure is elevated today at 147/90 mmHg in the setting of known hypertension.

Plan
Discussed the substantial cardiovascular risk associated with her lipid levels and the potential benefits and risks of treatment. Given her age and comorbid diabetes and hypertension, lipid-lowering therapy should be individualized through shared decision-making, taking into account functional status, goals of care, treatment preferences, and medication tolerance. Recommend consideration of statin therapy, with adjustment in intensity based on tolerance and overall goals; ezetimibe may be considered if adequate response is not achieved or statin therapy is not tolerated.

Recommend a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment decisions are made to establish response. Evaluation for potentially reversible contributors to severe dyslipidemia, including thyroid, hepatic, renal, and glycemic abnormalities, is appropriate using available clinical and laboratory information. ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement may provide additional risk assessment if this would influence management. A coronary artery calcium study is not routinely necessary if treatment decisions can be made clinically, particularly given her age.

Encouraged a heart-healthy eating pattern with reduction of saturated and trans fats, increased fiber, attention to carbohydrate intake given the elevated triglycerides and diabetes, and activity appropriate for her mobility and safety. Continue monitoring blood pressure and diabetes as part of overall cardiovascular risk reduction. She was advised to seek prompt evaluation for new chest discomfort, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain. Follow up after treatment discussion and repeat lipid assessment to update management.
