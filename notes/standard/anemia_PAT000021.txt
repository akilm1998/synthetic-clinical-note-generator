Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Denise Wheeler is a 91-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of longstanding anemia, present for approximately 39 years. She reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. Pallor is noted. She does not smoke.

Relevant chronic conditions include chronic kidney disease for approximately 30 years, chronic infection for approximately 40 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for approximately 18 years. No current anemia-directed medication or treatment regimen is documented.

Objective

Height 165 cm; weight 60.2 kg; BMI 22.1 kg/m².

Blood pressure 143/86 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Visible pallor. No tachycardia or respiratory distress evident based on current vital signs.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.4 g/dL
Hematocrit 26.0%
RBC 2.86 million/µL
MCV 91 fL
Serum iron 34 µg/dL
Ferritin 281 ng/mL
TIBC 246 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 13.8%

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia with significant reduction in hemoglobin and hematocrit. The low serum iron and transferrin saturation in the setting of elevated ferritin and reduced TIBC are most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic disease. Longstanding chronic kidney disease is also likely contributing through impaired erythropoietic support. Her chronic infection may additionally contribute to inflammatory iron restriction.

She is currently clinically stable without reported cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms, although pallor persists. The anemia has not been under active management and warrants reassessment given the hemoglobin of 8.4 g/dL.

Plan

Recommend updated anemia evaluation with repeat CBC, reticulocyte count, peripheral blood smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers. Review for any evidence of blood loss or other contributors to the anemia as clinically indicated.

Recommend coordination with the clinician managing her chronic kidney disease regarding renal contribution to anemia and consideration of anemia-directed therapy, including whether an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent or intravenous iron is appropriate after evaluation. Oral or intravenous iron should be guided by the overall clinical assessment and iron indices, recognizing that the ferritin is elevated despite low iron availability.

Continue monitoring for fatigue, weakness, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, chest discomfort, or declining functional capacity. Seek urgent evaluation for new or worsening cardiopulmonary symptoms, syncope, or evidence of active bleeding. Arrange outpatient reassessment after the recommended evaluation.
