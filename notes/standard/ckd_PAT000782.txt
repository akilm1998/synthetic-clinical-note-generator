Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Rebecca Burke is a 71-year-old woman with a 14-year history of chronic kidney disease and longstanding hypertension for approximately 39 years, presenting for routine outpatient follow-up. She reports ongoing fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, lower-extremity swelling, changes in urination, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. No interval fluid-overload symptoms are reported.

She continues to smoke. Current weight is 51.5 kg at 160 cm (BMI 20.1 kg/m²). No current medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 163/93 mmHg, pulse 79/min, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Recent laboratory results: serum creatinine 2.99 mg/dL, estimated GFR 20 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 89 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of 14 years’ duration with significantly reduced renal function and elevated BUN/creatinine. She has fatigue but no reported nausea, pruritus, cramps, urinary change, edema, or dyspnea to suggest current symptomatic fluid overload or prominent uremic symptoms. Disease control appears suboptimal, and the markedly elevated blood pressure is an important modifiable risk for further renal decline.

Longstanding hypertension, currently above goal.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active renal and blood pressure management to reduce risk of further kidney function decline. Recommend prompt medication reconciliation and review of adherence, home blood pressure readings, dietary sodium intake, and use of potential nephrotoxic agents such as NSAIDs before establishing or adjusting therapy.

Recommend nephrology consultation for evaluation and ongoing management of advanced chronic kidney disease, including assessment of renal-protective treatment options and preparation for potential future renal replacement needs if kidney function worsens.

Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as clinically appropriate. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously completed or if indicated by the clinical evaluation.

Blood pressure should be rechecked promptly and managed with a renal-protective regimen as appropriate after medication review. Encourage avoidance of NSAIDs, appropriate hydration without excessive fluid intake, and a low-sodium diet.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation. Reviewed symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including new or worsening shortness of breath, edema, markedly reduced urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, or chest pain. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after blood pressure and renal medication review.
