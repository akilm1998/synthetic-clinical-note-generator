Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Sadie Hunter is a 61-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease follow-up. She has an approximately 8-year history of hyperlipidemia that has not been actively managed. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 308 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 198 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 48 mg/dL, and triglycerides 312 mg/dL.

She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. She has a history of coronary artery disease for approximately 9 years. She does not smoke. No current lipid-lowering medication use is provided.

Objective

Height 153 cm; weight 66.2 kg; BMI 28.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 131/82 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Lipid profile: total cholesterol 308 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 198 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 48 mg/dL, triglycerides 312 mg/dL.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, longstanding and currently untreated. Given her known coronary artery disease, these results represent a high cardiovascular risk profile and warrant active lipid-lowering therapy. She is currently without chest discomfort, claudication, dizziness, or other reported cardiovascular symptoms.

Coronary artery disease, approximately 9-year duration, without reported anginal symptoms today.

Overweight, BMI 28.3 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the increased cardiovascular risk associated with LDL cholesterol of 198 mg/dL in the setting of coronary artery disease. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after reviewing risks, benefits, contraindications, and patient preference. If LDL reduction remains inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, consider addition of ezetimibe and, depending on response and overall risk, referral for consideration of additional lipid-lowering therapy.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after starting or adjusting therapy to assess response. Consider baseline and follow-up liver enzymes as clinically appropriate. Consider measurement of lipoprotein(a) or apolipoprotein B for further risk assessment; coronary calcium scoring is unlikely to change management given the established history of coronary artery disease.

Counseled on a Mediterranean-style eating pattern with reduction of saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess dietary sugars; increased intake of vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, and soluble fiber; and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage gradual weight reduction toward a healthier weight. She was advised to seek urgent evaluation for new or persistent chest pressure, shortness of breath, syncope, or exertional symptoms.

Continue routine coronary artery disease risk-factor management and preventive care, including regular blood pressure monitoring, age-appropriate cancer screening, immunizations, and review of diabetes screening at routine follow-up. Follow up after repeat lipid testing, preferably within 6–12 weeks of initiating therapy, or sooner for medication concerns or new cardiovascular symptoms.
