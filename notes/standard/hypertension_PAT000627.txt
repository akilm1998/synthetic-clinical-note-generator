Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Martin Shaw is a 48-year-old man presenting to establish outpatient care for hypertension diagnosed approximately 16 years ago. Blood pressure has not been actively managed. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. No acute hypertensive symptoms are reported today.

Medical history is significant for hyperlipidemia for approximately 17 years, chronic kidney disease for approximately 7 years, and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. He currently smokes. Height is 179 cm and weight is 90.4 kg, with BMI 28.2 kg/m².

Available serum electrolytes: sodium 143 mmol/L and potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 164/94 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

General: Patient is seen in the outpatient setting without reported acute distress.

Cardiopulmonary: No chest pain, dyspnea, or palpitations reported. Pulse and respiratory rate are within the documented ranges above.

Assessment

Longstanding essential hypertension with currently elevated office blood pressure of 164/94 mmHg and no reported symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Hypertension has been unmanaged in the setting of chronic kidney disease and coronary artery disease, placing him at increased cardiovascular and renal risk.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease, approximately 1 year in duration.

Chronic kidney disease, approximately 7 years in duration.

Tobacco use.

Overweight, BMI 28.2 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the importance of prompt, consistent blood pressure management given the duration of hypertension and comorbid chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease, and hyperlipidemia. Pharmacologic therapy is indicated; selection should account for renal function, albuminuria status, coronary disease, potassium level, and prior treatment history. The documented potassium is 4.6 mmol/L and sodium is 143 mmol/L.

Recommend obtaining a properly seated repeat blood pressure measurement and initiating home blood pressure monitoring with a written log. Review readings at close follow-up and adjust therapy toward an individualized blood pressure goal appropriate for his kidney and cardiovascular disease.

Recommend assessment of renal function and urine protein/albumin, along with urinalysis, lipid profile, and electrocardiogram as part of cardiovascular and hypertension evaluation. Continue monitoring serum electrolytes with any medication changes affecting renal function or potassium.

Counseled regarding reduced dietary sodium, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, avoidance of excess alcohol, and smoking cessation. Discussed that smoking cessation is particularly important given his coronary artery disease and hypertension.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure review and treatment initiation or adjustment.
