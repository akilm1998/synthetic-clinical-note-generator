Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal filtration and significant azotemia. Creatinine is 5.55 mg/dL, BUN 92 mg/dL, and eGFR 10 mL/min/1.73 m². Despite the severity of the laboratory abnormalities, she is currently without reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms. Given the longstanding 26-year history and lack of active disease management documented, she requires prompt reassessment for complications and clarification of treatment goals.

2. Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding for approximately 24 years. Current hemoglobin and iron indices are not available in the provided data.

3. Gout, longstanding for approximately 46 years. Renal function is substantially impaired and should be considered in any future urate-lowering or flare-treatment decisions.

4. Elevated blood pressure in the setting of chronic kidney disease, measured at 145/90 mmHg today.

5. Tobacco use. Continued smoking may increase cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease, assessment for renal replacement therapy appropriateness, and discussion of conservative kidney care and goals of treatment in the context of her age and overall preferences.

Obtain updated serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, CBC, iron studies, intact parathyroid hormone, urinalysis, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess for metabolic, hematologic, mineral-bone, and proteinuric complications. Consider renal ultrasound as part of structural assessment if not recently performed.

Review for interval development of nausea, vomiting, confusion, worsening fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, reduced urine output, edema, or dyspnea. Emergency evaluation is warranted for significant shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, or other symptoms concerning for uremic or fluid-overload complications.

Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic medications; all prescriptions, over-the-counter agents, and gout therapies should be reviewed for renal dosing. Encourage smoking cessation and avoidance of dehydration. Monitor blood pressure closely and reassess treatment needs after medication reconciliation and review of electrolytes and volume status.

Continue close outpatient follow-up while nephrology assessment is arranged.
