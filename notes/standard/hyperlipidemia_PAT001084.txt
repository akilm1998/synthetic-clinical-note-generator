Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

David Parker is a 60-year-old man with a 22-year history of hyperlipidemia, hypertension for 21 years, and known coronary artery disease for approximately 10 years. Lipid disorder has not been actively managed. He continues to smoke. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain/claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Height 187 cm, weight 86.4 kg, BMI 24.7 kg/m². Blood pressure 142/82 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 354 mg/dL, LDL-C 252 mg/dL, HDL-C 32 mg/dL, triglycerides 352 mg/dL.

Assessment

Severe mixed dyslipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C, elevated triglycerides, and low HDL-C. Given the degree and duration of lipid elevation and coexisting coronary artery disease, he is at very high atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk. Persistent tobacco use and suboptimally controlled blood pressure further increase risk. No current ischemic or peripheral vascular symptoms reported.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active lipid-lowering therapy for secondary cardiovascular prevention. Recommend prompt initiation of a maximally tolerated high-intensity statin, with consideration of additional LDL-lowering therapy such as ezetimibe or a PCSK9 inhibitor if LDL-C remains substantially above goal. Treatment selection should account for prior tolerance, contraindications, and medication reconciliation.

Recommend a heart-healthy diet emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, limiting refined carbohydrates and alcohol intake, and maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated. Strongly advised smoking cessation.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid assessment after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, with evaluation for secondary contributors to severe dyslipidemia as clinically appropriate. Continue cardiovascular risk-factor management, including blood pressure optimization, in coordination with his primary care and cardiology teams. He was advised to seek urgent care for new or worsening chest discomfort, dyspnea, or exertional leg symptoms.
