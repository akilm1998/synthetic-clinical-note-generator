Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Denise Wheeler is a 33-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 5 years ago. Blood pressure has not been actively managed recently. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and nosebleeds. No acute hypertensive symptoms reported.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and hyperlipidemia, each present for approximately 1 year. She does not smoke. Current weight is 86.2 kg at a height of 180 cm, with BMI 26.6 kg/m². Medication use was not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 159/97 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 139 mmol/L, potassium 4.5 mmol/L.

Patient is asymptomatic at today’s visit. No additional examination findings provided.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and no current documented management. She has important comorbid chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and hyperlipidemia, which increase cardiovascular and renal risk. Current electrolyte values are within expected range. No symptoms or vital-sign findings suggest an acute hypertensive emergency today.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management and reduction of long-term cardiovascular and renal risk. Recommend obtaining home blood pressure readings with a validated upper-arm cuff, recording readings consistently, and bringing the log to follow-up. Reinforced a lower-sodium eating pattern, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight control, and avoidance of excess alcohol.

Recommend prompt clinician review to establish an antihypertensive treatment plan appropriate for her chronic kidney disease and diabetes, with attention to renal function and potassium monitoring. Consider evaluation with urinalysis and renal function testing, as well as routine cardiovascular risk assessment including lipid review and ECG as clinically appropriate. Continue coordinated management of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and chronic kidney disease.

Reviewed symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic changes, or markedly worsening blood pressure. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after home readings and treatment planning.
