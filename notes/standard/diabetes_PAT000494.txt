Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Morgan Oliver is a 95-year-old woman seen for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 13 years ago. Current glycemic control is suboptimal, with recent HbA1c 9.6% and fasting glucose 220 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She has a history of chronic kidney disease for approximately 33 years and depression for approximately 43 years. She continues to smoke. No diabetes medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Height 157 cm; weight 60.9 kg; BMI 24.7 kg/m².

Blood pressure 143/78 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Recent laboratory results: HbA1c 9.6%; fasting glucose 220 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings are documented for this visit.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, present for approximately 13 years, with marked hyperglycemia based on HbA1c 9.6% and fasting glucose 220 mg/dL. Although she reports no current osmotic, constitutional, visual, infectious, or neuropathic symptoms, her biochemical control is inadequate. At age 95, treatment goals should be individualized with emphasis on avoiding hypoglycemia, falls, dehydration, and treatment burden.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Renal function is particularly relevant to selection and dosing of glucose-lowering therapy.

Depression, longstanding.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Review and reconcile all current medications with the patient and/or caregiver, including any prior diabetes therapies, adherence, dietary pattern, and ability to self-monitor glucose. Develop a simplified diabetes regimen appropriate for her age, functional status, kidney disease, and individualized glycemic target. Avoid overly aggressive glucose lowering and medications with a substantial hypoglycemia risk unless clearly indicated and closely monitored.

Obtain updated renal function and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio before selecting or adjusting renally cleared diabetes therapy. Consider evidence-based glucose-lowering treatment after review of kidney function, contraindications, tolerability, cost, and caregiver support. If insulin or another hypoglycemia-producing medication is used, provide individualized glucose-monitoring instructions and hypoglycemia precautions.

Counsel regarding consistent carbohydrate intake, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, adequate hydration, and regular activity within her abilities and fall-risk limitations. Review sick-day precautions and instructions to report persistent hyperglycemia, vomiting, poor oral intake, dehydration, confusion, or new infection.

Recommend fasting lipid profile and ongoing renal monitoring as clinically appropriate. Ensure diabetes-focused foot assessment and dilated retinal evaluation are addressed, with attention to skin integrity, sensation, and peripheral circulation. Encourage smoking cessation and offer supportive counseling.

Continue coordination of care for chronic kidney disease and depression. Follow up after medication reconciliation and recommended laboratory review to reassess glycemic control and treatment tolerability.
