Chief Complaint

Initial outpatient consultation for longstanding anemia.

Subjective

Thomas Foster is a 57-year-old man presenting for initial evaluation of anemia reported to have been present for approximately 15 years. He currently denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor. He does not smoke.

Relevant chronic medical history includes inflammatory bowel disease for 22 years, chronic infection for 18 years, and rheumatoid arthritis for 2 years. No current medication or treatment regimen is provided. His anemia is not currently under active management.

Objective

Height 174 cm; weight 64.5 kg; BMI 21.3 kg/m².

Blood pressure 128/81 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 9.8 g/dL
Hematocrit 29.9%
RBC count 3.48 million/µL
MCV 86 fL
Serum iron 47 µg/dL
Ferritin 466 ng/mL
TIBC 263 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 17.9%

The available CBC demonstrates anemia with a normocytic pattern. Iron studies show low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and relatively low TIBC.

Assessment

Longstanding normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation/chronic disease in the setting of inflammatory bowel disease, chronic infection, and rheumatoid arthritis. The pattern of low serum iron with elevated ferritin and low TIBC supports impaired iron availability related to inflammation rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Hemoglobin is 9.8 g/dL, although he is currently without reported cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms.

The prolonged duration and lack of current management warrant confirmation of the anemia pattern, assessment for additional contributing factors, and attention to control of the underlying inflammatory and infectious conditions.

Plan

Obtain or review a repeat complete blood count with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to confirm persistence and characterize marrow response.

Recommend evaluation for other potentially reversible contributors, including serum vitamin B12, folate, renal function testing, and inflammatory markers. Interpretation of iron studies should account for his chronic inflammatory conditions; empiric iron therapy should not be initiated without clinical assessment of the likelihood of concomitant absolute iron deficiency.

Coordinate management of the underlying inflammatory bowel disease, rheumatoid arthritis, and chronic infection, as control of chronic inflammation is central to improving anemia of chronic disease. Consider hematology consultation if the anemia remains unexplained, worsens, becomes symptomatic, or does not improve with management of the underlying conditions.

Discussed monitoring for new fatigue, weakness, dizziness, exertional shortness of breath, palpitations, chest discomfort, or other evidence of clinical deterioration, with prompt reassessment if these develop. Arrange outpatient follow-up after repeat evaluation to review results and establish an active management plan.
