Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus and hypertension.

Subjective

Valeria Ellis is a 49-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 19 years ago. Glycemic control is currently suboptimal, with recent HbA1c 9.1% and fasting glucose 211 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or frequent infections.

She reports tingling in the hands or feet. She does not report numbness. No other diabetic symptoms are reported. Diabetes management has not been active recently. She also has a 10-year history of hypertension. She currently smokes. No medication regimen is provided for review.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 143/91 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Height 174 cm; weight 76.9 kg; BMI 25.4 kg/m².

Laboratory Data: HbA1c 9.1%. Fasting glucose 211 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complication, with inadequate glycemic control. HbA1c and fasting glucose are substantially above goal, consistent with persistent hyperglycemia despite the absence of overt polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, or other constitutional symptoms. The long duration of diabetes increases risk for microvascular and macrovascular complications.

2. Paresthesia of the hands or feet. Tingling may represent early peripheral nerve involvement in the setting of long-standing hyperglycemia; however, a diabetic complication is not established on the information available.

3. Hypertension, longstanding, with blood pressure above goal today at 143/91 mmHg.

4. Tobacco use. Continued smoking increases cardiovascular and microvascular risk, particularly in the setting of diabetes and hypertension.

5. Weight in the overweight range by BMI, at 25.4 kg/m².

Plan

Diabetes control and complication prevention were reviewed. Discussed the significance of the HbA1c of 9.1% and fasting glucose of 211 mg/dL, along with the need for active treatment rather than continued observation. Recommend initiation or re-establishment of guideline-directed glucose-lowering therapy after medication reconciliation and shared decision-making. Reinforced a consistent carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, and regular physical activity as tolerated.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with documentation of readings for treatment adjustment and education regarding symptoms of marked hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia. Recommend assessment of renal function and urinary albumin excretion, fasting lipid profile, and comprehensive diabetes complication screening, including a dilated retinal examination and detailed foot examination. The reported tingling should be reassessed at follow-up and evaluated in the context of glycemic control and examination findings.

Blood pressure management was reviewed. Recommend repeat blood pressure assessment and medication reconciliation, with adjustment of antihypertensive therapy as clinically indicated to reduce cardiovascular and renal risk.

Smoking cessation was strongly advised; counseling and evidence-based cessation support should be offered.

Return for close outpatient follow-up after initiation or adjustment of diabetes management to review glucose data, treatment tolerance, blood pressure, and paresthesia.
