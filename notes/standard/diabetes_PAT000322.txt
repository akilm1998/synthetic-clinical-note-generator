Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Patrick Bell is a 48-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 5 years ago. Current disease control is poor, with no active diabetes management documented. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet.

He has a history of anxiety for approximately 30 years and depression for approximately 6 years. Smoking status is positive. No medication regimen was provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 127/75 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 175 cm; weight 87.6 kg; BMI 28.6 kg/m².

Laboratory results:
HbA1c 9.2%.
Fasting glucose 234 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled. HbA1c of 9.2% and fasting glucose of 234 mg/dL indicate significant hyperglycemia despite the absence of reported symptoms. Diabetes duration is approximately 5 years. He is overweight with a BMI of 28.6 kg/m². No neuropathic, infectious, constitutional, or visual symptoms are reported.

2. Depression, reported duration 6 years. Current symptom burden and treatment were not reviewed in the available information.

3. Anxiety, reported duration 30 years. Current symptom burden and treatment were not reviewed in the available information.

4. Tobacco use. Smoking cessation is clinically indicated, particularly in the setting of diabetes.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the increased risk of long-term vascular, renal, ocular, and neurologic complications with persistent hyperglycemia. Recommend establishing an active diabetes treatment plan, including review of prior and current therapies and shared decision-making regarding pharmacologic treatment. Medication initiation or adjustment should be based on a complete medication reconciliation, renal function assessment, contraindications, and patient preferences.

Counseled regarding a structured diabetic eating pattern, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, portion control, weight reduction, and gradual increase in regular physical activity as tolerated. Recommend home glucose monitoring if clinically appropriate and review of readings at follow-up.

Recommend urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine/eGFR, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes preventive care, including foot and retinal evaluation, as part of comprehensive complication screening. These should be arranged as outpatient follow-up investigations.

Encouraged smoking cessation and offered counseling and evidence-based cessation support.

Depression and anxiety should be reassessed at follow-up, including current mood, functional impact, safety assessment, and treatment preferences.

Follow up promptly to establish diabetes therapy and review glucose control, with repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval.
