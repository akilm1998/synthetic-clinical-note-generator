Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for hypertension.

Subjective

Scarlett Hughes is an 83-year-old woman presenting to establish outpatient care for longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 23 years and not currently under active management. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and nosebleeds. No acute hypertensive symptoms reported today.

Medical history is significant for coronary artery disease for approximately 17 years, hyperlipidemia for 19 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 8 years. Current medication list and allergies were not provided and should be reviewed carefully at the next medication reconciliation. She reports current tobacco use. No additional social, family, surgical, or hospitalization history is available in the provided history.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 146/86 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 175 cm; weight 89.1 kg; BMI 29.1 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Sodium 145 mmol/L; potassium 5.3 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms reported. Physical examination findings beyond the recorded vital signs were not provided.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding, currently suboptimally controlled based on today’s office reading and not actively managed. She is asymptomatic without features suggesting hypertensive emergency. Given her advanced age, coronary artery disease, diabetes, and elevated potassium, treatment selection should be individualized and based on medication reconciliation, repeat blood pressure assessment, and renal/electrolyte evaluation.

2. Mild hyperkalemia by the provided serum potassium of 5.3 mmol/L. This warrants confirmation and review of renal function, dietary contributors, and any current medications or supplements that may increase potassium. No medication changes can be safely recommended until the current regimen is clarified.

3. Coronary artery disease, longstanding.

4. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

5. Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

6. Current tobacco use.

7. Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Obtain a complete medication and allergy reconciliation, including prescription medications, over-the-counter agents, potassium-containing supplements, and nonprescription anti-inflammatory medications. Review prior antihypertensive treatment, adherence, tolerance, and history of adverse effects before initiating or adjusting therapy.

Repeat serum electrolytes and obtain renal function testing promptly to reassess the potassium level before selecting an antihypertensive regimen. Avoid empiric use of medications that may worsen hyperkalemia until this evaluation is completed.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a validated upper-arm cuff, recording readings at consistent times and bringing the log to follow-up. Confirm blood pressure control with repeat office measurements.

Consider baseline ECG, urinalysis, and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk and hypertension evaluation, if not recently completed. Coordinate ongoing management of coronary artery disease, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia within the overall cardiovascular risk-reduction plan.

Counseled regarding reduced sodium intake, avoidance of highly processed foods, gradual physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and limitation of alcohol if applicable. Strongly encouraged smoking cessation; offer cessation counseling and pharmacologic support after reviewing medical history and current medications.

Seek urgent medical attention for chest pain, new shortness of breath, severe headache, focal neurologic symptoms, confusion, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up in the near term after medication reconciliation, repeat electrolytes/renal assessment, and review of home blood pressure readings.
