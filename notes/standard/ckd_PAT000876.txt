Chief Complaint
Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective
Liam Matthews is a 52-year-old man, height 174 cm, weight 79.3 kg, BMI 26.2 kg/m², presenting for comprehensive annual review. He reports no fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. He does not smoke.

Chronic kidney disease has been present for approximately 16 years and is currently not under active management. He has no reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms today. Relevant comorbidities include anemia of chronic disease for 6 years and hyperlipidemia for 1 year. Medication use, allergies, family history, and prior specialist care are not documented in the available history.

Objective
Vital signs: BP 135/86 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 4.24 mg/dL
BUN 86 mg/dL
eGFR 11 mL/min/1.73 m²

No edema or other symptoms of fluid overload reported. No additional examination findings documented.

Assessment
1. Chronic kidney disease of longstanding duration with markedly reduced kidney function and elevated BUN and creatinine. Although currently asymptomatic, the degree of renal impairment requires prompt reassessment and specialist involvement. There is no formal disease stage assigned in this note.
2. Anemia of chronic disease, present for approximately 6 years; current hemoglobin is not available for assessment.
3. Hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 1 year; current lipid values and treatment status are not available.
4. Preventive health maintenance review.

Plan
Arrange prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation and management of advanced chronic kidney disease, including discussion of renal-protective care and future renal replacement planning if clinically indicated.

Repeat renal function testing and obtain a serum electrolyte panel, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as clinically appropriate. Review for complications of chronic kidney disease, including anemia, electrolyte or acid-base disturbance, mineral-bone disorder, and volume changes.

Perform a complete medication and supplement reconciliation at follow-up. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic over-the-counter products unless specifically approved by a clinician. Review dosing of all medications in the context of renal function. Encourage moderate sodium intake, avoidance of dehydration, and a balanced renal-appropriate diet pending nephrology assessment.

Monitor blood pressure and continue lifestyle measures including regular physical activity as tolerated, weight maintenance, and avoidance of tobacco. Review lipid management after obtaining current lipid results and medication history.

For anemia, obtain CBC and iron assessment as clinically appropriate before determining treatment. Do not initiate erythropoietin or other therapy without evaluation and specialist guidance.

Discussed urgent evaluation for nausea or vomiting, worsening fatigue, confusion, severe itching, new swelling, shortness of breath, chest pain, markedly reduced urination, or inability to maintain oral intake.

Update age-appropriate preventive care, including immunizations and routine cancer screening, based on prior records and shared decision-making. Follow up promptly after nephrology evaluation and laboratory reassessment; continue at least annual comprehensive primary care review.
