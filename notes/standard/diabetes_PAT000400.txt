Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Owen Myers is a 72-year-old man presenting for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes, diagnosed approximately 24 years ago. He is not currently reporting increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. Diabetes control has been suboptimal, with no active management documented. He does not smoke.

Relevant comorbidities include longstanding obesity and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. No interval complications are reported.

Objective

Height 171 cm; weight 116.4 kg; BMI 39.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 125/79 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98%.

Hemoglobin A1c 9.3%. Fasting glucose 232 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently poorly controlled based on elevated fasting glucose and HbA1c. Despite the absence of hyperglycemic or neuropathic symptoms, glycemic values indicate clinically significant hyperglycemia and increased long-term risk for microvascular and cardiovascular complications.

Severe obesity, longstanding, contributing to insulin resistance and diabetes burden.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding, requiring consideration when selecting diabetes therapy and counseling regarding weight reduction.

Plan

Discussed laboratory results and the importance of improving glycemic control. Lifestyle measures alone are unlikely to be sufficient at the current HbA1c; recommend initiation or intensification of guideline-directed diabetes pharmacotherapy after medication reconciliation and review of renal and hepatic function. Treatment selection should account for obesity and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Counseled on reducing refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened foods, portion control, increased dietary fiber, and gradual activity appropriate for functional status. Encourage a structured weight-reduction program.

Recommend monitoring home glucose as clinically appropriate and review readings at follow-up. Assess for diabetic complications with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine/eGFR, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive foot examination, and recommended retinal evaluation. Continue routine blood pressure and cardiovascular risk assessment.

Return for diabetes follow-up after treatment initiation or adjustment, with repeat HbA1c at an appropriate interval.
