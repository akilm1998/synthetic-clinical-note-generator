Chief Complaint

Annual review of type 2 diabetes and preventive health.

Subjective

Natalie Ross is a 49-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 10-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

Current diabetes management is not established, and no medication regimen was provided for review. Most recent HbA1c is 9.3% and fasting glucose is 205 mg/dL, consistent with suboptimal glycemic control. She also has an 8-year history of anxiety. She currently smokes. No additional social, family, medication, allergy, or surgical history was provided.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 127/78 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 167 cm, weight 74.5 kg, BMI 26.7 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.3%; fasting glucose 205 mg/dL.

No acute symptoms reported today. No physical examination abnormalities were provided.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without reported complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c 9.3% and fasting glucose 205 mg/dL. She denies current hyperglycemic, neuropathic, or infectious symptoms. Ten-year disease duration increases the importance of screening for renal, retinal, and peripheral neuropathic complications.

Anxiety, longstanding, with an 8-year history.

Tobacco use, active.

BMI 26.7 kg/m², in the overweight range.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the need for active diabetes management. Review barriers to care, dietary pattern, physical activity, home glucose monitoring, and treatment preferences at follow-up. Recommend initiation of appropriate pharmacologic therapy after medication reconciliation and review of renal function and contraindications; metformin would be a typical first-line option if clinically appropriate. Consider additional therapy if glycemic response remains inadequate.

Recommend nutrition counseling focused on reducing refined carbohydrates and sweetened beverages, portion control, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Encourage home glucose monitoring if a monitoring plan is established and review readings at follow-up.

Recommend diabetes complication screening, including serum creatinine/eGFR, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive foot examination with sensory and pulse assessment, and a dilated retinal examination. These should be arranged as part of ongoing diabetes care. Reinforce daily foot care and prompt evaluation of wounds or skin changes.

Provide tobacco-cessation counseling and discuss behavioral support and medication options when she is ready to quit.

Review anxiety symptoms, sleep, functional impact, and safety at a subsequent focused assessment; offer counseling or behavioral health support as appropriate.

Continue routine preventive care, including age-appropriate cancer screening, immunization review, dental care, and regular blood pressure and cardiovascular risk assessment. Follow up promptly to establish a diabetes treatment plan and reassess glycemic control after treatment is initiated.
