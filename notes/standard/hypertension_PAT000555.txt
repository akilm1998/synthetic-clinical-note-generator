Chief Complaint

Annual review and chronic disease follow-up.

Subjective

Elijah Stone is a 41-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review and evaluation of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 15 years and currently without active treatment. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, and epistaxis. He is a nonsmoker.

Known coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago, is noted. No current medication regimen is provided. No acute cardiovascular or neurologic symptoms are reported today.

Height is 184 cm, weight 101.2 kg, and BMI 29.9 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 145/99 mmHg. Pulse 76 bpm. Respiratory rate 16/min. Temperature 36.7°C. Oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Laboratory data: Sodium 135 mmol/L. Potassium 5.0 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently inadequately controlled based on today’s elevated reading. He is asymptomatic, with no reported features of hypertensive emergency. The duration of untreated hypertension and coexisting coronary artery disease increase his cardiovascular risk.

Coronary artery disease, present for approximately 1 year. No anginal or heart failure symptoms reported today.

Overweight, with BMI 29.9 kg/m².

Borderline elevated potassium at 5.0 mmol/L. This is clinically relevant when selecting antihypertensive therapy, particularly agents that may further increase potassium.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management, given the persistent elevation and history of coronary artery disease. Recommend confirming the blood pressure trend with properly performed repeat office measurements and home blood pressure monitoring, with a written log for review.

Recommend initiation of antihypertensive therapy after review of repeat electrolytes and renal function. Because potassium is 5.0 mmol/L, repeat serum electrolytes and assessment of kidney function should be obtained before starting or selecting a renin-angiotensin system inhibitor. Medication choice should take the coronary artery disease and potassium level into account.

Recommend a low-sodium, heart-healthy eating pattern, regular aerobic and resistance activity as tolerated, gradual weight reduction, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Continue smoking avoidance.

Recommend cardiovascular risk assessment including lipid profile and urinalysis, and an ECG as part of evaluation of longstanding hypertension and known coronary artery disease. Further preventive care should be reviewed, including age-appropriate cancer screening and immunizations.

Advised urgent evaluation for new chest pain, marked shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or other acute concerning symptoms.

Follow up after home blood pressure readings and repeat laboratory assessment to finalize treatment and continue chronic disease management.
