Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Brian Simmons is a 72-year-old man seen for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 33 years. He is not currently receiving active management for his blood pressure. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. No acute cardiovascular or neurologic concerns reported today.

He continues to smoke. Weight is 85.2 kg at 177 cm, with BMI 27.2 kg/m². Medical history is also notable for hyperlipidemia for approximately 40 years and coronary artery disease for approximately 20 years. Current medication use was not provided and should be reconciled before treatment decisions.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 153/95 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

General: Patient presents for routine ambulatory follow-up without reported acute distress.

Laboratory data: Sodium 137 mmol/L; potassium 3.6 mmol/L.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal at today’s reading of 153/95 mmHg. He is asymptomatic without reported features of hypertensive emergency. Blood pressure has not been actively managed, and treatment initiation or adjustment is indicated after medication reconciliation and confirmation of the outpatient blood pressure pattern.

2. Coronary artery disease, longstanding. He denies current chest pain, dyspnea, or palpitations. Elevated blood pressure and ongoing tobacco use increase cardiovascular risk.

3. Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

4. Tobacco use. Continued smoking contributes substantially to cardiovascular risk.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given his age, longstanding hypertension, and coronary artery disease. Recheck blood pressure with proper technique and obtain home readings if feasible to assess the usual pattern. Arrange close follow-up for review and initiation of an appropriate antihypertensive regimen after confirming current medications, contraindications, and relevant baseline renal function. Potential therapy should be selected with his coronary disease and potassium of 3.6 mmol/L in mind.

Counseled on reducing dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, limiting alcohol if applicable, weight control, and smoking cessation. Encouraged a heart-healthy diet for both hypertension and hyperlipidemia.

Recommend updating cardiovascular risk assessment with lipid profile and urinalysis, and obtaining an ECG as clinically appropriate. Continue monitoring serum electrolytes when antihypertensive therapy is started or adjusted.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, or significant visual change. Follow up soon for blood pressure review and treatment planning.
