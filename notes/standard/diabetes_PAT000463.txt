Chief Complaint

Annual review of diabetes and chronic medical conditions.

Subjective

Michael Brooks is an 83-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 9-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and has not been actively managing the condition. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

He has longstanding hypertension of approximately 38 years and obesity of approximately 50 years. He continues to smoke. No current medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Height 176 cm. Weight 110.6 kg. BMI 35.7 kg/m².

Blood pressure 148/83 mmHg, pulse 84 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

HbA1c 9.5%. Fasting glucose 216 mg/dL.

Assessment

1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled. HbA1c of 9.5% and fasting glucose of 216 mg/dL indicate persistent hyperglycemia despite the absence of reported osmotic, constitutional, visual, infectious, or neuropathic symptoms. He has had diabetes for approximately 9 years and is not currently engaged in active management. His age and longstanding hypertension increase his cardiovascular and renal risk.

2. Hypertension, longstanding, with blood pressure 148/83 mmHg today. Control is suboptimal based on the available office measurement. Current antihypertensive medications are not provided.

3. Class II obesity, BMI 35.7 kg/m², longstanding. This contributes to insulin resistance and increases cardiovascular and metabolic risk.

4. Tobacco use. Continued smoking substantially increases his cardiovascular, pulmonary, and cancer risk, particularly in the setting of diabetes and hypertension.

5. Preventive health maintenance. At age 83, preventive decisions should be individualized according to functional status, life expectancy, and patient preferences.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the importance of re-engaging in diabetes care. Recommend nutrition counseling focused on reducing concentrated carbohydrates and sugary beverages, portion control, and a sustainable eating pattern. Encourage gradual, activity-appropriate physical activity as tolerated, with attention to fall risk and overall functional status.

Recommend initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy through shared decision-making, taking age, comorbidities, renal function, hypoglycemia risk, cost, and treatment preferences into account. Renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio is recommended before selecting and dosing therapy. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate interval to assess response.

Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot evaluation, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensory testing, and a dilated retinal examination. Counsel regarding daily foot inspection and prompt evaluation of any wound, redness, or infection. Recommend fasting lipid profile and cardiovascular risk review as part of diabetes care.

Reassess blood pressure with repeat measurements and review the current antihypertensive regimen when available. Encourage dietary sodium reduction, weight reduction, and smoking cessation.

Smoking cessation counseling provided. Discuss pharmacologic and behavioral cessation support based on readiness and contraindications.

Review age-appropriate immunizations, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, shingles, and tetanus vaccination, and update according to prior vaccination history. Continue individualized cancer screening and preventive care based on overall health, preferences, and expected benefit.

Follow-up

Close outpatient follow-up is recommended to address hyperglycemia, review renal and cardiovascular risk assessment, establish a sustainable treatment plan, and reassess blood pressure and glycemic control.
