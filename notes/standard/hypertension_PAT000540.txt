Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Maria Armstrong is a 57-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension, diagnosed approximately 10 years ago. Her hypertension has not been actively managed, and no current antihypertensive regimen is documented. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis.

She has a 16-year history of chronic kidney disease. She reports current tobacco use. No additional interval concerns are reported.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 166/93 mmHg; pulse 73 bpm; respiratory rate 15/min; temperature 36.8°C; oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 178 cm, weight 64.6 kg, BMI 20.4 kg/m².

Recent serum electrolytes: sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 4.4 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and currently inadequate disease control. She is asymptomatic today without features concerning for hypertensive emergency. Chronic kidney disease is an important comorbidity and will influence antihypertensive selection and monitoring. Current tobacco use is an additional cardiovascular risk factor. Electrolytes are within the provided reference ranges.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current blood pressure and the importance of consistent treatment to reduce cardiovascular and renal risk. Recommend confirming persistent elevation with repeat measurements using proper technique and home blood pressure monitoring if available.

Recommend initiation or re-establishment of antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, with selection guided by chronic kidney disease, renal function, urine albumin assessment, contraindications, and shared decision-making. Obtain renal function testing and urinalysis with urine albumin assessment before or soon after treatment initiation; repeat electrolytes and renal function after medication changes. Consider ECG and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk assessment.

Counseled on reducing dietary sodium, maintaining regular physical activity as tolerated, limiting alcohol if applicable, and tobacco cessation. Reviewed avoidance of non-prescription medications that may worsen blood pressure or kidney function unless discussed with a clinician.

Advised urgent evaluation for severe or persistent headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, vision changes, or other acute concerning symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up for blood pressure review and treatment adjustment.
