Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Philip Donovan is a 31-year-old male presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 3 years and not currently under active treatment. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma. He continues to smoke. He has obesity of approximately 3 years’ duration and chronic kidney disease documented for approximately 1 year. No current lipid-lowering medication use is provided.

Objective

Height 162 cm; weight 98.7 kg; BMI 37.6 kg/m². Blood pressure 125/83 mmHg, pulse 84 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Recent lipid panel: total cholesterol 288 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 190 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 42 mg/dL, and triglycerides 280 mg/dL. No xanthomas or xanthelasma are reported. No cardiovascular or claudication symptoms reported.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, currently untreated. The lipid pattern is associated with increased long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of active tobacco use, obesity, and chronic kidney disease. He is presently without symptoms suggestive of ischemia or peripheral arterial disease.

Obesity, BMI 37.6 kg/m².

Chronic kidney disease, documented for approximately 1 year.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the importance of active treatment and cardiovascular risk reduction. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after shared decision-making and review of renal and hepatic considerations. If LDL reduction is inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, consider addition of ezetimibe. Triglyceride elevation should initially be addressed with dietary changes, weight reduction, smoking cessation, and improved overall lipid control.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, lean proteins, and unsaturated fats while reducing saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol. Encourage gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated and a structured weight-loss effort.

Strongly advised smoking cessation; offered counseling and discussion of pharmacologic cessation support.

Recommend repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Consider evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and review of kidney function before selecting and dosing therapy. Given LDL cholesterol of 190 mg/dL at a young age, assess for possible inherited lipid disorder and clarify family cardiovascular history at a future visit.

Continue routine management of chronic kidney disease and avoid nonessential nephrotoxic medications. Return for lipid treatment follow-up after laboratory reassessment or sooner for new chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or other concerning symptoms.
