Chief Complaint
Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective
Matthew Reed is a 50-year-old man seen for follow-up of anemia present for approximately 14 years. He reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or perceived pallor. Anemia has not been actively managed. Relevant comorbidities include chronic infection for 17 years, malignancy for 4 years, and chronic kidney disease for 1 year. He continues to smoke. No medication use is provided.

Objective
Height 184 cm, weight 96.2 kg, BMI 28.4 kg/m². Blood pressure 128/85 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory results: hemoglobin 9.0 g/dL, hematocrit 27.8%, RBC 3.05 million/µL, MCV 91 fL, serum iron 49 µg/dL, ferritin 330 ng/mL, TIBC 212 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 23.1%.

Assessment
Chronic, asymptomatic normocytic anemia. The reduced hemoglobin and RBC count with normal MCV, low serum iron and TIBC, and elevated ferritin are most consistent with anemia associated with chronic inflammation and chronic disease. Long-standing chronic infection, malignancy, and chronic kidney disease are likely contributing factors. The degree of anemia warrants further evaluation despite the absence of current symptoms. There is no current evidence of hemodynamic instability based on vital signs.

Overweight by BMI. Ongoing tobacco use.

Plan
Obtain updated CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to reassess severity and marrow response. Check vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers as part of the anemia evaluation. Continue assessment of the chronic infection, malignancy, and kidney disease as potential drivers of the anemia, with coordination of management among the clinicians involved in those conditions.

Review for occult blood loss and other contributors if anemia worsens or the pattern changes. Iron supplementation should not be initiated empirically based solely on the current iron studies; consider iron therapy or an erythropoiesis-stimulating approach only after review of the updated evaluation and underlying disease activity.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and weight management. Seek urgent care for new shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, palpitations, bleeding, or marked worsening of weakness. Follow up after repeat laboratory evaluation to reassess hemoglobin and management.
