Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Emma Carter is a 31-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of essential hypertension, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She reports no headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. Hypertension has remained well controlled with current management. She does not smoke.

She also has a history of coronary artery disease and hyperlipidemia, each present for approximately 1 year. No interval cardiovascular symptoms are reported.

Objective

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 122/78 mmHg; pulse 80 bpm; respiratory rate 15/min; temperature 36.8°C; oxygen saturation 99%.

Height 174 cm; weight 79.9 kg; BMI 26.4 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 138 mmol/L; potassium 4.8 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, currently well controlled without reported symptoms or evidence of acute complications.

Coronary artery disease, clinically stable by interval history.

Hyperlipidemia.

Plan

Continue current hypertension management without adjustment based on today’s controlled blood pressure and absence of symptoms.

Reinforced heart-healthy lifestyle measures, including moderation of dietary sodium, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight management, and avoidance of tobacco. Current BMI is in the overweight range.

Continue monitoring blood pressure periodically at home or in the clinic and bring readings to follow-up.

Recommend ongoing cardiovascular risk-factor management in the setting of coronary artery disease and hyperlipidemia. A lipid profile, urinalysis, and ECG may be considered as part of continued hypertension and cardiovascular surveillance if not recently completed.

Reviewed symptoms requiring prompt medical attention, including severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic or visual changes, or markedly elevated blood pressure.

Routine follow-up for blood pressure and cardiovascular risk management.
