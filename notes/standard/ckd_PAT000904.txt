Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Richard Lawson is a 63-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 11 years ago. He has a 14-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. He reports ongoing fatigue. He denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. No smoking history.

Recent renal laboratory results were reviewed: serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 60 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m². He is not currently documented as receiving active management for his kidney disease. Medication list and allergies were not provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 145/87 mmHg, pulse 70 bpm, respirations 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 190 cm, weight 76.9 kg, BMI 21.3 kg/m².

Patient-reported review is negative for edema, dyspnea, nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, urinary frequency changes, and sleep disturbance. Fatigue is present.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 60 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m². The patient has had kidney disease for approximately 11 years in the setting of longstanding type 2 diabetes. He is currently symptomatic with fatigue but has no reported findings suggestive of acute fluid overload or prominent uremic symptoms. Disease requires prompt specialty assessment and structured renal management.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, with chronic kidney disease.

Blood pressure elevated at today’s visit.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the current renal function and the need for prompt nephrology evaluation to establish a comprehensive management plan, assess renal replacement therapy needs, and provide education regarding future treatment options if indicated.

Recommend repeat renal assessment with serum electrolytes, creatinine/eGFR, BUN, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, CBC, and intact parathyroid hormone. Renal ultrasound is also recommended to assess renal structure and exclude an obstructive process.

Review and optimize diabetes and blood pressure management with attention to renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic medications, including NSAIDs. Medication reconciliation is needed before selecting or adjusting therapy.

Monitor for worsening fatigue, nausea or vomiting, confusion, pruritus, muscle cramps, reduced urine output, new swelling, or shortness of breath. Advised urgent or emergency evaluation for severe shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, inability to maintain oral intake, or markedly decreased urine output.

Continue close outpatient follow-up while specialty evaluation is arranged.
