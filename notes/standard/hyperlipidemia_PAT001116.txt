Chief Complaint

Annual review and evaluation of untreated elevated cholesterol.

Subjective

Victor Pierce is a 38-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. Hyperlipidemia has been present for approximately 4 years and has not been actively managed. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 354 mg/dL, LDL-C 238 mg/dL, HDL-C 36 mg/dL, and triglycerides 398 mg/dL. He reports xanthelasma. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness.

Medical history is also notable for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 2 years, chronic kidney disease for 1 year, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 12 years. He currently smokes. No lipid-lowering medications are provided in the current history.

Objective

Height 185 cm; weight 95.5 kg; BMI 27.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 135/90 mmHg, pulse 73 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Xanthelasma reported. No reported chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and substantially elevated triglycerides, associated with low HDL-C. The LDL-C of 238 mg/dL represents a significant long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, particularly in the setting of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, tobacco use, elevated BMI, and borderline-high blood pressure. Xanthelasma is also present. No current symptoms suggestive of active angina or claudication.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 2-year duration, requiring ongoing cardiovascular risk-factor optimization.

Chronic kidney disease, 1-year duration. Renal status should be considered when selecting and monitoring therapy.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding. This is not, by itself, a contraindication to statin therapy, but liver-related monitoring is appropriate.

Tobacco use.

Overweight by BMI, with BMI 27.9 kg/m².

Plan

Recommend initiation of guideline-directed high-intensity statin therapy after discussion of risks, benefits, contraindications, and patient preference; no current lipid-lowering regimen is documented. Given the degree of LDL elevation, treatment should not be delayed while awaiting lifestyle response alone. Consider addition of ezetimibe if LDL-C remains substantially elevated despite adherence and tolerated statin therapy. Management should account for chronic kidney disease and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.

Repeat a fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response, with consideration of ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further risk assessment. Review for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia, including glycemic control, thyroid disease, renal disease, dietary factors, and alcohol intake, using clinically appropriate testing and history.

Advise a heart-healthy eating pattern low in saturated and trans fats and refined carbohydrates, increased soluble fiber, regular physical activity as tolerated, and gradual weight reduction. Because triglycerides are 398 mg/dL, emphasize reduction of added sugars and refined starches and avoidance of excess alcohol.

Strongly recommend smoking cessation; offer counseling and evidence-based cessation therapy. Continue close monitoring and optimization of diabetes, kidney disease, and blood pressure, as these substantially increase cardiovascular risk.

Review preventive care during the annual visit, including age- and risk-appropriate immunizations, diabetes-related preventive care, renal monitoring, dental and eye care, and cancer screening based on current guidelines. Advise urgent evaluation for new or persistent chest pain, acute shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, or exertional leg pain.

Follow up after repeat lipid assessment and sooner to review treatment tolerance and adherence.
