Chief Complaint

Medication review and management of longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Monica Shaw is a 66-year-old woman with a 19-year history of hyperlipidemia, coronary artery disease diagnosed 7 years ago, and chronic kidney disease present for 21 years. She is a nonsmoker. Current weight is 66.7 kg with BMI 25.1 kg/m².

She denies chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness. She reports xanthomas and xanthelasma. No active lipid-lowering medication regimen is identified for review today; adherence to lipid therapy is therefore not established. Treatment tolerance cannot be assessed in the absence of a current documented lipid-lowering agent.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 138/87 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 163 cm, weight 66.7 kg, BMI 25.1 kg/m².

Recent lipid results: total cholesterol 345 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 232 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 45 mg/dL, triglycerides 338 mg/dL.

Xanthomas and xanthelasma are reported. No current ischemic symptoms reported.

Assessment

Longstanding severe hyperlipidemia with marked LDL elevation and elevated triglycerides, currently without an established lipid-lowering treatment regimen. The presence of xanthomas and xanthelasma supports prolonged lipid exposure. Her known coronary artery disease places her at particularly high risk for recurrent atherosclerotic cardiovascular events. Chronic kidney disease is an important comorbidity when selecting and monitoring therapy. Blood pressure is mildly elevated today.

Plan

Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin 40 mg daily, after confirming the complete medication list, allergies, prior statin exposure, and renal and hepatic considerations. Discuss expected cardiovascular benefit, adherence, and potential adverse effects, including myalgias or dark urine, with instructions to report concerning symptoms promptly.

Because of established coronary artery disease and LDL cholesterol of 232 mg/dL, reassess response after initiation and consider adding ezetimibe if LDL reduction remains inadequate or if high-intensity statin therapy is not tolerated. Persistent triglyceride elevation should be addressed primarily through LDL-directed therapy, dietary modification, weight maintenance, and improved physical activity; avoid excess refined carbohydrates and alcohol if applicable.

Reinforce a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fiber, legumes, whole grains, and unsaturated fats while limiting saturated and trans fats. Encourage regular activity as tolerated and continued tobacco avoidance.

Recommend repeat lipid testing after approximately 4–12 weeks of established therapy to assess effectiveness and adherence, with ongoing monitoring for medication tolerance. Given coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease, coordinate lipid management with her established medical care team. Advise urgent evaluation for new chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dyspnea, or other symptoms concerning for ischemia.
