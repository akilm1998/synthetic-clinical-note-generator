Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Brianna Gibson is a 61-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of anemia present for approximately 7 years. She has a 15-year history of inflammatory bowel disease. She reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, reduced exercise tolerance, shortness of breath, palpitations, or other anemia-related symptoms. She does not smoke. No active anemia-directed treatment is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 124/81 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 174 cm, weight 61.5 kg, BMI 20.3 kg/m².

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.9 g/dL
Hematocrit 26.3%
RBC count 3.09 million/µL
MCV 85 fL
Serum iron 46 µg/dL
Ferritin 353 ng/mL
TIBC 263 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 17.5%

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of longstanding inflammatory bowel disease. The anemia is normocytic with reduced serum iron and transferrin saturation, while ferritin is elevated and TIBC is not increased, supporting impaired iron availability related to chronic inflammation rather than a straightforward iron-deficiency pattern. Hemoglobin and hematocrit remain significantly reduced, although she is currently without reported cardiopulmonary or constitutional symptoms and has stable vital signs.

Inflammatory bowel disease, longstanding. Ongoing inflammatory activity may be contributing to reduced erythropoiesis and functional iron restriction. Current disease activity and treatment status are not documented in this encounter.

Plan

Discussed the laboratory pattern and likely contribution of chronic inflammation to the anemia. Recommend reassessment with repeat CBC, reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers to further characterize the anemia and exclude additional reversible contributors.

Recommend review of inflammatory bowel disease activity and coordination with the clinician managing that condition, as improved control of underlying inflammation is important for anemia management.

Given the persistent hemoglobin of 8.9 g/dL and longstanding anemia without active documented treatment, recommend consideration of oral or intravenous iron based on tolerance, gastrointestinal disease activity, and follow-up iron indices, with specialist input as clinically appropriate. Hematology evaluation may be considered if the anemia persists, worsens, or remains unexplained after initial evaluation.

Continue monitoring for fatigue, dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, or reduced exercise tolerance. Follow up after repeat laboratory assessment or sooner for new or worsening symptoms.
