Chief Complaint

Annual review and chronic disease follow-up.

Subjective

Avery Ward is a 67-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 34-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. Recent glycemic control is poor, with HbA1c 9.5% and fasting glucose 281 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, urinary frequency, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, or recurrent infections.

She reports numbness in the hands or feet without associated tingling. No other diabetes-related symptoms are reported.

Comorbidities include hypertension for 16 years, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 32 years, and anxiety for 10 years. She does not smoke.

Objective

Height 178 cm; weight 71.3 kg; BMI 22.5 kg/m².

Blood pressure 148/88 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.5%; fasting glucose 281 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complication, with markedly uncontrolled hyperglycemia after a prolonged disease duration. Although she denies typical hyperglycemic symptoms, the fasting glucose and HbA1c indicate clinically significant poor control.

Numbness of the hands or feet, concerning for possible diabetic peripheral sensory involvement; requires focused neurologic and foot assessment.

Hypertension with elevated office blood pressure today.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Anxiety, longstanding.

Annual preventive health review.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active diabetes management and reviewed the risks of persistent hyperglycemia, including eye, kidney, nerve, and cardiovascular complications. Develop an individualized nutrition, physical activity, glucose-monitoring, and medication plan at the earliest opportunity. Discuss initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy, with choice guided by renal function, hepatic status, hypoglycemia risk, treatment preferences, and overall cardiovascular risk.

Arrange repeat HbA1c after a reasonable treatment interval and obtain renal assessment, including serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, along with a fasting lipid profile. Review glucose monitoring technique and home readings if available.

Perform comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including skin inspection, pulses, and protective sensation, at follow-up. Recommend routine dilated retinal examination. Counsel regarding daily foot inspection, appropriate footwear, and prompt evaluation of wounds or infection.

Reassess blood pressure outside the office and review current antihypertensive management. Reinforce a lower-sodium, heart-healthy eating pattern and regular activity as tolerated.

Continue monitoring liver health in the context of longstanding non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and avoid excess alcohol and hepatotoxic over-the-counter products.

Review anxiety symptoms and impact on daily functioning at follow-up.

Update age-appropriate immunizations and preventive screenings, including cardiovascular risk assessment and routine cancer screening, based on prior screening history. Follow up promptly for diabetes treatment planning and blood pressure reassessment.
