Chief Complaint

Annual review of hypertension and chronic medical conditions.

Subjective

Charlie Brown is a 48-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. Essential hypertension has been present for approximately 3 years and has not been consistently controlled. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis.

Relevant comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 13 years, hyperlipidemia for 2 years, and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. He reports no tobacco use. Current medication list and adherence were not provided and should be reconciled as part of today’s chronic disease review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 134/85 mmHg, pulse 74 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 179 cm, weight 67.6 kg, BMI 21.1 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 136 mmol/L, potassium 3.6 mmol/L.

No hypertensive or cardiopulmonary symptoms reported today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, present for approximately 3 years. Current office blood pressure is mildly above the preferred target for a patient with diabetes and coronary artery disease, although there are no symptoms suggesting hypertensive urgency or emergency. Overall control requires continued attention, including confirmation with out-of-office readings and review of treatment adherence and regimen.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding, 13 years’ duration, with increased cardiovascular risk in the setting of established coronary artery disease.

Coronary artery disease, approximately 1 year since diagnosis. He is currently free of chest pain or exertional cardiopulmonary symptoms by history.

Hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 2 years, contributing to overall cardiovascular risk.

Normal BMI. Nonsmoker.

Plan

Review and reconcile all current medications, including antihypertensive, lipid-lowering, diabetes, and coronary artery disease therapies; assess adherence, tolerance, and affordability before making treatment changes. If blood pressure remains above individualized goal on repeat or home measurements, adjust therapy according to renal function, potassium level, diabetes status, and coronary disease.

Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a validated upper-arm cuff, seated after several minutes of rest, and maintenance of a written log for review. Reinforce a low-sodium, heart-healthy dietary pattern, regular aerobic and resistance activity as tolerated, and avoidance of excess alcohol. Continue avoidance of tobacco.

Recommend periodic monitoring of renal function and electrolytes, particularly if an ACE inhibitor, angiotensin receptor blocker, or diuretic is used. The current sodium is 136 mmol/L and potassium is 3.6 mmol/L; these should be followed with any medication adjustment.

For cardiovascular risk assessment, recommend updated lipid testing and diabetes monitoring, including hemoglobin A1c, as well as urinalysis and urine albumin assessment when appropriate. Recommend ECG as part of cardiovascular follow-up if not recently completed or if symptoms develop.

Continue coordinated management of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and coronary artery disease, with attention to individualized blood pressure and lipid targets. Seek urgent evaluation for chest pain, severe headache, acute shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Review age-appropriate preventive care, including immunizations and recommended cancer screening, during the annual health maintenance assessment.

Follow up after home blood pressure readings and medication reconciliation, or sooner for new cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms.
