Chief Complaint

Annual review of longstanding type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Deborah Lawson is a 61-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 27-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, currently without documented end-organ complications but with numbness and tingling in the hands and feet. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, and recurrent infections.

Her diabetes has not been actively managed recently. Most recent laboratory results show hemoglobin A1c 9.7% and fasting glucose 223 mg/dL, consistent with substantially above-goal glycemic control. She also has dyslipidemia of 11 years’ duration and depression of 16 years’ duration. She does not smoke.

Objective

Height 158 cm. Weight 72.1 kg. BMI 28.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 123/80 mmHg, pulse 69 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus with significantly elevated hemoglobin A1c and fasting glucose after a prolonged period without active management. She is not reporting symptomatic hyperglycemia, but the duration of disease and current glycemic burden increase her risk for microvascular and cardiovascular complications. Numbness and tingling in the hands and feet raise concern for possible diabetic peripheral neuropathic symptoms; objective assessment is needed. There is no reported history of wounds or recurrent infections.

Overweight, with BMI 28.9 kg/m².

Dyslipidemia, longstanding, requiring cardiovascular risk reassessment in the context of poorly controlled diabetes.

Depression, longstanding. Current mood symptoms and treatment status should be reviewed as part of chronic disease care.

Preventive health maintenance reviewed in the context of an annual visit, including diabetes-related eye, kidney, and foot care, immunizations, cardiovascular risk reduction, and age-appropriate cancer screening.

Plan

Discussed the importance of re-engaging in structured diabetes care. Recommend individualized nutrition counseling emphasizing portion control, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages, and a consistent meal pattern. Encourage gradual, regular physical activity as tolerated, with a goal of at least 150 minutes weekly if medically appropriate. Weight reduction would likely improve glycemic control and cardiovascular risk.

Arrange prompt medication-management discussion for diabetes, taking into account the markedly elevated A1c, comorbid dyslipidemia, renal function, cardiovascular risk, and patient preferences. She should begin or resume home glucose monitoring if appropriate and receive education regarding recognition of symptomatic hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia.

Recommend repeat hemoglobin A1c after an appropriate interval following initiation of an active treatment plan. Obtain renal assessment with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, as well as a fasting lipid profile. Consider additional diabetes evaluation with fasting or random plasma glucose as clinically indicated. Results should guide medication selection and risk reduction.

Perform a comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including skin inspection, pulses, and sensory testing, given the reported numbness and tingling. Counsel on daily foot inspection, appropriate footwear, and prompt evaluation of any blister, ulcer, color change, or nonhealing lesion. Recommend a dilated retinal examination, particularly given the 27-year duration of diabetes and current poor glycemic control.

Review dyslipidemia management and overall cardiovascular risk at follow-up; reinforce dietary modification, physical activity, and avoidance of tobacco. She is a nonsmoker.

Review depression symptoms, including mood, sleep, energy, anhedonia, and safety concerns, and coordinate treatment as clinically indicated. Continue routine preventive care, including age-appropriate cancer screening and recommended adult immunizations, with attention to influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, and herpes zoster vaccination recommendations.

Follow up soon for diabetes treatment initiation or adjustment and review of recommended laboratory and screening assessments, rather than waiting for the next annual visit.
