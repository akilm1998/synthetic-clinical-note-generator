Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Scarlett Hughes is a 32-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She is not currently described as having an active diabetes management regimen. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. She does not smoke.

Her medical history is also notable for chronic kidney disease of approximately 1 year’s duration. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Height 166 cm; weight 59.0 kg; BMI 21.4 kg/m².

Blood pressure 130/79 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Hemoglobin A1c 9.3%. Fasting glucose 213 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on HbA1c of 9.3% and fasting glucose of 213 mg/dL. Although she is clinically asymptomatic and has a normal BMI, the degree of hyperglycemia warrants prompt treatment planning and closer monitoring. There is no reported neuropathic symptomatology, visual complaint, recurrent infection, or impaired wound healing.

Chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 1 year. Renal function should be incorporated into diabetes treatment selection and dosing; no formal stage is assigned based on the information available.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the need for active diabetes management rather than observation alone. Review dietary carbohydrate intake, meal planning, and physical activity, with individualized counseling appropriate for her normal BMI and overall clinical status.

Obtain current renal assessment, including serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, to guide treatment selection and assess kidney involvement. A fasting lipid profile is also appropriate as part of comprehensive diabetes risk assessment.

Discuss initiation of glucose-lowering pharmacotherapy after review of renal function, with attention to kidney-safe dosing and contraindications. She should monitor for symptoms of marked hyperglycemia and seek prompt care for vomiting, inability to maintain hydration, confusion, or worsening urinary symptoms.

Recommend routine diabetic eye evaluation and comprehensive foot examination. Continue monitoring for neuropathy, visual changes, skin breakdown, and recurrent infections.

Return for diabetes follow-up after completion of the recommended laboratory assessment to review results and establish an ongoing treatment and monitoring plan.
