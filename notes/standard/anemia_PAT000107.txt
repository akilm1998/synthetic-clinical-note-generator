Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic anemia.

Subjective

Pamela Donovan is a 58-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of anemia present for approximately 18 years. She reports no current fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. She does not report pallor. Anemia has not been actively managed. She has a 10-year history of heart failure. She continues to smoke. No current medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Height 151 cm, weight 65.0 kg, BMI 28.5 kg/m². Blood pressure 125/76 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 23/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL, hematocrit 25.9%, RBC count 3.24 million/µL, MCV 80 fL. Serum iron 41 µg/dL, ferritin 486 ng/mL, TIBC 211 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 19.4%.

Assessment

Chronic anemia, currently asymptomatic by history, with hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL and hematocrit 25.9%. The borderline normocytic indices, low serum iron, elevated ferritin, and reduced TIBC are most consistent with anemia associated with chronic inflammation or chronic disease rather than uncomplicated absolute iron deficiency. Her longstanding heart failure may contribute to chronic inflammatory anemia; renal function and other potential contributors have not been characterized in the information available today. The anemia has been present for approximately 18 years and is currently not under active management.

Heart failure, longstanding for 10 years, without reported dyspnea, palpitations, dizziness, or reduced exercise tolerance today. Respiratory rate is mildly elevated at 23/min with preserved oxygen saturation.

Tobacco use.

Overweight, BMI 28.5 kg/m².

Plan

Further characterize the anemia with a repeat complete blood count, reticulocyte count, and peripheral blood smear. Recommend assessment of renal function, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers. Review the trend in hemoglobin and hematocrit and evaluate for chronic inflammatory, renal, nutritional, or occult blood-loss contributors as clinically indicated.

Given the low hemoglobin and prolonged lack of active management, discuss initiating anemia-directed management after clarification of the underlying cause. Iron replacement should not be assumed to be beneficial solely on the basis of the low serum iron, given the elevated ferritin and low TIBC; consider oral or intravenous iron only if follow-up assessment supports functional or absolute iron deficiency. Consider hematology consultation if anemia persists, worsens, or the initial evaluation is unrevealing. Any treatment decisions should be coordinated with management of her chronic heart failure.

Continue monitoring for exertional dyspnea, chest discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, syncope, worsening weakness, or reduced exercise tolerance. Seek urgent evaluation for new or severe cardiopulmonary symptoms. Counsel regarding smoking cessation and continued follow-up for heart failure. Reassess after completion of the recommended evaluation.
