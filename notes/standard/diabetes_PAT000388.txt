Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Ivy Henderson is a 35-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes diagnosed approximately 9 years ago. Diabetes control has been suboptimal, with most recent HbA1c 9.7% and fasting glucose 231 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling in the hands or feet.

Comorbid conditions include hypertension for approximately 2 years, dyslipidemia for 1 year, and obstructive sleep apnea for 3 years. She currently smokes. No current diabetes medication regimen is documented.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 136/83 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Height 180 cm, weight 78.4 kg, BMI 24.2 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 9.7%. Fasting glucose 231 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose. Despite marked hyperglycemia, she reports no osmotic, constitutional, visual, neuropathic, or infectious symptoms.

Hypertension, currently with mildly elevated office blood pressure.

Dyslipidemia.

Obstructive sleep apnea.

Current tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the elevated HbA1c and fasting glucose and the risks of ongoing hyperglycemia, including microvascular and cardiovascular complications.

Recommend prompt initiation or intensification of a comprehensive diabetes treatment plan, including structured nutrition counseling, regular physical activity, and appropriate pharmacologic therapy. Medication selection should be reviewed with the patient based on prior treatment history, preferences, renal function, cardiovascular risk, and tolerability.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with review of fasting and postprandial values at follow-up. Repeat HbA1c after an appropriate treatment interval to assess response.

Recommend screening for diabetes-related complications with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, serum creatinine/eGFR, fasting lipid profile, comprehensive foot examination, and dilated retinal examination. These are particularly important given the 9-year duration of diabetes and current poor glycemic control.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and avoidance of tobacco-related cardiovascular risk. Reinforced heart-healthy nutrition, weight maintenance, and regular physical activity.

Continue attention to blood pressure and lipid risk reduction in the setting of diabetes. Review management of hypertension, dyslipidemia, and obstructive sleep apnea at subsequent visits.

Follow up closely after treatment planning and glucose monitoring review; seek urgent care for severe weakness, vomiting, confusion, inability to maintain hydration, or markedly worsening hyperglycemic symptoms.
