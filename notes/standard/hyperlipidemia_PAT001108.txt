Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Hazel Fisher is a 41-year-old woman presenting for outpatient review of untreated hyperlipidemia. Her lipid disorder has been present for approximately 5 years and is not currently under active medication management. No lipid-lowering medication regimen is available for review today, so adherence and medication tolerance cannot be assessed against an established treatment plan.

She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, and dizziness. She reports xanthelasma. She does not smoke. Relevant cardiovascular risk factors include longstanding hypertension for approximately 10 years and overweight status with BMI 28.7 kg/m².

Objective

Height is 151 cm and weight is 65.4 kg, with BMI 28.7 kg/m². Blood pressure is 143/82 mmHg, pulse 71 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.8°C, and oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Available lipid results show total cholesterol 360 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 255 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 47 mg/dL, and triglycerides 292 mg/dL. Xanthelasma is present. No other examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol and moderately elevated triglycerides. The LDL level is substantially above usual treatment thresholds and, together with hypertension and xanthelasma, warrants prompt initiation of active risk-reduction therapy. She currently has no reported chest discomfort, claudication, or dizziness. Medication effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance cannot be meaningfully evaluated because no current lipid-lowering regimen is documented.

Hypertension by today’s elevated office blood pressure, with a reported 10-year duration.

Overweight, BMI 28.7 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the increased long-term cardiovascular risk associated with untreated severe LDL elevation. Recommend starting a high-intensity statin after shared decision-making, with selection based on contraindications, pregnancy considerations, and patient preference. If LDL reduction is inadequate or the medication is not tolerated, addition of ezetimibe or other therapy can be considered.

Reviewed expected benefits, potential muscle and liver-related adverse effects, and the importance of daily adherence. She should contact the office for significant muscle pain, weakness, dark urine, or other concerning symptoms. Encourage a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, lean proteins, and unsaturated fats while limiting saturated and trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol. Recommend gradual regular physical activity as tolerated and continued weight management.

Recommend evaluation for potentially reversible contributors to dyslipidemia and consideration of additional cardiovascular risk assessment, including ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement, as clinically appropriate. A fasting lipid panel should be repeated after initiation or adjustment of therapy to assess response and treatment tolerance. Continue blood pressure monitoring and address hypertension as part of overall cardiovascular risk reduction.

Follow up after initiation of lipid-lowering therapy to review adherence, tolerance, and repeat lipid results.
