Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Valeria Ellis is a 52-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 11 years and currently not under active treatment. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, or dizziness. She reports fatty-appearing deposits involving the skin and eyelids, consistent with xanthomas and xanthelasma.

She has known coronary artery disease of approximately 6 years’ duration and continues to smoke. She has obesity of approximately 5 years’ duration. No medication regimen is provided today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 126/79 mmHg, pulse 86 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Height 160 cm, weight 80.4 kg, BMI 31.4 kg/m².

Recent lipid results: total cholesterol 359 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 252 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 44 mg/dL, triglycerides 314 mg/dL.

No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms reported today. Xanthomas and xanthelasma are reported by the patient.

Assessment

Hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and elevated triglycerides, longstanding and currently untreated. The degree of LDL elevation, presence of xanthomas/xanthelasma, active tobacco use, obesity, and established coronary artery disease place her at very high cardiovascular risk.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding, without current chest discomfort or other reported ischemic symptoms.

Obesity, BMI 31.4 kg/m².

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the markedly elevated cholesterol levels and the need for prompt lipid-lowering therapy in the setting of known coronary artery disease. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy, such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin, after shared decision-making and review of contraindications and potential adverse effects. If LDL cholesterol remains substantially above goal despite adherence and tolerated statin therapy, consider addition of ezetimibe and further lipid-specialist or cardiology-directed therapy.

Recommend a fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation to assess response, along with evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia as clinically appropriate. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement for further cardiovascular risk assessment. Coronary artery calcium scoring is generally less useful when coronary artery disease is already established.

Counseled regarding a heart-healthy diet low in saturated and trans fats, increased soluble fiber, avoidance of excess refined carbohydrates, regular physical activity as tolerated, and gradual weight reduction. Strongly advised smoking cessation and offered assistance with a cessation plan.

Reviewed emergency precautions, including seeking immediate care for new or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, syncope, or other acute cardiovascular symptoms. Arrange close outpatient follow-up to review treatment decisions, adherence, tolerance, and repeat lipid results.
