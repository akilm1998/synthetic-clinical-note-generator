Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Matthew Reed is a 97-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding essential hypertension, present for approximately 36 years. Blood pressure control has not been actively managed recently. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or nosebleeds. No interval hypertensive symptoms are reported.

Relevant comorbidities include chronic kidney disease for approximately 44 years, hyperlipidemia for 64 years, and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 35 years. He continues to smoke. Weight is 82.4 kg at a height of 174 cm, with BMI 27.2 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 162/99 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 4.7 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding and currently above goal at today’s reading of 162/99 mmHg. He is asymptomatic without reported features of hypertensive emergency, but his advanced age and longstanding chronic kidney disease, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia increase his cardiovascular and renal risk. Current management appears insufficiently addressed and requires re-engagement with careful attention to tolerability and orthostatic symptoms.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding. Electrolytes are currently within the provided ranges, with potassium 4.7 mmol/L and sodium 142 mmol/L. Blood pressure control is important for renal and cardiovascular risk reduction.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

Tobacco use. Continued smoking increases cardiovascular risk in the setting of hypertension, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and hyperlipidemia.

Plan

Discussed the importance of addressing the elevated blood pressure and reducing cardiovascular and renal risk. Confirm blood pressure measurements with repeat readings, including home monitoring if feasible, and review for orthostatic symptoms given his age. Initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy should be considered through shared decision-making, with cautious titration and monitoring of renal function and electrolytes in the setting of chronic kidney disease.

Recommend review of a low-sodium eating pattern, avoidance of excess alcohol if applicable, gradual activity appropriate to his functional status, and weight maintenance. Smoking cessation was advised.

Recommend assessment of overall cardiovascular risk and end-organ effects, including an ECG, lipid profile, and urinalysis as clinically appropriate. Continue coordination of management for diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and hyperlipidemia. He was advised to seek urgent evaluation for severe headache, new visual changes, chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up promptly for blood pressure reassessment and treatment planning.
