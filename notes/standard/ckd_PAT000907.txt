Chief Complaint

Urgent follow-up for markedly abnormal kidney function.

Subjective

Valeria Ellis is a 92-year-old woman seen for focused follow-up of chronic kidney disease, reported for approximately 55 years. Recent testing shows creatinine 3.97 mg/dL, BUN 69 mg/dL, and eGFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m². She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, nausea, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. Her renal disease has not been actively managed. Relevant comorbidities include longstanding hypertension for 34 years and coronary artery disease for 27 years. She currently smokes.

Objective

Height 177 cm; weight 78.3 kg; BMI 25.0 kg/m².

Blood pressure 160/95 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Laboratory results: serum creatinine 3.97 mg/dL, eGFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m², BUN 69 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function and elevated creatinine and BUN. She has fatigue but no reported symptoms of fluid overload or overt uremic prodrome. Given her age, prolonged disease history, uncontrolled blood pressure, and coronary artery disease, this requires prompt reassessment and active management.

Hypertension, currently above goal at 160/95 mmHg.

Coronary artery disease.

Tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management and discussion of renal replacement planning as clinically appropriate.

Obtain prompt repeat renal assessment, including serum electrolytes, CBC, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and consideration of renal ultrasound, to further characterize current kidney function and complications.

Review blood pressure management urgently, with medication reconciliation and selection of kidney- and cardiovascular-appropriate therapy. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents; encourage avoidance of nonprescribed supplements.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and the importance of blood pressure control.

Seek emergency care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, marked weakness, inability to urinate, or new swelling.

Follow up promptly after nephrology assessment and repeat testing.
