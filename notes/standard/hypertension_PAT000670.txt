Chief Complaint

Annual review and assessment of blood pressure.

Subjective

Gavin Holland is an 83-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review and chronic disease assessment. He has a 52-year history of essential hypertension and is not currently receiving treatment. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. No symptoms suggestive of an acute hypertensive emergency are reported.

He has a 12-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. Smoking status is documented as current smoker. No medication or allergy history is provided for review today.

Objective

Height 189 cm. Weight 106.8 kg. BMI 29.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 153/95 mmHg. Pulse 76 bpm. Respiratory rate 19/min. Temperature 37.0°C. Oxygen saturation 97%.

Serum electrolytes: sodium 137 mmol/L, potassium 3.7 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and currently without active management. He is asymptomatic, with no reported features of hypertensive crisis. His age, long duration of hypertension, current smoking, overweight status, and coexisting type 2 diabetes increase overall cardiovascular and renal risk.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, present for 12 years; chronic disease status requires ongoing review alongside blood pressure management.

Current tobacco use.

Overweight, with BMI 29.9 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given the current reading, longstanding disease, age, and diabetes. Repeat blood pressure measurement and home blood pressure monitoring are recommended to confirm the usual pattern and guide treatment. Review promptly if readings are persistently elevated or if chest pain, shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or visual changes develop.

Recommend initiation of an individualized antihypertensive treatment plan after review of medication history, renal function, orthostatic symptoms, and overall goals of care. Serum electrolytes should be monitored when treatment is started or adjusted; current sodium and potassium are within the reported reference ranges.

Recommend cardiovascular risk assessment including ECG, lipid profile, urinalysis, and renal function testing as clinically appropriate. Continue coordinated follow-up of diabetes, including review of glycemic control and diabetic kidney and eye health.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation and offered support with cessation planning. Encourage a lower-sodium, heart-healthy diet, regular physical activity appropriate for age and functional status, moderation of alcohol if applicable, and gradual weight reduction. Review immunizations and age-appropriate preventive screening at the annual health maintenance visit, with attention to individualized benefit given his age and comorbidities.

Arrange outpatient follow-up after home blood pressure readings and medication review, sooner for worsening symptoms or markedly elevated readings.
