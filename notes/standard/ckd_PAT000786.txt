Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Aria Patterson is an 85-year-old woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease, present for approximately 9 years. She reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination, or shortness of breath. No interval uremic or volume-overload symptoms are reported.

She is a nonsmoker. Current weight is 77.5 kg at 161 cm, with BMI 29.9 kg/m². Relevant comorbidities include hyperlipidemia for 47 years, anemia of chronic disease for 33 years, and coronary artery disease for 13 years. Chronic kidney disease is not currently under active disease-directed management.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 147/90 mmHg, pulse 83 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory data: Serum creatinine 4.28 mg/dL, BUN 61 mg/dL, estimated GFR 13 mL/min/1.73 m².

No additional examination findings are documented today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with substantially reduced renal function, longstanding for approximately 9 years. Despite the markedly abnormal creatinine, BUN, and estimated GFR, she currently denies symptoms suggestive of uremia or fluid overload. Blood pressure is above goal at today’s visit. The absence of symptoms does not lessen the need for timely nephrology-directed evaluation and planning.

Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding.

Coronary artery disease.

Hyperlipidemia.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Recommend prompt re-establishment of active nephrology care for assessment of advanced chronic kidney disease, review of renal trajectory, and discussion of conservative management versus future kidney replacement therapy planning based on symptoms, laboratory trends, overall health, and patient preferences.

Recommend monitoring serum electrolytes, renal function, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone as part of CKD evaluation. Renal ultrasound may be considered if not previously characterized or if clinically indicated.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications with renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic agents, particularly NSAIDs. No medication regimen is documented today; treatment decisions should account for renal function, blood pressure, coronary artery disease, and overall goals of care.

Continue monitoring blood pressure and obtain home readings if feasible. Counsel regarding sodium moderation, avoidance of dehydration, and prompt evaluation for new edema, dyspnea, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, worsening fatigue, or significant change in urine output.

Follow up after renal evaluation and laboratory reassessment, or sooner for any new symptoms.
