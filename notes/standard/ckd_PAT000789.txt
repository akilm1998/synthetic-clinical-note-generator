Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Eliana Matthews is a 52-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 10-year history of chronic kidney disease, currently without reported fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. She reports ongoing tobacco use. No medication regimen is provided for review.

Relevant comorbidities include gout for 8 years, secondary hyperparathyroidism for 5 years, and coronary artery disease for 6 years. There is no reported acute change in symptoms today.

Objective

Height 152 cm; weight 62.6 kg; BMI 27.1 kg/m².

Blood pressure 134/88 mmHg, pulse 82/min, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Laboratory data: serum creatinine 3.35 mg/dL, estimated glomerular filtration rate 12 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 86 mg/dL.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease of 10 years’ duration with markedly reduced renal function and significant azotemia. Although she currently denies symptoms suggestive of uremic prodrome or fluid overload, the available renal indices indicate advanced disease requiring prompt reassessment and specialist-directed management. No formal disease stage is assigned here.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism associated with chronic kidney disease, documented for 5 years.

Gout, documented for 8 years.

Coronary artery disease, documented for 6 years, representing elevated cardiovascular risk in the setting of chronic kidney disease and ongoing tobacco use.

Tobacco use.

BMI 27.1 kg/m², consistent with overweight status.

Plan

Recommend prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation and management of markedly reduced eGFR, including discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if clinically appropriate. She should seek urgent medical attention for new nausea or vomiting, progressive weakness, confusion, chest pain, worsening shortness of breath, edema, or a substantial decrease in urine output.

Recommend repeat renal assessment with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium and phosphorus, complete blood count, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone level. Renal ultrasound may be considered as part of structural evaluation if not previously performed. These investigations should be coordinated promptly with nephrology or primary care.

Review all prescription and over-the-counter medications with renal dosing in mind. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents unless specifically directed by a clinician. Management of secondary hyperparathyroidism and gout should be coordinated with renal function and specialist recommendations.

Continue careful blood pressure and cardiovascular risk monitoring. Reinforce tobacco cessation; offer counseling and pharmacologic cessation support after medication reconciliation and assessment of contraindications. Encourage a kidney-appropriate dietary pattern with individualized sodium, protein, potassium, and phosphorus guidance from nephrology or a renal dietitian rather than empiric restriction.

Address weight management through gradual nutrition and activity measures appropriate for her cardiovascular and renal status.

Ensure age-appropriate preventive care, including influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, and other indicated immunizations, along with routine cancer screening and assessment of bone health. Follow up promptly after renal evaluation and laboratory reassessment, with routine annual review thereafter or sooner for any clinical change.
