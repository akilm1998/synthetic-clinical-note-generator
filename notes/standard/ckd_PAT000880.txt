Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, present for approximately 36 years. Current creatinine is 3.54 mg/dL, eGFR 14 mL/min/1.73 m², and BUN 85 mg/dL. Although she is presently without reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms, the degree of renal impairment is clinically significant and warrants prompt comprehensive evaluation and specialist involvement. No formal disease stage is assigned here.

Blood pressure is mildly elevated at 147/86 mmHg. The treatment approach should account for advanced age, renal function, orthostatic risk, and overall goals of care.

Anemia of chronic disease, present for approximately 16 years. No current hemoglobin or iron studies are available in the provided data.

Hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 26 years.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology consultation for evaluation and management of advanced chronic kidney disease, including discussion of prognosis, treatment goals, and whether renal replacement planning is appropriate based on functional status and patient preferences.

Obtain or review a complete medication reconciliation before initiating or adjusting therapy. Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents when possible, and review all prescriptions and over-the-counter medications for renal dosing.

Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphorus, and BUN/creatinine. Recommend urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess proteinuria, complete blood count for anemia assessment, and intact parathyroid hormone to evaluate for CKD-related mineral and bone disorder. Renal ultrasound may be considered to assess kidney size and exclude structural obstruction if not previously performed.

Monitor blood pressure regularly. Dietary counseling should emphasize avoidance of excess sodium and individualized renal dietary recommendations, with attention to nutritional status and avoidance of unnecessary dietary restriction in this 91-year-old patient.

Assess anemia with CBC and iron studies before considering treatment. Management of anemia-specific therapy should be guided by nephrology and current laboratory findings.

Review lipid management and cardiovascular risk reduction in the context of advanced age, comorbidities, renal function, and patient goals.

Educate the patient to seek urgent care for new or worsening shortness of breath, progressive swelling, markedly reduced urination, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness.

Follow up promptly after nephrology evaluation and repeat laboratory testing.
