Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for anemia.

Subjective

Jane Watson is a 33-year-old woman presenting for follow-up of anemia identified approximately 1 year ago. She reports headache and pallor. She denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. No interval change in these symptoms is reported.

Relevant medical history includes chronic kidney disease for approximately 1 year and inflammatory bowel disease for 13 years. She currently smokes. She is not documented as receiving active treatment for the anemia.

Objective

Height 170 cm, weight 65.6 kg, BMI 22.7 kg/m².

Blood pressure 126/81 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Patient appears clinically stable. Pallor is present. Cardiovascular examination is normal with regular pulse and no tachycardia. Respiratory examination is normal.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.3 g/dL
Hematocrit 25.3%
RBC count 2.98 million/µL
MCV 85 fL
Serum iron 20 µg/dL
Ferritin 451 ng/mL
TIBC 206 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 9.7%

Assessment

Anemia consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation in the setting of chronic kidney disease and longstanding inflammatory bowel disease. This is a significant normocytic anemia with low serum iron, low TIBC, elevated ferritin, and low transferrin saturation, supporting impaired iron availability associated with chronic inflammation rather than isolated absolute iron deficiency. The anemia is currently not under active management. She is hemodynamically stable, without cardiopulmonary symptoms, but has headache and pallor.

Plan

Review and address the underlying inflammatory bowel disease and chronic kidney disease as contributors to the anemia. Recommend updated evaluation with CBC, reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, renal function testing, inflammatory markers, vitamin B12, and folate levels. Consider repeat iron studies to monitor iron availability and assess whether iron supplementation is appropriate; elevated ferritin should be interpreted in the context of chronic inflammation.

Discuss treatment options based on renal function, inflammatory disease activity, reticulocyte response, and repeat laboratory assessment, including consideration of oral or intravenous iron and, if clinically indicated, an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent in coordination with the clinician managing her kidney disease.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, particularly given its potential adverse effects on overall vascular and inflammatory health.

Advise prompt evaluation for worsening headache, new dizziness, shortness of breath, chest discomfort, palpitations, syncope, bleeding, or marked decline in exercise tolerance. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after repeat assessment to review hemoglobin response and refine management.
