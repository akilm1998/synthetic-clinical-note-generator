Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Isaac Henderson is a 48-year-old man presenting for outpatient medication review. He has a 10-year history of chronic kidney disease, with associated type 2 diabetes mellitus for 5 years, coronary artery disease for 2 years, and secondary hyperparathyroidism for 2 years. No current medication regimen is reported for review; therefore, adherence and medication tolerance cannot be confirmed today.

He denies fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, lower-extremity swelling, change in urination frequency, sleep disturbance, or shortness of breath. He continues to smoke. No medication-related adverse effects are reported.

Objective

Height 181 cm; weight 77.3 kg; BMI 23.6 kg/m².

Blood pressure 134/84 mmHg, pulse 81 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Recent laboratory results:
Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL.
Estimated GFR 7 mL/min/1.73 m².
BUN 60 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings documented.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, longstanding for approximately 10 years. Current creatinine, eGFR, and BUN are significantly abnormal despite the absence of reported uremic or fluid-overload symptoms. Kidney disease is not currently documented as being under active treatment or medication management.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 5-year history, a contributing renal risk factor.

Secondary hyperparathyroidism associated with chronic kidney disease.

Coronary artery disease.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Arrange urgent nephrology evaluation for comprehensive management of advanced chronic kidney disease and assessment of renal replacement therapy planning, even though he is currently asymptomatic.

Obtain updated renal and metabolic assessment as clinically indicated, including serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone. Consider renal ultrasound if not previously completed.

Complete a careful medication reconciliation with the patient and pharmacy records. Confirm all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use, with particular attention to renal dosing and avoidance of NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents. Do not start, stop, or adjust medications without review of renal function and current treatment goals.

Continue close monitoring of blood pressure and diabetes management with renal-appropriate treatment selection. Review cardiovascular risk reduction in the setting of coronary artery disease.

Provide counseling regarding smoking cessation.

Discuss urgent evaluation for new nausea or vomiting, worsening fatigue, confusion, severe itching, muscle cramps or weakness, reduced urine output, swelling, chest pain, or shortness of breath. Follow up promptly after nephrology assessment and medication reconciliation.
