Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Alan Wheeler is a 94-year-old man, height 164 cm, weight 60.2 kg, BMI 22.4 kg/m², presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of chronic kidney disease diagnosed approximately 6 years ago. He reports no current fatigue, pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urinary frequency, or shortness of breath. No uremic or fluid-overload symptoms are reported today.

His chronic medical history is also notable for anemia of chronic disease of approximately 35 years’ duration and hyperlipidemia of approximately 27 years’ duration. He continues to smoke. Current medication use was not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 144/88 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

Laboratory data:
Serum creatinine 6.0 mg/dL
BUN 74 mg/dL
Estimated GFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m²

Review of systems is negative for fatigue, itching, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, edema, urinary frequency change, or dyspnea.

Assessment

1. Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function. Creatinine is 6.0 mg/dL, BUN 74 mg/dL, and estimated GFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m². Although he is currently without reported uremic or volume-overload symptoms, these values represent severe renal impairment requiring prompt reassessment and active management. His condition has not been actively managed, and there is no medication or renal replacement therapy information available in the current record.

2. Anemia of chronic disease, longstanding. No current hemoglobin or hematocrit values are available for assessment today.

3. Hyperlipidemia, longstanding.

4. Elevated blood pressure reading. BP today is 144/88 mmHg. The degree of chronic blood pressure control cannot be determined from this visit alone.

5. Tobacco use. Continued smoking increases cardiovascular and renal risk.

Plan

Chronic kidney disease: Arrange prompt nephrology evaluation given creatinine 6.0 mg/dL and estimated GFR 5 mL/min/1.73 m², even in the absence of current symptoms. Recommend repeat renal function testing with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and intact parathyroid hormone to assess complications of advanced kidney disease. Renal ultrasound is also appropriate if not previously completed. Review renal replacement therapy options and goals of care with the patient and family in conjunction with nephrology, taking age, functional status, preferences, and overall goals into account. Avoid nephrotoxic medications and over-the-counter NSAIDs; medication dosing should be reviewed for renal adjustment. Educated regarding urgent evaluation for new shortness of breath, chest pain, progressive swelling, persistent nausea or vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, or substantially decreased urine output.

Anemia of chronic disease: Reassess CBC as part of renal follow-up and coordinate management with nephrology. No erythropoietin or other anemia therapy is documented and none is initiated based on the information available today.

Hyperlipidemia: Continue cardiovascular risk assessment and review lipid-lowering therapy at medication reconciliation. Reinforce a kidney-conscious, heart-healthy diet as tolerated.

Blood pressure: Recheck blood pressure and review home readings if available. Management should be individualized in the context of advanced kidney disease and advanced age; avoid assuming current antihypertensive therapy in the absence of a medication list.

Tobacco use: Counseled regarding smoking cessation and the associated cardiovascular and renal risks.

Follow-up

Close outpatient follow-up after renal evaluation, with earlier assessment for any new symptoms suggestive of uremia or fluid overload.
