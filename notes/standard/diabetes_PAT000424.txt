Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Shannon Douglas is a 53-year-old female presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 22 years ago. Diabetes control is currently suboptimal, with recent hemoglobin A1c of 9.2% and fasting glucose of 219 mg/dL. She reports increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, and frequent infections are absent. She does report numbness in the hands or feet without associated tingling.

She has chronic kidney disease of approximately 5 years’ duration and obstructive sleep apnea of approximately 2 years’ duration. She does not smoke. Current medication regimen was not provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 131/79 mmHg, pulse 70 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 158 cm, weight 65.4 kg, BMI 26.2 kg/m².

Recent laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.2%. Fasting glucose 219 mg/dL.

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus with inadequate glycemic control based on hemoglobin A1c 9.2% and fasting glucose 219 mg/dL. She is not reporting symptomatic hyperglycemia, but the degree of elevation warrants active treatment and closer monitoring. Numbness in the hands or feet may represent diabetes-related peripheral sensory symptoms; further foot and neurologic assessment is appropriate.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, requiring consideration in diabetes treatment selection and dosing.

Obstructive sleep apnea, longstanding.

Overweight, BMI 26.2 kg/m².

Plan

Reviewed the elevated A1c and fasting glucose with the patient and discussed the importance of improving glycemic control to reduce microvascular and cardiovascular risk. Medication reconciliation and initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy should be addressed, with renal function taken into account given her chronic kidney disease. Avoid assuming an existing regimen; treatment options may include metformin or other appropriate agents if not contraindicated, with consideration of agents that provide renal and cardiovascular benefit when clinically appropriate.

Recommend home glucose monitoring with documentation of fasting and occasional postprandial readings, along with education regarding symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia. Reinforced a balanced, carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, portion control, regular physical activity as tolerated, and gradual weight management.

Recommend obtaining renal function, estimated glomerular filtration rate, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio if not recently assessed to guide medication safety and kidney risk management. Continue blood pressure and kidney-protective care.

Perform a comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including assessment of protective sensation and skin integrity, at the next available visit and counsel on daily foot inspection and protective footwear. Recommend routine dilated retinal examination given longstanding diabetes. Persistent or progressive numbness should prompt further evaluation.

Continue management of obstructive sleep apnea and reinforce adherence to prescribed sleep-related therapy if applicable.

Follow up after medication review and glucose monitoring, with repeat hemoglobin A1c at an appropriate interval to assess response to treatment.
