Chief Complaint

Initial evaluation of anemia.

Subjective

Marcus Cross is a 39-year-old man, 163 cm tall and weighing 74.9 kg, with BMI 28.2 kg/m², presenting for initial outpatient consultation regarding anemia identified over approximately 2 years. He denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, headache, pallor, reduced exercise tolerance, dyspnea, palpitations, or other cardiopulmonary symptoms.

He has type 2 diabetes mellitus of approximately 2 years’ duration. He currently smokes. No medication use, allergies, bleeding history, dietary history, family history, or prior hematologic evaluation is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 117/78 mmHg, pulse 87 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.8°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Cardiovascular examination is normal. Respiratory examination is normal. No pallor is noted.

Laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL
Hematocrit 26.3%
RBC count 2.89 million/µL
MCV 91 fL
Serum iron 48 µg/dL
Ferritin 427 ng/mL
TIBC 207 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 23.2%

Assessment

Anemia characterized by significant normocytic, normochromic reduction in hemoglobin and hematocrit, with low RBC count. The iron profile demonstrates low serum iron and reduced TIBC with elevated ferritin, a pattern more consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation or chronic disease than isolated absolute iron deficiency. He is clinically stable and currently asymptomatic, without reported exertional intolerance, dyspnea, chest symptoms, dizziness, or palpitations.

The etiology of the presumed anemia of chronic disease/inflammation remains to be established. Type 2 diabetes is present but does not by itself explain the degree of anemia. Renal function, inflammatory burden, nutritional status, reticulocyte response, and peripheral smear have not yet been characterized. There is no current evidence of hemodynamic instability based on the provided vital signs.

Plan

Obtain a repeat complete blood count with red cell indices and reticulocyte count to confirm persistence and assess marrow response.

Recommend peripheral blood smear, serum vitamin B12, serum folate, and renal function testing as part of the evaluation of normocytic anemia.

Recommend inflammatory markers to assess for an active inflammatory process. Review for chronic inflammatory disease, chronic infection, autoimmune disease, renal disease, and other systemic contributors based on history and examination.

Although the iron pattern is not typical of isolated iron deficiency, reassess iron studies in clinical context and avoid empiric iron supplementation unless additional evaluation supports iron deficiency or there is another clear indication.

If the anemia persists, worsens, or the initial evaluation is unrevealing, recommend hematology follow-up for further assessment and consideration of additional marrow-directed evaluation.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation, which is relevant to overall cardiopulmonary and vascular health.

Seek urgent medical attention for new shortness of breath, chest pain, syncope, persistent tachycardia, marked weakness, bleeding, or rapidly worsening exercise tolerance.
