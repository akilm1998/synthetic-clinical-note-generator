Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Vanessa Warren is a 44-year-old woman presenting for outpatient medication review and follow-up of chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She reports fatigue. She denies pruritus, muscle cramps, nausea, sleep disturbance, peripheral edema, change in urination frequency, or shortness of breath. She does not smoke.

No active CKD-directed medication regimen is listed for review today; therefore, medication effectiveness, adherence, and treatment tolerance cannot be assessed for a specific renal medication. Hyperlipidemia has been present for approximately 12 years.

Objective

Height 162 cm; weight 55.1 kg; BMI 21.0 kg/m².

Blood pressure 135/84 mmHg, pulse 72/min, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 98% on room air.

Available laboratory results: serum creatinine 3.96 mg/dL, BUN 75 mg/dL, estimated GFR 11 mL/min/1.73 m².

No peripheral edema, respiratory distress, or other abnormal physical findings are documented in the information reviewed today.

Assessment

Chronic kidney disease with markedly reduced renal function, associated with fatigue and elevated creatinine and BUN. She currently denies nausea, pruritus, muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, edema, urinary changes, or dyspnea. Kidney disease appears insufficiently managed in the outpatient setting, and the degree of renal impairment warrants prompt specialist assessment and medication planning.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding, with no active lipid-lowering medication documented for review.

Medication review: no specific active regimen available to assess for adherence, efficacy, or adverse effects. Renal dosing and avoidance of nephrotoxic medications are particularly important.

Plan

Arrange prompt nephrology referral for comprehensive evaluation and management of advanced chronic kidney disease, including discussion of renal replacement therapy planning if clinically indicated.

Obtain repeat renal function and serum electrolytes, CBC, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, intact parathyroid hormone, and renal ultrasound as part of CKD evaluation. Monitor renal function and blood pressure closely.

Perform full medication reconciliation at the next contact and review all prescription, over-the-counter, and supplement use. Avoid NSAIDs and other potentially nephrotoxic agents; ensure all medications are renally dosed. Do not initiate or adjust renal-directed therapy without review of current medications and electrolyte status.

Continue blood pressure monitoring and a kidney-conscious diet with avoidance of excess sodium. Review management of hyperlipidemia and confirm whether lipid-lowering therapy is indicated after medication reconciliation.

Seek urgent medical care for new or worsening shortness of breath, swelling, markedly reduced urine output, persistent vomiting, confusion, chest pain, or severe weakness. Follow up promptly after laboratory testing and nephrology evaluation.
