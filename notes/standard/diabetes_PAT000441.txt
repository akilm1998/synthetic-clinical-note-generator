Chief Complaint

Medication review and follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Austin Dean is a 74-year-old man seen for outpatient review of diabetes treatment effectiveness, adherence, and tolerance. He has had type 2 diabetes for approximately 36 years and is not currently actively managing the condition. No established diabetes medication regimen is reported for review, and there is therefore no current medication adherence or treatment-tolerance response to assess.

He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. He continues to smoke. Comorbidities include longstanding dyslipidemia of approximately 29 years, depression for approximately 46 years, and anxiety for approximately 20 years.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 125/76 mmHg, pulse 77 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 168 cm, weight 59.3 kg, BMI 21.0 kg/m².

Laboratory results: HbA1c 10.0%. Fasting glucose 234 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently markedly above glycemic goal based on HbA1c 10.0% and fasting glucose 234 mg/dL. Despite the degree of hyperglycemia, he reports no osmotic, constitutional, visual, infectious, or neuropathic symptoms. He is lean rather than overweight, and the long duration of diabetes increases the importance of assessing renal, retinal, and peripheral neurologic complications even in the absence of symptoms.

Dyslipidemia, longstanding.

Depression and anxiety, longstanding.

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose control is inadequate and that lifestyle measures alone are unlikely to be sufficient at this level of hyperglycemia. Reviewed the importance of initiating a structured diabetes treatment plan with medication selection individualized to his age, overall health, renal function, nutritional status, and tolerance. Potential benefits and adverse effects of standard glucose-lowering therapy were reviewed in general terms; no specific medication regimen is documented as started today.

Emphasized consistent medication use once therapy is prescribed, avoidance of missed doses, nutrition appropriate for diabetes, adequate hydration, and regular home glucose monitoring if recommended as part of the treatment plan. Reviewed symptoms requiring prompt medical attention, including worsening thirst or urination, vomiting, confusion, marked weakness, or inability to maintain oral intake.

Recommend assessment of renal function, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and a comprehensive diabetic foot examination. Recommend routine dilated eye evaluation given the prolonged duration of diabetes. Continue discussion of cardiovascular risk reduction, including smoking cessation and management of dyslipidemia.

Arrange close outpatient follow-up after treatment initiation and repeat HbA1c assessment at an appropriate interval to evaluate effectiveness and tolerance.
