Ashton Murray is a 62-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of hypertension, present for approximately 10 years and currently without active management. He does not smoke. His medical history is also notable for chronic kidney disease of approximately 21 years’ duration and hyperlipidemia of approximately 27 years’ duration. No current antihypertensive regimen is provided.

Assessment

Persistent essential hypertension with markedly elevated office blood pressure in a patient whose hypertension has been untreated for approximately 10 years. The presence of chronic kidney disease substantially increases cardiovascular and renal risk and makes confirmation of renal function and careful selection of therapy important. Current sodium and potassium are within the provided reference context, although the potassium of 4.8 mmol/L warrants consideration when selecting and monitoring renin-angiotensin system therapy.

Intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue may occur in the setting of uncontrolled blood pressure but are nonspecific. He currently denies symptoms suggestive of hypertensive emergency, including chest pain, dyspnea, severe headache, visual disturbance, or epistaxis.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding, with additional cardiovascular risk related to age, overweight status, hypertension, and chronic kidney disease.

Plan

Repeat blood pressure measurement after appropriate seated rest and arrange home blood pressure monitoring with a written log to assess persistence and degree of elevation. Review technique, including a properly sized cuff and measurements taken at consistent times.

Given the degree and duration of blood pressure elevation and the history of chronic kidney disease, initiate a structured treatment discussion promptly rather than continuing observation alone. Medication selection should be guided by current renal function, urine protein assessment, potassium level, comorbidities, and tolerance. Any antihypertensive therapy affecting renal perfusion or potassium should be followed with repeat renal function and electrolyte testing after initiation or dose adjustment.

Obtain clinically appropriate baseline evaluation, including serum creatinine with estimated renal function, urine albumin or protein assessment, urinalysis, fasting lipid profile, and electrocardiogram. Review prior renal and lipid records if available. Assess for target-organ involvement and cardiovascular risk during follow-up.

Counseled regarding reduction of dietary sodium, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight control, moderation of alcohol if applicable, and adherence to a consistent blood pressure monitoring and treatment plan.

Discussed urgent evaluation for chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic symptoms, severe headache, acute visual change, confusion, or substantially worsening blood pressure readings.

Follow-up should be arranged promptly for blood pressure reassessment, review of home readings and recommended testing, and establishment of an ongoing hypertension management regimen.
