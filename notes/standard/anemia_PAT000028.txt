Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Andrew Morris is a 72-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of longstanding anemia, reported duration approximately 37 years. He denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or noticeable pallor. No interval anemia-related concerns are reported.

Relevant comorbidities include heart failure for 9 years and type 2 diabetes mellitus for 10 years. He continues to smoke. Current anemia management has not been established. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 131/75 mmHg, pulse 88 bpm, respiratory rate 22/min, temperature 37.0°C, oxygen saturation 97%.

Height 166 cm, weight 59.0 kg, BMI 21.4 kg/m².

Recent laboratory results show hemoglobin 8.6 g/dL, hematocrit 25.3%, RBC count 2.69 million/µL, and MCV 94 fL. Serum iron is 42 µg/dL, ferritin 483 ng/mL, TIBC 225 µg/dL, and transferrin saturation 18.7%.

Assessment

Anemia consistent with chronic disease/chronic inflammation. The anemia is substantial but currently without reported fatigue, weakness, dizziness, cardiopulmonary symptoms, or reduced exercise tolerance. Red cell indices are normocytic. The low serum iron and transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin and reduced TIBC support impaired iron availability in the setting of chronic disease rather than straightforward absolute iron deficiency. His longstanding heart failure and diabetes may contribute to the chronic disease burden. Further evaluation is appropriate because of the degree and prolonged duration of anemia.

Heart failure, longstanding. No acute cardiopulmonary symptoms reported today; oxygen saturation is 97% and pulse is 88 bpm.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, longstanding. No diabetes-related symptoms were discussed during this visit.

Tobacco use. Continued smoking may adversely affect cardiovascular health and overall chronic disease control.

Plan

Obtain updated CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear to confirm the current pattern and assess marrow response. Check renal function, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers as clinically appropriate. Continue evaluation for contributing chronic inflammatory, renal, nutritional, or occult blood-loss causes based on the updated results and clinical history.

Review the anemia results with the patient and discuss that the current pattern is compatible with anemia associated with chronic disease, but the hemoglobin level warrants follow-up rather than observation alone. Treatment should be directed at the underlying contributing condition after evaluation. Iron replacement, intravenous iron, or erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy should not be started empirically without reviewing the complete workup and renal/inflammatory status.

Advised to seek urgent care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, palpitations, fainting, significant dizziness, unusual bleeding, or marked weakness.

Continue routine management of heart failure and type 2 diabetes with attention to renal function and overall disease control. Smoking cessation was encouraged given his heart failure and diabetes. Arrange outpatient follow-up after repeat testing to reassess hemoglobin and determine next management steps.
