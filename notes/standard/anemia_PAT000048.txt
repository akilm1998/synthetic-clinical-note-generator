Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of chronic anemia.

Subjective

Ryan Hughes is a 63-year-old man presenting for comprehensive annual review. He has a 17-year history of anemia in the setting of longstanding inflammatory bowel disease for approximately 25 years and malignancy for approximately 17 years. He also has type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 7 years ago. The anemia has not been actively managed.

He reports headache but denies fatigue, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or awareness of pallor. He continues to smoke. No medication list, allergies, or treatment history were provided for review.

Objective

Height 171 cm; weight 78.7 kg; BMI 26.9 kg/m².

Blood pressure 127/82 mmHg
Pulse 89 bpm
Respiratory rate 17/min
Temperature 36.8°C
Oxygen saturation 95%

Recent laboratory results:
Hemoglobin 8.0 g/dL
Hematocrit 23.7%
RBC 2.69 million/µL
MCV 88 fL
Serum iron 27 µg/dL
Ferritin 279 ng/mL
TIBC 209 µg/dL
Transferrin saturation 12.9%

Assessment

1. Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic disease/inflammation. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are significantly reduced, with low serum iron and transferrin saturation, elevated or preserved ferritin, and low TIBC, supporting impaired iron availability in the setting of chronic inflammation. Longstanding inflammatory bowel disease and malignancy are relevant contributing conditions. The headache may be related to the anemia, although he currently reports no cardiopulmonary or functional symptoms.

2. Inflammatory bowel disease, longstanding, approximately 25 years. Chronic inflammatory activity may be contributing to the anemia. Current disease activity and gastrointestinal blood loss status require review.

3. Malignancy, longstanding, approximately 17 years. Ongoing disease status and treatment history are important in determining the cause and management of the anemia.

4. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, approximately 7 years. Chronic disease status should be reviewed as part of routine annual care.

5. Tobacco use. Continued smoking increases overall cardiovascular, pulmonary, and malignancy-related risk.

6. Overweight by BMI, 26.9 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed that the anemia is clinically significant despite the absence of fatigue, dyspnea, palpitations, or reduced exercise tolerance. Recommend prompt reassessment with repeat CBC, reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, renal function testing, vitamin B12, folate, and inflammatory markers. Repeat iron studies may help assess iron availability and response to treatment.

Review for ongoing gastrointestinal blood loss, nutritional deficiency, inflammatory bowel disease activity, and current malignancy status is recommended. Coordinate management with the clinicians involved in his inflammatory bowel disease and malignancy care as appropriate. Iron replacement, including consideration of intravenous iron, and erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy should be determined after reassessment of the underlying disease, iron availability, renal function, and overall treatment goals; no treatment regimen is assumed today.

Advised urgent medical evaluation for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, sustained palpitations, syncope, marked dizziness, progressive weakness, or evidence of gastrointestinal bleeding.

Continue routine diabetes monitoring and preventive care, including review of glycemic status, renal health, cardiovascular risk, immunizations, age-appropriate cancer surveillance, and colorectal surveillance in the context of longstanding inflammatory bowel disease. Smoking cessation counseling provided; offer cessation support at follow-up.

Follow-up should be arranged promptly to review anemia evaluation and establish an active management plan, with routine annual review of chronic conditions thereafter.
