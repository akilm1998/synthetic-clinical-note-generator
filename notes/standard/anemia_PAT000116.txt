Chief Complaint

Initial evaluation of anemia.

Subjective

Simone Bishop is a 36-year-old woman presenting as a new patient for evaluation of anemia present for approximately 10 years. She reports headache but denies fatigue, generalized weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or noticeable pallor. She has a chronic infection documented for approximately 1 year. Anemia has not previously been actively managed. She does not smoke.

Objective

Height 153 cm, weight 68.4 kg, BMI 29.2 kg/m². Blood pressure 117/79 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Laboratory results: hemoglobin 8.3 g/dL, hematocrit 24.1%, RBC count 2.68 million/µL, MCV 90 fL, serum iron 44 µg/dL, ferritin 376 ng/mL, TIBC 230 µg/dL, transferrin saturation 19.1%.

Cardiovascular and respiratory findings are clinically stable based on normal pulse, blood pressure, and oxygen saturation. No pallor is reported.

Assessment

Chronic normocytic anemia, most consistent with anemia of chronic inflammation in the setting of chronic infection. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are significantly reduced, with low serum iron and TIBC and elevated ferritin, supporting impaired iron availability related to inflammation rather than uncomplicated iron depletion. Transferrin saturation is borderline low. The reported headache may be associated with anemia, although she currently denies cardiopulmonary or exertional symptoms. No evidence of hemodynamic instability is present today.

Plan

Obtain repeat CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral blood smear to confirm the degree and pattern of anemia and assess marrow response. Check vitamin B12, folate, renal function, and inflammatory markers as part of the evaluation for other contributors to chronic normocytic anemia.

Review and address the underlying chronic infection with appropriate clinical evaluation and treatment planning, as ongoing inflammation may be contributing to the anemia. Further assessment should clarify the duration, activity, and source of the infection.

Given the long duration of anemia and hemoglobin of 8.3 g/dL without active management, arrange close outpatient follow-up after the above evaluation. Consider hematology consultation if the anemia remains significant, worsens, or if the initial evaluation does not identify a reversible cause. Iron replacement should be guided by the completed evaluation; the elevated ferritin and low TIBC do not support empiric iron treatment as the sole intervention.

Seek urgent care for new or worsening shortness of breath, chest discomfort, syncope, persistent palpitations, marked weakness, bleeding, or rapidly worsening headache.
