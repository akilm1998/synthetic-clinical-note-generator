Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension and cardiovascular risk management.

Subjective

Charlotte Bailey is an 82-year-old woman presenting for outpatient follow-up of longstanding hypertension, present for approximately 29 years and currently without active treatment management. She reports intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue. She denies headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis.

Her cardiovascular history is notable for coronary artery disease of approximately 25 years’ duration and hyperlipidemia of approximately 40 years’ duration. She currently smokes. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 148/98 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Height 177 cm, weight 89.9 kg, BMI 28.7 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: Sodium 147 mmol/L, potassium 3.6 mmol/L.

Assessment

Essential primary hypertension, longstanding and suboptimally controlled based on today’s blood pressure of 148/98 mmHg. The patient is symptomatic with intermittent dizziness, palpitations, and fatigue, but has no reported chest pain, dyspnea, visual disturbance, severe headache, or epistaxis to suggest an acute hypertensive emergency. Given her advanced age and coexisting coronary artery disease, blood pressure reduction should be gradual and individualized, with attention to orthostatic symptoms and fall risk.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding. She denies current anginal symptoms or shortness of breath. Persistent hypertension and smoking represent important modifiable cardiovascular risks.

Palpitations and intermittent dizziness. Heart rate is 75 bpm today. These symptoms warrant rhythm assessment, particularly in the setting of longstanding hypertension and coronary artery disease.

Hyperlipidemia, longstanding, with no current lipid values or treatment regimen provided.

Tobacco use. Ongoing smoking increases cardiovascular risk and may complicate blood pressure control.

Mild overweight by BMI at 28.7 kg/m².

Serum sodium is mildly elevated at 147 mmol/L, with potassium at the low end of the normal range at 3.6 mmol/L.

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management and home blood pressure monitoring, including seated readings and assessment for dizziness or symptomatic blood pressure reduction. Recommend review of home readings and confirmation of blood pressure with repeat office measurement, including orthostatic measurements given her age and dizziness.

Recommend initiation or adjustment of antihypertensive therapy after review of the complete medication history, renal function, orthostatic measurements, and cardiovascular status. Treatment should be titrated cautiously because of her age, dizziness, and known coronary artery disease. Avoid abrupt overcorrection.

Recommend a 12-lead ECG for evaluation of palpitations and assessment for hypertensive or ischemic cardiac changes. Further rhythm monitoring can be considered if palpitations persist or the ECG is unrevealing.

Recommend repeat serum electrolytes with assessment of renal function before and after antihypertensive therapy is started or adjusted. Encourage adequate hydration and moderation of dietary sodium, particularly processed and packaged foods.

Recommend lipid profile assessment and review of cardiovascular risk-reduction therapy in the context of established coronary artery disease and longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Counseled regarding smoking cessation. Encourage gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated, weight control, and a heart-healthy, reduced-sodium diet.

Advised urgent evaluation for new chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, severe or persistent headache, syncope, or markedly elevated blood pressure with concerning symptoms.
