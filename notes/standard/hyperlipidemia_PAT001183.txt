Chief Complaint

Annual review and follow-up of longstanding hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Brooklyn Barnes is a 97-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. Hyperlipidemia has been present for approximately 28 years and is not currently being actively treated. Recent lipid results show marked elevation of total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol with moderately elevated triglycerides. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain or intermittent claudication, dizziness, xanthomas, and xanthelasma.

She has longstanding obesity, present for approximately 30 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for approximately 7 years. She does not smoke. No current lipid-lowering medication is provided in the available history.

Objective

Height 151 cm; weight 74.8 kg; BMI 32.8 kg/m².

Blood pressure 132/81 mmHg, pulse 82 bpm, respiratory rate 21/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 95%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 296 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 200 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 47 mg/dL, triglycerides 247 mg/dL.

No xanthomas or xanthelasma are reported. No additional examination findings are provided.

Assessment

Longstanding hyperlipidemia with substantial elevation of LDL cholesterol at 200 mg/dL and elevated triglycerides at 247 mg/dL. She currently reports no cardiovascular or cutaneous symptoms attributable to dyslipidemia. Given her age, treatment decisions should be individualized according to overall health, functional status, life expectancy, treatment preferences, and potential adverse effects, while recognizing the significant atherosclerotic risk associated with this lipid profile.

Obesity, BMI 32.8 kg/m², longstanding.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding. The combination of obesity, fatty liver disease, and hypertriglyceridemia supports continued attention to dietary quality, weight, and metabolic risk reduction.

Plan

Discuss the lipid results and the potential benefits and risks of initiating lipid-lowering therapy in the context of her advanced age and personal goals. If consistent with her preferences and overall clinical status, consider starting a statin such as atorvastatin or rosuvastatin at an individualized dose, with monitoring for muscle symptoms and other adverse effects. Ezetimibe may be considered if statin therapy is not tolerated or does not adequately reduce LDL cholesterol. No medication is documented as started today.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, fiber, legumes, whole grains, and unsaturated fats while limiting saturated fats, trans fats, refined carbohydrates, and excess alcohol. Encourage activity appropriate to her mobility and safety, with gradual weight reduction if feasible and desired. Avoid crash dieting given her age.

Recommend confirmation with a fasting lipid panel if the current testing was not fasting and reassessment after any lifestyle or medication intervention. Consider evaluation for potentially reversible contributors to marked dyslipidemia based on clinical context. Additional risk stratification testing should be pursued only if it would change management.

Continue clinical management of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease through weight and metabolic risk reduction; avoid assuming liver-directed medication therapy without further evaluation.

Continue routine annual preventive care, including individualized review of immunizations, fall risk, vision and hearing, bone health, and age-appropriate cancer screening according to functional status and preferences.

Follow up after discussion of treatment goals and within a clinically appropriate interval to review the lipid management plan.
