Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Alice Mathew is a 39-year-old woman seen for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension diagnosed approximately 14 years ago. Blood pressure has not been actively managed recently. She denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. No acute hypertensive symptoms are reported today.

Her medical history is also significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 9 years, chronic kidney disease for 1 year, and coronary artery disease diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. She currently smokes. No medication regimen is provided for review today.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 159/90 mmHg, pulse 85 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99%.

Height 174 cm, weight 89.9 kg, BMI 29.7 kg/m².

Serum electrolytes: sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 4.1 mmol/L.

No symptoms or vital-sign evidence of an acute hypertensive emergency is present at this visit.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistently elevated office blood pressure and prolonged inadequate management. She is currently asymptomatic, without reported chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, visual changes, or epistaxis. Her diabetes, chronic kidney disease, coronary artery disease, overweight status, and active tobacco use increase cardiovascular and renal risk.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, 9-year duration, requiring integrated cardiovascular and renal risk reduction.

Chronic kidney disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. Electrolytes are currently within the provided reference range, with potassium 4.1 mmol/L.

Coronary artery disease, diagnosed approximately 1 year ago.

Overweight, BMI 29.7 kg/m².

Active tobacco use.

Plan

Discussed the importance of consistent blood pressure management given her diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease. Recommend confirming the blood pressure with repeat office measurements and home blood pressure monitoring, with a written log for review.

Recommend initiation or re-establishment of clinician-directed antihypertensive therapy, with medication selection guided by renal function, albuminuria status, diabetes, coronary disease, potassium level, and overall tolerance. Serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin assessment should be reviewed or obtained before and after treatment changes as clinically appropriate. Avoid unsupervised use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other agents that may worsen blood pressure or renal function.

Recommend ECG, urinalysis or urine albumin assessment, and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular and hypertension risk evaluation if not recently completed. Continue coordinated management of diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease.

Counseled regarding sodium restriction, avoidance of highly processed foods, gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and moderation of alcohol intake. Smoking cessation was strongly advised; offer cessation counseling and pharmacologic support at follow-up.

Reviewed warning symptoms requiring urgent evaluation, including chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, new neurologic symptoms, acute visual change, or confusion. Arrange close outpatient follow-up after blood pressure monitoring and treatment initiation or adjustment.
