Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Subjective

Ivy Henderson is a 71-year-old woman with a 19-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia for 10 years, anxiety for 21 years, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease for 32 years. She presents for routine outpatient follow-up and disease-control review. She reports no increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet. She does not smoke.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 128/76 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Height 161 cm, weight 57.3 kg, BMI 22.1 kg/m².

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.4%. Fasting glucose 226 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications. Glycemic control is substantially above goal based on HbA1c of 9.4% and fasting glucose of 226 mg/dL, despite the absence of reported hyperglycemic, constitutional, infectious, or neuropathic symptoms. Her normal BMI does not exclude clinically significant hyperglycemia. No diabetes-related complications are reported today.

Dyslipidemia, longstanding.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, longstanding.

Anxiety, longstanding.

Plan

Discussed that current glycemic indices indicate inadequate diabetes control and reviewed the importance of active treatment and ongoing monitoring. Clarify current diabetes treatment history and adherence, then initiate or intensify evidence-based glucose-lowering therapy as clinically appropriate, with consideration of renal function and comorbid conditions before selecting therapy.

Counseled regarding a consistent carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Recommend home glucose monitoring if appropriate to the eventual treatment plan, with education regarding symptoms of significant hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia.

Recommend assessment of renal status with serum creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, fasting lipid profile, and routine diabetes preventive care, including a dilated retinal examination and comprehensive foot assessment. Continue management of dyslipidemia and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease in the context of overall cardiometabolic risk. Follow up after treatment clarification and monitoring to reassess glycemic control.
