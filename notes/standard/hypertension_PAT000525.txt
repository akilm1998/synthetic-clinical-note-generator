Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for longstanding hypertension.

Subjective

Alexander Fisher is a 92-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of hypertension, present for approximately 47 years and currently without active management. He reports intermittent palpitations. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, or epistaxis. He does not smoke. History is notable for coronary artery disease of approximately 26 years’ duration. No interval hypertensive crisis symptoms are reported.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 150/90 mmHg, pulse 76 bpm, respiratory rate 17/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 95% on room air.

Height 161 cm; weight 73.4 kg; BMI 28.3 kg/m².

Laboratory data: Sodium 140 mmol/L; potassium 5.1 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings documented today.

Assessment

Essential hypertension, longstanding and above goal today in the setting of no current management. He is asymptomatic for acute hypertensive complications. Given his advanced age and coexisting coronary artery disease, blood pressure reduction should be gradual and individualized, with attention to orthostatic symptoms and fall risk.

Palpitations, intermittent, without associated chest pain, dyspnea, dizziness, or syncope reported.

Coronary artery disease, longstanding history.

Mildly elevated serum potassium at 5.1 mmol/L; this should be reassessed before considering agents that may further increase potassium.

Overweight by BMI.

Plan

Discussed the importance of addressing persistent hypertension and reducing cardiovascular risk. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with seated readings, including review of technique, and documentation of readings for follow-up. Encourage a lower-sodium eating pattern, regular activity as tolerated, weight management, and avoidance of excess alcohol. He remains a nonsmoker.

Recommend obtaining an ECG to evaluate the reported palpitations and assess for hypertensive or ischemic cardiac changes. He should seek urgent evaluation for sustained or worsening palpitations, chest pain, shortness of breath, syncope, new neurologic symptoms, severe headache, or markedly elevated blood pressure with symptoms.

Recommend repeat serum electrolytes with assessment of renal function before initiating or selecting antihypertensive therapy, particularly in view of potassium 5.1 mmol/L. Consideration should be given to cautious initiation of blood pressure treatment appropriate for an older adult with coronary artery disease, with close monitoring for orthostasis, renal function, and potassium. Lipid profile and urinalysis are also reasonable cardiovascular and hypertension evaluations if not recently completed.

Follow up after home blood pressure readings and recommended evaluation to update treatment and assess tolerance.
