Chief Complaint

Annual review of diabetes and chronic kidney disease.

Subjective

Kayla Pierce is an 81-year-old woman presenting for comprehensive annual review. She has a 13-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and a 33-year history of chronic kidney disease. Current glycemic control is markedly above goal, with hemoglobin A1c 9.4% and fasting glucose 263 mg/dL. She denies increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, recurrent infections, numbness, or tingling of the hands or feet.

She does not smoke. Weight is 53.4 kg at a height of 154 cm, with BMI 22.5 kg/m². No current diabetes medications are provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 137/81 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.9°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

General: Older woman presenting for routine outpatient follow-up; no acute symptoms reported.

Laboratory results: Hemoglobin A1c 9.4%. Fasting glucose 263 mg/dL.

Assessment

Type 2 diabetes mellitus without documented complications, currently poorly controlled based on A1c and fasting glucose. Despite significant hyperglycemia, she reports no classic osmotic, constitutional, visual, infectious, or neuropathic symptoms. Given her advanced age and longstanding chronic kidney disease, treatment intensification should be individualized with attention to renal function, hypoglycemia risk, nutritional status, and overall goals of care.

Chronic kidney disease, longstanding, reported duration 33 years. Current renal function parameters are not provided, so medication selection and dosing should be guided by updated kidney function testing.

Blood pressure is acceptable at today’s visit. BMI is normal. She is a nonsmoker.

Plan

Discussed that current glucose values indicate inadequate diabetes control and reviewed the importance of individualized glycemic targets in an older adult with chronic kidney disease. Review current medication use and adherence at the next medication reconciliation; no specific regimen is documented today. Consider initiation or adjustment of glucose-lowering therapy after assessment of renal function, oral intake, hypoglycemia risk, and patient preferences. Avoid overly aggressive glucose lowering.

Recommend updated serum creatinine with estimated glomerular filtration rate and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess kidney function and diabetic kidney involvement. Obtain a fasting lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk assessment. Continue coordination of diabetes and chronic kidney disease care, with renal dosing of any medications as appropriate.

Encourage a consistent carbohydrate-conscious eating pattern, adequate hydration appropriate to her kidney status, and regular low-impact physical activity as tolerated. Recommend home glucose monitoring if feasible, particularly with any treatment change, and review recognition and treatment of hypoglycemia.

Recommend comprehensive diabetic foot examination, including skin assessment, pulses, and sensation, at follow-up. Recommend routine dilated retinal examination and ongoing dental and skin care. Review age-appropriate immunizations and preventive screening based on health status, functional goals, and patient preferences.

Follow up after medication reconciliation and laboratory assessment, or sooner for persistent glucose elevation, vomiting, confusion, dehydration, new visual symptoms, foot wounds, or symptoms of hypoglycemia.
