Chief Complaint

Initial consultation for hypertension.

Subjective

Trevor Warren is a 36-year-old man presenting for initial outpatient evaluation of hypertension, reported to have been present for approximately 5 years and not currently under active management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, and epistaxis. He reports fatigue.

Past medical history is significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed approximately 1 year ago. No antihypertensive medications are provided in the current history. He does not smoke.

Height is 189 cm and weight is 105.4 kg, with BMI 29.5 kg/m².

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 140/94 mmHg, pulse 75 bpm, respiratory rate 15/min, temperature 36.6°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Available laboratory data:
Sodium 137 mmol/L.
Potassium 4.3 mmol/L.

No acute cardiopulmonary or neurologic symptoms are reported today.

Assessment

1. Essential primary hypertension, present for approximately 5 years and currently untreated/not actively managed. Blood pressure today is 140/94 mmHg, with diastolic elevation. He is asymptomatic without features suggestive of hypertensive emergency. Electrolytes are within expected limits.

2. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration approximately 1 year, increasing overall cardiovascular and renal risk in the setting of hypertension.

3. BMI 29.5 kg/m², in the overweight range, contributing to cardiometabolic risk.

4. Fatigue, nonspecific; may be multifactorial and warrants assessment in the context of his cardiometabolic comorbidity.

Plan

Discussed the importance of sustained blood pressure control, particularly given concurrent type 2 diabetes mellitus and the duration of untreated hypertension. Recommend home blood pressure monitoring with a validated upper-arm cuff, recording twice-daily readings under resting conditions, and bringing the log to follow-up.

Recommend initiation of clinician-directed antihypertensive therapy after review of repeat blood pressure measurements and baseline renal assessment. An agent acting on the renin-angiotensin system would be a reasonable consideration in a patient with diabetes, provided renal function and potassium remain appropriate. Medication selection and dosing should be individualized after review of the complete medication and allergy history.

Recommend lifestyle measures including reduction of dietary sodium, regular aerobic and resistance physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction toward a healthy range, moderation of alcohol intake if applicable, and a heart-healthy dietary pattern.

Recommend evaluation of cardiovascular and hypertension-related risk with renal function testing, urinalysis including assessment for albuminuria, lipid profile, and electrocardiogram. Continue routine diabetes care and coordination of blood pressure goals with diabetes management.

Seek urgent medical attention for severe or abrupt headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurologic symptoms, acute visual change, or markedly elevated blood pressure accompanied by symptoms.

Follow up in the outpatient setting after a period of home blood pressure monitoring, sooner if readings rise substantially or symptoms develop.
