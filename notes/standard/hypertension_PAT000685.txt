Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

John Jacob is a 50-year-old man presenting for outpatient follow-up of longstanding essential hypertension, present for approximately 17 years. Blood pressure has not been under active management. He reports fatigue but denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, or nosebleeds. No acute hypertensive symptoms are reported today.

Medical history is significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus for 6 years, coronary artery disease for 4 years, and chronic kidney disease for 2 years. He currently smokes. Weight is 62.0 kg at a height of 160 cm, with BMI 24.2 kg/m². No medication regimen is provided.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 149/104 mmHg, pulse 78 bpm, respiratory rate 14/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory data: Sodium 138 mmol/L, potassium 3.6 mmol/L.

General: No acute distress documented.

Assessment

Essential hypertension with persistent diastolic elevation in the setting of prolonged inadequate management. He is currently asymptomatic without findings suggestive of hypertensive emergency. His coexisting diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease increase his cardiovascular and renal risk and warrant prompt initiation or re-establishment of structured blood pressure management.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus, duration 6 years.

Coronary artery disease, duration 4 years.

Chronic kidney disease, duration 2 years.

Tobacco use.

Fatigue, nonspecific; may be multifactorial and should be reassessed after blood pressure and comorbidity management are addressed.

Plan

Recommend prompt clinician-directed initiation of antihypertensive therapy, with medication selection tailored to his coronary artery disease, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, renal function, and potassium level. A blood pressure goal should be individualized, with avoidance of overly rapid reduction.

Obtain home blood pressure readings using a validated upper-arm cuff, recording measurements at consistent times and bringing the log to follow-up. Reinforce sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, avoidance of excess alcohol, and smoking cessation.

Repeat serum electrolytes and obtain renal function testing before and after initiation or adjustment of therapy. Recommend urinalysis and assessment for urinary albumin as clinically appropriate in the setting of diabetes and chronic kidney disease. Recommend ECG and lipid profile as part of cardiovascular risk assessment.

Continue close management of diabetes, coronary artery disease, and chronic kidney disease. Review all current prescription and over-the-counter medications at the next visit to identify agents that may worsen blood pressure or renal function.

Seek emergency care for chest pain, new shortness of breath, neurologic deficits, severe headache, acute visual changes, or markedly higher blood pressure with symptoms.

Follow up promptly for blood pressure reassessment and treatment adjustment.
