Chief Complaint
Urgent follow-up for markedly elevated cholesterol and triglycerides.

Subjective
Joshua Ward is a 56-year-old man presenting for focused follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 13 years and currently untreated. Recent lipid results show total cholesterol 392 mg/dL, LDL-C 272 mg/dL, HDL-C 42 mg/dL, and triglycerides 389 mg/dL. He denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, xanthomas, or xanthelasma. He does not smoke. Comorbidities include type 2 diabetes mellitus for 4 years and obesity for 9 years.

Objective
Height 168 cm; weight 107.0 kg; BMI 37.9 kg/m². Blood pressure 125/79 mmHg, pulse 79 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 96%.

Lipid panel: total cholesterol 392 mg/dL, LDL-C 272 mg/dL, HDL-C 42 mg/dL, triglycerides 389 mg/dL.

Assessment
Severe untreated hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL-C and elevated triglycerides. No current symptoms suggestive of angina, claudication, or other acute vascular complication. His type 2 diabetes and obesity substantially increase cardiovascular risk.

Plan
Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the importance of prompt treatment and sustained lifestyle modification. Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after medication reconciliation and review of contraindications, with shared decision-making regarding treatment options. If LDL-C remains substantially elevated despite adherence, consider addition of ezetimibe and further risk-based therapy.

Recommend a heart-healthy eating pattern emphasizing reduced saturated and trans fats, avoidance of trans fats and excess refined carbohydrates, increased dietary fiber, weight reduction, and regular physical activity as tolerated. Continue tobacco avoidance.

Recommend evaluation for secondary contributors to dyslipidemia and consideration of familial or inherited lipid disorder given the markedly elevated LDL-C. Repeat a fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation to assess response. Consider ApoB and lipoprotein(a) measurement, with coronary artery calcium assessment only if needed for cardiovascular risk discussion. Coordinate ongoing management of diabetes and obesity. Seek urgent care for new chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, or acute leg pain with exertion.
