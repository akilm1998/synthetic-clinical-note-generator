Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hypertension.

Subjective

Tristan Burke is a 59-year-old man presenting for routine outpatient follow-up of essential hypertension, diagnosed approximately 7 years ago. Hypertension is not currently under active management. He denies headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, fatigue, or epistaxis. No interval hypertensive symptoms are reported.

Relevant cardiovascular history includes coronary artery disease of approximately 8 years’ duration. He currently smokes. Weight is 105.7 kg at a height of 189 cm, with BMI 29.6 kg/m². Dietary sodium intake, alcohol use, and physical activity were not characterized in the available history.

Objective

Vital signs: Blood pressure 155/92 mmHg; pulse 80 bpm; respiratory rate 15/min; temperature 36.8°C; oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

Laboratory data: Sodium 135 mmol/L; potassium 3.6 mmol/L.

No additional examination findings are documented for this encounter.

Assessment

1. Essential hypertension, currently above goal at 155/92 mmHg and not under active treatment. He is asymptomatic without features suggesting an acute hypertensive emergency. His cardiovascular risk is increased by established coronary artery disease, active tobacco use, and excess weight.

2. Coronary artery disease, longstanding, approximately 8 years by history. He denies current angina or heart failure symptoms.

3. Tobacco use, ongoing.

4. Overweight, BMI 29.6 kg/m².

Plan

Discussed the importance of active blood pressure management given the persistent elevation and coexisting coronary artery disease. Confirm blood pressure with repeat seated measurements and encourage home monitoring with a written log, including technique review and avoidance of nicotine or exertion immediately before measurement.

Recommend initiation or re-establishment of clinician-directed antihypertensive therapy, with selection individualized to his cardiovascular history, renal status, electrolyte profile, tolerability, and follow-up measurements. Review adherence and barriers at the next visit. Reassess serum electrolytes and renal function after treatment is initiated or adjusted; current sodium is borderline low-normal at 135 mmol/L and potassium is low-normal at 3.6 mmol/L.

Recommend dietary sodium reduction, regular physical activity as tolerated, weight reduction, and limitation of alcohol intake. Strongly counsel smoking cessation and offer evidence-based cessation support.

Recommend cardiovascular risk reassessment, including lipid profile and urinalysis, and obtain an ECG as part of hypertension and coronary artery disease evaluation. Advise urgent evaluation for chest pain, severe headache, acute dyspnea, focal neurologic symptoms, or visual changes.

Follow up after a short interval for blood pressure review and treatment adjustment.
