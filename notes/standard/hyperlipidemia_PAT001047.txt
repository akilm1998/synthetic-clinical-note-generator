Chief Complaint

Routine follow-up for hyperlipidemia.

Subjective

Pamela Donovan is a 43-year-old woman presenting for routine follow-up of hyperlipidemia, present for approximately 12 years and not currently under active management. She denies chest discomfort, exertional leg pain, dizziness, or other reported cardiovascular symptoms. She reports xanthelasma. She does not smoke. Weight is 67.3 kg at 173 cm (BMI 22.5 kg/m²). Chronic kidney disease has been present for approximately 1 year. No medication regimen was provided for review.

Objective

Vital signs: BP 132/80 mmHg, pulse 68 bpm, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.7°C, oxygen saturation 99% on room air.

Recent lipid profile:
Total cholesterol 355 mg/dL
LDL cholesterol 253 mg/dL
HDL cholesterol 38 mg/dL
Triglycerides 321 mg/dL

Xanthelasma is reported. No chest discomfort, intermittent claudication, or dizziness reported.

Assessment

Severe mixed hyperlipidemia with markedly elevated LDL cholesterol and hypertriglyceridemia, currently inadequately addressed. The LDL level is in a range associated with substantially increased lifetime atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, and the presence of xanthelasma supports longstanding lipid disorder. There are no reported ischemic or claudication symptoms. Chronic kidney disease is an important comorbidity when selecting and monitoring lipid-lowering therapy.

Plan

Discussed the significance of the lipid results and the need for active risk reduction despite normal BMI and absence of current cardiovascular symptoms.

Recommend initiation of high-intensity statin therapy after review of renal function, hepatic status, medication interactions, and shared decision-making. If LDL reduction remains inadequate or statin therapy is not tolerated, consider addition of ezetimibe and referral for specialist-directed management.

Recommend a heart-healthy diet emphasizing reduction of saturated and trans fats and refined carbohydrates, increased soluble fiber, and regular aerobic physical activity as tolerated. Continue tobacco avoidance.

Obtain a repeat fasting lipid panel after treatment initiation or adjustment to assess response. Recommend evaluation for secondary contributors to severe dyslipidemia and consideration of apolipoprotein B and lipoprotein(a) testing. Given the degree and duration of LDL elevation, assess for possible inherited dyslipidemia; family risk assessment should be obtained at follow-up.

Coordinate lipid management with monitoring of chronic kidney disease and renal function. Return for follow-up after updated testing to review response and finalize long-term risk-reduction strategy.
