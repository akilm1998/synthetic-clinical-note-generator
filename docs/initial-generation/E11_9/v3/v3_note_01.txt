Bob Hill is a 63-year-old male seen for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes mellitus. He reports feeling well overall. He denies increased thirst, frequent urination, polyuria, blurred vision, unexplained weight loss, chest pain, dyspnea, or lower-extremity swelling. No hypoglycemic symptoms, including tremor, diaphoresis, confusion, or syncope. He reports generally taking his diabetes medication as prescribed and checking glucose intermittently at home, with fasting readings typically 110–135 mg/dL. Diet has been variable, and physical activity is limited. He continues to smoke cigarettes. Uses CPAP for obstructive sleep apnea.

Vital signs: Blood pressure 132/78 mmHg, pulse 72 beats/min, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 98.1°F, oxygen saturation 96% on room air. Height 5 feet 10 inches, weight 218 lb, BMI 31.3 kg/m².

He is alert and in no acute distress. Oral mucous membranes are moist. Neck is supple without thyromegaly. Cardiovascular examination reveals regular rate and rhythm without murmur. Lungs are clear bilaterally with normal respiratory effort. Abdomen is soft and nontender. No peripheral edema. Skin is intact without ulcers. Diabetic foot examination shows warm feet with intact skin, palpable dorsalis pedis pulses bilaterally, and preserved light-touch sensation at all tested sites. No calluses or deformities noted.

Recent laboratory results reviewed: hemoglobin A1c 6.9%, fasting glucose 124 mg/dL, creatinine 0.96 mg/dL, estimated glomerular filtration rate 88 mL/min/1.73 m², and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio 9 mg/g. Lipid panel shows total cholesterol 168 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 91 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 46 mg/dL, and triglycerides 156 mg/dL. Liver enzymes within normal limits.

Assessment: Type 2 diabetes mellitus without current complications. Glycemic control is near goal without reported hypoglycemia. Renal function and urine albumin screening are normal. No evidence of peripheral neuropathy or active foot lesion on examination.

Continue current diabetes regimen and home glucose monitoring. Reinforced portion control, reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugary beverages, increased intake of vegetables and fiber, and gradual increase in physical activity as tolerated, with a goal of at least 150 minutes weekly. Encouraged weight reduction. Reviewed daily foot inspection and appropriate footwear. Recommend annual dilated retinal examination; patient will schedule this if not completed within the past year. Repeat hemoglobin A1c, metabolic panel, urine albumin screening, and lipid profile before the next follow-up visit.

Smoking cessation counseling provided. Discussed nicotine replacement and prescription treatment options; patient is not ready to set a quit date but will consider reducing use. Continue CPAP therapy for obstructive sleep apnea.

Follow up in approximately 3 months, or sooner for persistent glucose elevations, hypoglycemia, visual changes, foot wounds, or other concerns.
